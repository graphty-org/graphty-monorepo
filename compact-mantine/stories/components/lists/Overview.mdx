# Lists and trees

These are lists of the reader's own objects: things they select, open, rename, move and
search for. Each one keeps Figma's look (32px rows, a 24px pill for hover and selection) and
adds the keyboard model Figma lacks, with one Tab stop and the arrow keys inside.

The components render; your code owns the data. Selection and the current item can be
controlled or left to the component, but renaming and moving are only ever reported
(`onRename`, `onMove`), so your state stays the one source of truth.

## Reach for

- **`Tree`** when the rows are objects the reader selects, renames or reorders, nested or not:
  layers, style layers, a scene graph. A flat list that can be reordered is a Tree whose items
  have no `children` (see the `FlatReorderableList` story on the Tree page).
- **`PageList`** when the reader switches between a handful of flat places (pages,
  collections, saved views) and exactly one is current.
- **`ResultRow`** for search results driven from a `SearchInput`, with focus staying in the
  field.
- **`InlineRename`** when a row of your own needs Tree's and PageList's rename-in-place field.
  Tree and PageList open it for you on F2 or a double-click.
- **`DataRow`** (Data display) when a row is a reading: a name and a number, a statistic or a
  ranking, not an object the reader renames or moves.
- **`DataTable`** (Data display) when there are columns, or thousands of rows.

## Keyboard

Both lists have one Tab stop; the arrow keys work inside.

- **ArrowUp / ArrowDown, Home / End** move focus in both. PageList skips dividers.
- **ArrowRight / ArrowLeft** (Tree) open or close a parent, or step into and out of it.
- **Enter / Space** select in a Tree (Shift extends, Control / Command toggles) and switch to
  the page in a PageList.
- **F2 or a double-click** renames in both, while `onRename` is given.
- **Alt+ArrowUp / Alt+ArrowDown** (Tree) move the focused item one place among its siblings,
  while `onMove` is given.
- **`*` / Alt+L** (Tree) open every sibling / close everything.

Alt+ArrowUp and Alt+ArrowDown are the keyboard twin of dragging a row: the move is reported
through `onMove({ id, parentId, index })` exactly like a drop, an item never leaves its parent,
and focus stays on the item after your code has moved it.
