# Panels and rows

The containers and the layout pieces of a property panel. Everything here holds
other things: it decides where a control sits and how the panel is divided, not
what a control does. The controls themselves are under Inputs, Selection and
Colour.

Every piece is measured against one grid, Figma's 240px panel column, so rows
you build from these components line up with each other and with rows you lay
out yourself.

## Reach for...

- **[ControlSection](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-controlsection--docs)** when a subject of the panel needs a name, a rule below it and, where it folds, a chevron. The workhorse container.
- **[ControlGroup](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-controlgroup--docs)** when a cluster of rows inside a section or a pop-out needs a small legend and must never fold.
- **[ControlSubGroup](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-controlsubgroup--docs)** when a handful of rarely-opened settings must stay in the panel's flow, folded under a section. Usually an advanced settings button is the better answer.
- **[FieldRow](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-fieldrow--docs)** when one or two fields share a line. It owns the widths and the gaps.
- **[CompoundRow](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-compoundrow--docs)** when two or three values are parts of one thing, such as a colour and its opacity, and belong in one box.
- **[TrailingSlot](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-trailingslot--docs)** when you lay out a row by hand and need the fixed 24px slot every row ends with.
- **[ResizeHandle](?path=/docs/components-panels-and-rows-resizehandle--docs)** when the reader should be able to widen a side panel or move a split.

Outside this group:

- **[AdvancedButton](?path=/docs/components-actions-advancedbutton--docs)** (Actions) when a row or a section has settings most people never change; it is a gear that goes in a trailing slot or a section's `actions` and opens a pop-out.
- **[PanelField](?path=/docs/components-inputs-panelfield--docs)** (Inputs) for the field a FieldRow lays out.
- **[DataRow](?path=/docs/components-data-display-datarow--docs)** (Data display) for a read-only name and value, and **[Tree](?path=/docs/components-lists-and-trees-tree--docs)** (Lists and trees) for a list of objects the reader selects, renames or reorders.

## The grid these components keep

A panel is 240px wide and is spent like this:

```
16  +  88  +  8  +  88  +  8  +  24  +  8  =  240
pad   field  gut  field   gap  trail  pad
```

`PANEL_GRID` names every number in it, so your own rows can match without
retyping them. Three consequences are worth knowing:

- **A field is 88px, 184px beside a trailing control, or 192px alone.** Those
  are the only widths, which is what makes a column of rows line up.
- **The 24px trailing slot is always drawn**, even when it is empty. Dropping it
  when there is nothing to put in it is what makes one row end 32px short of the
  row above it.
- **A row is 32px tall**, and so are toggle rows and data rows: a 24px control
  in a 32px row. A section header is 40px.

## Choosing between a section and a group

Both name a run of rows. The difference is one question: does it fold?

- **`ControlSection` can fold.** It has a 40px header with the name, a chevron
  in the left gutter where it collapses, a dot for "something in here is not the
  default", an empty state, an explanation bubble, and buttons of its own in the
  header. It draws the panel's 16px and 8px padding itself and a 1px rule below.
- **`ControlGroup` never folds.** It draws a 16px legend above its rows and no
  rule, and it holds no state. Use it for a named cluster inside a section, such
  as Position or Alignment, or inside a pop-out.

If you find yourself passing `opened` and never changing it, you wanted a group,
or a section with `collapsible={false}`.

## The rule a section enforces

**A section always expands.** A chevron that reveals nothing is a defect, so
`ControlSection` draws a chevron only when there is something to expand, and it
warns in development if you give it neither children nor `empty`.

The controls a section shows without being opened are the ones commonly adjusted
*for that section's own job*. Everything rarer goes behind an `AdvancedButton` in
the section's `actions`, which opens a pop-out holding the rest.

**An advanced button is always an addition to a section that already shows
something**, never a section's only content. A section whose sole content is a
gear hides everything it does and leaves the reader nothing to read.

## Showing a word beside every drawing

An icon-first panel needs a way out for readers who do not yet know the
drawings. `PanelLabelsProvider` carries one preference, off by default, to every
component below it. A field row then draws a small caption above each field and
grows to 50px; with `labelPosition="inline"` a pair instead splits into two rows,
each with its word in a 72px column. A compound row moves its one name into a
column of its own. The word is always printed once.

```tsx
import { PanelLabelsProvider } from "@graphty/compact-mantine";

<PanelLabelsProvider showLabels={settings.showLabelsOnControls}>
    <NodeSizeSection />
</PanelLabelsProvider>
```

Read the preference in your own components with `usePanelLabels()`. Offering it
as a user setting is recommended; nothing in the library requires it.
