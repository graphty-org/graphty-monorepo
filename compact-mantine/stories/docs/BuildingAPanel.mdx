# Building a panel

The containers and the layout pieces. Everything here holds other things: it
decides where a control sits, not what the control does.

- **`ControlSection`** -- a run of controls needs a name, a rule above it, and a chevron that folds it away. The workhorse container.
- **`ControlGroup`** -- the same, but it must never fold, or its rule has to reach the edges of a padded container such as a pop-out.
- **`ControlSubGroup`** -- a handful of rarely-opened settings belong under a section you already have. Quieter than a section.
- **`FieldRow`** -- one or two fields share a line. It owns the widths and the gaps.
- **`TrailingSlot`** -- you are laying out a row by hand and need the fixed 24px slot every row ends with.
- **`AdvancedButton`** -- a row or a section has settings most people never change, and they belong behind a gear.

## The grid these components keep

A panel is a 280px column, and it is spent like this:

```
16  +  108  +  8  +  108  +  8  +  24  +  8  =  280
pad    field  gut   field   gap  trail  pad
```

`PANEL_GRID` names every number in it, so your own rows can match without
retyping them. Three consequences are worth knowing:

- **A field is 108px, or 224px if it is alone.** Those are the only two widths,
  which is what makes a column of rows line up.
- **The 24px trailing slot is always drawn**, even when it is empty. Dropping it
  when there is nothing to put in it is what makes one row end 32px short of the
  row above it.
- **A row is 32px tall**, except toggle rows, which pack at 24px, and data rows,
  which pack at 28px.

## Choosing between a section and a group

Both draw a rule, a name and some controls. The difference is one question: does
it fold?

- **`ControlSection` folds.** It also has a chevron, a keyboard-operable header,
  a dot for "something in here is not the default", an empty state, an
  explanation bubble, and buttons of its own in the header. It lines up on the
  panel's own 16px and 8px padding.
- **`ControlGroup` does not fold.** Everything it holds is on screen at once.
  Its `bleed` prop pulls its rule out through the padding of whatever contains
  it, which is what a group nested inside a pop-out needs.

If you find yourself passing `opened` and never changing it, you wanted a group.

## The rule a section enforces

**A section always expands.** A chevron that reveals nothing is a defect, so
`ControlSection` is built to make one hard to write: it draws a chevron only
when there is something to expand, and it warns in development if you give it
neither children nor an empty state.

The controls a section shows without being opened are the ones commonly adjusted
*for that section's own job* -- not the ones commonly adjusted across the whole
application. Everything rarer goes behind an `AdvancedButton` in the section's
`actions`, which opens a pop-out holding the rest.

**An advanced button is always an addition to a section that already shows
something**, never a section's only content. A section whose sole content is a
gear hides everything it does and leaves the reader nothing to read.

## Showing a word beside every drawing

An icon-first panel needs a way out for readers who do not yet know the
drawings. `PanelLabelsProvider` carries one preference -- off by default -- to
every component below it, and each one degrades in its own way: a field grows
its label word beside its glyph, and a row holding a pair of fields becomes two
single rows, each with its word in a 76px column. A pair never becomes a
two-line stack.

```tsx
<PanelLabelsProvider showLabels={settings.showLabelsOnControls}>
    <NodeSizeSection />
</PanelLabelsProvider>
```

Read the preference in your own components with `usePanelLabels()`. Offering it
as a user setting is recommended; nothing in the library requires it.
