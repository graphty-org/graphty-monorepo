# graphty brand: what was considered and set aside

This is the record of the colors, marks, type and details that were designed and compared before the
brand in [brand-guide.md](brand-guide.md) was chosen. Each entry has a thumbnail and the reason it
was set aside, so any of them can be picked up again. The labels (PK-A, G-W1 and so on) are the names
the options had while they were compared.

## Palettes

Three two-color palettes were drawn on the same mark and wordmark. Ink and lime won; its first
version used a darker olive lime, which was later brightened.

| Option                                    | Thumbnail                                                         | Why it was set aside                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Graphite + magenta (`#2e323c`, `#dd2c6d`) | ![graphite + magenta](explorations/palettes/graphite-magenta.png) | The strongest accent node at 16 px and unused in the field, and one magenta works on white and dark alike. Set aside for lime, which is more distinctive next to the many pink and red brands. |
| Ink + olive lime (`#2a3045`, `#6f9c00`)   | ![ink + olive lime](explorations/palettes/ink-olive-lime.png)     | The direction that won, but this olive read as dull and too dark; the brighter lime scale replaced it.                                                                                         |
| Indigo + amber (`#4f46e5`, `#c97c00`)     | ![indigo + amber](explorations/palettes/indigo-amber.png)         | Calm and safe, but indigo is the most common color in developer tools, and amber needs a darker step on white.                                                                                 |

## The mark's green on white

Bright lime `#c4f23a` is superb on dark and nearly invisible on white (1.3:1), so the mark's node on
white needed its own answer. Each thumbnail shows the mark on white, then on black with the bright
lime. G-W5, `#97c800`, was chosen: a solid green dot that still holds at 16 px.

| Option                                    | Thumbnail                             | Why it was set aside                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G-W1, bright lime with a thin ink outline | ![G-W1](explorations/greens/g-w1.png) | One green everywhere, but at 16 px the outline and the lime blend into a gray-green dot, and the children's lime details had no outline. |
| G-W2, lime 500 `#75a300`                  | ![G-W2](explorations/greens/g-w2.png) | Exactly 3:1, but too dark: it reads as olive, not lime.                                                                                  |
| G-W3, always on a dark tile               | ![G-W3](explorations/greens/g-w3.png) | Keeps lime on dark, but turns every white placement into a tile.                                                                         |
| G-W4, `#8fbf00`                           | ![G-W4](explorations/greens/g-w4.png) | Good, and a step duller than G-W5; the owner preferred the brighter step.                                                                |
| G-W6, `#a2d400`                           | ![G-W6](explorations/greens/g-w6.png) | Livelier, but the node starts to look lighter than the rings and softens at 16 px.                                                       |
| G-W7, `#aedf10`                           | ![G-W7](explorations/greens/g-w7.png) | The node turns pale on white and the ink rings carry the mark.                                                                           |
| G-W8, `#b8e826`                           | ![G-W8](explorations/greens/g-w8.png) | Washed out: a highlight, not a color, and very faint on a light gray tab bar.                                                            |
| G-W9, `#c4f23a` everywhere                | ![G-W9](explorations/greens/g-w9.png) | One value everywhere, but the faintest node on white.                                                                                    |

## Which circle carries the color

The accent can sit on any of the mark's three circles. N1, the solid ear node at the top right, was
chosen: it keeps the g whole and puts the color on the one part that is only a node.

| Option                     | Thumbnail                                      | Why it was set aside                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| N2, the middle ring (bowl) | ![N2](explorations/circles/n2-middle-ring.png) | Striking on dark and more color at 16 px, but on white a pale ring breaks the letter shape, and the ear node stops looking special. |
| N3, the bottom ring (loop) | ![N3](explorations/circles/n3-bottom-ring.png) | Same trade as N2: more color, but a pale ring on white and an ordinary ear node.                                                    |

## The heart in the Cytoscape co-brand

H1, rose red `#e8304f`, was chosen: it sits between red and pink and is a near complement of lime,
so neither color muddies the other.

| Option                 | Thumbnail                                  | Why it was set aside                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| H2, hot pink `#ff4f8b` | ![H2](explorations/hearts/h2-hot-pink.png) | The friendliest, but the lightest on white (3.11:1) and close to the set-aside pink palette. |
| H3, true red `#e02424` | ![H3](explorations/hearts/h3-true-red.png) | The clearest "love" sign, but reads as an error color inside an app.                         |

## Wordmark typefaces

Five typefaces were set beside the mark, all converted to outlines and all under the SIL Open Font
License. Outfit 500 with its alternate g (`ss01`) was chosen: its round, even strokes match the
mark's rings. Thumbnails are in the ink + olive lime palette of the time.

| Option                 | Thumbnail                                                        | Why it was set aside                                                                              |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Figtree 600            | ![Figtree](explorations/wordmarks/figtree-600.png)               | Clean and friendly, but its strokes are less round than the rings and it is common in product UI. |
| Onest 600              | ![Onest](explorations/wordmarks/onest-600.png)                   | Crisp, but narrower and more angular than the mark.                                               |
| Outfit 500, standard g | ![Outfit plain g](explorations/wordmarks/outfit-500-plain-g.png) | The chosen face; the alternate g suits the mark's looping g better.                               |
| Source Sans 3 600      | ![Source Sans 3](explorations/wordmarks/source-sans-3-600.png)   | Its two-storey g repeats the mark too literally and the face feels corporate.                     |
| DM Sans 600            | ![DM Sans](explorations/wordmarks/dm-sans-600.png)               | Pleasant, but heavier and more geometric than the mark's even strokes.                            |

## Mark directions

Many marks were drawn before the linked-circles g. These are the main directions set aside, in their
original colors.

| Option                                                       | Thumbnail                                                           | Why it was set aside                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Loop g: a two-storey g whose lower loop is a self-loop edge  | ![loop g](explorations/marks/round-1-loop-g.png)                    | The best family system of its round, but the pill-shaped loop read as a snowman or a person in a tub.                                |
| Depth: four nodes on a leaning line, shrinking with distance | ![depth](explorations/marks/round-1-depth.png)                      | The strongest non-letter silhouette at 16 px, but it can read as a matchstick, guitar or music note, and it does not carry the name. |
| Depth graph: one near node and three far ones                | ![depth graph](explorations/marks/round-1-depth-graph.png)          | Closest of all to Cytoscape's mark, and a blob that reads as "R" or "k" at 16 px.                                                    |
| Binocular g: ring node, cycle loop and ear                   | ![binocular g](explorations/marks/round-1-binocular-g.png)          | Its open bowl read as a smile.                                                                                                       |
| Perspective: four nodes on a tilted plane                    | ![perspective](explorations/marks/round-1-perspective.png)          | Read as a price tag.                                                                                                                 |
| Axis node: a hub with edges along a cube corner              | ![axis node](explorations/marks/round-1-axis-node.png)              | Generic ball-and-stick, close to igraph and NetworkX; three spokes can read as a share icon.                                         |
| Cut vertex: two cycles sharing one node                      | ![cut vertex](explorations/marks/round-1-cut-vertex.png)            | Read as an hourglass or a stick figure, and needed a separate 16 px drawing.                                                         |
| Depth pyramid                                                | ![pyramid](explorations/marks/round-3-pyramid.png)                  | The best 3D at large sizes, but a paper plane at 32 px and a smudge at 16 px.                                                        |
| Triangle                                                     | ![triangle](explorations/marks/round-3-triangle.png)                | Simple, but reads as a delta or a letter A.                                                                                          |
| Heavy triangle                                               | ![heavy triangle](explorations/marks/round-4-g1-heavy-triangle.png) | The clearest at 16 px and the runner-up, but five of six first-time readers also saw a phone's share icon in it.                     |
| Hub and three, the y                                         | ![hub and three](explorations/marks/round-4-g5-hub-and-three.png)   | Bold at 16 px, but read as a slingshot or a plain letter Y.                                                                          |
