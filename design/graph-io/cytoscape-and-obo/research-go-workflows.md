# How people who work with the Gene Ontology load the graph

## The direct answer

Almost everyone loads the whole ontology into memory, and almost nobody looks at the whole ontology. The full file is small enough to parse in seconds. The Gene Ontology (GO) had 43,303 terms in 2023: 27,993 biological process, 11,271 molecular function and 4,039 cellular component [1]. So tools read the complete file, then compute a section and show only that.

The section is almost always one of these:

- one namespace;
- the ancestors of a set of chosen terms (the "induced subgraph" up to the root);
- a GO slim of about 150 terms;
- the terms enriched in one experiment, often clustered or deduplicated.

Joining pieces is the normal case, not the exception. Every analysis joins the ontology to a gene annotation file. Visualization tools then join several experiments or gene lists into one network. Editors join GO to imported modules of other ontologies.

Who does what:

- Curators and editors load everything.
- Enrichment analysts load everything but test one namespace.
- People making figures view tens to a few hundred terms.
- Web browsers (QuickGO, AmiGO) serve one term's neighborhood on demand.

## Curators and ontology editors

GO editors open the whole editing file, go-edit.obo, in Protege. They need at least 4 GB of Java heap and run the ELK reasoner over it [2][3]. Terms from other ontologies are not edited by hand. Imports such as Uberon, ChEBI and CL arrive as modules that are regenerated by the Ontology Development Kit (ODK) and ROBOT, and are refreshed rather than edited [4].

ROBOT extract is how a piece of another ontology gets cut out. It works from a seed list of terms and offers several module types [5]:

- MIREOT: the seed terms plus their ancestors up to an optional top term;
- BOT: the seeds and everything above them;
- TOP: the seeds and everything below them;
- STAR: the smallest module that keeps the logic.

Releases are produced in three flavors [6]:

- go-basic: acyclic, using only is_a, part_of and the regulates relations, and safe for propagating annotations up the graph;
- go: adds has_part and occurs_in, which can create cycles;
- go-plus: also imports ChEBI, CL and Uberon, and ships only as OWL or JSON.

GO-CAM models are distributed one model per file, as JSON or as Turtle (TTL) from the noctua-models repository. The combined TTL file is deprecated, so anyone who wants a combined graph merges the models themselves [7].

## Bioinformaticians doing enrichment analysis

GOATOOLS loads the full go-basic.obo as a directed acyclic graph. It then reads annotations from GAF, GPAD or NCBI gene2go files and propagates gene counts up the is_a edges [8][9].

topGO works on one namespace at a time (BP, MF or CC). It builds the subgraph induced by the annotated genes and drops sparse terms with the nodeSize setting. In its example, that subgraph still has 822 nodes and 1,707 edges. Its plots show only the top few significant terms, set with firstSigNodes, and the package warns that plotting many nodes is unreadable [10][11].

clusterProfiler's goplot draws the induced graph of the enriched terms. Its simplify() function removes redundant terms by semantic similarity, and its showCategory argument caps how many terms are plotted [12][13].

PANTHER offers two annotation sets: "GO complete" and a reduced PANTHER GO-slim. Its earlier slim had 655 terms, and the newer one is more than four times larger [14].

g:Profiler pins each analysis to a quarterly Ensembl-based release and keeps archived versions for reproducibility [15].

In all of these the namespace is the unit of analysis. The annotation file is the second half of the input, and slims come from map2slim or GOATOOLS map_to_slim. The generic slim has 149 terms [16].

## People who visualize GO

BiNGO tests a gene list or a selected Cytoscape subnetwork. It draws only the significant terms plus the ancestors needed to connect them, and it can remap a full annotation onto an organism slim [17].

ClueGO compares two or more gene lists in one network and groups terms by kappa scores computed from the genes they share [18]. EnrichmentMap draws gene sets as nodes, with edges where they share genes. It loads several experiments at once, and its main tool for managing size is a pair of p-value and similarity cutoff sliders [19][20].

REVIGO exists because enriched lists are "long, unintelligible". It keeps a representative subset of terms and shows them as scatterplots, treemaps or graphs [21].

QuickGO and AmiGO show one term at a time: its ancestors, its children and an "inferred tree view". These views are served from an API (QuickGO's REST API, AmiGO's Solr backend, and the GO API), not from a downloaded file [22][23][24].

The typical view is tens to a few hundred terms, or one term's path to the root. The full DAG is never drawn. Cytoscape-style networks become unreadable well before 1,000 nodes, which is why every tool above ships a pruning control.

## Ontology engineers using OBO tools

obonet reads a whole OBO file into a networkx MultiDiGraph, and ancestors and descendants come from networkx calls [25]. pronto, backed by the Rust fastobo parser, also loads whole files. fastobo reads the Drosophila anatomy ontology in under half a second, while other Python libraries take from seconds to minutes. Some files, such as CL and Uberon, fail to parse in pronto and fastobo because of OBO syntax edge cases [26][27].

## Versions

GO asks users to cite the monthly release by its DOI. Releases are archived at release.geneontology.org and on Zenodo [28]. Enrichment results drift between GO versions, and early and later releases agree poorly [29].

An analysis is defined by two dates: the ontology release and the annotation date. Mixing them causes errors. Annotations can point at obsolete or merged terms, and the 2025 release removed about 10% of terms [1].

## Implications for a graph tool

**Loading a subset.** Parse the full file; it is about 43,000 nodes. Offer filters as first-class views:

- by namespace;
- the ancestors of a seed set (the MIREOT/BOT shape);
- descendants (the TOP shape);
- a slim, by subset tag.

go-basic should be the default. For go and go-plus, edge types must be selectable, because has_part and the cross-ontology edges add cycles.

**Expanding a neighborhood on demand.** This matches QuickGO and AmiGO: start from one term and grow its parents and children step by step. Doing this against the loaded graph is cheaper and works offline. An API backend is optional.

**Joining annotations to terms.** A GAF/GPAD join that attaches gene counts or lists to terms is essential. So is propagating counts up is_a and part_of. Enrichment p-values and fold changes should arrive as term attributes, and filters on them should drive what is shown.

**Several sources at once.** Users overlay two to many gene lists or experiments on one term graph (ClueGO, EnrichmentMap), and editors merge imported ontology modules. The tool needs:

- per-source attributes, rather than overwriting one value;
- a recorded ontology release and annotation date for each source;
- a warning when sources mix releases or refer to obsolete term IDs.
