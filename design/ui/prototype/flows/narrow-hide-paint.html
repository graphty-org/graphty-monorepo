Narrow, hide or paint -- which one did you do?

Three moves take a kind of node "out of" a graph, and on the canvas they can look alike. Only one of them changes the numbers. Believing you filtered when you only hid or painted is a likely wrong conclusion -- a hypothesis the user study will test, not a finding yet. This flow shows what each move changes, how it is undone, and the one place that tells you which you did: the filter chip under the project name.

The situation. Sarah, a fraud analyst, has opened transfers-2026-03.csv: 3,000 accounts and 9,113 transfers from March 2026. Sixty accounts are merchants, and 5,302 of the transfers have a merchant at one end. The busiest merchant, ACC-393859, has 907 transfers and alone sets the graph's maximum degree. She wants statistics that describe people and businesses without the merchants' pull. She starts either from the value merchant in the table's kind column, which is a rule, or from that merchant on the canvas, where Select same value gives her 60 selected nodes, which is a fixed list.

What she can claim at the end. With Filter out: "these numbers describe the 2,940 accounts that are not merchants." With Hide on canvas: "the merchants are out of the picture, and every number still describes all 3,000 accounts." With paint: "merchants are drawn in their own color by a layer anyone can see, reorder or remove; no number changed." Why the start matters: a step made from the kind value is a rule, so after Update with new data it also catches next month's new merchants; a step made from the 60 selected nodes is a fixed list and does not.

The flow

From either start, one question routes the analyst: should the merchants still count in the numbers? Each exit is named by its consequence, and the command's name is on the box it leads to. The double line is the way back when the chip shows the wrong move was made.

Where graphty departs from Figma, and why. Figma keeps a hidden layer in the layers list with its eye off; graphty keeps hidden elements on the legend's hidden line instead, because nodes are not layers and the order of the Styles list means precedence. The command says "Hide on canvas" rather than being a plain toggle because hiding and filtering look alike on the canvas and do opposite things to every number. What stays as in Figma: the hide chord, visibility kept separate from opacity, and Select same value working like Select matching layers.

Counts, failures and the keyboard

  • Steps from rest (a step is a command or a choice; opening a menu is travel): Filter out, 2 steps and 1 travel from the value (the value; Filter out), 3 steps from the canvas (the merchant; Select same value; Filter out). Hide on canvas, the same counts. Color by kind, 1 step and 1 travel (the header menu; Color by). A set's color from the canvas, 4 steps and 1 travel (the merchant; Select same value; Create set; a color). By hand, 3 steps and 1 travel. Figma, for all merchants: select one; Select matching layers; the hide chord -- 3 steps. Painting them there: select one; Select matching layers; a fill color -- 3 steps and 1 travel. From the canvas, graphty's hide matches Figma's count; the set route costs one more step, Create set, which makes the color a layer anyone can find again.
  • First failure: taking the hide or paint exit when later numbers should have left the merchants out. The chip does not move, and the absence of a change is a weak signal. The failure question sends her to Show all, then Filter out. If she only filtered and left the hide on, turning the filter step off would bring the chip back to Full graph while the merchants stay invisible -- a canvas that looks filtered and is not. That is why the route shows everything first.
  • Second failure: a result run before the filter still describes all 3,000 accounts. Its state line says "on: full graph" and it is not "Out of date"; Run computes it again on the filtered graph.
  • Questions for the sessions (hypotheses, not observations): an analyst who uses Figma presses the hide chord on the merchants and, as in Figma, they vanish. Does she notice that the chip still reads Full graph before quoting a statistic? Does an analyst who painted the merchants gray believe they are "gone"?
  • Keyboard: Quick actions reaches Filter out, Hide on canvas, Show all and Color by. A column value's menu opens with Shift+F10 or the Menu key; set rows follow the tree keys; a legend entry has no keyboard route yet.

Changes this flow proposes, and what the study will test

Recorded in framework-changes.md. The two wording ideas are design reasoning to be tested in sessions (the hypotheses and tasks), not findings.

  • The router draws its failure question and recovery route, so every exit of the named first failure is visible.
  • An undo entry is named by what the step did: "Extract filtered graph (2,940 nodes) as graph", not after a rule that Filter out removed.
  • The legend's hidden line counts edges as well as nodes, and the two wordings the design gives for it are made one. Whether it should add "still counted" is to be tested.
  • Statistics keeps no scope line of its own, as the design's principle "marks only on departure" says. Whether a stronger chip is enough, or Statistics needs its own scope line, is to be tested before anything changes.
  • Figma's route is counted honestly: Select matching layers then the hide chord, 3 steps, not a 2-step type filter.
  • Hidden, then filtered out: what the hidden line counts while a filter step also leaves those elements out is not defined yet.
