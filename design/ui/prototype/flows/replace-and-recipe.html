Update with new data, apply a recipe, compare with the last version

Sarah, a fraud analyst, built her mule-ring review on March's 3,000 accounts. April's two files arrive with 3,093 accounts. She updates the project with new data, which swaps the data under the same analysis, reads what changed and what replayed, re-runs the one result that was too slow to replay on its own, applies her team's recipe and a recipe of the team's colors, and then compares April's communities with March's before she exports the table for her manager. A second telling follows a biologist opening a colleague's recipe that brings its own network and needs his table.

Every shape with a screen links to the state it shows (the 12 states of the update and recipe screens, light and dark, with annotations). Counts are the kit's March and April transfer data. The screens still show the earlier command label and the earlier replay report; the three new pieces this flow adds (the version row's What changed, the large-change mark and the export's comparison column) are drawn inline below.

Not buildable yet. graphty-element does not have these today, and the app must not stand in for any of them: a load preview before anything changes; data versions and Data > Versions; the count split between two versions (what is in both, new, and not in the newer one) and the large-change flag on it; applying a recipe with its report of what matched; replacing the whole style stack; one undo entry for an update or a recipe; the replay queue, its combined cost and its report; results marked out of date; comparing a partition across two data versions; and the table export's comparison column. The app only shows what graphty-element publishes: it never counts nodes, splits counts or decides that a change is large.

The route: two directions, two endings

Update with new data keeps the analysis and swaps the data; Apply recipe keeps the data and brings the analysis. Each opens a binding step only when something did not match. They share that pattern, not their endings: only an update replays results and adds a data version with its What changed summary.

Update with new data... is one command in two places: the Data panel's header, where the data lives, and the project-name menu, where a weekly user looks first on a reopened project. Both carry the same description under the label: "Replaces nodes and edges. Keeps styles, sets, notes and runs." When the new files have the same columns as the data loaded now, the load step's main button is Replace March data and Enter chooses it; Add to March data is the second button.

a place in the app a dialog or picker a decision a trust check, on screen without navigating a committed change: one undo entry error, cancellation, the other button or a way back

Back is the arrow in a binding step's header: it returns one step (to the load step, or to the recipe picker or preview) with every choice kept. Esc never goes back; it is Cancel. Add to March data is the load step's second button, drawn dashed because the weekly user rarely means it: it stacks April on March, and the step says so in numbers before she chooses. A recipe opened first from the start screen waits on a blank canvas for data, then takes the file picker and the load step; its whole route is drawn in the recipe travels storyboard.

Update with new data, step by step

1 Update with new data...

On the Data panel's header and in the project-name menu. Under the label, in smaller type: "Replaces nodes and edges. Keeps styles, sets, notes and runs." and then the cost, "1 slow result will wait for Re-run". The four quick replays finish in seconds, so they carry no band. Screen 1 (drawn on the File menu, under the earlier label)

2 The load step

She picks both April files. "Same columns as accounts-2026-03.csv and transfers-2026-03.csv, the data already loaded." April beside March: 3,093 accounts (March: 3,000), 8,370 transfers (March: 9,113), no rows dropped; 2,961 in both, 132 new, 39 not in April. The main button, focused, is Replace March data. Beside it, Add to March data says what it would leave: 3,132 accounts and 17,483 transfers, the 39 not in April still in. Screen 2

3 Replace, or bind first

Every column matched, so Enter replaces: 3 steps. Had April's export renamed riskScore to risk_score, the main button would read Continue to binding, the step would ask one choice per unmatched column (each saying what it feeds), and its Apply would replace. Either way it is one undo entry, "Replace data with the April files". Screen 3, the binding branch

4 The replay

April is drawn at once, arranged positions kept. Four results replay under one notice with Cancel. Cycles up to 6 transfers is not started: it reads Out of date, a few minutes, Re-run. Screen 4

5 What changed

The notice: "Data updated: 4 of 5 results replayed". Show report opens Data > Versions on April's row. Its What changed lines restate count splits and nothing else: "27 components (was 1): 26 accounts have no transfers in this version", marked large change; "65 communities (was 35): 26 are single accounts with no transfers in this version", marked large change. No line guesses why an account went quiet. Drawn below; Screen 5 shows the earlier report

6 Re-run

The out-of-date result is pinned at the top of the Results panel, says what it was computed on ("March data, Apr 3") and is counted on the Results rail button. Re-run starts it with its band; the count clears. Screen 6

One undo entry added 3 sets and 3 layers, each marked new with "recipe" as its kind. No data changed, nothing replayed and no data version was added. The notice says what arrived and carries Undo; the unbound layer offers Bind.... Screen 9

A recipe that brings its own network and needs your table

Tom, a biologist, opens Expression overlay, a recipe his colleague Maren sent. It brings the lab's network, "Sender's network: STRING v12" (ppi-core-300.graphml), which carries a log2FoldChange column from an earlier screen, and it asks for a fold change from Tom's own table. The recipe marks that need as supplied by the recipient, so the network's column is never offered for it, even though its name is the one Maren used.

Apply waits, and says why

Before Tom adds anything, the card reads "Recipe waiting for your table" and, under the name, "Saved by Maren Holt on Sep 26 2026", so he knows whose recipe it is before he adds his data. Add your table... is the main button and takes focus. Apply is disabled but still focusable, so a screen reader reads it with its reason, which is printed beside it, not hidden in a tooltip: "Waiting for your table". Nothing on the page says everything was found: the network's own fold-change column does not count. Start screen, recipe pending

Only his columns

He adds qpcr-hits-2026-09.csv (symbol, log2FC, padj); 84 of his 96 genes are in the network. The fold-change row offers the table's columns only: log2FC, 84 matched values, -2.41 to 2.98. The network's log2FoldChange, 300 values, is never listed for a need he supplies. Apply commits one undo entry. Recipe travels storyboard

The column is missing

A table without log2FC stops the route: "Your table has no log2FC column. Ask the sender which column they meant." Apply stays disabled and no column is proposed in its place, because only Maren knows which of his columns she meant. Close recipe leaves nothing open.

A recipe of styles

A team's colors arrive as a recipe that holds only style layers. It comes in by a drop, by Main menu > Recipes > Apply recipe..., or by the Style stack's + > From a recipe or file..., and every route opens the same Apply dialog with two choices, neither preselected: Use these styles... (the recipe's layers take the place of each of hers that writes the same property; layers from an algorithm always stay) and Add these styles on top.... A layer whose attribute is not in this graph gets one row in the binding step.

Compare with the last version, then hand it on

The version row says 65 communities, was 35, and that 26 of them are single accounts with no transfers. Sarah's next question is whether the groups that do hold transfers really changed, or whether Louvain, which is random, would group March differently on a second run anyway. The comparison answers against that yardstick; the export then carries the version comparison into her manager's spreadsheet. The full comparison surface is drawn in Compare with the previous data version.

13 Compare with another run...

On the Louvain row, or on April's row in Data > Versions, where it reads "Compare with another version...". The picker lists March data, Apr 3 first, because it is the version before this one. Two steps from the result. Compare flow

14 Read it against a re-run

The two months agree at AMI 0.449, over the 2,961 accounts in both. Beside it, what randomness alone does: re-runs on March agree at 0.759 to 0.768, on April at 0.811 to 0.851. The month-to-month change is larger than a re-run's, so it is real. Save comparison keeps it, with its sides and counts.

15 The export column

Export..., Table. Because the project has an earlier data version, the dialog offers one more column, "Compared with Apr 3", ticked. Errors, ways back and out-of-date results

The file cannot be read

The load step stays open with "Could not open transfers-2026-04.csv: {cause}" and the one verb the cause needs (Read as... for a wrong format). March is untouched.

Cancel, Esc, close

In the picker, the load step or a binding step: nothing is applied, no undo entry is made, and focus returns to what opened the dialog. Esc is always Cancel, as in Figma's stepped dialogs.

Back

Only the arrow in a binding step's header goes back: to the load step, the recipe picker or preview, or a dropped file's choices, with every choice kept. A binding step opened straight from a menu has no arrow.

Add when she meant Replace

Add to March data is the second button; choosing it stacks April on March (3,132 accounts, 17,483 transfers), as the load step said beside the button. One Undo takes it back, and Update with new data... again replaces.

Left unbound

Never skipped silently: what the attribute feeds is kept, switched off and marked "missing attribute", listed in the report, with Bind... on its row.

Out of date after the replay

A result estimated at a few minutes or more is not replayed on its own: it reads Out of date, names the version it was computed on, gives its band and offers Re-run. It stays pinned in the Results panel and counted on the rail button until it runs.

The replay is canceled

Cancel on the running notice stops the replay only. The data stays replaced; unfinished results read Out of date and wait for Re-run like the slow one. Undo, not Cancel, takes back the data.

A replayed run fails

Its row reads Failed with Re-run and the cause on its state line; the notice counts it ("Data updated: 3 of 5 results replayed; 1 failed").

Undo

Undo takes back the newest entry first. After a Re-run, the first Undo discards the re-run result; the second takes back the whole update, and March returns with its results current and April's version row gone. One Undo takes back a whole recipe, styles-only or not.

A version with no earlier one

A project's first data version has no What changed and the export offers no comparison column: there is nothing to compare with.

What these flows claim

TasksReuse an analysis on new data (Update with new data); start from a recipe, including one that holds only styles (Apply recipe), including one a colleague sent with its own network; compare with the previous data version.
ServesThe weekly return: update with the new files, read what changed, show the manager. A recipe travels: apply it to your own table.
Starts fromThe graph at rest, with nothing selected; a file dropped on the canvas; or a recipe opened from the start screen.
Size of graphThe same route at every size. The command names a result that will wait; the load step counts what replays and gives the waiting result's band.
How Figma does itApply recipe is Figma's Swap library: 2 steps, matched by name, instances updated in place, a notice with Undo, and no report page. graphty adds one choice per unmatched attribute, after Figma's Missing Fonts dialog. A recipe of styles is Figma's enabling a library: 1 step, adds styles and replaces none; graphty's Apply dialog adds Use these styles, which replaces her layers that write the same property. Update with new data has no Figma counterpart: Figma never swaps content under kept styling. The comparison is Figma's branch review: two states side by side.
Where it differs from Figma, and why
  • Each data version keeps its What changed and replay report in Data > Versions (Figma's version history holds no reports), so which results replayed on which data, and how the counts moved, can be checked later.
  • An out-of-date result stays pinned at the top of Results with a count on the rail button (Figma's toolbar carries no counts), because a result computed on last month's data must stay visible until it is re-run.
  • A dropped recipe opens its preview (Figma applies a library without one), because a recipe shows what it carries and needs before Apply; the preview is where Apply sits, so it adds no step.
  • Update with new data shows the replay's cost on the command, because it starts runs a style swap never does.

Questions for the study

The design has an intended answer to each; the sessions check it. Tasks are worded so they do not name where to look.

Where she may stallIntendedTask for the session
Does she find the update on a reopened project, and replace rather than add?Update with new data... in the project-name menu or the Data panel; Enter on Replace March data."This week's files are in. Do what you did last week."
Can she explain the jump from 1 to 27 components without working it out by hand?She reads the marked line on April's row and says the 26 accounts had no transfers, without saying why they went quiet."Your manager asks why the network fell apart this month. What do you tell her?"
Does the export column go into her spreadsheet as it is?She keeps "Compared with Apr 3" ticked and filters on "no longer present"."Send your manager a list of the accounts that were here last time and are not now."
Does she read Esc as going back a step?She uses the header's arrow to go back and Esc to leave."You picked the wrong column for the risk score two screens ago. Fix it without starting over."

What the sessions showed, and what this flow now does about it (round-3 findings):

  • Jumps were not explained, or explained wrongly. 35 to 65 communities and 1 to 27 components stood with no reason; one participant built a wrong one from two unrelated 39s, another worked it out by hand. Now: April's version row says what each count splits into ("26 accounts have no transfers in this version") and marks the two jumps large change. It never gives a motive: a count can be checked, a reason cannot.
  • "Closed" invented a fact. The load step and the comparison called the 39 accounts closed; the data says only that they had no row in April, and one participant would not put the word in front of her manager. Now: "not in April" on every screen, and "no longer present" in the export.
  • No export marked what was new. Participants would rebuild the week's changes with lookups in a spreadsheet. Now: the Table export's "Compared with Apr 3" column.
  • The weekly update had no visible home, and Add data was the blue button. Now: Update with new data... on the Data panel and the project-name menu, and Replace is the main button when the columns match.
  • Kept, because it worked: the load step's counts, which every participant reconciled; the same-columns warning that caught stacking a second month by mistake; the one slow result waiting for Re-run; one undo entry per update or recipe; unbound parts kept and switched off, never dropped; a recipe that waits for the recipient's table.

Where this flow departs from the framework

  • Replace data is labeled Update with new data..., on the Data panel's header and in the project-name menu, with one description: "Replaces nodes and edges. Keeps styles, sets, notes and runs." A rename, not a second command. It replaces the earlier proposed label, Update with a new export....
  • The load step's main button follows the columns: Replace March data when every column matches the data loaded now, Continue to binding when some do not, and Add to March data is always there as the second button with what it would leave.
  • A data version carries What changed: count splits against the version before it, which graphty-element computes, with a large-change mark when a count moved by half or more of its earlier value. It never states a motive. This replaces the earlier "new single-node groups" line.
  • "Not in April", never "closed"; the export says "no longer present", never "dropped", which already means rows lost at import.
  • The Table export gains a "Compared with {date}" column whenever an earlier data version exists, with the values new, in both and no longer present.
  • A recipe marks the needs the recipient supplies. For those, Add your table... is the main button, Apply waits with "Waiting for your table" printed beside it, the picker never lists a column from the recipe's own network, a missing column says to ask the sender, and no column is proposed in its place.
  • An update and Apply recipe end in different places. The framework joins them at one commit and one report; a recipe changes no data, so only an update replays, reports and adds a version.
  • Esc in a binding step is Cancel, not "back to the picker"; going back is the header's arrow. This follows Figma's stepped dialogs.
  • A recipe of styles opens the same Apply dialog as any recipe, with Use these styles and Add these styles on top, neither preselected; there is no separate style-file command.
  • A dropped recipe opens its preview before the match check, where Apply sits.
  • The update command names what will wait, the load step counts what replays, a slow result waits for Re-run naming the version it was computed on, Cancel stops only the replay, and a binding row says what it switches off.
  • Undo order after a replay: the Re-run is taken back before the update.

Each is written up with its evidence in framework-changes.md.
