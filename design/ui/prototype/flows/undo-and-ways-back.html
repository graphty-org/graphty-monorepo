Undo and the other ways back

An analyst has narrowed Les Miserables (77 characters) in three filter steps. The middle one was a mistake; the other two are work to keep. A stray click has also cleared the 18 characters they had picked out. In graphty, Undo never deletes a filter step: it unticks it, and the row stays in the steps list. So Undo pressed once too often costs a tick, not a step, and every good step is one checkbox away. This flow shows that model end to end, then the other ways back -- turning a step off directly, deleting a step, and Ctrl+Z bringing back a cleared selection -- and the one place a step can still be lost.

Where it starts

The filter chip under the project name reads Filtered: 27 of 77 nodes · 3 steps. Its list of filter steps shows them in the order they apply, each row saying what it took out and what is left:

StepRuleThe row readsThe analyst meant it?
1. Filter todegree >= 2took out 17 · 60 leftYes: drop the characters with a single tie
2. Filter todegree >= 5took out 20 · 40 leftNo: it cut too deep
3. Filter outgroup 8took out 13 · 27 leftYes: the barricade students, out on purpose

The goal is steps 1 and 3 on and step 2 off: 47 of 77, 2 of 3 steps, and the 18 characters selected again. The clickable screen is Undo and ways back; it opens in exactly this state.

The model, in four rules

  1. Undoing a step unticks it. The row stays in the list, off, with "--" for its count. Only Delete step removes a row.
  2. Every undo and redo of a filter step shows one line in the notice area: Undone: Filter out group 8 with the action Show in steps (Redone: ... for Redo). It is announced politely and never takes focus. It stays until the next change, never on a timer, and Undos in a row add to it, so a second Undo names both steps instead of replacing the first line unread. It shows whether or not the filter chip is in view, because a filter step changes counts, not the look of the canvas.
  3. Ticking a step on or off is an ordinary change. It goes on the undo list and clears Redo like any change. Nothing is lost when it does, because the rows are still there.
  4. Every count says what it was counted on while a step is on: Statistics reads Components 3, on: filtered graph. The count explains the loose characters on the canvas by itself; no step is explained by another step.

Why it changed

In the second round of the study this was the most severe finding, seen in seven sessions. Participants pressed Ctrl+Z by reflex, which removed the good last step from the list with no message; only the chip's count changed. One then unticked the wrong step, which cleared Redo, and the good step was gone from the list, Redo and Undo history ("That's me reconstructing my own work from a legend."). Six of the seven roles in the focus group arrived at the same rule: undo unticks, it never deletes. What worked is kept: unticking the wrong step directly, Ctrl+Y for Redo off macOS, Undo history saying how far each entry goes. In the fourth round every analyst who lost a selection pressed Ctrl+Z first, so Ctrl+Z now brings a cleared selection back, and the separate Previous selection command is gone.

Figma, and where this differs

As in Figma: Delete never asks and shows no notice; selection is outside undo; undo is linear, with no branches; there is no Undo button in the header. graphty departs in these places, each for a reason that comes from the graph:

  • Undo of adding a filter step unticks it, where Figma's undo of adding a layer removes it. A filter step changes what every number is computed over, and one that silently disappears cannot be found again.
  • Undo and Redo of a filter step show a line, "Undone: {name} Show in steps", which stays until the next change; Figma gives Undo no feedback. A filter step changes counts, not the look of the canvas, so without the line an undo passes unseen.
  • Edit > Undo names the next step ("Undo Filter out group 8"); Figma's reads plain "Undo".
  • Ctrl+Y also redoes, off macOS. Figma on Windows takes only Ctrl+Shift+Z.
  • The Undo history submenu, which Figma does not have. Interim; its entries say how many steps they take back.
  • A checkbox, not an eye, turns a filter step off. Turning a step off changes what is counted, so it is not hiding.
  • Ctrl+Z restores a cleared selection while nothing else has changed; Figma keeps selection out of undo. Photoshop's Select > Reselect is the nearest model.
