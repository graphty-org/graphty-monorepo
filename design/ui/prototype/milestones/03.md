# Milestone 3: the second user study, and the mocks revised after it

Nineteen simulated personas tried 30 tasks (18 repeated, 12 new): 105 sessions and 4 focus
groups. 101 of 105 finished, 8 without a wrong turn or a hint (round 1: 1 of 71), and average
ease rose from 3.65 to 4.20 of 7. Counting four tasks the screens could only partly support as
partial, 85% fully met the task. Participants are simulated: compare rounds with each other, not
with published norms.

What worked: the finished community result, rank ranges on sampled runs, the "where your data
goes" page and the Add-data warning. Comparing groups rose from 2.8 to 5.0 in ease. What got
worse: a reflex Ctrl+Z took back the filter step people wanted to keep, once for good.

The most severe problems, each now changed: Undo unticks a step and never deletes it. No measure
uses a weight until you say what a higher number means. The export preview is the file written,
and names colors that print as the same gray. A shared recipe waits for the recipient's own
table, never the sender's column. Transfers show dates. An empty Find says what it searched.
About 30 sessions were thrown off by the mocks (counts that disagreed, a wrong edge in Les
Miserables, the dataset changing mid-task); every study task now has consistent screens. The
design has not converged: round 3 must show no new severe problem.
