Apply a recipe: what matched, before anything changes

For the owner and the studio. Not for study participants: the captions describe what the reader does. Session tasks are at the bottom of the page.

A recipe is a colleague's saved analysis with no data inside. The person who saves it says which inputs the person applying it must bring from their own table. Applying one opens the Apply recipe dialog, which shows what the recipe adds and what it reads. Until the reader's own table is added, Apply is off and the reason is printed beside it: the sender's network may carry a column of the same name, and it is never used in place of the reader's. Once the table is in, the dialog says how many genes matched, names each one that did not, asks only what it cannot decide, and commits everything with Apply, which one Undo takes back as a whole.

The same lab recipe throughout: "Expression overlay", saved by Maren Holt, the lab lead, and applied by Tom, a lab member, on the lab's 300-protein interaction network (ppi-core-300, built from STRING v12). Every number comes from the kit's protein network and Tom's 96-row qPCR table.

What a weight means is asked once, on the column, and every measure and the Path tool read that one answer. A recipe carries the answer its runs used; the dialog asks only when your column already has a different answer.

0. What the sender sees: the same recipe, before it goes

Maren shares the overlay from the Export dialog with Recipe checked (the whole dialog). Its preview is the other side of what Tom will meet. After "No data inside", one line says what her recipient has to do, in the same words Tom's dialog uses. Travels lists the steps in the order they run when applied, the filter first and then the runs, and says the runs read the filtered graph. The layout is named, ForceAtlas2, and its settings sit under Details. Asked for says where each input comes from, the recipient's table or the network. The last column is called Not included: the network is named there, so she can see that the recipe points at it but does not carry it.

4. Nothing matched

This time Tom added the export his qPCR software makes by default, which names genes by a different kind of ID. Not one of its 96 rows matched a protein. The step says so first, as a warning, says why from the shape of the values alone, and offers the two ways out. The join row, the part that failed, has focus. His log2FC was found but has no gene to color, so the fold change colors are kept but switched off. Apply still works for the parts that read the sender's network, and the footer says exactly what it will and will not add, so a network with no fold change colors is never mistaken for his result.

What a weight means: asked once, on the column

The weight row's select is the one question every measure and the Path tool read; it is the same select wherever a column's meaning is set. Left: Tom's own copy of the network, where he once answered that a higher confidence means a longer or costlier step. That disagrees with the recipe's runs, so the row moves under Needs a choice, the select opens with both answers named, and Apply waits. Right: he chose Don't use confidence. The column then reads "confidence, not used", and the two runs that read it are kept but switched off, like any part left unbound.

While matching

Matching is graphty-element's work. Until its report arrives, the step shows the files it read and a loading row in place of the report; the rows below wait, and Apply is off. For 96 rows this lasts a moment; for a table of tens of thousands of rows it is visible.

Tasks for a study session

Show state 1, then 3 or 4, with the annotations off and without the captions above; show state 0 to a participant playing the sender. Ask these in order and write down the answers before showing anything else. The pass condition, from the framework's pattern for bringing a recipe in, is that the participant can say, before Apply, what each part of the recipe will read and where it comes from.

  1. (State 1) Is this recipe ready to use? What is missing, and what would you do next?
  2. (State 1) Where did this network come from? Whose fold change will the colors show?
  3. Before you press anything: how many of your genes will be colored, and which will not?
  4. Pick one gene that will not be colored. Why not, as far as this window tells you? Whose problem is it, yours or the file's?
  5. If you press Apply now, what will change on your network, and how would you take it back?
  6. (State 4 only) What would you do next to get your genes colored?
  7. (State 0, as the sender) What will your colleague have to do to use this? Which of your data leaves with it? Is the layout the same when they open it?
  8. (Any state) The recipe groups proteins using confidence. For this network, what does a higher confidence mean, and if a colleague disagreed, where would you change it?

Record where the participant looks first, any word they stop on, and whether they open anything to answer. In state 1, record whether they reach for Apply.
