Comparing two results answers one question: do they rank the same things the same way? The table's Scatter view opens in the bottom dock and plots every account's rank on one measure against its rank on the other, rank 1 (the top) at the top left, so agreement is the diagonal. Beside the scatter, in this order: how many of the top 50 are in both, in words; Top, to choose the length of the top list; and one Spearman line that leaves out the accounts tied at the bottom of both and gives the number with them in brackets. The sides are named by their measures, never A and B; two runs of one measure are named by the one option that differs ("Damping 0.85", "Damping 0.5"). The right column names each side, opens its run record from Details, and lists the biggest differences as "ranked higher by" one measure. The comparison is temporary: Save comparison keeps it; Done leaves without keeping it.

Not buildable yet. It waits on graphty-element for the comparison readings: ranks with their tie blocks, the top-k overlap at each choice of k, Spearman with and without the tie block both sides share at the bottom, matching by id, each run's record, and the table's Scatter view data; and, for groups, the match by overlap that keeps a group's color from one partition to the next, and the one statistic each column shows on every surface.

1. Two measures: PageRank against betweenness on the April transfers

Alex asks whether the accounts that collect money are the same accounts money passes through. The scatter answers first: the top of one is not the top of the other. Beside it, in words: 0 of the top 50 are in both, and the rankings disagree at the top; only at Top 100 do 18 appear in both. Then Spearman, with its reason: 0.40 once the 1,153 accounts tied at the bottom of both are left out, 0.78 with them. Those accounts are the leaves both measures give the least, so they agree with each other and lift the number. Above the diagonal, the brokers at the top of betweenness sit around #70 to #300 on PageRank.

3. Around the two readings: computing, the other direction, two runs of one measure, one side only, saving, leaving, entering, and a group against the rest

Parts of the surface in the states around the two readings above, each at its real size.

Two drawings. The split canvas stays only for comparing two drawings: two layouts of one graph, or two groupings drawn by color (the weekly return's month-to-month communities). A ranking comparison never opens it.

Past the drawing limit. The Scatter view needs no drawing, so on the 124,318-patent citation graph the scatter, the statistic and the list read in full while the canvas carries its not-drawn line. Past about 2,000 accounts dots overlap; binning them is deferred until a large comparison is tested.

No tie at the bottom of both. When the two sides share no tie block at their lowest value, the Spearman line is one number over every matched account, with nothing in brackets. When a tie block is over 10% of a side the scatter draws it as a band either way.

The sentence under the overlap. The overlap leads in numbers ("8 of the top 10 in both"); the sentence under it chooses its verb from the share of the chosen top list that both sides hold: all of it, "agree at the top"; 7 in 10 or more, "mostly agree at the top"; 3 in 10 or more, "partly agree at the top"; fewer, "mostly disagree at the top"; none, "disagree at the top". It changes with Top: at Top 100 the overlap reads "18 of the top 100 in both" and the sentence "The rankings mostly disagree at the top."

4. Groups across two partitions: the file's modules, then Louvain communities, on the 300 proteins

A reader who colors the protein network by the file's modules and then by Louvain communities sees the same group in the same color. Each community that is the mutual best match by overlap of a module takes that module's color; a community with no mutual match takes the next unused color, or Other when none is left. This is the matcher the weekly update uses to keep March's community names and colors in April, and it is graphty-element's, like the statistics below it. Every surface that shows a group's value on a column shows the same statistic, with the same sign and the same precision.

Without the match. Louvain numbers its communities by size and the palette colors them in that order, so Community 1, mostly Ribosome, would be amber, the color that means Proteasome under the file's modules, and Community 2, mostly Proteasome, would be light blue, Ribosome's color. A reader who has learned amber as Proteasome then reads the Ribosome community as Proteasome.

Where the match lives. Group identity across partitions by overlap is one mechanism in graphty-element, used by the weekly update (March to April), by a re-run of a partition, and here, by one partition shown after another. The app only draws the legend line the element reports.
