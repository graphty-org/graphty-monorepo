        A time window is a filter step

        The three Transfers tabs above draw these in the screen. Les Miserables has no dates, so this part uses the March transfers (3,000 accounts, 9,113 transfers, each with a timestamp). Three places write the same step, and the step is an ordinary row on the chip: it can be turned off, moved, edited or deleted like any other.

        The chip at its width steps

        On a larger graph the chip's text is longer than its slot (216 px, the left panel header). It drops words in this order until it fits, and its tooltip and accessible name always carry the full text. Measured live below on the March transfers, with one of two steps on: merchants filtered out, a risk-score step turned off.

        While a step is being recounted

        On a large graph a count is not instant. The step that changed and every step below it show a spinner in place of their counts, and the chip says it is counting instead of showing a number that is about to be wrong. Each step can still be turned off while it counts.

        When transfers repeat a pair

        The March transfers never send twice between the same two accounts, so their pair count equals their transfer count. A bank export often does: here one account paid another more than once, so the edge count and the pair count differ, and the pair count sits beside it. Density and components count pairs; degree counts every transfer, and each line says which.
