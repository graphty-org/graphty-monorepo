Undo and ways back

For the facilitator only; never show this part to a participant. Les Miserables (77 characters) has been narrowed in three filter steps: keep characters with at least 2 connections, keep characters with at least 5 connections, leave out group 8 (the students at the barricade). The middle step is the mistake; the first and last are work to keep. A stray click on empty canvas has also just cleared a hand-built selection of Valjean and the 17 characters beside him. The table was on its Selected scope, so its rows stay, marked as the selection just cleared.

How a cleared selection comes back. Clearing a selection (Esc, or a click on empty canvas) keeps it in one slot and says so on the undo line: "Selection cleared (18 nodes)" with Bring it back. While the slot holds it and nothing undoable has happened since, Ctrl+Z brings it back instead of undoing a filter step, and Edit > Undo says so: "Undo Restore selection (18 nodes)". The line then reads "Selection restored (18 nodes)". Restoring never touches the undo or Redo lists, so a Redo waiting before the stray click is still there, and Ctrl+Shift+Z never clears the selection again. Any new selection or any undoable change (a tick, a delete, an Undo or a Redo) empties the slot. There is no separate command or key for it. Both lines are the undo line's own message with a parameter, and the slot belongs to graphty-element's history, so every page that embeds the graph gets the same behavior. The other arm (Arm: the line only) draws the same line and Bring it back, but Ctrl+Z always undoes the last filter step; the next study compares the two in separate sessions.

How undo works here. Undo never removes a filter step. Undoing a step unticks it: its row stays in the steps list, turned off, and ticking it again brings it back at any time, even after Redo is gone. Only Delete step, on a row's menu, removes a step. Ticking a step on or off is an ordinary undoable change, and like any change it clears Redo; nothing is lost, because the row stays. Every Undo and Redo shows one line in the notice area above the toolbar, "Undone: Filter out group 8" with Show in steps, whether or not the filter chip is in view. The line is announced politely and never takes focus; its action opens the steps list with focus on that step's row. It stays until the next change (a tick, a delete, a selection change or Show in steps), never on a timer, and Undos in a row add to it: after two it reads "Undone: Filter out group 8 and Filter to degree >= 5". The steps list is the filter chip's own list, row for row.

Participant view. No bar, no notes, no annotations, starting in state 1. Give the task aloud, in these words: "After your last few actions the numbers changed in a way you did not expect. Get back to where you were, without losing work you meant to keep." Do not name any menu, button or key.

What to record. Whether the selection comes back first (Ctrl+Z or Bring it back) or after a filter change, which empties the slot and loses it; the first thing they reach for (the undo key, Edit, the chip, the table's scope line, the rail); whether they read the line after the first Undo and see that it names Filter out group 8, the step worth keeping; whether they find the steps list, and how (the chip or the line's action); whether they tick group 8 back on or press Redo; and the end state. Done is state 5: 47 of 77 nodes, 2 of 3 steps (the degree step off, group 8 on) with the same characters selected again. Also run once with the left panel closed.

Keys. Ctrl+Z undo (Cmd+Z on a Mac). Redo is Ctrl+Shift+Z, and also Ctrl+Y off macOS (Cmd+Shift+Z on a Mac). While a cleared selection is held, Ctrl+Z restores it first; the next Ctrl+Z undoes the last filter step. Esc closes a menu, then the steps list, then clears the selection; a click on empty canvas clears it too. In the steps list, Up and Down move between steps and Space turns the focused step off or on. The line stays until the next change. Turning off the first step (degree >= 2) is not drawn in this mock.
