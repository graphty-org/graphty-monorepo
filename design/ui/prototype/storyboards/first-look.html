First look: a file arrives and is worth an afternoon (or not)

Elena, a product manager who has never analyzed a network, wants to know whether graphty could say something about her company's export of which customers use which integrations. First she tries it on a network she already knows: the Les Miserables co-appearance file from a tutorial she never finished, which she once round-tripped through a spreadsheet.

WhoElena, first-time graph user. Trackpad, 14-inch laptop, browser at 1440 by 900. No recent projects.
Her filemiserables.json: 77 characters and 254 links, with a value on each link counting shared chapters. It is the published file, plus one change of hers: the spreadsheet export wrote every value as text ("8", not 8), as sheet-to-JSON exporters commonly do.

EndsShe has run at least one analysis and written down why she stopped.

If the design works

The claim the study must test: a first-time user who brings a file she knows runs one analysis and reads its number against its distribution, within her first visit, without asking what a word means and without the tool changing her data under her.

Counted as decisions she makes (a click, a pick or a key that commits), the path above has twelve: open the sample; drop the file; choose Update with new data; open "For value, a higher number means" and leave it; press Replace data; click Valjean; open Quick actions; choose Betweenness; click Myriel; pick the Note tool; click Valjean; post the note. Two of them also need typing (the question and the note). The first analysis runs at the eighth.

What the session must watch

  • Size read as importance (frames 5 and 6). Does she say the biggest dot is the most important one before any measure runs? After frame 6, what does she say about it? Do the legend's "Size: degree" or Degree's "already shown as size" play any part? Record her words; nothing in the story counts as a correction.
  • The weights (frames 3 and 6). Load stays on and the default already reads the numbers, so: does she read the warning at all, and can she say afterwards what "Read as number" did? Does she blame herself for the quotes? Can she answer "For value, a higher number means", or does the question stop her? Does she expect Betweenness to use the weights she just saw read, and what does she make of "Unweighted: value's meaning is not set"? Does she open "Set what value means..."?
  • The privacy line (frame 1). Does she mention it unprompted? Does the note under Connect to data source... read as contradicting it? Does it change what she is willing to load next?
  • The drop choice and the sample copy (frames 2 to 6). Which choice does she pick? If she picks Open as a new project, does she miss the sample's colors on her gray drawing? What does she think "Not saved" means, and does she notice when the run keeps the copy?
  • Finding the note (frame 7). Does she find Add note in the node's menu, or the Notes panel? The Notes section does not exist until a note does, so there is nothing to click in the inspector. Does she think to cite the run her note is about?
  • Her own data. At the end, ask what she would do with her integrations export. It has two kinds of node, customers and integrations, so a measure like betweenness does not mean the same thing on it until it is projected onto one kind (Bipartite projection...). Does she realise that, or would she run Betweenness on the export as it is?

When this becomes a study task

Give her the goal only: "Find out whether this tool can tell you something about a network you already know", with miserables.json in her Downloads folder. Do not mention the sample, the drop, Update with new data or any measure; this path is one of several she might take, and the session records the one she does.

Proposals this storyboard draws where the design is silent

Each is in the proposed framework changes with its evidence. Where no evidence outside this storyboard exists yet, the entry says it is a hypothesis for the study.

  • The start screen carries one privacy line under its title, and Connect to data source... says what it sends.
  • A sample opens as a copy marked Not saved; the first thing made in it keeps it.
  • On an unkept sample copy, the drop choice step leads with Open as a new project and Update with new data..., the rest under More ways; each row commits.
  • The load step carries the chosen action at its top, so it can be changed without a second drop.
  • The weight's meaning is asked on the value row of the load step, in the column's words with the technical term under each, and nothing is preselected: unanswered, no measure uses the column. (A weight column whose values are all numbers written as text is a warning whose default reads them as numbers, so Load stays on: the entry "which load issues block Load".)
  • A result run unweighted because the weight's meaning is not set says so on its state line, with the verb that sets it.
  • A file chip under the project name says which file the graph was read from.
  • Quick actions lists Degree as "already shown as size" when a style layer already paints it.
  • Small wording changes: "Size by betweenness instead" for Apply anyway; "Change overview..." on hover for the Overview row's Replace; no id row when it equals the label.
