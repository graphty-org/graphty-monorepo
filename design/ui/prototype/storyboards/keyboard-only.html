Keyboard and screen reader: walking the protein network

Morgan Reyes, a blind analyst who works in NVDA, explores a network without a mouse or a screen: walks from one node to its strongest partners and on through one of them, keeps three, checks them, slips four times and recovers from each, reads the three as table rows and keeps them as a named set. Every frame says where keyboard focus is and exactly what the screen reader says.

A structural walkthrough, not a usability result. It shows that every step has a key, a place for focus and one announcement, and that there is always a way back and a way out. It cannot show that the walk is pleasant or fast. It passes only when the owner has run these nineteen steps with the keyboard alone in the built app, and at least one real screen-reader user (NVDA or JAWS) has done the same task unaided and could say, at every step, where they were and how to get back.

The data and the task

This runs on the sample protein network, not on Morgan's own work. Morgan studies referral networks (which clinics refer patients to which hospitals), and that data may not leave the agency. Morgan has no opinion about proteins; the task is the one they would bring to a clinic: who is this node's strongest partner, whom does that partner connect to, and how do I get back to where I started (goals 1 and 3 in Morgan's persona).

The network (300 human proteins, 1,262 interactions; ppi-core-300.graphml) is filtered to TP53 and its 32 neighbors: 33 proteins and 59 interactions, colored by module and sized by degree. Each interaction carries a confidence score from 0.40 to 0.99, the graph's declared edge weight, which the walk orders by unless the order is switched. Degree and rank are over all 300 proteins.

Usage data: already chosen, and off

The story starts in a project that is already open, so graphty's usage-data question is behind Morgan. The first time graphty opened on the agency laptop, before any graph, it asked once whether it may collect how the app is used. The question is a dialog answered with the same keys as everything else: NVDA reads it on arrival, focus starts on Not now, Tab reaches Share usage data, and Enter on Not now (or Esc) says "Usage data off. Start screen." Morgan pressed Enter on Not now, so usage data is off, and nothing about this walk is sent. Usage data is off unless the person says yes.

Had Morgan said yes, graphty would send anonymous task events with their timings, errors and performance, and a replay of the screen with every node name, attribute value, label and file content masked; no file contents ever leave the computer. The choice can be changed at any time in Preferences > Usage data, which is reached by keyboard like any other setting. The dialog and its keys are drawn in the keyboard walk flow; Where your data goes lists what would be collected.

  • Mod is Ctrl on Windows and Linux and Cmd on macOS. The announcements below are what NVDA says on Windows, so they name Ctrl.
  • The keys were chosen against NVDA and JAWS on Windows in Chrome. On macOS, Ctrl+Z is Cmd+Z and Quick actions Cmd+K. VoiceOver takes the arrow keys itself while its Quick Nav is on, so the walk needs Quick Nav off; none of this has been tried with VoiceOver.
  • Unresolved: today Shift with the arrows pans the 3D camera in the app. That binding has to move before the walk can have Shift+Arrow.

The focus pill

While the canvas has keyboard focus, a pill above the toolbar shows what the screen reader says, so a sighted keyboard user sees the same values: before a walk, the node the walk will start from; in the walk, the focused node, where it sits in the list, whether it is selected, its values, and the order the list is in, with a switch to change it. It is silent (the announcements already speak) and it goes away when focus leaves the canvas. Here it is at frame 4:

RPA24 of 32 from TP53 confidence 0.86degree 11rank 40 of 300 Neighbors byWeightDegreeNameO Shift+Arrow: walk. Enter: select. ?: keys.
  • The readout names the column the walk orders by, here "confidence", the graph's declared edge weight (the interaction's confidence score, 0.40 to 0.99), never a bare "weight". By confidence means highest first, ties by name; by degree, highest first, ties by name; by name, A to Z.
  • On a step through the selection (] and [) there is no edge, so the pill shows "selected 1 of 3" and no confidence. At the start it shows "start of the walk"; before a walk, "Start: TP53, the walk starts here"; after a walk is left, "Walk kept: on RPA2, from TP53". Its first words name the region: "Walking the drawing" in the walk, "Graph drawing" otherwise.
  • O switches the order (a proposed key), as do the pill's switch and the three "Walk order" commands in Quick actions. The node stepped from is kept and focus goes to the first neighbor in the new order: "Neighbors by degree, highest first. UBC, neighbor 1 of 32 of TP53, confidence 0.80, degree 21, rank 7 of 300."

The storyboard

Each picture is the screen mock at that exact moment, cropped to the canvas (frames 17 and 19 show the whole app, because focus is in the table and the side panel); click one to open it full size. The strip under each picture spells out the focus pill and the selection in readable type.

The "Expects" lines are the designers' guesses at what Morgan would expect or worry about at that moment: hypotheses to test in the session with a real screen-reader user, not findings. Do not quote them as evidence.

Outcome

In nineteen steps -- 31 key presses plus typing "set" and the set's name -- Morgan went from "TP53 is selected" to a named set of three proteins, TP53, RPA2 and 53BP1, found by following the strongest interactions. Eight of those presses were four slips (a plain Down, a doubled Space, a stray Delete, a second Esc), their recoveries, and the read-back the doubled Space made necessary. Each slip was announced, and each had a way back that the announcement named or that Morgan already had.

What a real session has to answer:

  • Whether "where you came from" and "selected" at the end of a neighbor are wanted, or become noise after an hour. The one screen-reader participant so far found "where you came from" exactly what they needed two hops out; it needs more than one user to confirm.
  • Whether saying "confidence" on every step (frame 4) costs too much speech at a fast rate, now that the order and the unit are also on the pill.
  • Whether a changed selection count (frames 7 and 8) should name what changed, so a doubled key does not force a read-back.
  • Whether the restore should name what it brought back, not only how many.
  • Whether "rank" needs its name spoken ("rank 247 by degree") until it has been heard once.
  • Whether O is found, from the pill or the key sheet, when the question is "who is the best-connected partner", or whether people still step through every neighbor. O is a new published key, for the owner.
  • Whether a sighted keyboard user reads the pill's values, or still has to open the inspector (Alt+Enter) to see them.
  • Whether Enter adding to the selection and Space adding or removing are both used, or Space can go back to being only the pan key.

Where the framework was silent

This storyboard had to decide how Shift and the four arrows divide the walk, the neighbor order and its announcement, where Tab lands on leaving the canvas, what the focus pill holds and where it is drawn, the one-time hint on a plain arrow, the "in filtered graph" count, what Delete says on a node that is not selected, and how a cleared selection comes back. Each is in the proposed framework changes with its evidence.
