A recipe travels: recipe out, recipe in, what matched

Maren, a fourth-year cancer biology postdoc who does her lab's computational work, has settled how the lab colors its protein interaction network for lab meeting: genes by log2 fold change, red up and blue down, the other proteins in muted module colors, weak interactions hidden. She saves that as a recipe with none of the lab's data inside, says that the fold change must come from whoever opens it, and emails it. Tom, the lab manager, opens it on his own laptop and puts this week's qPCR hits on it, because in lab meeting the principal investigator will ask him one question: how many of ours are in there?

Success line: Tom can state how many of his genes matched and which did not. Every number below comes from the kit's protein network (300 proteins, 1,262 interactions) and Tom's 96-row qPCR table, both in the kit's fixtures.

What this version settles. Participants stopped at the recipe's card because it asked for "a protein network" without saying which, and whether it came with the recipe. Now the card lists what the person opening it must supply under "Expects:", naming the network the recipe was made on and saying it is not inside, and Maren's Export preview shows that list word for word, as Tom will see it. The Apply dialog is the one Apply dialog every recipe uses: how the recipe's styles go in is its own choice, "Use these styles" first and "Add these styles on top", neither preselected. A recipe for data already open comes in from the Style stack's +, "From a recipe or file...", in the inspector. Matching genes by alias or previous symbol is deferred, so TP53BP1 and H2AFX still do not match. Everything else is as the sessions showed it working: Apply waiting for his table, the count in words, the unmatched genes by name, "ask the sender", and one Undo.

How to read the frames. Each picture is the part of the screen that matters, at a readable size. Frames 1, 7 and branch A are crops of the screen mock, with a small whole-screen locator beside them. The card in frame 3, the list beside the crop in frame 1, and the Apply dialog in frames 4 to 6 and branch B are drawn on this page, because the screen mocks still draw the earlier versions. Lines marked "What we expect him to say" are the design's hypothesis in Tom's voice, not findings. Session tasks come from the goal ("Find out how many of your genes are in the lab's network, and which are not"), never from these lines.

Screens: Share and apply a recipe (export, start screen, applied, undo) and The binding step (the dialog body). The changes to the framework this storyboard needs are in framework-changes.md, under "Recipe travels".

1Maren saves the recipe

Maren, at her desk, in her project "Knockdown screen, September".

She chooses Main menu, Recipes, Save as a recipe... The Export dialog opens with only Recipe checked; she names it Expression overlay. Scope is grayed out, because a recipe holds no data; its tooltip says so.

She sees the preview's first line: no data inside, no genes, interactions, fold-change values, positions or notes. Below it, what travels (two color layers, the confidence filter, overview readings), what stays behind, including her 38-gene knockdown set, and, under "The person who opens it sees", the "Expects:" list exactly as it will appear on Tom's screen. It names the network she made the recipe on, ppi-core-300.graphml, and says it is not inside. Under the list, each input says where it comes from. The gene id reads "Their table", as a join always does. The fold change starts at "The network", because her layer reads the network's log2FoldChange; she switches it to "Their table" and gives the name Tom's sheets use, log2FC, and the list above changes to say so. Module and confidence stay "The network".

What we expect her to say
"So that's what Tom gets. The network off the shared drive and his own log2FC, not the old fold change in the network. Good, that column is from the 2024 screen."

Then she exports one file: Expression overlay, a recipe. The recipe records who saved it and when, taken from her project's author setting.

Full screen: Applied

Outcome

The success line is met if, in the session, Tom can say something like this without opening anything after Apply: "84 of my 96 genes are in the network. No match for 12: 7-Sep and 2-Mar, which look like Excel dates; Mdm2, which differs from MDM2 only in case, I'll ask Maren; TP53BP1, H2AFX, VEGFA, HIF1A, IL6, CXCL8 and SERPINE1; and GAPDH and ACTB, our reference genes." He could read the count before he applied, in the notice after, and in the graph's Statistics once the notice had gone. He colored by this week's numbers, because the recipe could only take the fold change from his table, and Apply would not start until his table was in. His hits never left the laptop.

Branches, drawn

The project after Undo: the card on the canvas; on the right, Add data... and the Style stack with the recipe's two layers waiting

Branch AOne Undo, or Cancel

After frame 7, Edit, Undo (Ctrl+Z); or during frames 4 to 6, Cancel or Esc in the dialog.

One Undo takes back the whole apply: the colors, the filter, the recipe's two layers and the two loaded files. The project stays, with the recipe waiting for data: the canvas card, the Graphs section's one empty row, and in the inspector Add data... and the Style stack's two layers marked "waiting"; Export... in the project-name menu is disabled. The card says his files on disk were not changed. There is no "Undone" notice, since the canvas visibly empties. Cancel and Esc in the Apply dialog land in the same place, with nothing applied. Redo (Ctrl+Shift+Z) brings all of it back, the count with it.

Not narrated as success. Whether seeing his data vanish on Undo reassures Tom or scares him is a study task: "Take back what you just did, then get it back."

The dialog body: The binding step

Behavior this storyboard specifies

ExpectsThe recipe's card on the start screen says "No data inside." and then "Expects:" with one line per thing the person opening it must supply: the network the recipe was made on, by file name, and that it is not in the recipe; and their own table, with the column name the recipe will look for. The Export preview shows the same list, word for word, under "The person who opens it sees", and it changes as the sender changes where an input comes from.
Where each input comes fromSave as a recipe... (the Export dialog with only Recipe checked) lists every input the recipe will ask for with a Select, "Their table" or "The network", and the name the recipient's column should have. A join key starts at Their table; any other input starts where its column lives in the sender's project. The preview says in one line what Their table means. The recipe file records it (proposed; a published format).
Apply before the tableWhile any "Their table" input has no table, the dialog lists "Your table: not added yet" with a full-size Add your table... button, focus on it; those inputs read "waiting for your table"; Apply is off and the reason is printed beside it: "Add your table to apply. This recipe needs {column} from your data." The dialog never says everything was found while an input is waiting.
Never the network's columnFor a "Their table" input, only columns of the recipient's tables are candidates, even when a network file holds a column with the recipe's own name. The dialog says once that the network's column is not used.
Column missing: ask the senderWhen the table has no column of the recipe's name, the row is marked, the picker starts empty and lists the table's columns of the right level with where they live, how many values and their range; the row says "Your table has no {column} column. Ask the sender which column they meant." No column is proposed or bound for him, not even a close name. Apply is off with "Choose the fold-change column, and how the styles go in, to apply." A column he picks is recorded as "chosen by you".
How the styles go inThe one Apply dialog, the same for every recipe, ends with a Styles section: "Use these styles" first (its layers take the place of any of the reader's layers that write the same thing, each named; layers an algorithm suggested are never replaced; Base style stays), then "Add these styles on top". Neither is preselected, and Apply waits for the choice.
Where the count livesAlways in words, "84 of 96 genes matched", never as a fraction or a percentage. Three places, in order of time: the Apply dialog before anything changes; the notice after Apply ("84 of 96 genes matched", Show the 12, Undo); a row in the graph's Statistics naming the applied recipe and its count, which opens the full report. The report never lives in the notice alone.
Unmatched idsListed by name, never only counted: a case-only near miss on its own row with its hand match (Use MDM2, recorded as matched by hand); the rest under "Not in this network: 11 ids, for example 7-Sep", a date-shaped id tagged "date?" with one general line ("2 ids look like spreadsheet dates (SEPT2 -> 2-Sep)"). Copy the 12 symbols puts the list on the clipboard.
A signed columnWhen the recipient's column runs below and above 0, as the recipe's colors do, nothing is asked; the row shows what it will do on his numbers ("36 up, 48 down"). A question is asked only when the column's sign disagrees with the recipe's scale.
Focus and keyboardThe dialog opens with focus on the first thing that needs him: Add your table... (frame 4), the fold-change picker (branch B), or Apply when nothing does. Tab walks the rows in order, then Cancel and Apply; a disabled Apply stays in the Tab order so its reason is read with it. Esc or Cancel closes it with nothing applied and lands on the project's canvas with the recipe waiting, focus on the card's Add data.... After Apply, focus goes to the canvas and the notice is announced politely; Ctrl+Z is the one Undo, Ctrl+Shift+Z the Redo.
UndoOne entry, "Apply recipe Expression overlay", that removes the layers, the filter and the files loaded for it, and returns to the same project with the recipe waiting for data. No notice: the empty canvas and its card are the feedback. Files on disk and the recipe file are never written.
Where data goesAnswered on the start screen before any data is added. When a recipe names a data source, the same line names the host and Apply waits for a separate confirm to contact it; nothing is fetched first.

Branches this storyboard does not draw

  • An older network file with protein names only (ppi-core-300-2024.graphml): module and confidence have nothing to read. Each row says what it switches off (the other 216 proteins stay in the Base style; all 1,262 interactions show), Apply still applies the rest, and the notice and Statistics add "2 parts off". The fold change is unaffected, since it comes from his table. Drawn, with the earlier fold-change row, in Two parts unbound.
  • 0 genes matched (the table's ids are Ensembl, not symbols): the join row is marked "0 of 96 matched" and its Select opens on the table's other text columns. See Nothing matched.
  • A file that raises a load question (numbers stored as text, parallel edges): the load step comes first for that file, its button reads Continue to binding, and Back from the Apply dialog returns to it with every choice kept.
  • A recipe that names a server (a data-source query): the start screen's line reads "This recipe will read from {host} when you confirm", and the dialog has a separate Confirm for that host before Apply.
  • The recipe applied to data already open (the Style stack's +, From a recipe or file..., or dropping the recipe on the canvas): the same dialog; a "Their table" input still waits for a table added in this dialog or already joined, never a network column. Undo leaves the data loaded.
  • Making this recipe the default overview is not part of this journey. An overview never paints, and Tom's motive here is colors; if that behavior needs a picture it gets its own flow, with a reader whose motive is the readings.

What the study watches for

  • Apply before the table. Does Tom read the reason beside the disabled Apply and find Add your table... without help? The last round's participants found the small link only after about 50 seconds, and one only because the counts did not add up to his rows.
  • Ask the sender (branch B). Does he stop and ask, or pick logFC? Either is fine; the failure is picking without reading the line. Does the list of his table's signed columns help him, or read as a suggestion to take?
  • The count check. Does he still add up "36 up, 48 down" against his 84? If he does not need to any more, the dialog is doing its job.
  • Reading direction. Can he tell up from down from the legend on the first try? The recipe uses red for up and blue for down, the usual expression convention, and red-blue avoids the red-green pair his mild color weakness confuses. Does he read a muted sky-blue Ribosome node as down-regulated?
  • Expects. Does Tom know which network to add from the card alone, without the email? Does "It is not in this recipe" read as a missing attachment?
  • Use these styles. With no layers of his own, both choices do the same thing here. Does Tom stop at a choice that changes nothing for him, and does the line "none of yours do" settle it?
  • "Not in this network". Does he read it as "these genes do not exist"? Does he notice that TP53BP1 and H2AFX name proteins the network holds under other names (53BP1, H2AX)? Matching by alias or previous symbol is deferred; a lab could add an alias column to its network.
  • Mdm2. Does he take Use MDM2 as a capitalization fix, when a mouse-case symbol on a human list can mean a mouse gene?
  • Undo unloading the files. Does seeing his data vanish reassure him or scare him?
  • Maren's side. Does she understand "Their table" against "The network" at export, and does she think to switch the fold change to "Their table" when it starts at "The network"? Does seeing the "Expects:" list as Tom will see it make the switch obvious? If not, the export needs to ask rather than default.
