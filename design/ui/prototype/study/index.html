graphty user study

A simulated usability study of the graphty mocks: task sessions and focus groups with personas built from real users' forum posts, videos and reviews. Every participant is simulated, so each finding is a hypothesis to confirm with real people. Newest round first. Back to the gallery.

Round 7 (2026-10-02)

The clickable skeleton of refined structure B, tested four ways: a tree test (16 personas, 16 jobs), a first-click test (16 personas, 14 prompts on still renders), 280 think-aloud sessions over 61 tasks clicked through the skeleton, and 4 focus groups. Earlier rounds put still mocks in front of participants; this one made them click, so ease is not directly comparable. Every participant is simulated from the persona files: a failed task is a strong signal, a passed one weak until real people confirm it. Read first: round-7/insights.md.

Headline

  • The round does not pass any of its four bars. 11 of 21 top and new tasks reach 80% success or success with difficulty; mean ease is 3.54 of 7 (bar 5.5); 9 of 16 tree tasks reach 70% direct success; three confirmed severity-4 design problems remain.
  • Much of it is the skeleton, not the design: 18 of 61 tasks (67 sessions) could not reach the screen they test, and 29 more crossed a defect that drove the ratings (another dataset's summary after "Compute the overview", a filter carried in from another task, a table stuck on rows 381 to 420, labels and layouts never drawn, a study tool that cannot type). On the 14 tasks with no deciding defect, 70 of 77 sessions succeed (91%) at mean ease 4.86.
  • Severity 4, design: there is no way to add another month or week of rows to a loaded table (the matching-file state refuses, and "Add data to this graph" opens a screen headed "Open as a new graph"; 12 sessions); Select where is found from none of the places people look (0 of 5 opened the main menu; tree 1 of 16 direct); an exported picture leaves out the legend with no switch to add it (6 of 6).
  • File actions are looked for in the main menu, which has none: 13 of 16 went there first for Export (round 6, with a File menu: 16 of 16 direct), 15 of 16 first clicks for a colleague's style file, 0 on the project name.
  • What worked: several tables into one graph (18 of 18), weight set at load, nested JSON (12 of 12), the match report and the "Makes" line, the comparison screen, Why this look, the subject chip on a new note, Replace with file..., and 212 of 224 first clicks.

Focus groups

  • Notes on paths and their tags -- times are relative only (6 of 6 unprompted); no author shown; the path tag must say it is a path, with hops and the middle stop; a note should keep the run it came from. Discount: steered agreement on the path tag.
  • Importing several tables -- rows left out at Load must survive Load (6 of 6, severity 4); leading zeros need a rule that matches without rewriting ids; save the setup and rerun it next month. Discount: the data was built with these problems.
  • Stacked paint -- "hidden still paints" reads as a bug (4 of 5 independently); the hidden row is the size row, which nobody connected; name the result that lost a channel in the canvas legend. Discount: deference to one expert on Degree.
  • Very wide data -- unlabeled fill percentage, unusable columns listed first in Size by (a skeleton defect against the specification), defaults that look accidental, middle truncation hiding the difference, an invisible search rule. Discount: a scripted query drew the pile-on.
