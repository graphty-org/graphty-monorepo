graphty user study

A simulated usability study of the graphty mocks: task sessions and focus groups with personas built from real users' forum posts, videos and reviews. Every participant is simulated, so each finding is a hypothesis to confirm with real people. Newest round first. Back to the gallery.

Round 5 (2026-09-28)

The design as decided after round 4, tested four ways: a two-arm tree test (16 personas, 16 tasks; arm A keeps Results in the right panel, arm B makes them a place on the rail), a first-click test (16 personas, 14 prompts), 135 think-aloud sessions across 26 tasks (18 repeated from round 4, 3 regressions from round 3, 5 new), and 4 focus groups. Read first: what round 5 found -- whether the round met its bars, where Results live, 21 design findings with severity, evidence and a recommendation each, the problems caused by the mocks, and what to keep.

Headline

  • Most round-4 decisions were not on the screens when the round ran (weighted degree, the table's footer sum and near-tie marks, the per-run weight choice, the legend's too-close flag, the cleared-selection notice, style files folded into recipes). The sessions therefore re-tested the round-4 design, and nearly every round-4 finding reproduced. Seven repeated tasks and two new ones are void for that reason. The text-only tree test is the one method that tested the decided design.
  • Where Results live: by the rule frozen before the round, Results become a place on the rail, and round 6 tests that. In arm A only 4 of 16 reached a run's settings without going back; in arm B 12 of 12 did. Arm B was run by 12 of 16 personas only, but the arm-A miss decides it on its own.
  • The round met none of its five bars. Tree test: 11 of 16 tasks pass (a team's colors file 19% correct; comparing a group with the rest 0% direct; money in against out 50% direct; a cleared selection 69%). First click: 7 of 14 prompts pass, 72% overall. Ease on the 18 repeated tasks rose from 4.08 to 4.25 (4.31 to 4.64 on the 11 that are not void), but all three round-3 regressions fell below their round-3 means.
  • What now works: one File list. A picture for the paper and next month's file are 100% direct in the tree test (round 4: 13% and 38%), and picture export on first click went from 2 of 16 to 14 of 16. The flagged-account task rose from 3.00 to 4.75 now that its account is on the screens.
  • Most severe: nothing adds up money per account (4 of 5 failed the new money task); the meaning of a weight is filled in before anyone chooses, and one column cannot mean a cost for a route and a strength for a ranking; money cannot be followed forward in time; Ctrl+Z is everyone's reflex for a lost selection and it takes a filter step instead; people bring a team's look in through the style stack's "+", not through Recipes (12 of 16).
  • The round's tree-test, first-click, session and focus-group files were first written into the round-4 folders, over round 4's own files. The round-5 copies are in round-5/; round 4's findings stand in its insights page.

Focus groups

  • Where results live -- a "run" tag under Styles files a calculation as a color (5 of 6); people want a ranked, sortable, exportable table (all 6 by the end); a value must say which run made it; a second run must not overwrite the first; an empty Results reads as "nothing has run".
  • Data as one area -- "Recipes applied" and "Style files" read as the same thing (5 of 5); Update with new data must say what changed before it redraws; a recipe must only change how things look; "Nothing has been sent" is a claim, and a dated, printable log is the ask.
  • A color you can name -- one source for a group's color that every place follows; a color should stay with the group's name through a re-run, with a real split on unmatched groups; every swatch spoken with its name, count and color.
  • What a number used -- the screen gives two answers to "was the weight used?" (5 of 5); the weight's meaning belongs to the run and each result must print it; say who set the reading and when; a copied number must carry scope, weight reading, period and who set it.

Round 4 (2026-09-28)

The rebuilt navigation after the owner's review, tested four ways: a tree test (16 personas, 14 tasks), a first-click test (16 personas, 9 prompts), 58 think-aloud sessions across 18 tasks (17 repeated from round 3), and 3 focus groups. Read first: what round 4 found -- whether the round met its bar, 30 design findings with severity, evidence and a recommendation each, the problems caused by the mocks, and what to keep.

Round 5's runs wrote their files into this round's folders, over round 4's own answers, so the per-participant links below now open round 5's files (copies are also in round-5/). Round 4's findings, counts and tables on this page and in its insights page are unchanged.

Headline

  • The round did not meet its bar. Tree test: 90% correct and 63% direct overall, but 5 of 14 tasks miss (a run's settings, the whole ranking, a team's style file, next month's file, a picture for a paper). First click: 89% overall; 8 of 9 prompts pass, and exporting a picture is 2 of 16.
  • Results and the rail: by the rule set before the round, Results go back to the rail, because the result tasks for one node's score, a run's settings and the whole ranking miss 70% direct. But every miss on the first and last landed in the bottom table (its Search, a column's sort), and a run's settings are looked for on the data column in the Data panel (13 of 16), which a rail button would not change. The findings recommend keeping Results off the rail as a studio decision and settling it with an A/B tree test in round 5.
  • People look under File for Export (14 of 16) and for next month's file (10 of 16; 3 would have opened it as a new project and lost the setup). Recommended: File routes into the Data panel's own dialogs.
  • Ease on the 17 repeated tasks rose from 3.80 to 4.17. Biggest gains: the reviewer's gray figure (3.0 to 5.0), the forward money trace (2.5 to 4.0), the signed gray figure and past the drawing limit (+1.3 each). Fixing the wrong middle step is again the easiest task (5.33). 54 of 58 finished; 3 without a wrong turn or a hint.
  • Most severe: what a bigger weight means is one setting per column and defaults to reading a transfer amount as a stronger link, so "cheapest route" prefers big transfers; the data page leaves hosting, telemetry and an organization-wide Assistant switch blank; the legend is where people change a color but clicking it selects the group (the new restyle task is the hardest, 2.67); colors have no names for a screen reader.
  • The mocks still cost signal: counts still disagree between mocks (unfixed from round 3), the rebuilt navigation reached only some task screens, and the navigation page renders blank in the participant view. These are listed apart and not counted against the design.

71 task sessions with 15 personas across 18 tasks, and 4 focus groups. Read first: what round 1 found -- 26 design findings with severity, evidence and a recommendation each, plus the problems caused by the mocks themselves.

Headline

  • 63 of 71 sessions finished the task (89%), but only 1 without a wrong turn, a guess or a moderator step-in. Mean ease rating 3.65 of 7.
  • The hardest tasks start from a question in the user's own world: clearing a flagged account (0 of 3 finished), a figure readable in gray (2 of 4), this week's export.
  • Most severe design findings: numbers not labeled with what they were counted over read as contradictions; "how sure are you" about a ranking cannot be answered; community detection has no finished result; a print figure cannot be checked in gray; the weekly refresh double-counts; an investigation cannot start from an alert.
  • Strongest things to keep: the import check before loading, the "uploads nothing" line, the run's state line, cost shown before a run, and filter steps with a count after each.
  • A third of the sessions were disrupted by the mocks themselves (a screen after Load showing another dataset, two mocks disagreeing on a count). Those are fixed before round 2 and are not counted against the design.

Focus groups

  • Leaving Gephi or Cytoscape -- group names that stick, provenance on exported numbers, a named layout method.
  • Investigations, evidence and approval -- time on every link, source records behind a link, paperwork IT can approve.
  • Code-first users and access -- the full definition of every result, data out with original ids, screen-reader output.
  • First-timers and people handed a file -- the weight question in plain words, legends that say what they show, the gene-matching summary as the most trusted screen.
