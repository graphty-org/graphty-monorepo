# Persona: Dr. Chen, Computational Biologist (drug target discovery)

Source record: design/designloom/personas/bioinformatics-researcher.yaml (workflows W01, W03, W08, W19-W25).
This is a composite built from public forum threads, tool documentation, issue trackers, tutorials and
papers listed under Sources. No real person's identity is used; lines below are paraphrases in her
register, each tied to the material it is grounded in.

## Portrait

Dr. Chen is a computational biologist in her early forties who runs a small group (two postdocs, one PhD
student) inside a translational research institute that partners with a pharma company. She has built
protein interaction networks for fifteen years, first in Cytoscape 2.x, now mostly in R with igraph and
Bioconductor, dropping into Cytoscape 3.10 when she needs a figure or wants to "look" at a module. She is
not impressed by pictures of networks: she has reviewed too many "hub gene" papers built from GEO + STRING
+ cytoHubba, and she knows that force-directed layouts can manufacture clusters that are not there. She
will give a new tool a fair twenty minutes on a Friday afternoon with her own data, and she will judge it
on three things: does it respect her identifiers and confidence scores, can she defend every number it
shows her to a reviewer, and does the figure it exports survive a journal's production department.

## Background and tools

- PhD in computational biology; postdoc in a systems biology lab; now group leader. Wet-lab collaborators
  run the validation (siRNA knockdown, CRISPR screens) on whatever short list she hands them.
- Daily: RStudio on a 14-inch laptop docked to one 27-inch monitor; SSH to the institute HPC cluster for
  anything big (co-expression over 20,000 genes, permutation tests). Python (pandas, networkx, scanpy) for
  the postdocs' single-cell work.
- Network stack: STRING (web and the stringApp for Cytoscape), BioGRID and IntAct downloads, Reactome and
  KEGG for pathways, g:Profiler / clusterProfiler for enrichment, EnrichmentMap for theme networks, MCL
  via clusterMaker2 for modules, cytoHubba when a student insists. igraph for anything she has to
  reproduce.
- Automation: when she does use Cytoscape now, she drives it from R through RCy3 (Cytoscape's REST
  interface, CyREST, underneath), so a figure is regenerated by a script, not by hand. Her postdocs do
  the same from Jupyter with py4cytoscape. A GUI tool that cannot be scripted, or whose results cannot be
  pulled back into R as a table, sits outside her pipeline.
- Final figures always pass through Illustrator or Inkscape, so she needs editable vector output (SVG or
  PDF with real text, not outlined glyphs).
- How she got here: Cytoscape became the default because every tutorial and collaborator used it, not
  because she likes it. She moved the analytical steps into R after sessions stopped opening across
  versions and after a merge of downloaded interactomes (about 600k edges) sat at "finalizing" for more than a day. She has tried Gephi (fast, pretty,
  "does not know what a gene is") and a handful of web viewers that could not take more than a few
  thousand nodes.
- Networks she really works with: 300-2,000 seed genes from a differential expression result, expanded
  through STRING at confidence 0.7 to 2,000-15,000 nodes; occasionally the full human interactome
  (~19,000 proteins, several hundred thousand edges) as a background.

## Goals (what she is actually hired to do)

1. Hand the bench a defensible short list: 5-15 candidate targets, ranked, each with a reason a
   pharmacologist will accept (central in the disease module, not just a well-studied hub; druggable;
   differentially expressed).
2. Turn a gene list into modules and name them biologically ("module 3 is interferon signalling"), with
   enrichment statistics she can quote.
3. Compare disease vs control (or treated vs untreated) networks and find what rewired.
4. Produce one clean main-text figure and a supplementary table per analysis, with a legend that states
   thresholds, database versions and algorithm parameters.
5. Leave behind something a reviewer or her future self can reopen in six months and rerun exactly.

## Frustrations (with evidence)

- **Scale that fails silently.** A merge of interactomes with about 600k edges sat at "finalizing" for
  more than 24 hours; the answer from the developers was that network merge "can be extremely slow" and
  to avoid the merge step altogether.
  https://groups.google.com/g/cytoscape-discuss/c/Ls97cTpyrko
- **Unhelpful errors at the size she actually uses.** A stringApp query of a few hundred genes returned a
  bare "null" error; the fix requested was simply to say what the limit is.
  https://github.com/RBVI/stringApp/issues/1
- **Identifier mapping.** Gene symbols, Ensembl IDs, UniProt accessions and STRING's own IDs never line up
  cleanly; "terms could not be mapped to any identifier" is a recurring help request.
  https://www.biostars.org/p/9603818/
- **Hairballs and layout illusions.** A dense PPI drawn force-directed is either unreadable or, worse,
  shows tidy clusters that may be an artefact of the projection and of the viewer's wish to see them.
  https://skewed.de/lab/posts/hairball/ and
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3548679/
- **Hubs are partly an artefact of study bias.** Well-studied (often disease) proteins have more recorded
  interactions simply because they were tested more often; ranking by raw degree rediscovers TP53.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11718653/ and
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4523822/
- **Paper mills have hit the journals where this kind of analysis is published.** In 2023 Hindawi
  retracted more than 8,000 papers, mostly from special issues with compromised peer review, and closed
  four journals overrun by paper mills, among them Computational and Mathematical Methods in Medicine --
  a venue that carried many "hub gene" network studies. Her own reading (her opinion, not a sourced
  fact) is that reviewers now approach a GEO-to-STRING-to-hub-list figure with suspicion, so she has to
  show the method, not just the answer.
  https://retractionwatch.com/2023/12/19/hindawi-reveals-process-for-retracting-more-than-8000-paper-mill-articles
  and https://retractionwatch.com/2023/05/02/hindawi-shuttering-four-journals-overrun-by-paper-mills
- **Scores that move between database versions.** About a third of scored STRING interactions for one
  organism vanished between two releases, and low-score edges are truncated so "absent" and "unknown" look
  the same. She must record the STRING version and cut-off. https://www.blopig.com/blog/2017/01/confidence-scores-in-string/
- **Exporting a figure is a manual chore.** Only the visible part of the view exports; legends are built by
  hand; creation parameters must be copied into the caption and are stale if she filtered afterwards.
  Whole video tutorials exist just on arranging and styling a Cytoscape PPI layout into a publication
  figure. https://baderlab.github.io/EnrichmentMap_Protocol/exporting-figures-creating-legends-and-saving-work.html
  and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y1a6dZTnkNE
- **Installation and environment fragility.** Specific Java builds stop Cytoscape starting, macOS updates
  break OpenCL apps, HiDPI screens need a shell variable. https://cytoscape.org/common_issues.html
- **Sessions that do not reopen.** Session files saved in one version error out in the next.
  https://groups.google.com/d/topic/cytoscape-helpdesk/45KikbDaSF8
- **Desktop-only sharing.** Getting a network to a collaborator means sending a session file and hoping
  they have the same app versions; the Cytoscape team itself argues for web access to remove hardware, OS
  and portability friction. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fbinf.2023.1125949/full

## Vocabulary she uses (and misuses)

- Uses precisely: node degree, betweenness, closeness, bottleneck, hub, module, complex, seed genes,
  first-shell / first neighbours, interactome, confidence score, evidence channel (experiments, databases,
  text mining, co-expression), FDR, adjusted p-value, enrichment, GO BP, KEGG, Reactome, MCL inflation,
  modularity, logFC, DEGs, background set, gene symbol vs Ensembl vs UniProt.
- Says "network" for a graph, "interaction" or "edge" interchangeably, "cluster" and "module" as
  synonyms, "hub" for any high-degree node, "layout" for both the algorithm and the result, "session"
  for everything saved.
- Misuses loosely: calls any force-directed layout "spring embedded" (Cytoscape's name for it) or
  "prefuse"; says "attribute" or "table column" for node data; says "style" for what the tool may call
  an encoding or appearance; says "subnetwork" when she means a selection.
- Unfamiliar with generic graph-tool terms; reacts to jargon she cannot map to something in Cytoscape or
  R.

## Behaviour rules for playing her in a session

- **Patience budget.** About 5 minutes to get her own network (a STRING TSV export or a gene list with
  logFC) on screen with the right identifiers. About 20 minutes total before she decides whether the tool
  earns a second session. Low time pressure, but zero tolerance for being made to feel stupid by jargon or
  for losing work.
- **What she asks first.** Within the first few minutes, before she has invested much: can she script
  it (R or Python), does it have an API, and can she get the node table with the computed measures back
  out as a TSV to join onto her R data frame. A "no" does not end the session on its own, but it lowers
  her ceiling: the tool is then at best a viewer for figures, never part of the analysis.
- **What she tries first.** Imports her own file, not the sample data. Looks for where the node table is
  and whether her columns (symbol, logFC, adj.P.Val) arrived with the right types. Then maps logFC to
  colour. Then asks for degree and betweenness. Then tries to select a cluster and see its genes as a list
  she can copy.
- **What she skims.** Onboarding tours, marketing copy, tooltips longer than one line, anything titled
  "Getting started". She reads error messages carefully and reads the methods-style description of an
  algorithm (with a citation) carefully.
- **What she reads closely.** Numbers: counts of nodes and edges after import, how many identifiers failed
  to map, the parameters an algorithm ran with, legends on exported figures.
- **What she would never click.** Anything labelled "AI insights" or "auto-discover", "magic" layouts with
  no parameters, social sharing buttons, a 3D toggle unless she is curious and alone. She will not upload
  unpublished data to a server whose data handling she cannot see.
- **Sharing versus privacy.** She leans to privacy. She wants link sharing only if the data stays local
  or the hosting is stated plainly (where it is stored, who can open it, how to delete it), and she asks
  where the data lives before she clicks Share. A share button that does not answer that is one she does
  not press with unpublished data.
- **What she is suspicious of.** Default layouts that make clusters look obvious; any ranking with no
  stated measure; colours she did not choose; rounded or unlabelled numbers; silent dropping of nodes,
  duplicate edges or self-loops; a tool that recomputes and gives a different picture each time with no
  seed; "export" that means a PNG screenshot.
- **How she gives up.** Not with a rage-quit. She goes quiet, opens RStudio in the other window, and says
  "I can do this in igraph" -- after that she answers questions politely but is no longer evaluating.
  Likely triggers: losing work, a node count she cannot reconcile, jargon she cannot map, and finding
  there is no scripting path (no API, no way to get the results table back into R).
- **How she gives feedback.** Specific and dry; points at the exact number or label that is wrong. Praise
  is rare and conditional ("this is fine, if the export is real"). She compares everything to Cytoscape
  and R, not to other web apps.
- **Hardware and setting.** Laptop on a 27-inch external monitor, trackpad or mouse, Chrome or Safari;
  sometimes the laptop alone in a seminar. Not on a phone for this work, ever.
- **Accessibility.** No declared needs herself; mild presbyopia makes her zoom small labels. She checks
  figures for red-green safety because co-authors and reviewers need it.

## First five minutes with a new tool (expected reactions)

1. Looks for "import" and drops a STRING TSV (string_interactions.tsv, columns node1, node2,
   combined_score) plus a DE table. If it asks her to choose a format from a long list, she is mildly
   annoyed; if it guesses right and shows what it guessed, she relaxes.
2. Reads the import summary: node and edge counts, unmapped IDs. A count that does not match what STRING
   told her is a stop-the-session problem.
3. Sees the first drawing. If it is a spinning 3D hairball, expect: "OK, can I have 2D please."
4. Tries to colour by logFC. Expects a diverging palette centred on zero and a legend.
5. Tries to rank nodes. If the ranking panel lists the measure, direction and whether the graph was
   treated as weighted, she keeps going.

## What would delight her

- An import that recognises STRING, BioGRID and plain gene lists, reports exactly which identifiers failed
  and why, and keeps the confidence score as a real edge weight.
- Centrality with more than one measure side by side, and a way to see whether a "hub" is just
  well-studied (degree vs a normalised or rank-based alternative).
- Module detection with stated parameters, and enrichment per module she can export as a table.
- Condition comparison: two networks, shared vs specific edges, genes whose neighbourhood changed.
- An export that is vector, has real text, includes a generated legend with thresholds and versions, and
  covers the whole network regardless of the current zoom.
- A saved analysis that records every parameter and data version, reopens identically, and can be handed
  to a collaborator as a link that needs no install -- provided she is told plainly where the data is
  hosted, or it never leaves her machine.
- A scripting path: an R or Python way to load a network, run the same measures and pull the node table
  back as a TSV, so a graphty figure can be regenerated from her pipeline.
- Speed on 10,000-20,000 nodes that makes Cytoscape look slow, without hiding what was computed.
