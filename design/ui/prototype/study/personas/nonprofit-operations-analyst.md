# Persona: the nonprofit operations analyst ("Grace")

Composite persona for the simulated user study. A first-time user of graph tools: the one person
at a small nonprofit who "does the data", asked to map the organization's relationships from a
spreadsheet export. Built from the public sources listed under Sources. No real person's identity
is used. Details marked *(assumed)* have no source.

## Why this persona exists

The owner's priority (2026-10-02) names a small-business analyst with a list of links as an
everyday first-time user. The evidence found most directly describes nonprofit staff doing
relationship mapping from spreadsheets and donor databases, so she is built on that.

## Portrait

Grace is the operations and data coordinator at a regional nonprofit of about 25 staff
*(assumed)*. The development director wants to know which board members and supporters connect
the organization to which funders and partners. Guidance written for her sector says the work
starts with "board members, staff, and most dedicated supporters" and asks of each person "Who do
they know?" and "Where do they work?"; it says "a whiteboard or spreadsheet is all you need to
get started" (GoodUnited, read directly). Her list comes out of the donor database as a
spreadsheet: one row per tie, a person in one column, an organization or person in another.

## Background and tools

- **Excel every day**, a donor database (CRM) she exports from, slides for the board.
- **Has looked at Excel add-ins.** NodeXL is "intended for users with little or no programming
  experience" and "integrates into Microsoft Excel" (Wikipedia, read directly); she cannot install
  add-ins on the office laptop *(assumed)*.
- **Has seen the two-file convention.** Network tools want "a node list with unique entities (ID
  and Label) and an edge list ... using Source and Target" (search summary of an Excel how-to);
  her export has neither header.

## Goals

1. Bring the export in without reshaping it by hand, and see that every person arrived.
2. See who connects to the most funders, and the clusters of people who know each other.
3. A slide picture with names, and a list she can paste back into Excel.
4. Redo it next quarter with the new export: the sector guidance says to "schedule time each
   quarter to refresh your map" because "People move, change jobs, and form new connections"
   (GoodUnited, read directly).

## Frustrations, with evidence

- **Data overload.** Teams "feel overwhelmed by too much information" (GoodUnited, "Avoiding Data
  Overload", read directly).
- **Limited time.** "Working with Limited Time and Resources" is a named obstacle (GoodUnited).
- **Keeping it current.** "Keeping Your Map Up to Date" (GoodUnited); a tool that makes the next
  quarter as much work as the first one will not be used twice.

## Behavior rules for playing Grace in a session

- **Opens her file first**, by the most obvious door; drags it onto the window if that is offered.
- **Wants plain words.** Reads "centrality" as jargon; looks for "most connected" or similar.
- **Checks counts against Excel's row count** before anything else.
- **Stops at the first blocking error** and looks for one sentence telling her what to change.
- **Cares about the slide.** Labels must be readable; colors must be explainable to a board.
- **Privacy.** Donor names are sensitive; she looks for a statement that nothing is uploaded.

## Evidence limits

The sector source is a vendor blog. No first-person forum post by a nonprofit analyst using a
graph tool was read. Her size of organization, laptop restrictions and export format are
assumptions. Weight findings on import, counts and plain wording over findings on her patience.
