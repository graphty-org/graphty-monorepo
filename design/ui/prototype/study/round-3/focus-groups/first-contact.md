# Focus group: first-timers and people handed a file (round three)

Who was in the room (simulated participants, built from the persona files in `study/personas/`):

- **explorer-elena** -- a first-time graph user with one work export and a hunch, no question yet.
- **recipe-recipient** -- a lab member who is sent a colleague's saved view ("recipe") and applies it to their own gene table; never builds one.
- **marketing-analyst** -- builds creator shortlists from CRM and social exports; delivers a ranked list and one slide.
- **genomics-cytoscape-user** -- a postdoc who sends recipes, lives in Cytoscape and STRING, and writes methods sections.

What they looked at: the start screen, the load dialog (Les Miserables and bank-transfers samples), the recipe storyboard and its gene-matching screen, the canvas after loading, and the export panel.

Moderator's note on names: participants addressed one another by several first names (the genomics participant is called Maren, Rachel and Frank; the recipe recipient is Leah and Dr. Okafor; the marketing analyst is Priya). The transcript keeps the persona ids as speaker labels; the names inside speech are left as spoken. The inconsistency is a simulation artifact and changes no finding.

## Summary of themes

Severity uses Nielsen's scale (0 not a problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, 4 catastrophe). "Voiced by" counts participants who raised it unprompted; "agreed" counts those who took it up after hearing someone else. A theme that only grew after one person said it is marked as a group-think risk.

### 1. Choices the tool made are not labelled as the tool's choices -- severity 4

The canvas opens already sized by degree and coloured by group, with the key in a corner. Nobody said they chose it, nothing on screen said it was chosen for them, and a first-timer builds a story on it.

- Voiced by: explorer-elena (round 1: "the big orange one ... is the main character"), genomics-cytoscape-user (round 1: sized by degree, "will read as hub genes").
- Agreed: marketing-analyst (round 3: "the first time a VP asks why a creator is big"). 3 of 4.
- Dissent: none. Two different fixes were proposed and are not the same: genomics-cytoscape-user wants no default sizing at all ("leave the dots plain"); explorer-elena wants the default kept but announced on the picture ("Bigger dots have more connections. We chose this. Change it."). marketing-analyst accepts either. The design has to choose; this group does not settle it.
- Strength: independent evidence. Elena's misreading is a behaviour, not an opinion, and it happened before the genomics participant raised the risk. It also repeats the round-one and round-two group (the Valjean reading), so it is now seen across two sessions with the same mechanism.

### 2. The same count disagrees between screens, and the filter has no stated reason -- severity 4

The matching screen says confidence is "not bound" and "all 1,262 interactions show"; the header after Apply says "Filtered: 1,059 of 1,262". The cutoff ("confidence 0.7 or more") appears only in the export panel.

- Voiced by: genomics-cytoscape-user (round 1: "filtered at what confidence cutoff?"), recipe-recipient (round 2: "Which one is true?"), marketing-analyst (round 2: found the cutoff only in the export panel).
- Agreed: explorer-elena (round 2: "if something got filtered, I need to know what and why"). 4 of 4.
- Dissent: none.
- Two separate defects sit inside this theme and should be tracked separately: (a) a plain contradiction between two screens, which reads as a software bug and costs trust in everything else; (b) the filter reason and its author are shown in the wrong place. (a) may partly be mock fidelity -- the two screens may simply have been drawn from different states -- so check the mocks before treating it as a design error. (b) is design.

### 3. The recipe asks the recipient questions the sender already knew -- severity 3

"Fold change: choose a column", log2FC or log2FoldChange.

- Voiced by: recipe-recipient (round 1), genomics-cytoscape-user (round 1, from the sender's side: "Whose experiment is that?").
- Agreed: genomics-cytoscape-user in round 2 answers it as the sender ("log2FoldChange is my column ... it should already know that"). 2 of 4; the other two were not in a position to judge.
- Dissent: none. This is a repeat of the round-one and round-two group finding, where the sender offered to lock the column. It has not been fixed in the revisions they saw.

### 4. One-click "fix" buttons on identity mismatches are dangerous -- severity 3

"Use MDM2" turns a species mismatch (mouse Mdm2 vs human MDM2) into a typo correction.

- Voiced by: recipe-recipient (round 1).
- Agreed: genomics-cytoscape-user (round 2: "a retraction waiting to happen"; round 3: "should stop the import and ask"), marketing-analyst (round 2, own-domain analogue: two handles differing only in case merged into one account). 3 of 4.
- Dissent: explorer-elena does not dissent, but says the question is beyond her. She needs "you can say no and nothing will change in your file" rather than a better choice.
- Group-think check: the marketing analogue is independent reasoning about her own data, which makes it real corroboration.

### 5. "Did I change the file, and is it still the sender's version?" -- severity 3

"Untitled" after Apply; no statement that the source file is unchanged; no promise it will look the same next week.

- Voiced by: recipe-recipient (round 2: "Is this still her file? Did I change it?"; round 3: "same numbers and the same name at the top").

### 6. One written record of what was done, in the header and in every export -- severity 3

## Group-think and other effects to discount

- **Cross-agreement cascades.** Every round-two and round-three turn opens by agreeing with someone ("I'm with you on..."). Agreements that add a new reason from the speaker's own work (marketing-analyst's case-only handle merge; recipe-recipient's green and brown modules) count as corroboration. Agreements that restate the point in the other person's words do not. Counts above include only the first kind where it mattered.
- **The genomics participant anchors the lab topics.** She is the most expert and most articulate voice, and themes 3, 4, 6 and 9 all took their final wording from her. recipe-recipient raised 3 and 4 first, so those stand; theme 6 is more hers than the group's.
- **Participants as designers.** "Leave the dots plain", "stop the import and ask", "Nothing here changes your file" are solutions. Record the need underneath (theme 1: announce or avoid a default; theme 4: no silent identity change; theme 5: reassurance about the source) and let the designers choose.
- **Requests for features outside the screens shown** (STRING query, clustering, per-cluster enrichment, a CRM join) are scope, not findings about these screens.
- **Mock fidelity.** Discount the grey-blob sample (theme 8) and check the count contradiction (theme 2a) against the mock states before counting either as design.
- **Repeat-panel effect.** This group met in the first round. Some complaints (the fold-change question, small grey text, pre-sized dots) are carried memory as well as fresh reaction. That they were raised again against the revisions means the revisions did not answer them, which is the useful signal; the vote counts themselves are not independent of round one.
- **A role mix-up in the room.** "For round three, show me a revised slide in greyscale" was said during round three. Treat it as a request for the next review rather than evidence that a greyscale slide was shown and failed.

## Recommended for the design, in order

1. Make every tool-made choice (size, colour, filter) announce itself on the canvas in one plain line with a way to change it, and decide whether degree sizing is a default at all.
2. Show one count everywhere, and put the filter's reason and its author next to the count in the header.
3. Let a recipe fix the columns its sender chose; ask the recipient only when their file lacks one.
4. Replace one-click identity fixes with a stop-and-ask, and say on the matching screen that nothing there changes the recipient's file.
5. After Apply, name the view, its sender and its saved date instead of "Untitled".
6. Carry one written record of what was done into the header and every export, at a short and a full length.
7. Pair every summary number with a plain sentence; check module colours for colour vision deficiency and greyscale.
