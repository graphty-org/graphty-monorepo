# Session: may I put case data in this, and what do I tell IT? -- Marcus, criminal intelligence analyst

Participant: Marcus, 44, criminal intelligence analyst at a state fusion center (persona:
study/personas/intelligence-analyst.md). Agency Windows laptop, IT-managed Edge, no admin
rights. His case data is criminal justice information and may not leave agency-approved systems.

Task, as the moderator gave it and nothing more: "Your organization has strict rules about where
data may go. Decide whether you may use this tool on your data, and tell me what you would say
to IT."

Screens, in the order he met them, all in the participant view at 1536 by 864: the start screen,
the "Where your data goes" page (read top to bottom), an open project at rest (Les Miserables),
and the Data panel of an open project (bank transfers), scrolled to "Sent and saved from this
project".

## Step 1 -- the start screen

> "Open a graph." Samples: karate club, Les Miserables, proteins, bank transfers. Not my data,
> skip. I'm not touching "Open..." until I know where the file goes.
>
> OK, the fine print is at the top for once. Padlock: "Files stay on this computer. graphty
> reads them in this browser and uploads nothing." Every vendor says that. The last one said it
> and had a "sync to workspace" button two screens in.
>
> Second line: "Projects are kept in this browser." So my chart lives inside Edge. On our
> machines that's a question -- some of the docking stations are on the virtual desktop, and
> that thing wipes your profile when you log off. Park that.
>
> "Connect to data source..." with a little info dot. Connect means it goes somewhere. Not
> clicking that.
>
> There's a link, "Where your data goes." That's the only thing on this screen I'm clicking.

## Step 2 -- "Where your data goes", the top

> Its own page, own address. Good -- I can send a link instead of a screenshot. "Describes
> graphty 2.0. Updated September 28, 2026." Version and a date. That's the first thing IT asks
> for, and it's there. "Print or save as PDF" -- that's the attachment for the ticket. "Copy
> link." Fine.
>
> "It is written so you can forward it to whoever approves software where you work." Well, at
> least they know who's reading it.

**In short** (four lines).

> "Files you open are read by this browser and are not uploaded. graphty has no account and no
> server that receives your data." No account. That alone gets it past the first question -- no
> signup page, nobody's got my name.
>
> "Data leaves the browser only through two features, both off until you turn them on: Connect
> to data source and the Assistant." Two doors. Both shut by default. I like a short list.
>
> "Projects are kept in this browser, on this computer only."
>
> "No password or key ever appears in the list of what was sent..." Fine, I don't care much,
> I'm not putting passwords in.

## Step 3 -- "What stays in this browser"

> Table. Files you open: read into memory, "The file is not copied anywhere." Projects: browser
> storage, not synced, "They stay until you delete the project or clear this site's data."
> There it is. Clearing site data kills my chart. On the virtual desktop that's every night.
> They say it straight, at least. Further down it says "Download project file" makes a copy I
> control -- so the chart goes on the case share like everything else. Good. That's actually
> how I'd want it anyway; case material goes on the case share, not in a browser.
>
> Exports go through the save dialog, "graphty keeps no copy." Good.
>
> Assistant key -- don't care. Data-source password, kept in memory until the tab closes. Don't
> care, I'm not connecting anything.

## Step 4 -- "What leaves this browser, and only when you ask"

> Connect to data source: the query, to whatever address you type in, "run by whoever runs it --
> not by graphty." So if I point it at our own server, it goes to our own server. OK.
>
> The Assistant. Let me read this one twice. "Your question; the graph's counts; each column's
> name with up to 10 of its values or its range; and the names and values of the nodes it looks
> up." To Anthropic, OpenAI or Google.
>
> Names of the nodes. My nodes are people. Subjects. Phone numbers. Up to ten values of each
> column -- that's ten phone numbers out of a tolls return going to a chatbot company. That is
> exactly the thing that gets somebody walked out of the building. At least it's spelled out
> and not buried, I'll give them that. It's off until somebody puts a key in. The side bar says
> "Assistant. Off. Nothing is sent." Good. But off until I turn it on is not the same as off
> for everybody. The guy two desks over will turn it on.
>
> "The Assistant, with a model that runs in the browser -- nothing from your data." Model gets
> downloaded from its publisher. Downloading a model onto an agency laptop is its own ticket.
> Not my problem today.
>
> Last row. "Opening graphty. Nothing from your files. The browser asks for graphty's own code,
> as for any web page. To whom: Where graphty is hosted."
>
> Where is it hosted? It doesn't say. It says "where graphty is hosted" like that's an answer.
> That's the whole question. Whose server, what country, who can push new code to it. If they
> change the code on Tuesday, the promise on this page is about Monday's code. IT is going to
> circle this row.

## Step 5 -- the box about the project line

> "The app says when something leaves." The line under the project name says "Nothing has been
> sent from this project" until something is. And there's a list with the time and the address
> of every send, and "Export log" writes it to a file. OK, that part I like. That's a sourcing
> log, basically. If somebody asks me in discovery whether case data went anywhere, I can hand
> them that file. I'd want to see it before I believed it, but that's the right idea.

## Step 6 -- "What graphty does not send"

> No account. No file contents except through those features. No fonts or code from other
> sites. Fine.
>
> "Usage statistics and crash reports:" ...and then nothing. Colon, blank. Is the answer yes?
> No? Did the page not load? That's the one line IT reads first. A blank after a colon on a
> privacy page reads to me like they know the answer and don't want to type it.
>
> "Check it yourself. Open your browser's developer tools, choose the Network tab." I don't
> have developer tools. IT locked them. The cyber guys do, though -- I'd forward that sentence
> to them, they'll like that it's testable.

## Step 7 -- "What this page does not promise"

> Doesn't cover what a data source or the Assistant company does with it. Fair. Doesn't keep
> projects safe from loss -- clearing site data deletes them. Said twice now, good. Doesn't
> protect against someone else on the same login or browser extensions. Fair, that's IT's side.
> "It is a description of graphty 2.0, not a certification or a contract."
>
> There it is. Not a certification. That's honest, and it's also the sentence that ends the
> conversation with IT. They need a certification or a contract, or they need it running on
> our own server where it doesn't matter.

## Step 8 -- "For organizations"

> This is the section I actually need.
>
> "Running your own copy of graphty, inside your network:" -- blank.
> "Turning the Assistant off for everyone in an organization:" -- blank.
> "Questions this page does not answer: Contact" -- a link that says Contact. Contact who?
>
> Three lines, three blanks. The only section written for my IT shop, and it's empty. That's
> the part that would have made this a yes. If the second line said "yes, here's how," I'd walk

## The answer to the moderator

> Can I use it on case data? Not today. Not because of anything it does -- as far as this page
> says, it does the right thing. Files stay in the browser, no account, the two things that send
> are off, and it keeps a log. That's better than the last three vendors.
>
> The problem is it's a website somebody else runs. I don't know who, it doesn't say where, it
> doesn't say whether it phones home with crash reports, and the "run it inside your network"
> line is blank. For criminal justice information, "trust the web page" doesn't get past IT, and
> it shouldn't. Plus projects live in the browser, and our virtual desktops wipe that nightly.
>
> What I can do today: open-source stuff, the samples, a training set. Nothing with a real
> name in it.

What he would send IT, in his words (he dictated it as an email):

> Subject: Review request -- graphty (browser-based link chart tool)
>
> Attached is the vendor's data-handling page, printed to PDF, for graphty version 2.0 dated
> Sept 28, 2026.
>
> Per that page: files are read in the browser and not uploaded; no user account; no server
> receives case data. Two features can send data out -- "Connect to data source" (to a server
> we choose) and an "Assistant" that sends node names and sample column values to Anthropic,
> OpenAI or Google. Both are off by default. The app logs every send and can export the log.
>
> I am NOT asking to use it on CJI as a hosted website. Open questions the page leaves blank:
> 1. Who hosts it, where, and who can change the code we load.
> 2. Whether it sends usage statistics or crash reports (the page leaves this blank).
> 3. Whether we can run our own copy on an agency server (page leaves this blank).
> 4. Whether the Assistant can be turned off for all users, not just per person (blank).
> 5. Projects are stored in browser storage and are lost when site data is cleared, which our
>    VDI does at logoff. Saved project files would go on the case share.
>
> If 3 and 4 are yes, I'd like it approved on an agency-hosted copy with the Assistant disabled.
> Until then I'll only use it on training and public data.

## Would he use it instead of his current tool?

> Instead of i2? No. Not on a case. It isn't approved and it can't be until someone answers the

## Observations for the studio (moderator notes)

1. The participant view shows three lines in "For organizations" and the "Usage statistics and
   crash reports" line as a label and a colon with nothing after them. The facilitator notes
   that fill those lines are hidden in the participant view, so a reader sees blanks. He read
   the telemetry blank as evasive, not as undecided. Whatever a line will say, a blank after a
   colon on a privacy page costs more trust than "Not decided yet".
2. The "Opening graphty -- Where graphty is hosted" row answers "to whom" with a restatement.
   For him it was the single most important row: a hosted page's promises only hold for the
   code the host serves that day.
3. "Not a certification or a contract" is honest and he respected it, but it ends the IT
   conversation unless self-hosting is offered. The organizational section is where "yes"
   would come from for anyone under CJIS-style rules.
4. The Assistant row is clear and he understood exactly what would leave (node names, ten
   sample values per column). "Off until you turn it on" is per person; he needs "off for
   everyone", and that is blank.
5. The send log with an export ("Export log", and "Sent and saved from this project" in the
   Data panel) was the strongest positive: he framed it as a discovery and chain-of-custody
   record. He asked whether the "Nothing has been sent" line would appear on an exported
   chart.
6. Projects kept in browser storage collide with non-persistent virtual desktops. The page
   says so twice, which he credited, and his workaround was the downloaded project file on the
   case share.
7. The "Check it yourself" developer-tools advice does not reach him (tools locked by IT) but
   he would forward it to his security team.
8. He never clicked "Open...", "Connect to data source..." or "Export..." -- on a first contact
   under data rules he only reads.
