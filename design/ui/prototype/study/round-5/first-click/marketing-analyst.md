# First-click test -- Jordan, marketing network analyst

Jordan looked at one still screen per prompt and said the one thing she would
click first, and how sure she was (1 = pure guess, 7 = certain). Her answers
were recorded before they were checked against the intended targets, and were
not changed afterwards.

## Answers

### 1. A picture of the network for the paper (Les Miserables, nothing selected)
Click: "The whole novel" under Views, because of the little camera icon.
Sure: 4 of 7.
She said: "Camera means picture, right? That's a snapshot of the whole thing.
Honestly I'd have hit export if I could see the word. And it's a paper, not a
deck, so I'd need it big."
Result: WRONG. The intended first clicks were the main menu, the project name,
or Data on the rail. The camera icon on saved views reads as "take a picture".

### 2. Repeat the earlier bridges calculation exactly (Les Miserables, nothing selected)
Click: the "Bridges -- done" row under Results on the right.
Sure: 5 of 7.
She said: "That's the only place it says 'done', so that's the thing that ran.
I'd hope clicking it shows the settings. If it just re-runs it I'll be annoyed."
Result: CORRECT.

### 3. Rank the characters a second way (Les Miserables, nothing selected)
Click: the + next to Results.
Sure: 4 of 7.
She said: "Results has Bridges in it, so plus should add another one. I'd
rather have a button that says 'Find influencers', but fine."
Result: CORRECT.

### 4. Groups 2 and 3 look alike -- recolor one (Les Miserables, nothing selected)
Click: the orange swatch next to "2" in the legend box.
Sure: 5 of 7.
She said: "Group 3 isn't even in the key, it's under '6 more', so I'd change 2.
Also, these are numbers. My VP will ask what group 2 is."
Result: CORRECT.

### 5. Get back a selection a stray click cleared (Les Miserables, nothing selected)
Click: the three-line menu at the top left, looking for Edit or Undo.
Sure: 2 of 7.
She said: "Honestly I'd hit Command-Z first and hope. If that does nothing I'd
look in the menu. I don't see an undo anywhere on screen."
Result: CORRECT (main menu). Her real first move would have been the keyboard
undo, which the test logs separately.

### 6. Why is Valjean this color? (Les Miserables, Valjean selected)
Click: "Group color" under Appearance on the right.
Sure: 5 of 7.
She said: "It lists what's painting him. Group color, he's group 2, orange.
Makes sense. Took a second to realise Appearance is about him, not the whole
map."
Result: CORRECT.

### 7. Where does Valjean's betweenness come from? (Les Miserables, Valjean selected)
Click: the "betweenness 0.57, highest" row under Results.
Sure: 5 of 7.
She said: "That's the number, so click the number. If it doesn't tell me which
method and whether it's normalised I won't put it in front of anyone. 0.57 of
what?"
Result: CORRECT.

### 8. Bring in next month's transfers file (Transfers, nothing selected)
Click: Data on the left rail.
Sure: 4 of 7.
She said: "Data, that's where files go. The little file chip under the name
was tempting too. I'd want it to say 'replace' so I don't end up with two
Marches."
Result: CORRECT.

### 9. Accounts that take in far more than they send (Transfers, nothing selected)
Click: the Table strip at the bottom, to sort by money in and money out.
Sure: 3 of 7.
She said: "I'd just want the table and sort. That's what I'd do in Excel.
There's a blob of grey hexagons and no labels, so the map's not helping."
Result: WRONG. The intended first clicks were the + on Results, the Quick
actions (lightning) button, or the main menu. She went for the table out of
habit and did not think of in-versus-out money as something to calculate.

### 10. Cheapest route, where a bigger transfer costs more (Transfers, nothing selected)
Click: the second toolbar button (the icon that looks like a winding route).
Sure: 3 of 7.
She said: "That one looks like a path, like a map route. I don't know how I'd
tell it bigger transfers cost more. I saw 'amount not used yet' up on the
right but I'm not touching that until I know what it changes."
Result: CORRECT (Path tool).

### 11. IT asks whether anything has left the computer (Transfers, nothing selected)
Click: "Nothing has been sent from this project" under the project name.
Sure: 6 of 7.
She said: "It's right there with a lock. That's the first thing I'd want to
screenshot for IT. I'd click to see if there's more detail -- is it stored?
Where?"
Result: CORRECT.

### 12. Ribosome and Spliceosome are two blues -- change one (Proteins, nothing selected)
Click: the swatch next to Spliceosome in the legend.
Sure: 6 of 7.
She said: "Yeah, they're both blue. On a projector those are the same color.
Click the square."
Result: CORRECT.

### 13. Bring in the lab's file of team colors and sizes (Proteins, nothing selected)
Click: the + next to Style stack.
Sure: 3 of 7.
She said: "Colors and sizes is style, so the style plus. Maybe it lets me load
one. I wouldn't think to go to Data -- that's for the network, not the look."
Result: WRONG. The intended first clicks were the main menu or Data on the
rail. She treats a style file as a style thing, not a data thing, and looked
where the styles live.

### 14. How is the Ribosome module different from the rest? (Proteins, nothing selected)
Click: "Ribosome" in the legend.
Sure: 4 of 7.
She said: "Click it and hopefully it picks all of them and tells me something
-- how many connect outside, who the hubs are. If it just highlights them,
okay, then I'd open the table."
Result: CORRECT.

## Score

11 of 14 correct.

Misses:
- A picture for the paper: she went to the camera icon on a saved view.
- Accounts that take in more than they send: she went to the table to sort.
- The lab's style file: she went to the + on the style stack.

## In her words, at the end

"Most of it I could find. The camera icon thing -- if that's not a picture,
change the icon. And the Les Mis groups are just numbers, which is fine for a
novel but my segments have names. The 'nothing has been sent' line is the best
thing on the screen, honestly; that's the question legal asks me every time.
The money one I'd just do in a spreadsheet unless something told me the tool
could do it."
