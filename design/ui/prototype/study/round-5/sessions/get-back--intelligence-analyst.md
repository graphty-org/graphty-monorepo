# Get back -- Marcus, criminal intelligence analyst

**Participant:** Marcus, criminal intelligence analyst at a state fusion center. Ten years of Army
all-source work, then i2 Analyst's Notebook and Excel every day. Windows laptop, Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y
from Office. Distrusts any number he cannot trace back.

**Task as given by the moderator:** "Something on the screen just changed that you did not expect.
Get back to where you were."

**Screens seen:** the undo mock in its participant view (the Les Miserables sample, 27 of 77 nodes,
three filter steps, a hand-built selection of 18 characters just cleared by a click on empty canvas);
the same screen after one Ctrl+Z, after Ctrl+Y, the line's Show in steps, the steps list with one
step unticked, and the table's Previous selection button. He did not open the main menu.

## Think-aloud

**The starting screen.** "All right. What moved. Picture's about the same -- the orange bunch up
top, Valjean in the middle, the green crew on the right. Top left says 27 of 77 nodes, 3 steps.
Right side used to be my people -- the count, the colours by group. Now it just says
'Co-appearances, Graph' and some statistics. So my selection's gone."

"Table says 'Selected: none, showing the previous selection.' Which is it? Selected none, or
showing the selection? I read that twice. I think it means: nothing's picked, but these rows are
what I had picked. OK. The rows are still Valjean, Fantine, Thenardier, Javert. That's my list. At
least it didn't wipe the table."

"I must have clicked on the white. i2 does the same thing to me."

**First move: Ctrl+Z.** "Every program I own, Ctrl+Z."

(Presses Ctrl+Z.)

"No. No, no. It went the other way. There's a whole blue crew back at the bottom -- Marius,
Gavroche, Enjolras -- that's the students, I took them out on purpose. Top left says 40 of 77, '2
of 3 steps'. And there's a black box over the tools: 'Undone: Filter out group 8. Show in steps.'"

"So undo didn't undo my click. It undid my filter. The last thing that happened to me was the
click. Why does undo skip it? In Excel if I undo, it undoes the last thing, period."

"Good news is it told me what it did. I'd rather it told me before than after, but at least it
named it. 'Filter out group 8' -- I don't know what group 8 is, I'd have called it the students.
But the blue ones came back, so that's what it means."

**Second move: Ctrl+Y.** "Put it back. Ctrl+Y."

(Presses Ctrl+Y.)

"OK, students gone again, 27 of 77, three steps. Box says 'Redone: Filter out group 8.' Fine. I'm
where I started, still without my selection. Two keystrokes to stand still."

"That box is still sitting there. Didn't fade out on me before I'd read it, I'll give it that --
I hate those little popups that vanish while you're squinting at them."

**Third move: Show in steps.** "What's 'Show in steps'? Let me see what steps it thinks I have."

(Clicks Show in steps. A panel titled Filter steps opens at the top left with three rows, the last
one marked.)

"Filter to degree >= 2, took out 17, 60 left. That's mine -- drop the one-offs. Filter to degree
>= 5, took out 20, 40 left, 'keeps only nodes with at least 5 neighbors among the 60 it reads'.
Filter out group 8, took out 13, 27 left."

"Hang on. Five? I didn't want five. I wanted anybody with two or more. Five's why I'm down to 27 --
that cut twenty people. That's the thing that changed. Maybe I fat-fingered a 5 in the box earlier,
maybe I did it on purpose an hour ago and forgot. Either way it doesn't say. No time on it, no 'you
added this at 2:14'. In my world every step has a date on it. If I have to explain on the stand why
twenty people aren't on the chart, 'there was a step' isn't an answer."

"I like that it says how many each one took out. That's the thing Excel filters never tell you. I
can read that chain like a ledger: 77, 60, 40, 27."

**Fourth move: untick the five.** "There's a checkbox. Untick it."

(Unticks Filter to degree >= 5.)

"47 of 77, '2 of 3 steps'. The row stays, greyed, 'off, takes nothing out'. Good -- I'd rather it
stayed where I can see it than disappeared. If the sergeant says 'what about only the heavy
callers', I tick it back."

"Now the right side says components 3. And look at the right of the picture -- there's a dot out
on its own and a pair off by themselves. First step says at least 2 connections. That guy on his
own has zero lines on my screen. So which is lying, the filter or the picture?"

"...Oh. Probably he had his two connections to the students, and I took the students out after. So
he passed the first test and then lost his friends. It makes sense once I work it out, but nothing
told me. The five step had a little note saying what it counts against; the students step, and the
one before it, don't. A juror won't work that out. I'd have to."

**Fifth move: the selection.** "Now my people. Table still says 'showing the previous selection',
and there's a button right next to it: 'Previous selection'. That's plain enough."

(Clicks Previous selection.)

"There. 18 nodes on the right, the rings are back on the chart, table says 'Selected: 18 of 47
nodes'. Same colours list -- group 2, 4, 5, 3. That's my Valjean crew."

"So the button did what I wanted Ctrl+Z to do in the first place. It was sitting right there in
the table the whole time. I went past it because the first thing any of us does is Ctrl+Z."

**Did he get back?** "Yes. Students out, one-offs out, the five gone, my 18 picked. Took me five
moves and one of them made it worse. Nothing got lost, though, and that's the part that matters.
If I'd hit Ctrl+Z three or four times the way I do in Excel when I panic, I don't know what I'd
have been looking at. I'd have had to go read that box."

## Single Ease Question

**5 of 7.** "Not hard, once I read the box. But the first thing I tried went the wrong way, and the
thing that fixed my selection wasn't undo. The steps list saved me -- I found my mistake there, not
from undo."

## Would he use this instead of his current tool?

"For getting back after a slip -- this is better than i2. i2's undo is a stack you can't see into;
this told me what it undid and the list shows every filter with how many it cut. I'd take that."

"Instead of i2, no. Not on this. Nothing here tells me where the file lives, there are no phones or
cars, and a filter step with no date and no name on it doesn't go in a case file. Give the steps a
time stamp and a who, make Ctrl+Z bring back the thing I just clicked away, and I'd put it in front
of my supervisor."

## Moderator observations

- **First reach: Ctrl+Z, without looking at anything else.** He had already read the table's scope
  line and understood his selection was gone, and still pressed Ctrl+Z. Undo reversed a filter step
  instead of the selection clear he had just suffered. He called this out immediately as undo
  "skipping" the last thing that happened to him.
- **He read the undo line word for word and recovered with Ctrl+Y**, not by ticking the step back.
  One press, read, reversed. He never risked a second Undo, but said unprompted that in a panic he
  would have pressed it several times.
- **He found the wrong step through the line's Show in steps, used out of curiosity after Redo**, not
  through the filter chip. He did not recognise the degree >= 5 step as a mistake until he saw its
  row with "took out 20".
- **He asked when and by whom the step was added.** The steps list has no time or author. For him
  this is an evidence question, not a convenience.
- **He unticked, never deleted,** and liked that the row stays visible, off.
- **The isolated nodes after the fix confused him.** With the middle step off and group 8 still out,
  the statistics read 3 components and one character sits alone on the canvas, under a first step
  that says "degree >= 2". He worked out why (the step order) on his own after a pause, and said a
  jury would not. Only the middle step carries the note about which graph it counts against.
- **"Previous selection" in the table's scope line was the control that restored the selection**, and
  he found it by reading the scope line. He did not open the main menu or the Edit submenu at all.
- **"Selected: none, showing the previous selection" read as contradictory to him** on first pass;
  he had to read it twice.
- **"group 8" meant nothing to him**; he identified it only by which characters came back on screen.
- **End state:** 47 of 77 nodes, 2 of 3 steps (degree >= 5 off, group 8 on), 18 selected. The
  intended done state, reached in five moves with one wrong turn (the first Ctrl+Z).
