# Session: put my name on my notes before a colleague reads them -- Marcus, criminal intelligence analyst

Participant: Marcus, 44, criminal intelligence analyst at a state fusion center (persona:
study/personas/intelligence-analyst.md). Works in i2 Analyst's Notebook and Excel; not a
programmer. Writes every product expecting to defend it in discovery.

Task as the moderator gave it, and nothing more: "A colleague opens this project next week and
will read your notes beside her own. Make sure she can tell which ones are yours."

Pages looked at, as a participant sees them (design notes hidden): the Preferences screen, the
Notes panel screen, the Take a note screen and the Export dialog screen. Renders:
shots/record/r4-marcus-names-prefs-full.png (every Preferences state, one under the other),
shots/record/r4-marcus-names-notes-s1.png to -s10.png (the Notes panel states),

## The answer Marcus gave the moderator

"I can put my name on the notes I write from today: main menu, Preferences, 'Your name on notes
and recipes', type it, Save. She'll see my name next to mine and hers next to hers, because
there'll be two of us in the file. What I can't do is put my name on the notes I already wrote,
and I can't see my name on my own notes until she's added one, so I'm taking it on faith. To be
safe I'd also type my initials at the front of every note, like I do now. And I'd check her
copy of the report before anyone else sees it, because I can't tell whether it names who wrote
each note."

## Single Ease Question

**4 out of 7.**

"Finding the box took two tries -- Preferences isn't where I'd look for who I am -- but once I
found it, typing a name is easy. The hard part is I can't tell it worked, and it won't fix the
notes I already have. The job was 'make sure she can tell', and I can't make sure of anything I
can't see."

## Would he use this instead of his current tool?

"For this? No. In i2 nothing records who wrote what either, so we put initials and a date in the
text, and I can see those, print them, and nobody can change them without it showing. This is a
step up in one way -- the byline is automatic and it sits on every note, like our report
headers. If it showed my name on my notes all the time, let me put my name on notes I wrote
before I set it, showed which notes were edited and by whom, and printed each note's author in
the report, I'd drop the initials habit. Until then I'd do both, and doing both means the tool
isn't doing the job."

## Observations for the study (plain, for the designers)

1. The name setting applies only to notes written after it is set, and nothing lets a person put
   their name on notes they already wrote. The task starts from exactly that situation -- notes
   written before anyone thought about a second reader -- so the only move available leaves the
   existing notes unattributed. He called this the task failing, not a rough edge.
2. Because the byline appears only once a project holds work by two people, a single analyst can
   never see his own name on his own notes before handing the project over. He could not confirm
   the setting worked and said he would not trust a signature he cannot see. He asked for the
   name on every note, always, at least on the note he just added.
3. None of the pages show how an unnamed note looks among named ones. He feared older, nameless
   notes would read as the colleague's, or as something the software wrote.
4. He looked for the name first in the note editor's overflow, then under the project menu's
   "Project info...", and found it in Preferences on the second menu he opened. He said who he is
   is not a preference. The Preferences row also shows the current name under the label, which he
   read correctly once he got there.
5. The dialog does not say where the name is kept. He assumed the browser, and raised the case of
   a remote desktop that clears the browser profile: the name would silently go blank and later
   notes would save with no author and no warning.
6. "Exactly as typed" means anyone can type anyone's name. He accepted the reason (no accounts,
   nothing leaves the building) but said the byline is a label, not a signature he could defend,
   and he would keep typing his initials in the note text.
7. Nothing shows what happens to the byline when the colleague edits his note. He said an edit
   that keeps his name on her words is worse than no name.
8. He could not tell whether Find in notes matches an author's name, and the filter icon beside
   Find on the April notes list has no defined behaviour. For a long list he wanted "only mine" or
   "only hers".
9. In the Export dialog, "Project file: Everything, with the data" does not say that note authors
   travel, and the findings report's first page names one person ("written ... by Sarah Okafor")
   while its notes page does not say whether each note keeps its own author. He read that as a
   risk of attributing one analyst's conclusions to another in an evidence file.
10. The byline format itself -- name, then full date and time, on every row and again on the
    opened note -- was the part he liked; he compared it to the "prepared by" line on his reports.
    "Nothing is uploaded" in the Export footer and the absence of any sign-in were again the lines
    he trusted.
