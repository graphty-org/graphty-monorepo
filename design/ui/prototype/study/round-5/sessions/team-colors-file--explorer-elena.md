# The team's colors file -- Explorer Elena

**Participant:** Elena, a product manager with no graph training. For this task the moderator tells
her to treat the open project, "Mule ring review" (a network of 3,093 payment accounts and the
transfers between them), as her own, and hands her an email attachment from a colleague, Mark:
`fraud-team-colors`. Mark's email says: "Here's our colors and sizes file, so your picture matches
the rest of the team's decks." Clock: curious afternoon (no deadline, but she wants it done).

**Task as given by the moderator:** "Your team always draws its networks the same way, and a
colleague just mailed you the file with those colors and sizes. Make this project look like the
team's."

## The project, before anything

"OK, so this is 'my' project. Big grey... honeycomb thing, and some orange dots with rings. The key
at the bottom says 'Flagged, yes, 14'. So orange is flagged. Fine."

"Mark's decks -- I've seen them -- have green dots and yellow dots and the lines get thicker for
bigger payments. This has none of that. So I need to get his file in here."

She scans the right side. "Right side... 'Data', version April. 'Style stack': Risk color, Size by
PageRank, Mule ring, Base style. Style stack. Hm. Is that like a theme? Like in Slides where you
pick a theme?" She does not click it. "I don't know what 'run' means next to PageRank."

## Getting the file in

"First thing I'd do is just drag it in from Gmail. That usually works or it doesn't."

*She drags the attachment over the picture. The picture gets a blue outline.* "Oh, it's
lighting up. Good, it wants it."

*The "Dropped file" box opens.*

"'fraud-team-colors: style file.' OK, yes, that's Mark's. 'Four layers, bound by attribute name.'
... No idea. Skip."

"Two choices. 'Apply style file on top.' 'Replace style stack with style file.' Hm."

"I want it to look like the team's. So... replace? That sounds like the one that makes it the
team's. But 'the 9 style layers here are removed'." She looks right. "Nine? I see four. Risk color,
PageRank, Mule ring, Base style. That's four. Where are the other five?" Pause. "I probably have
some hidden ones I don't know about. Whatever."

"I'm not removing nine things I can't even see. 'On top' says 'those stay'. That's the safe one.
And it's already blue, so it's the normal one."

"'Either is one undo step.' OK, good. That I like. That's the sentence that makes me click."

"Last line: 'Chargeback heat needs chargeback_rate, which this graph lacks.' Chargeback... we
don't have that column, I think. 'You choose next whether to leave it off.' Fine, I'll leave it
off, whatever that means."

*She clicks "Apply style file on top..."*

## The step in between

"'1 layer to bind.' Bind. Like, tie something? '3 layers matched by name: Cleared accounts,
Merchant hubs, Amount width.' OK, those sound like Mark's -- cleared is the green ones, I think."

"'Chargeback heat, reads chargeback_rate, numbers.' And the box says 'Leave unbound', and next to
it 'no attribute fits'. So there's nothing to pick anyway. Why ask me?"

She opens the drop-down, closes it without choosing. "I'm leaving it how it came."

"'Left unbound, Chargeback heat is kept and switched off, marked missing attribute, with Bind...
on its row.' Too long. Apply."

*She clicks Apply.*

## After Apply

The dark message at the bottom: "'fraud-team-colors applied: style; 1 missing attribute.' Undo.
OK, it did something."

She looks at the picture for a few seconds. "...It's the same. It's the same grey honeycomb and the
same orange dots. Where's the green?"

"Maybe it takes a second." She waits. "No."

She looks right. "OK, the list changed. Cleared accounts, Merchant hubs, Amount width, Charge-
something. They all say 'new'. New... oh, like the new accounts? Up here it says '132 new'. So
it's marking the new accounts from April? That's... actually not what I asked for, but OK." She
says this confidently and moves on.

"And one of them has a yellow warning and a crossed-out eye. That's the chargeback one. I'm not
touching the one with the warning."

*She hovers it. A tooltip: "missing attribute: chargeback_rate. Bind... in its row's menu."*

"Its row's menu. What menu? There's nothing on the row. Right-click?" She tries a two-finger tap.
"Nothing. OK, forget that one, it's off anyway."

"Wait. Where did Risk color go? And the PageRank one? Now it says 'Watchlist ring, recipe', 'Risk
ramp, recipe', '6 more'. I didn't add a recipe. Did Mark's file add a recipe? ... Or did I click
something earlier." She scrolls the panel a little. "Six more. So now there's twelve things. I
don't know what's on my picture anymore."

"The key at the bottom still just says 'Flagged'. If Mark's colors were on, it would say green is
cleared, wouldn't it?"

"And sizes. He said colors and sizes. The dots are the same size as before. 'Amount width' -- is
width a size? Width of what?"

She considers Undo. "I could undo. And then do the Replace one instead. But the Replace one is the
one that removes nine things I can't see." Pause. "I'd just email Mark and ask him to send me a
screenshot of what it's supposed to look like. Or ask him to do it."

## Moderator probe: other ways in

The moderator asks whether she noticed anywhere else the file could have gone, and shows the Data
button's panel.

"Oh. 'Style files', with a plus. Down here, under 'Recipes applied'. No, I never clicked Data. I
thought Data was the spreadsheet." In the navigation mock's Data frame she reads the heading as
"Recipes and style files". "Same thing, one line? Fine. I'd have dragged anyway."

"Would that have told me if it worked? It would say it's there. It wouldn't show me green dots."

The moderator opens the main menu and points at "Recipes". "Is a recipe what Mark sent? He said
'the colors file'. I don't know." The moderator shows the Apply recipe picker. "'Mule ring triage.
3 sets, 2 runs, Personalized PageRank from the watchlist, Cycles up to 4 transfers.' No. That's
way more than colors. That's someone's whole analysis. Not that."

The moderator shows the color picker's Libraries tab on another project. "'Style layers, from
stress-response.graphty-style.' So that's what a style file is called. OK. I would never have
opened a color picker to look for a file."

## Single Ease Question

"Three. Getting the file in was easy -- I dragged it and it asked me something sensible and it said
I could undo. But then the picture didn't change, the list got longer, and I have no idea if it
looks like the team's now. I don't even know what the team's is supposed to look like in this
tool."

**SEQ: 3 / 7**

## Would she use this instead of her current tool?

"For this? No. Today I'd ask Mark or our designer to make the picture, or I'd paste Mark's slide
theme in Slides and I'd see it change right away. Here I did the thing and I still have to ask
Mark. If it had shown me Mark's picture next to mine, or just turned the dots green, then yes --
dropping a file in and done is exactly what I'd want."

## Where engagement dropped

At the project after Apply, when the picture did not change. From then on she stopped trying new
controls: she did not open the new rows, did not try the Undo-then-Replace route she talked about,
and did not open "6 more". Her answers during the moderator probe were short ("Not that", "Fine").

## What the moderator noted (not said to the participant)

- **The picture does not change when the style file is applied.** The canvas after Apply is the
  same drawing as before (3,000 accounts as grey density with the 14 flagged accounts ringed in
  orange); none of the file's colors (Cleared accounts green, Merchant hubs yellow) or the Amount
  width lines appear, and the canvas key still lists only "Flagged". The one signal of success is
  the toast and four "new" badges in a list she does not understand. She had no way to tell whether
  the task was done.
- **The counts do not agree with what she can see.** The drop dialog says "Adds 4 style layers
  above the 9 already here" and "The 9 style layers here are removed", while the Style stack beside
  it lists 4 rows. After Apply the lower rows are different ones (Watchlist ring and Risk ramp,
  marked "recipe", then "6 more"); Risk color, Size by PageRank and Mule ring are no longer shown.
  She blamed herself ("I probably have some hidden ones", "did I click something earlier") and
  concluded she no longer knew what was on her picture.
- **"Make it look like the team's" has no direct answer in the choice.** Replace is the literal
  answer, but its line names only what is removed, and she avoids anything that removes. On top is
  the default, and the dialog does not say what the result will look like, or that the new layers
  win where they overlap the old ones. Neither choice offers a preview of the file's look.
- **"new" collides with "132 new".** The badge on the file's rows reads, to her, as "new accounts
  in April", the same word the Data section uses two inches above. She stated the wrong reading
  confidently.
- **The missing layer's fix points at a menu she cannot find.** "Bind... in its row's menu": the row
  shows no menu button, and a trackpad secondary click did nothing she could see. Together with the
  warning icon, she treated the row as "broken, leave it".
- **Sizes.** Mark's "colors and sizes" arrive as "Amount width" (line thickness); dot size stays
  Size by PageRank. She noticed the dots had not changed and did not know whether "width" was the
  size she expected.
- **Vocabulary she skipped or guessed:** layers, bound by attribute name, bind, unbound, attribute,
  style stack, run, recipe. The binding step asks her to decide something where "no attribute fits"
  leaves nothing to decide.
- **Other routes she did not find.** She never opened the Data panel, so she did not see "Style
  files +" (data-panel mock) or "Recipes and style files +" (navigation mock; the two mocks name the
  section differently). What those "+" buttons open is not drawn, nor is the main menu's Recipes
  submenu, so the menu route could not be followed. She did not know whether a "recipe" was what
  Mark sent; the picker's contents (sets, runs, PageRank) told her it was not.
- **What worked.** The canvas lit up as a drop target on her first instinct; the dialog named the
  file and its kind; "Either is one undo step" and the Undo in the toast were the lines that made
  her willing to click.
