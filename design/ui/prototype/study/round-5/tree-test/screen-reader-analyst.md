# Tree test, round 5: Morgan, screen-reader analyst

Simulated participant from `../../personas/screen-reader-analyst.md`. Morgan heard only the text
outline in `../../round-5/tree-test.md`, one level at a time, read top to bottom with the arrow
keys (the outline has no headings to jump by, so Morgan read it linearly, which is how they read
anything new). Two separate sessions, one per arm, with no memory of each other; arm A came first.
Tasks are listed in the order Morgan took them. Confidence is Morgan's own, 1 (guessing) to 7
(certain). Scoring was added afterwards against the answer key; the answers were not changed.

Opening remark: "A tree of text. Finally something I can read at my own speed. Let's see if the
words mean what they say."

A general complaint, made in both sessions: several places are named by where they sit or how
they look -- "Filter chip", "Floating toolbar", "Canvas", "the line under the project name", "the
button at the top of the rail". Morgan went into "Canvas" only when nothing else fit: "Canvas is
where the picture is. The picture usually says 'image'."

## Closing remarks

"Most of this reads like it was written by someone who has heard of words. 'Previous
selection', 'Update with new data', 'Sent and saved', 'Details: method, seed, settings' -- I'd
find those blind, which is the point. What I'd take away: every place named after where it sits
on the screen. 'Chip', 'floating toolbar', 'the line under the name' mean nothing to me until I
open them. And 'recipe' -- I would never have guessed that's where colours live."

On the two arms: "A Results place I can go to is easier to hold in my head than 'the right
panel, with a result selected'. I don't know how I select a result. I know how I go to a place."
