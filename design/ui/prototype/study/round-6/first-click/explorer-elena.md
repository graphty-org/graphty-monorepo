# First-click test -- Explorer Elena

Elena is a product manager who has never used a graph tool. She looked at one still screen per
prompt and said the one thing she would click first. Her confidence runs from 1 (a guess) to
7 (certain). Her answers were marked against the correct targets only after she had given them,
and were not changed.

## Les Miserables, nothing selected

**fc-1. A picture of the network for the paper.**
"A picture... there's still no Download button. The Views have little cameras next to them. A
camera is a photo. I'd click 'The whole novel' and hope it gives me the picture."
Clicked: the "The whole novel" row under Views (camera icon). Confidence 3. **Incorrect.**
She read the camera icon as "take a photo" again. She did not look at the three-line menu, the
project name or Data on the left strip.

**fc-2. Show a colleague how the earlier bridges calculation was set up.**
"Bridges... I see 'Bridges' on the right, in that stack thing, it says 'off'. That's the bridges
one. I'd click that."
Clicked: the "Bridges off" layer in the Style stack. Confidence 4. **Incorrect** (counted
separately: a style layer, not the run's record).
The only place the word "Bridges" appears on the screen is the style layer, so that is where she
went. She did not think of "Results" on the left strip as the place calculations live.

**fc-3. Rank the characters a second way.**
"It says 'Sorted by degree' over the table. So I'd sort by something else. Click 'group'? There
isn't really anything else to click."
Clicked: the "group" column header in the table. Confidence 3. **Incorrect.**
She treated "rank a second way" as re-sorting the columns she already had. The lightning button
has no word on it here and she skipped it.

## Les Miserables, selection just cleared

**fc-4. A stray click cleared the characters she had picked out; get them back.**
"Oh, it literally says it. 'Selection cleared, Bring it back.' Click that."
Clicked: "Bring it back" on the dark notice above the toolbar. Confidence 6.
**Correct in the Ctrl+Z restore design. Correct in the notice-only design.**
She never thought of Ctrl+Z; the notice was the first thing she read.

## Les Miserables, Valjean selected

**fc-5. Where does Valjean's betweenness number come from?**
"Betweenness, 0.57, highest -- on the right under Results. I don't know what betweenness is, but
that's the number, so I'd click it and see if it explains itself."
Clicked: the betweenness row under Results in the right panel. Confidence 4. **Correct.**

## Transfers, nothing selected (3,000 accounts drawn as a grey blob)

**fc-6. Next month's file arrived; bring it in so her setup carries over.**
"That little file thing at the top, 'transfers-2026...'. That's the file. I'd click it and swap
it for the new one."
Clicked: the file chip under the project name. Confidence 4. **Correct.**
She noticed the blue "Change..." on the right too, but the chip was closer to the top.

**fc-7. Accounts that take in far more money than they send out.**
"No idea. It's a grey blob. 'Quick actions' is the only button with words on it. Try that."
Clicked: Quick actions on the floating toolbar. Confidence 2. **Correct.**
Correct by elimination rather than by recognition: it was the only labelled button she saw.

**fc-8. The cheapest route between two accounts, where a bigger transfer costs more.**
"On the right it says 'amount not used yet. Change...'. The amount is the point here, so I'd
turn it on. Click Change."
Clicked: "Change..." on the Loaded line in Statistics. Confidence 3. **Incorrect** (counted
separately).
The word "amount" pulled her in. She did not see the two-dots-and-line button on the toolbar as
anything to do with routes.

**fc-9. IT asks whether anything from this project has left her computer.**
"Right at the top: 'Nothing has been sent from this project'. It's underlined, so I'd click it
to show IT."
Clicked: the line under the project name. Confidence 6. **Correct.**

## Human protein interactions, nothing selected

**fc-10. Ribosome and Spliceosome are two blues she cannot tell apart; change one.**
"The colored square next to Ribosome in the box. Click it and pick another color."
Clicked: the Ribosome swatch in the legend. Confidence 6. **Correct.**

**fc-11. The lab's file of standard colors and sizes.**
"Colors... the little paint palette at the top right, next to Graph. That's where colors go."
Clicked: the palette icon beside Graph in the right panel. Confidence 3. **Incorrect.**
She read the palette icon as "colors". The '+' on the Style stack did not register as a way to
bring in a file.

**fc-12. How does the Ribosome module differ from the rest of the network?**
"Ribosome is the light blue bunch on the right. I'd click on that bunch and see what it says."
Clicked: a dot inside the light-blue cluster on the canvas. Confidence 2. **Incorrect.**
She went to the picture first, as she usually does, and expected clicking the cluster to pick
the whole group. The legend name did not look clickable to her for this.

**fc-13. One protein has no name showing; make its name always show.**
"I know its name, so I'd search for it. There's a magnifying glass up by 'Graphs'."
Clicked: the search icon beside Graphs in the left panel. Confidence 3. **Incorrect.**
She wanted to type the name somewhere. She did not read the small line "7 more hidden where they
overlap" at the bottom of the legend, and the Table strip at the bottom did not look like a
place to search.

## Les Miserables, the betweenness run opened

**fc-14. Run it again with one setting changed, and keep this one to compare.**
"'Compare with...' -- I want to compare. And 'Re-run' sounds like it would wipe this one. So
Compare."
Clicked: "Compare with..." in the opened run. Confidence 4. **Incorrect** (counted separately).
She avoided Re-run because she assumed it would overwrite the run she wanted to keep.

## Score

6 of 14 correct (fc-4, fc-5, fc-6, fc-7, fc-9, fc-10). fc-4 is correct under both undo designs.

What her misses have in common: she clicked the control whose word or icon matched a word in the
task ("picture" to a camera, "bridges" to the Bridges layer, "amount" to Change..., "colors" to
the palette, "compare" to Compare with...), and she never once tried the left strip (Data,
Results) or the three-line menu.
