# Session: may I put case data in this, and what do I tell IT? -- Marcus, criminal intelligence analyst

Participant: Marcus, 44, criminal intelligence analyst at a state fusion center (persona:
study/personas/intelligence-analyst.md). Agency Windows laptop, IT-managed Edge, developer tools
locked, no admin rights. His case data is criminal justice information and may not leave
agency-approved systems.

Task, as the moderator gave it and nothing more: "Your organization has strict rules about where
data may go. Decide whether you may use this tool on your data, and tell me what you would say
to IT."

Screens, in the order he met them, all in the participant view at 1536 by 864: the start screen
(shots/r6-marcus-dsh-start-screen.png), the "Where your data goes" page read top to bottom
(shots/r6-marcus-dsh-data-location.png), an open project at rest on the protein sample
(shots/r6-marcus-dsh-frame-at-rest.png), and the Data panel of an open project, first at the top
and then scrolled to "Sent and saved from this project" (shots/r6-marcus-dsh-data-panel.png,
shots/r6-marcus-dsh-data-panel-s7.png).

## Step 3 -- "What stays in this browser"

> Files: read into memory, "not copied anywhere." Projects: this browser's storage, not synced,
> "They stay until you delete the project or clear this site's data." That's the VDI problem,

> the case share, where case material belongs anyway.
>
> Exports through the save dialog, "graphty keeps no copy." Good. Assistant key, data-source
> password -- not my problem, I'm not using either.

## Step 4 -- "What leaves this browser, and only when you ask"

> Data source: the query goes to the address you type, "run by whoever runs it -- not by
> graphty." If it's our server, it's our server. OK.
>
> A project file that names a data source -- "Nothing, until you confirm." So somebody can't mail
> me a file that quietly calls out. Good. I wouldn't have thought to ask, and IT would have.
>
> The Assistant. Reading it twice. "Your question; the graph's counts; each column's name with up
> to 10 of its values or its range; and the names and values of the nodes it looks up." To
> Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. My nodes are subjects and phone numbers. Ten numbers off a tolls
> return going to a chatbot company is how somebody gets walked out of the building. It's off

>
> Last row, "Opening graphty." To whom: "Where graphty is hosted." That's not an answer, that's
> the question read back to me. Whose server? What state, what country? Who can push new code to
> it? This whole page is a promise about today's code. If the host changes it Tuesday, the page is
> about Monday. IT is going to circle this row in red.

## Step 5 -- the box about the project line

## Step 6 -- "What graphty does not send"

>

>

## Step 7 -- "What this page does not promise"

## The answer to the moderator

> Can I use it on case data? No. Not today.
>
> Not because of anything it does. As far as I can tell it does the right thing: files stay in the
> browser, no account, the two things that send are off, a project file can't call out without
> asking, and it keeps a log of every send and every export that I could hand over in discovery.
> That's better than the last three vendors I sat through.
>
> The problem is it's a website somebody else runs, and the app itself tells me nobody's decided
> who. It hasn't decided about crash reports. It hasn't decided whether we can run our own copy or
> kill the Assistant for everybody. For criminal justice information, "trust the web page" doesn't
> get past IT, and it shouldn't. And projects live in the browser, which our virtual desktops wipe
> every night.
>
> What I can do today: the samples, public data, a training set. Nothing with a real name in it.

## Observations for the studio (moderator notes)

1. The data page and the app now disagree. The Data panel's "Sent and saved from this project"
   says "Not decided yet" for hosting, telemetry and an organization-wide Assistant switch; the
   "Where your data goes" page shows the same three topics as a label, a colon and nothing
   (hosting shows as "Where graphty is hosted", a restatement). He found the app's wording more
   trustworthy than the page's blanks, and was bothered that the page -- the thing he forwards --
   is the weaker of the two. He had to cite the Data panel in his email to IT. Whatever the
   final answer, the page should say at least what the app says.
2. "Running your own copy, inside your network" appears only on the page, blank; the Data panel
   does not list it among the undecided items. For him it is the single line that could turn "no"
   into "yes", and it is the one with the least said about it.
3. "Contact" with no name or address reads as a word, not a way to reach anyone.
4. The send log with "Export log", and the "Saved to this computer" list that says which exports
   carry data ("Project file, with its data" versus a recipe with "no data"), were again the
   strongest positives: he framed them as discovery and chain-of-custody records.
5. He noticed and credited the row saying a project file that names a data source sends nothing
   until confirmed -- a question he said he would not have asked but IT would.
6. The Assistant row is clear; he understood exactly what would leave. "Off until you turn it on"
   is per person, and he needs "off for everyone".
7. He asked again whether the "Nothing has been sent from this project" line can appear on an
   exported chart, so the exhibit carries it.
8. Projects in browser storage collide with non-persistent virtual desktops; the page says so and
   he accepted the downloaded project file as the answer.
9. "Check it yourself" (developer tools) does not reach him; he would forward it to his security
   unit, and asked them to do it in his email.
10. He clicked only "Where your data goes" and the project-name line. He never clicked "Open...",
    "Connect to data source..." or "Export..." -- under data rules, first contact is reading only.
