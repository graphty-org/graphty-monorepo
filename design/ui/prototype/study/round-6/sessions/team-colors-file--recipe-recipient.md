# Session: the team's colors file -- Tom, the recipe recipient

**Task, as the moderator read it:** "Your team always draws its networks the same way, and a
colleague just mailed you the file with those colors and sizes. Make this project look like the
team's."

**Participant:** Tom, lab manager, cell biology lab. Receives files from the lab's postdoc; never
builds them. Mild red-green colour weakness, reading glasses, 13-inch laptop, no admin rights.

## Think-aloud

### 1. The project is open. Where does her file go?

> "Right. 'Mule ring review'. Grey cloud of dots, a handful of orange ones, and a little box
> bottom left that says 'Flagged, yes 14'. Fine. She said 'just drop it on', so I'm dragging the
> attachment out of Outlook onto the picture. That's the first thing I'd do anyway."

He did not open the File menu first. Asked afterwards where he would have gone had the drag not
worked, he opened the main menu, went to File, and read the list aloud:

> "Open, Add data, Add as another graph, Join, Update with new data, Connect, Load set
> collection, Export. None of those is 'colours'. 'Add data' -- no, it's not data, she said it
> had no data in it. I wouldn't know. There's a 'Recipes' further up but nobody told me it's a
> recipe, she called it 'the file with the colours'."

### 2. The dialog after the drop

> "OK, it took it. 'Apply recipe fraud-team-colors'. Well, that's the file name from the email,
> so at least it's the right thing. So it IS a recipe. Good to know."

He read the first line under the file name and stopped at the end of it:

> "'A recipe that holds only styles: 4 layers, bound by attribute name.' Bound. What's bound?
> Is that going to change her file? ... I'll leave that."

Then the two big boxes. He read only the bold first line of each, then went back to the first:

> "'Use these styles.' That's what I want. I want to use her styles. The other one is 'Add these
> styles on top', and I don't want on top, I want it to look like hers.
>
> And it tells me what goes: Risk ramp, Risk color, Mule ring. Three of mine go. Then 'Your
> other 5 stay'. Five plus three is eight, and I count eight rows over on the right. OK. That
> adds up. Last time it didn't."

He paused on "Your other 5 stay":

> "Hang on. If five of mine stay, then it's not the team's look, it's the team's look plus
> five of mine. Watchlist ring, Pass-through edges... I don't know what those are, somebody put
> them there. Does the team's picture have those or not? I'd ask her. I'm still going with the
> first one, because the other one says 'all 8 stay' and that's even further from what she
> sent."

He noticed the "Mule ring" line:

> "'Mule ring' goes. The orange dots are the mule ring, aren't they? So I lose the orange dots.
> Is that what the team does? I don't know. It told me before I clicked, I'll give it that."

Nothing was preselected. He clicked "Use these styles...".

### 3. The one question

> "'Bind attributes'. There it is again. 'Chargeback heat -- reads chargeback_rate, numbers.'
> And the box says 'Leave unbound'. 'This graph has no such attribute. Ask the sender which one
> they meant.'"

He did not open the dropdown.

> "So one of her colours doesn't work on this. That's the one line I understood: ask the
> sender. Fine, I'll ask her. The grey line underneath says it's 'kept and switched off, marked
> missing attribute'. So is my picture wrong now, or just missing a bit? I'd say missing a bit.
> I'd rather it said that in those words."

> "At the bottom: 'One undo step'. Good. Apply."

### 4. After Apply

He looked at the picture first, as he always does.

> "...It's grey. It's the same grey cloud. And now the orange dots are gone too, and the little
> box in the corner is gone. So I have LESS colour than before I started.
>
> Black bar at the bottom: 'fraud-team-colors in use: 3 of your layers replaced; 1 missing
> attribute'. So it says it's in use. Where? Nothing on the picture is green or yellow or
> anything."

Second look, this time at the right-hand side (he admitted he only looked because the picture
gave him nothing):

> "Over here it says 'new', 'new', 'new', 'new' with a green square, a yellow one, a grey ball
> thing, and a pink one with a yellow warning. Cleared accounts, Merchant hubs, Amount width,
> Chargeback heat. So they went in. But the green and the yellow aren't on the picture. Either
> the picture is wrong or the list is wrong, and I can't tell which.
>
> And there's no key any more. If the PI walked in now and asked 'what does green mean', I've
> got nothing on the picture to point at. Before, at least it said 'Flagged, yes 14'."

He hovered by accident over the warning on Chargeback heat and read the tooltip:

> "'missing attribute: chargeback_rate. Bind... in its row's menu'. Bind again. No."

He did not press Undo, but said:

> "I'm not sure whether I've made it look like the team's or wrecked it. That's the thing I
> always worry about. The bar doesn't say her file on my drive wasn't touched, either. I assume
> not. I'd want it to say."

That was his first failure in the sitting. He gave it the usual second try by looking for
anything else that might show the colours.

### 5. The Data panel

The moderator brought up the Data panel (Tom would not have opened it himself; he said "Data?
It's not data, it's colours").

> "Top: 'Nothing has been sent from this project.' That I like. Every screen has it. That's the
> first thing I'd look for before I put our hits in anything."

He skipped the Sources section ("that's her spreadsheet stuff") and read the grey paragraph at
the bottom only because it was the only sentence with the word "colors" in it:

> "'Applied recipes. None yet. A recipe is a file of styles, sets or runs, from a colleague or
> another project; a file of colors and sizes is a recipe too.' Oh. So THAT's where it tells you
> the colours file is a recipe. Down here, under 'Data', in grey. I'd never have seen that. If
> it had been in the File menu I'd have found it. And there's a little plus next to it, which
> I wouldn't click, because I don't know what it adds."

### 6. The + in the Style stack

Shown the + menu on the stack header:

> "'From a recipe or file... Only a recipe's styles; your data stays here.' That's plain. That
> I'd understand, if I'd ever click a plus next to a heading called 'Style stack', which I
> wouldn't. The other two, 'Mute categories', 'Shape by kind'... no, not mine to decide."

### 7. The gene recipe, for comparison

The moderator showed him the expression-overlay recipe on a protein network, the kind the
postdoc actually sends him.

> "Now this one I understand. 'Recipe waiting for data. Expression overlay. Saved by Maren on
> Sep 26.' It tells me who made it and when. 'Colors your genes by log2 fold change, red for up
> and blue for down.' Red and blue, not red and green. Good. 'No data inside.' 'Data stays on
> this computer ... not uploaded.' That's my question answered before I asked it.
>
> Then '84 of 96 genes matched' in big letters, and the twelve that didn't, and two of them
> 'look like a spreadsheet date'. 7-Sep. That's SEPT7. That's exactly what Excel did to us. I'd
> fix that in the spreadsheet. And Mdm2 has a button to use MDM2. I can tell the PI 84, and which
> ones not."

On the question with two fold-change columns:

> "'Two columns could be the fold change.' log2FC is ours, it's in my table. The other one is in
> the network file. I'd pick mine, because it says 'in the table'. I didn't like being asked,
> but at least it told me which was which."

On the applied result:

> "There. Colours on the picture, a key in the corner that says 'Fold change color, log2FC, 84
> genes', minus 2.41 down, 2.98 up. And the bar says '84 of 96 genes matched', with 'Show the 12'.
> That's what I wanted the team file to do. This one I'd put on a slide."

On the variant where the network is missing module and confidence:

> "'Not bound', 'Not bound', yellow warnings. 'Module color is kept and switched off; the other
> 216 proteins stay in the Base style.' So it's still coloured by my genes, just not the
> modules. That's 'missing a bit', and it says the rest is still right. I can live with that."

## Single Ease Question

**3 of 7.**

> "Getting the file in was easy. I dragged it, it knew it was her file, it said which three of
> mine would go before I agreed, and the numbers added up this time. That part's better. Then I
> pressed Apply and the picture went greyer, the orange went, and the key disappeared. The only
> sign it worked is a list on the right with 'new' on it. I couldn't tell you in lab meeting
> whether that's the team's look. The gene one, I'd have given a five."

## Would he use this instead of his current tool?

> "Instead of Cytoscape, for opening her things: probably yes. No install, no Java, no ticket,
> and every screen tells me nothing has left the building. For the gene overlay, yes, I'd rather
> do it myself than wait for her; it told me the count and the ones that didn't match.
>
> For 'make it look like the team's': not yet. She could send me a PNG and I'd know what it's
> supposed to look like. Here I did everything it asked and the picture doesn't show me her
> colours or a key. After that I'd email her. That's my second go used up."

## What the session showed (observer notes, in plain terms)

1. **After Apply, the picture does not show the team's colors, and it loses what it had.** The
   canvas stays the same grey cloud; the orange Mule ring dots go (the dialog said they would);
   and the on-canvas key disappears with nothing in its place. The only evidence that the
   team's four layers arrived is four "new" rows in the Style stack, which he reads only after
   the picture gives him nothing. To him this is "applied, nothing changed" made worse: less
   color than before. The gene recipe's applied state, by contrast, shows the colors and a key
   naming the attribute, range and count, and he rated that path far higher.
2. **The dialog's counts now reconcile, and he checked them.** "Take the place of these 3" plus
   "Your other 5 stay" equals the eight rows he counted in the stack, and after Apply the stack
   has four new rows plus the five kept. He trusted the dialog more for it. This had failed for
   him last round.
3. **"Use these styles" matched his goal and he chose it without hesitation.** It sits first,
   says in its first line what he wants, and lists by name which of his layers go. Nothing was
   preselected, so he had to choose, and he chose correctly.
4. **Even "use these styles" leaves five of the project's own layers in place, and that
   contradicts his idea of "the team's look".** He asked whether the team's picture includes
   Watchlist ring and Pass-through edges and could not answer it; he would ask the postdoc.
5. **"Bind", "bound by attribute name", "Bind attributes", "Leave unbound" and "Bind... in its
   row's menu" are still on screen, and "bind" still makes him ask whether her file will
   change.** The one sentence he understood on the question screen was "Ask the sender which one
   they meant". He read "kept and switched off" as "missing a bit", which is correct, but said
   he had to work it out.
6. **Nothing on the style-file path says the colleague's file on disk is untouched.** The
   success bar names the file and what was replaced, but not that. He assumes it, and would
   rather be told.
7. **The places that explain "a colors file is a recipe" are where he does not look.** The File
   menu, his fallback, has no entry that says colors, styles or recipe; the Recipes menu is
   there but he did not know the file was a recipe. The explanation sits in grey under "Applied
   recipes" at the bottom of the Data panel, and the plainest wording ("From a recipe or
   file... your data stays here") sits behind a + on the Style stack header. He would open
   neither on his own.
8. **What worked:** the drag onto the canvas was accepted as his first move; the dialog named
   the file as the email did; "Nothing has been sent from this project" at the top of every
   panel answered his standing worry; "One undo step" reassured him. The gene recipe's card
   (author, date, plain description, "No data inside", "Data stays on this computer"), its
   "84 of 96 genes matched" headline, the list of the twelve with "looks like a spreadsheet
   date", and its red-blue colors with a key on the canvas were all things he said he wanted.
