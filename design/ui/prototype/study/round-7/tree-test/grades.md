# Round 7 tree test: grades

Sixteen simulated participants each placed sixteen jobs in the text outline of the refined B
navigation (`../tree.md`). The tasks, prompts and answer key are in `../tree-test.md`; each
participant's path and reasoning is in the file named after them in this folder.

**Read every number here as weak evidence.** Sixteen simulated participants share blind spots and
are far below the 50 or more a real tree test wants. A task most of them failed is a strong signal;
a task they all passed is a weak one.

## How each answer was graded

- **Correct** -- the participant's final pick is one of the key's locations for that task.
- **Direct** -- they reached their final pick without backtracking: no level of the outline (a
  node that has children, such as Main menu, Toolbar > View, Data > Attributes, a right-click menu,
  the data page's column roles) was opened and then left for another branch.
- Considering a leaf (a node with no children) and passing over it is not a backtrack. That
  matters on four tasks: the Analyze headings (task 1), Undo (task 5), and the two header chips,
  Full graph (filter) and Local only (privacy) (tasks 6 and 12), are all leaves in the outline.
  Several participants call these "back" in their own notes; by the rule above they did not
  backtrack. Where that changes a reading it is said under the task.
- When a participant ended on two places used together (for example Label by for the name, then
  the Style tab's label line for the department), the pick is correct if both are key locations.
- **Success** = correct / 16. **Directness** = direct / 16, right or wrong. **Direct success** =
  correct and direct / 16. The round's bar is 70 percent direct success on every task.

## Notes on each task

**Task 1 -- go-betweens (Analyze).** Everyone ended in Toolbar > Analyze; the key accepts any
heading in it. Twelve chose Rank nodes and edges, two chose Search, or say what to find (explorer
Elena, fraud analyst), two chose Find paths and edge sets (alert reviewer, recipe recipient). The
key's "any heading" is generous: a person on Find paths will not get betweenness. The two
indirect answers left Analyze for another menu (a node's right-click menu; the Graph list) and came
back. Several participants call a heading they passed over "back"; those are leaves and do not count.

**Task 2 -- a colleague's note.** All sixteen went straight to Rail > Notes. No one used the note
count on a grouping's rows or Find rows and notes.

**Task 3 -- picture for slides.** All correct, but 13 of 16 opened the Main menu first looking for
Export, found none, and backed out. Only expert Emma, the knowledge engineer and the screen-reader
analyst went straight to the project-name menu. This is the clearest single pattern in the round:
people expect file actions in the three-line menu.

**Task 4 -- colleague's colors and settings file.** All sixteen ended on Project name > Apply
recipe or style file..., so the correct rate is 100% both ways the plan asked for: with all three
key doors, and with the project-name menu alone. No one settled on the other two doors. Ten
participants looked at Main menu > Open... (a key-correct door) and rejected it because it "opens
a whole project" and they feared losing their own; nine opened Settings thinking "settings"
meant the file's settings. Only the genomics user and the recipe recipient went direct. The
File-style doors do not carry this job; the participants read Open... as replacing their work.

**Task 5 -- before Tuesday.** All direct to Project name > Version history. Eight tried or glanced at
Undo first; Undo is a leaf, so it is not a backtrack.

**Task 6 -- leave out the small ones.** All ended at Rail > Data > Filters (+ adds a step). All sixteen
looked at or clicked the header's Full graph (filter) chip first but none trusted it, because the
outline does not say what it opens; it is a leaf, so this is not graded as a backtrack, but no one
chose the chip as their answer. At least four (analyst Alex, cybersecurity analyst, fraud analyst,
recipe recipient) were unsure a filter changes the numbers and not only the drawing; analyst Alex
read "Show filtered-out nodes faintly" as a sign that filters are visual only.

**Task 7 -- 40 appearances = closer tie.** Graded against the key's route, `data-page/entries-pair`,
which is One edge per: Pair with the pair count taking the Weight role. Thirteen picked One edge
per: Pair and are graded correct; three (alert reviewer, marketing analyst, recipe recipient) set a
column to the Weight role but left One edge per on Row, saw the Row | Pair choice and did not
understand it -- under Row there is no count column to weigh, so they are graded wrong. Only
expert Emma named both halves (Pair, and the count as Weight) and is the one direct success.
Nearly everyone first went to the Weight role, found no column holding "40", and had to back out.
The Pair choppers mostly "guessed it counts them". Read literally (final pick must be the Weight
role), success would be 4/16 (alert reviewer, expert Emma, marketing analyst, recipe recipient);
either way the job does not land.

**Task 8 -- a different arrangement.** All correct under the key, which accepts Re-run layout and
Reshuffle layout seed as well as Inspector > Style tab > Layout > Method. Only the cybersecurity
analyst went direct (to Re-run layout). Twelve ended at Layout > Method, every one after trying
Toolbar > View, the canvas right-click menu or both. Most who reached the right-click menu rejected
Re-run and Reshuffle as "the same method shaken up". If only Method answers the job, success is
12/16 (75%) and direct success 0/16: the four who stopped at the canvas menu (alert reviewer,
cybersecurity analyst, explorer Elena, fraud analyst) never found Method. The recipe recipient
nearly skipped the Style tab because "style means colors".

**Task 9 -- this angle on Monday.** All direct: eight to Rail > Views > Save view, eight to Toolbar
> View > Save view.

**Task 10 -- groups between months.** All ended at Graph switcher > Compare graphs...; most noticed
Compare with another row... on a row and preferred the switcher. The recipe recipient first opened
the Data place's graph switcher.

**Task 11 -- select by a typed rule.** Fourteen correct, one direct. Every correct answer is Main
menu > Select where...; expert Emma alone went straight there, and said she would not have in the
real app. Two participants (alert reviewer, marketing analyst) and the fraud analyst say they found
it only because they had read the main menu on an earlier task. Four (analyst Alex, bioinformatics
researcher, intelligence analyst, ML engineer) opened Data > Attributes > Create set where this
is... -- a key-correct location -- and left it because it covers one attribute and they needed two.
Explorer Elena and the recipe recipient ended in Analyze > Search, or say what to find (wrong); the
recipe recipient passed Select where... and read it as choosing a location. Six rejected the
Analyze search box as sounding like an AI chat.

**Task 12 -- what leaves the computer.** All correct and direct: the Local only (privacy) chip,
then Main menu > Settings... > Privacy (the chip is a leaf and both lead to the same place).
About half also opened or looked at Diagnostics. The marketing analyst noted the chip sounds like it
covers her data, not usage reports.

**Task 13 -- March's work on April's file.** Fifteen correct at Data > Sources > the file's menu >
Replace with file...; ten direct. The recipe recipient saw Replace with file..., was afraid to press
it, and ended at Sources (+ adds data), which adds April beside March (wrong). Four backed out of
Main menu > Open...; analyst Alex detoured into Export > Recipe. At least four (alert reviewer,
cybersecurity analyst, expert Emma, screen-reader analyst) expect to press Rerun on each row
themselves afterward, and the screen-reader analyst could not tell whether March is kept.

**Task 14 -- name with department under it.** All correct (both Label by and the Style tab's label
line are key locations), five direct. Eleven of sixteen used or opened Label by on an attribute and
worried that a second Label by would replace the name rather than add a line; all eleven turned
to Inspector > Style tab > Label (+ adds a label line). Explorer Elena and the fraud analyst
stayed with Label by twice, unsure it adds a line. Expert Emma could not tell what must be selected
for the Style tab's label line to apply.

**Task 15 -- hide but keep counted.** All correct and direct, to Hide on canvas on the selection bar
or a node's right-click menu. No one chose Data > Filters, the wrong answer that would drop the
three from every count. The screen-reader analyst was confused by "Show hidden elements" versus
"Show hidden rows".

**Task 16 -- scores as words.** All ended at Data > Attributes > the attribute's menu > Read as...;
twelve direct. Four (analyst Alex, fraud analyst, genomics user, supply-chain analyst) first opened
the data page's column roles, a key-correct location, and left because the roles list (Key, Name,
Weight, Attribute...) has no "number"; the column Type on the data page was not recognized as the
place.
