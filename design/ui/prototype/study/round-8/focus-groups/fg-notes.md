# Focus group notes -- the Notes panel and what a note carries

Six simulated participants looked at the clickable app skeleton (refined structure B): the
left rail (Graph, Data, Views, Notes, Assistant), the Notes list with seven sample notes on
the Les Miserables character graph, the note composer, and the right-hand inspector. The
group ran three rounds: first impressions, a response to each other, then "what would make
you quit, and the one thing you would change".

These are simulated voices, not observed behavior. Everything here is self-report about a
static-content skeleton: nobody pressed "Add current value", reloaded data, or saved a
note. Treat the themes as hypotheses for the task-based sessions, not as findings.

Participants:

## Themes

Severity uses Nielsen's 0-4 scale and is the researcher's rating, not the group's. "Voiced
unprompted" means the participant raised it before anyone else in the session had; it is the
count to trust. "Adopted" means the participant agreed after hearing it.

### 1. A note does not say what data or run it was written against (severity 4)

A date records when someone typed, not what they were looking at. If the data is reloaded or
a measure is re-run, the note's number may belong to a different graph and nothing on the
note says so. Sarah's line became the group's frame: "a date with no version looks like
provenance and isn't".

- Voiced unprompted: Sarah (round 1, "what data was it run on?"), Emma (round 1, "whether
  that is the run that is loaded now"), Marcus (round 1, "Saved from what?").
- Adopted: Tom, Morgan, Ruth (round 2).
- Agreement: all six by round 3 want a dataset stamp recorded on every note automatically
  at save. Sarah: "The stamp has to happen without me doing anything."
- Dissent is about form, not need:
  - Plain words (Marcus, Sarah, Tom, Ruth): a file name and a load date, or a row count.
    "written against the statements loaded Sep 28".
  - Machine identity (Emma; Morgan in part): node and edge counts, a hash, and the cited
    run's parameters (weighted, normalization, seed). Morgan wants counts and settings
    read out, not a hash.
  - Ruth wants run settings kept off her notes, or out of the way.
- Implication: one stamp whose surface is plain words (source name, load date, counts), with
  run parameters on demand. Recording it belongs to graphty-element, which owns notes as
  public API; the app should only display it.

### 2. Staleness is shown as "Earlier run" plus a clock icon; it must be said in words (severity 3)

- Voiced unprompted: Marcus ("Earlier than what?"), Sarah, Emma, Ruth ("means nothing to me").
- Adopted: Tom ("it's whispering about it"), Morgan ("Tell me what it's earlier than").
- Agreement: all six. The note should state the change in a sentence ("data reloaded Oct 1,
  after this note"; "this number has changed since") in normal-weight text.
- Additional asks, single voice each: Emma wants the old value next to the current value,
  not an "Add current value" button. Marcus wants it to survive black-and-white printing
  (this aligns with the owner's printed-report and grayscale-check decisions). Tom: gray
  small text is unreadable for him.
- Not tested: nobody activated "Add current value"; the interaction itself has no evidence.

### 3. A missing author must be stated, not left blank (severity 2)

- Voiced unprompted in round 1, independently: all six noticed there is no author on any
  note (Marcus, Ruth, Tom, Sarah, Emma, Morgan).
- The specific remedy -- print "No author" / "unsigned" / "No name given" -- came from Morgan
  in round 2 ("I can't tell 'nobody signed it' from 'the reader skipped it'") and was adopted
  by Marcus, Ruth, Tom and Sarah. Emma did not comment on the wording.
- Underlying reason differs: Morgan for a screen reader (absence is inaudible); Tom and Ruth
  because a blank looks like an error; Marcus and Sarah for chain of custody.
- Tension with an owner decision: the owner decided the author comes only from the project's
  author setting, will usually be empty, and is shown only when a project holds more than one
  author. An explicit "unsigned" on every note in a one-author project would show on all of
  them. Candidate studio reconciliation (to test, not decided): say "unsigned" in the
  accessible name of every note, and show it visibly only where the project mixes signed and
  unsigned notes.

### 4. The "Add your name in Settings" hint is not seen (severity 2)

- Voiced unprompted: Marcus ("I read right past it"), Sarah ("never read that line").
- Adopted: Ruth and Tom said they also missed it, but only after Marcus had said so.
- Morgan: the hint is either outside the composer's reading order (never heard) or inside it
  (heard on every note); neither works.
- Proposed remedy, ask for the name once at first save: Marcus, Ruth, Tom, Sarah; Morgan
  conditionally (only as a real dialog).
- Dissent: Emma. "I'll dismiss it, and then the app will believe I've answered." She would
  not set a name for a month and wants notes to stand without one.
- The owner has decided the name comes only from Settings and the design must work well

  invisible, and Emma's position (notes must stand without a name) matches the owner's.
  The studio should make an unsigned note complete on its own (themes 1 and 3) rather than
  push harder for a name.

### 5. Human claim vs computed number vs source (severity 2)

- Marcus: a note citing a record and a hunch look identical; he wants a grade.
- Ruth: her editor must tell whether she wrote a claim or the computer produced it, and

- Emma: the "Cites" chip already marks machine output; keep it. A source line "records what

- Sarah: a source line is good, but she would fill it in half the time.
- Agreement: low. Three distinct proposals (grade, source line, cites chip). The owner said

### 10. Single-voice items (record, do not act on alone)

- Emma praised "4 more readings not computed" and the log-log degree plot.

Every participant who mentioned it (all six) said they could find their way. No dissent.

## Group-think to discount

- The "date without version is fake provenance" framing came from Sarah in round 2 and was
  repeated nearly verbatim by Marcus, Emma and Morgan. The underlying need had three
  independent voices in round 1 (Sarah, Emma, Marcus); count it as three, not six.
- "No author" wording is one idea (Morgan's) adopted by four. Count the remedy as one voice
  with support; count the observation "there is no author" as six independent voices.
- "Ask for my name at first save" snowballed after Marcus proposed it; Ruth, Tom and Sarah
  echo his phrasing. Emma's dissent is the more informative signal, and the owner's decision
  rules it out regardless.
- "I missed the gray line too": only Marcus and Sarah said so before hearing it. Ruth and Tom
  said so after; self-report under social influence.
- Everyone converged on "not a clock icon". The icon was discussed only as described; no one
  was observed failing to notice it. The task sessions should check whether participants
  find the stale state unaided before this is rated as observed.
- The whole round is reaction to static sample notes. No participant saved a note, reloaded
  data, or activated a stale value. Mock-fidelity caveat applies to every severity above.

## What to carry into the task sessions

- Can a participant tell, from a note alone, what data it was written against? (Expect: no.)
- After a reload changes a cited value, does the participant notice unaided, and what do they
  think "Earlier run" means?
- Does a screen-reader participant distinguish a cited measure from the note's subject?
- Does anyone notice the author hint in the composer before saving?
- Does "Open in Notes" read as the same notes or as copies?
