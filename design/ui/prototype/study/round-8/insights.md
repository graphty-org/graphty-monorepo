# Round 8: what the study found

Round 8 tested the clickable skeleton of refined structure B (`../../app-b/`, specification
`../structure-comparison/structure-b-refined.md`), as round 7 did, but with the tasks tiered by
the owner's priority of 2026-10-02: tier 1 is a first-time user's core path from an empty app,
tier 2 is common repeat work, and a small regression set re-tests last round's fixes. Four
methods, all with **simulated participants** built from the persona files in `../personas/`:

- **Tree test** (`tree.md`, key in `tree-test.md`, answers and grades in `tree-test/`): 21
  personas, 15 jobs, the skeleton's navigation as a text outline.
- **First click** (`first-click/`): 21 personas, 14 prompts on still renders of the skeleton.
- **Think-aloud sessions** (`sessions/`): 264 sessions over 32 tasks, each participant clicking
  through the skeleton with the study tool. 169 sessions are tier 1 (r8-t01 to r8-t15), 6 are the
  empty-project moment (r8-t16), 70 are tier 2 (r8-t20 to r8-t26), 19 are the regression set
  (r8-t30, r8-t32, r8-t33, r8-t34). One grade sheet per task (`sessions/grades-<task>.md`).
- **Focus groups** (`focus-groups/`): the first session, notes, several tables, and what colors
  the graph.

**Every number here comes from simulated participants.** Twenty-one personas generated by one model
share blind spots, so a failed task is a strong signal and a passed one a weak signal until real
people confirm it.

How to read this page:

- **Grades** come from what ended on screen and what the participant concluded, not from their own
  rating. S = success, SD = success with difficulty, F = failure, G = gave up. The bar counts S + SD.
- **Ease** is the Single Ease Question, 1 (very hard) to 7 (very easy), read from each transcript.
- **Severity** is Nielsen's scale: 0 not a problem, 1 cosmetic, 2 minor, 3 major, 4 catastrophe
  (the task cannot be done, or the user leaves or reports a wrong answer without knowing).
  **Observed** means a participant did something; **said** means an opinion. A finding resting on
  opinion alone is held one level down. **Confirmed** means observed in two or more participants.
- **First-time personas** are the six who model a newcomer: explorer-elena, recipe-recipient,
  alert-reviewer, class-project-student, nonprofit-operations-analyst and data-journalist. Tier 1 is
  reported for them separately, as the owner's priority asks.
- **Skeleton defects** are problems in the clickable prototype or the study tool, not in the
  design: a control the skeleton does not wire, a fixed screen that ignores what was chosen,
  sample data from another domain's task. They are in Part 1 and are not counted against the
  design. Part 2 holds the design findings, tier 1 first.
- **Routes** are written `app-b/#/<section>/<state>` and open at http://dev.ato.ms:9825/app-b/#/...
  Participant-view renders of the routes cited here are in `../../shots/app-b/<section>--<state>.png`
  (re-rendered for this synthesis on the current skeleton).

| Bar | Result | Met? |

What the round can and cannot say:

- **Tier 1 is 127 of 169 sessions (75%) and passes on 12 of its 15 tasks.** Take out the first
  session, which chains every tier 1 step and failed for all 21, and tier 1 is 127 of 148 (86%).
- **Four tasks were decided by the skeleton, not the design.** In the first session a Betweenness
  run never finishes and hiding or moving a row never repaints the drawing (r8-t01); a size choice
  is never applied on the Les Miserables sample, so 0 of 12 saw a dot change (r8-t09); Add on the
  add-rows page lands on a bare fresh load that drops every layer and the filter (r8-t30); and
  after Load three screens show the original import setup instead of the loaded one (r8-t25 on
  transfers). Without those four, 200 of 221 sessions succeed (90%) and ease is 4.59.
- **The design problems that remain are about the step after the first click.** First clicks are
  87% correct and tree-test correctness is 99.7%: people find the right control. What fails is what
  the control does next: a menu item that does nothing visible, a count with no names behind it, a
  setting the drawing does not show, a dialog whose default answers a different question.
- **Ease is held down by the same few places.** Mean ease is 2.00 on sizing by a value, 2.17 on
  one character's ties, 2.42 on names, 3.10 on the first session, 2.20 on select-by-rule and 2.00
  on adding rows. Every other tier 1 task is 3.58 or higher, and 8 of the 15 are 4.75 or higher.

## Part 2: design findings

Ranked by tier, then severity. Each gives the evidence count (participants who hit it, out of the
participants who could) and the routes it concerns. Recommendations respect the owner's decisions
in `../../owner-feedback.md`; anything else is a studio decision, labeled with its reason.

### Tier 1

**1. The Label menu offers "Show labels" beside "Label line", and "Show labels" is the one people
pick. Severity 4, confirmed.** In the first session 12 of 21 opened the Label "+" and chose Show
labels; it adds an unticked checkbox, ticking it changes nothing, and 10 stopped there believing the
names were on. In the names task 8 of 12 chose it and 3 stopped there. Only 2 of 21 in the first
session and 6 of 12 in the names task ever bound a label line to the name column, and the 6 got
there by guessing that something must also say *what* text to show. Around it: the Label heading
is not a control (12 of 12 in the names task clicked the word and nothing happened); the "+" has no
tooltip of its own (its name "Add to Label" was learned from a neighboring "+"); and the first
explanation of missing names, "64 labels hidden to avoid overlap", appears only in the Export
dialog. The still screen is not the problem: 20 of 21 first clicks went to the Label "+".
- Routes: `app-b/#/inspector-selection-and-everything/everything`,
  `app-b/#/inspector-group-set-path-row/label-empty`, `app-b/#/style-pickers/bind`,
  `app-b/#/export-dialog/image`

  make the Label "+" add a label line and open its column picker at once, with no Show labels item
  while no label line exists; make the Label heading open the same thing; give the "+" its own
  tooltip, "Add a label line"; and state the result on the line itself ("77 names, 64 hidden to
  avoid overlap -- show all"), not only at export. One way to put names on, in the reader's words.
