Structure A against structure B

Two ways to arrange graphty's app, walked through every task in the framework's task list. Structure A is the gallery as drawn today: kept sets in the left Graph panel, the style stack in the right-hand inspector, runs in a Results rail place. Structure B is the owner's proposal: one left-hand list, Groups and styles, that is the style stack and holds sets, run groups and encodings, with runs under an Algorithms rail place. As of 2026-09-29. Back to the gallery.

The two specifications: structure-a.md and structure-b.md, with B's wireframes in screens/structure-b/. Every walk is simulated by the design studio. Nothing here has been through testing with people. This page makes a recommendation; the owner decides.

Recommendation: a hybrid, "two lists on the left"

Neither A nor B as they stand. Keep B's move of the style stack into the left panel, and keep A's separate list of kept sets. The Graph panel holds Graphs, then Sets and paths (the things the analyst kept, in a stable order), then Styles (the paint order, top wins), then Views. Both lists are always on screen, whatever is selected. This is close to what the canonical framework already specifies (information-architecture.md: Styles below Sets and paths in the Graph panel, sharing its lower region through a split handle); the gallery moved the stack to the right after the third study.

What the hybrid takes from each:

  • From B: the style stack on the left and never hidden by a selection; a set with paint has exactly one style row, named after the set, edited from the set's swatch; a measure paints nothing until Colour by or Size by; a run that makes groups shows them folded under its style row (about ten largest, then "N more"); the nothing-selected inspector is Overview plus Statistics, each reading linked to its run and naming its scope; full row inspectors; a searchable catalog place.
  • From A: a kept set is an object, not a paint position, so unpainted sets never sit in the paint order; an opened run stays in its own left-hand rail place (renamed Algorithms or kept as Results -- round 7's label test decides) and never in the selection-driven right panel; Re-run keeps earlier runs side by side ("Re-run (keeps Run 1)") and the style row paints whichever run is shown; one kind of selection -- clicking a set row selects its members, as A does.

Why. After the reviewers' corrections (below), B's structural wins all come from one cause: the style stack is on the left and always visible. B's structural losses almost all come from another: kept sets and style layers are the same rows, so a set that paints nothing still has a paint position, and deleting, restoring, exporting or rerunning must cut a row in half. The hybrid keeps the first and removes the second. Most of the other differences are portable -- fixes either structure can take -- and the hybrid takes them.

Biggest risks. (1) The stack was moved right after study 3 on the owner's second comment; putting it back on the left needs evidence, and the existing between-subjects prototypes (left, inspector) are a pilot below 50 participants each. (2) Two lists in one panel share about 700 px of height; at 1366 by 768 each shows few rows before scrolling. (3) A painted set now appears twice -- its set row and its style row -- which is the "two homes" cost A was criticised for, reduced to one panel. (4) The hybrid is undrawn; its advantages are argued, not seen. (5) It is not the owner's structure; if the owner's core aim is one list of groups that is also the paint order, the hybrid gives that up for kept sets.

The reviewers' corrections, applied

  • One standard of evidence. A was walked on drawings, B mostly on its text. Walks lost by B only because its spec is silent are now marked spec gap, not counted as structural losses (MS-01, MS-08, MS-10, TT-02, TT-11, TL-04, FU-08). Walks B won only from text are marked the same way.
  • A's paint default is settled for scoring. Whether a finished run paints on its own is an open decision in A, and A already draws the no-paint option (state 12 of the results page). B wins that depend on it (BK-04, EV-09, TL-01) are marked portable.
  • The displaced run inspector is B's placement, not B's list. Six walks (FU-13, TT-03, TT-06, EV-01, EV-10, MS-06) turn on B opening a run in the right panel. Keeping the run on the left, in its rail place, removes the cost without touching the list. Marked portable.
  • Rerun-replaces-the-row is one rule. TT-04, MS-07, EV-05 and TL-06 all follow from it. Changing the rule to "Re-run keeps the earlier run; the row shows the chosen one" removes all four. Marked portable.
  • Aggregate by cause, not by walk. See the table above.
  • The app-invented "Graph statistics" family behind TT-01's B win breaks the rule that catalog families come from graphty-element (TL-08 flags it). TT-01 stays a B win for its inspector, not for its catalog.
  • MS-03 and MS-04 reconciled. They walk nearly the same act. Starting from the style list, B's always-visible stack wins (MS-03). Starting from one node, A's full stack inside the node's Appearance wins (MS-04). The hybrid takes both: a visible stack on the left, and the node's Appearance shows the full order with its own rows highlighted.
  • Still to settle in B's spec before any retest: which results arrive as rows and with what Paint state (load-time components, overviews, recipes, comparisons, path queries); group identity across reruns; row selection against element selection; what Paint means and which key toggles it; an out-of-date state; and the row inspector's Filter to, Hide on canvas, Layout and encoding scale.

Where each structure is clearly better

B is clearly better

  • The style stack never disappears. In A it shows only with nothing or one element selected; seeing it means pressing Esc, which clears the selection being checked and turns the next Ctrl+Z into a selection restore (BK-01, MS-03).
  • A set and its paint in one place. A second colour revises the same style; hand edits land in a visible Overrides row; a recipe's sets and layers are reviewed in one list (MS-02, BK-02, TL-10).
  • Measures paint nothing until asked. No figure is silently repainted by a later run (portable to A).
  • Graph-wide readings beside the drawing, and "Nothing has run yet" in words on first load (FU-07, TT-01, TT-09; portable to A).

A is clearly better

  • Kept sets are objects in a stable list. Evidence, case sets and set algebra do not have to sit in paint order, drift down as new rows land, or change the figure when moved (TT-07, MS-05, MS-09, TL-11, TL-02).
  • A set is not a layer. Deleting, restoring, noting and exporting a set have one meaning each (TT-08, BK-03, EV-06).
  • One kind of selection, and no paint toggle on groups. A row click never silently replaces a built selection; nothing next to a group looks like hide (EV-02, EV-03, TT-10, EV-08).
  • A run stays open beside the selection, and earlier runs stay side by side (portable to B).
  • Several graphs. Paint order is not mixed between one graph's rows and every graph's rows (EV-13).

Open problems of each

Structure A

  • The style stack and the kept set it colours sit in opposite panels; the stack hides behind any multi-selection.
  • A run's group has three homes: the run, its table tab, and the legend under its layer.
  • Whether a finished run paints on its own is undecided; the gallery walks it both ways.
  • The gallery contradicts itself: the nothing-selected inspector is drawn three ways; the start screen two ways; Preferences two ways; the framework's own interface-specification.md 4.1 puts no style stack in the nothing-selected inspector.
  • Delete for a style layer is never drawn.

Structure B

  • Kept sets that paint nothing take paint positions; about nine rows fit at 1366 by 768; finding a set by name goes through Find.
  • A row is both an object and a style: delete, restore, recipe replace, and set-scoped recipe layers are each ambiguous.
  • Rerun replaces the run inside its row; group names and colours are keyed on community ids and land on other members.
  • Two kinds of selection (row and elements) are never named; the Paint checkbox reads as a hide eye and shares Space with canvas selection.
  • A run that makes groups "adds a row on top, painting" conflicts with load-time components, overview recipes, recipes, comparisons and path queries.
  • No out-of-date state; the run inspector sits in the selection-driven right panel.
  • Categorical encodings' categories are not rows, while a partition's groups are.

Shared by both (fix whichever wins)

  • Start screen: dropping a file cannot be discovered. Preferences: no Usage data item. The AI provider dialog never says what is sent.
  • graphty-element gaps: partition agreement (ARI or AMI), bridges and articulation points, catalog aliases for question words, merge, layout scope, per-element drawn state.
  • No drawn delete for a style row in either structure.

What round 7 should test head to head

Precondition: settle the rules listed under corrections for B, and A's paint default, before drawing the trees. Otherwise the test measures how finished each spec is.

Tree tests (both trees, every persona, same wording)

  • "You coloured the hubs last week. Where do you change that colour?"
  • "Where do you find the set of 12 accounts you kept on Monday?"
  • "Why is TP53 dark brown and not the module colour?"
  • "Where do you go back to the Louvain run from before you changed the resolution?"
  • "Where do you see which results are out of date since the data changed?"
  • "Where do you lay out only Community 8?"
  • "Where do you stop the Louvain colours covering the module colours?"
  • If the owner accepts the hybrid, add its tree as a third arm; the questions above do not change.

First-click prompts (on drawn frames of both)

  • Betweenness is open; check TP53's rank without losing the ranking (TT-03, TT-06).
  • Hide Community 3 from the canvas (EV-03, TT-10) -- counts clicks on Paint.
  • After a 14-node selection built by hand, recolour the Hubs set (EV-02).
  • You are back after a week: are the styles as you left them? (BK-01).
  • Rerun Louvain with a new seed and keep the old result (TT-04).
  • Apply a colleague's style recipe and say what it replaced (BK-03).

Flows walked end to end, where the structures differ most

  • Restyle and reorder: MS-02, MS-03 and MS-04 in one session, starting once from a node and once from the list.
  • Communities and seeds: TT-04 and TL-06, including a 20-step resolution sweep.
  • A case over days: TT-07 then TL-11, with 50 kept sets.
  • Return and replay: BK-01 then MS-10.
  • Not walked yet, and needed: a partition with 800 or more groups; applying two algorithms' suggested styles and checking both still show; deleting a run row; an overlapping partition; edge groups and edge encodings; keyboard reorder in the tree with a screen reader; a social-science or citation analyst on the style tasks.
