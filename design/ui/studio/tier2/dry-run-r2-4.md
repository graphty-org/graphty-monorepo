# Tier 2 fourth dry run before round 2

Before any round 2 session, every tier 2 task half (A and B of T4, T17 to T24 and T12R) was
piloted from its start on the round 2 study build 2dcea6dd5bba, which holds the fixes the third
dry run asked for (`dry-run-r2-3.md`). The walks are in `rounds/r2d3/pilot/<task><half>/`. The
pilots reported 118 items. Each is listed below once, with its class and the reason. Every item
classed as a fix was checked in source and on its screenshot before it was classed; the reason
names the file.

The question this dry run answers: would a participant spend session time on a flaw in the build
instead of on what the task asks? Rarely. Of the 118 items, 7 are defects a participant could
hit, in four small units: a wrong icon, two misalignments, the find box's rule text and its
unmarked Enter target, and a click on a drawn name that does nothing. Every success path landed,
and no walk logged a script error, a console error or a failed request. The rest are the design
questions the sessions exist to answer, behavior already decided on purpose, or repeats.

Classes:

- **fix: <unit>** -- an implementation or polish defect a participant could hit. Fixed before the
  first session in the package that owns it; the units are listed after the tables.
- **design** -- a question the sessions exist to answer (where something lives, what a returning
  user expects, what to call it). Left alone.
- **no change** -- the behavior is right or deliberate, or the claim did not hold; the reason says
  which.
- **owner** -- already waiting on the owner in `../owner-decisions.md`.
- **process** -- about how the pilot ran or what graders are told, not the app.

Five reasons recur, so they are named once here:

- **3D overlap**: two nodes, or a label and a node, drawn on top of each other. It is
  graphty-element's label and node placement issue, expected to fail bar 10, and too large to
  change between rounds.
- **Names by default**: whether a returning user expects names on the drawing. Left to the
  sessions every round.
- **Focus on a heading**: after an action, focus goes to the inspector's title, a heading that
  takes no mark. It was boxed, then underlined, and each mark read as a control or a link
  (dry runs three and four of round 1). WCAG 2.4.7 asks a visible mark on an operable control;
  the heading is not one, and the next Tab reaches a control that is marked (T21A/08 shows the
  ring on the "ran ..." link after one Tab).
- **Covers until closed**: an open menu, list or tooltip covers what is under it until a choice,
  Escape or the pointer leaving closes it.
- **Tooltip after a click**: a clicked control's tooltip stays closed until the pointer leaves and
  comes back, as in Figma and macOS.
