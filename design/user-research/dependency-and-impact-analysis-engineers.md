# Engineers Who Analyze Dependencies: IT Services, Networks, Software and Data Lineage

## 1. Summary

This population is the engineers whose daily question is "what depends on what". It has twelve groups:

- site reliability engineers (SREs) who read service maps during incidents;
- IT operations and change managers who query configuration management databases (CMDBs) before changes;
- migration architects who map server-to-server connections to plan "move groups" and waves;
- network engineers who read Layer 1, 2 and 3 topology maps;
- application security engineers and developers who inspect transitive package graphs and SBOMs;
- developers, software architects and build engineers who keep a codebase's dependency graph clean: rules against cycles and layer violations that fail CI, drawn with dependency-cruiser, madge, Nx, ArchUnit, import-linter or dependency structure matrices (DSMs), and what a change affects in the build graph (Nx, Bazel, Terraform);
- data engineers who read table- and column-level lineage (dbt, OpenLineage, DataHub, Dataplex);
- SEO and site-architecture analysts who crawl a website and analyze its internal link graph (Screaming Frog, Sitebulb, Gephi, NetworkX);
- systems engineers in model-based systems engineering (MBSE) who trace requirements to design in SysML models (Cameo, Capella) and need typed models laid out automatically;
- enterprise architects and application portfolio managers who keep hand-curated application, interface and business capability models (SAP LeanIX, Ardoq, Sparx Enterprise Architect) and ask who still uses an application before it is retired;
- engineering managers, open source program office (OSPO) and community managers, and mining-software-repositories researchers who build people-and-code graphs from version-control history: who knows which code, which files change together, and who collaborates with whom (CodeScene, code-maat, GrimoireLab, git2net, TNM);
- robotics developers who read the running robot's ROS computation graph (which nodes publish and subscribe to which topics) to find a missing or broken connection (rqt_graph, Foxglove's Topic Graph panel).

Enterprise architects do the same closure work on hand-curated models, then roll the result up a business capability hierarchy and hand over a list of owners. All work on directed, typed graphs. The dominant operation is the upstream or downstream closure of a node, to a chosen depth, through only the right edge types, with health or risk overlaid. Migration architects also partition the server graph, and developers who own a codebase's structure collapse cycles. Three groups differ in kind: SEO analysts rank pages by internal PageRank and compare link graphs before and after a change; MBSE engineers mostly need readable, automatically laid-out diagrams of nested, typed models with ports; and the version-control group starts from a git log rather than an edge table, so its graph is a time-stamped two-mode graph of developers and files, projected onto files (change coupling) or onto developers (collaboration), with author aliases merged and results rolled up to teams. Robotics developers read a live, directed two-mode graph of nodes and topics that the running system reports, and the question is whether a wire exists at all, not what fails downstream. Most do not think of themselves as graph users: the graph is a view inside another product, judged by how fast it answers their question.

## 2. Population

**Job titles.** Site Reliability Engineer, DevOps / Platform Engineer, NOC analyst; Configuration Manager, Change Manager (ITIL); Cloud Migration Architect; Network Engineer; Application Security Engineer; Senior Software Engineer, Software Architect, Build / Release Engineer; Enterprise Architect; Data Engineer, Analytics Engineer; Technical SEO Specialist, SEO Consultant; Systems Engineer, MBSE Engineer, Requirements Engineer; Engineering Manager, Staff Engineer, OSPO or Community Manager, software engineering researcher; Robotics Software Engineer, ROS Developer.

**Scale of the problem (evidence).**

- Meta (2022): 18,500 active services, 393,622 inter-service edges, maximum fan-in 14,084 (https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc23-huye.pdf).
- Netflix: a real-time dependency graph of "thousands of microservices" (https://infoq.com/news/2026/06/netflix-microservices-realtime/).
- Jack Henry (banking software): about 250 services, 212 behind one gateway (https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/jack-henry-bubble-up-service-map).
- Azure Migrate (now branded Azure Catalyst) runs agentless dependency analysis on up to 3,000 servers per appliance for VMware and 1,000 for Hyper-V and physical servers (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/migrate/how-to-create-group-machine-dependencies-agentless).
- The npm graph analyzed by Zimmermann et al.: almost 700K nodes and 4.5M edges; an average package implicitly trusts about 80 other packages (https://blog.acolyer.org/2019/09/30/small-world-with-high-risks/).
- Log4Shell: 35,863 Maven Central artifacts (about 8%) depended on affected log4j code; over 80% only indirectly, most about five levels deep, some nine (https://security.googleblog.com/2021/12/understanding-impact-of-apache-log4j.html?m=1).
- Large dbt projects ship manifests of "dozens of megabytes"; GitLab's dbt docs site downloaded a 58 MB JSON payload and the stock lineage graph crashed (https://www.dagster.io/blog/dbt-docs-on-react).
- Website crawls: Screaming Frog's free version crawls up to 500 URLs (https://beanstalkim.com/learn/visualizing-your-site-structure-in-advance-of-a-major-change); its built-in crawl diagrams show "up to 10k URLs at a time, as they are extremely memory intensive" (https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/guides/visualisations); a practitioner reports full inlink exports that "can easily reach 1GB to 20 GB" (https://www.briggsby.com/internal-link-analysis-with-python).
- INCOSE, the systems engineering professional society, reported 26,567 members and corporate associates in 2024 (https://www.incose.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024_incose_annualreport.pdf); not all practice MBSE. A graph layout vendor, Tom Sawyer Software, names Airbus, BAE, Lockheed Martin, NASA, JPL, Raytheon and Toyota as customers of the platform behind its SysML layout product (https://www.omg.org/sysml-directory/vendor/Tom_Sawyer_Software.html, vendor).

- Enterprise architecture repositories: when SAP announced it would acquire LeanIX, the two companies had "more than 500 joint customers" (https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/news/366551416/SAP-acquires-enterprise-architecture-vendor-LeanIX?amp=1). A third-party technographic estimate counts 2,484 companies using LeanIX, 1,464 of which name it in their own job posts; the most common role in those posts is software architect (https://sumble.com/tech/leanix.md). A 2019 LeanIX webinar reported that its Ultimate-edition workspaces averaged about 6,700 fact sheets (records such as applications, interfaces and capabilities) and 23,000 relations, with maxima of 70,000 and 215,000 (https://www.slideshare.net/leanIX_net/leanix-virtual-workspaces, vendor, May 2019 data).

- Version-control histories: one Linux kernel release (7.1) merged 15,849 non-merge changesets from 2,479 developers (https://lwn.net/Articles/1077425/). git2net's co-editing studies mined "more than 1.2 million commits and more than 25'000 developers" (https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09484). Knowledge concentration is common: of the 133 most popular GitHub applications, 46% had a truck factor of 1 and 28% of 2 (https://peerj.com/preprints/1233v2.html, preprint). No source found for how many engineering managers or OSPOs run these analyses, or for CodeScene's customer count.
- ROS (Robot Operating System) users: the 2025 ROS Metrics Report counts 1,315,867 unique users or IP addresses downloading ROS packages in October 2025, up from 843,959 a year earlier, and 984,135,185 package downloads in 2025; ROS 2 is 91.2% of downloads. The report calls these figures "ballpark estimates" and a lower bound (https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/2025-ros-metrics-report/52575). The official ROS 2 beginner tutorials teach rqt_graph as the way "to visualize the changing nodes and topics, as well as the connections between them" (https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Tutorials/Beginner-CLI-Tools/Understanding-ROS2-Topics/Understanding-ROS2-Topics.rst), so the population that has opened the graph at least once is large. No source found for how many use it regularly.

No source found for a head count of these roles worldwide, or for Cameo, Capella, Ardoq or Sparx Enterprise Architect seat counts.

**How often they touch graph tools.**
SREs at every incident; change managers per change request (ServiceNow opens a dependency map from any CI record, https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/servicenow-platform/dependency-views/c_NextGenBSMMaps.html); migration architects for weeks per program; AppSec continuously, in bursts on events such as Log4Shell; developers and build engineers on every CI run, mostly through automation (rule checks and affected analysis), and by hand when a rule fails or a result surprises them; data engineers per pull request and per incident; network engineers, SEO analysts and systems engineers per incident, audit or design review (no source found for their frequency). Monte Carlo's commissioned surveys report data incident resolution averaging of 9 hours (2022) and 15 hours (2023) (https://dzone.com/articles/data-quality-survey-resolving-data-issues-takes-16, https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2023/05/02/data-quality-is-getting-worse-monte-carlo-says/).

**Skill profile.** Most are strong coders and command-line users who write a selector or query before they drag nodes. SEO analysts work mostly in crawlers, spreadsheets and Gephi; systems engineers in modeling tools. They know "upstream", "transitive", "blast radius" and "cycle", rarely centrality vocabulary. Edge direction matters: the wrong way "sends responders to the victims' suppliers instead of the victims" (https://edgedelta.com/company/blog/blast-radius-is-a-graph-query).

**Team context.** Work is collaborative and time-boxed (war rooms, change boards, wave reviews, pull-request review, client audits, design reviews). Ownership metadata on nodes is the usual pivot once impact is known (https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/dependency-map-navigator/).

## 3. Personas

Persona ids are new; none of the existing personas in design/designloom/personas covers this population.

### 3.1 `sre-incident-responder` -- Priya, Senior Site Reliability Engineer

- **Background.** On call for a payments platform of about 300 services on Kubernetes.
- **Tools.** Datadog Service Map and Dependency Map Navigator (https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/dependency-map-navigator/), Grafana Tempo service graphs, Kiali, AWS X-Ray, Backstage, PagerDuty.
- **Data.** Trace-derived service graphs (nodes = services, edges = aggregated calls with rate, error rate, latency), Kubernetes topology, deploy events, ownership catalog.
- **Goals.** Decide quickly whether the alerting service is the cause or a victim; find the upstream dependency that broke; tell the incident commander which customer-facing services are affected.
- **Frustrations.** The unfiltered map is unreadable (Jack Henry's "extremely connected" gateway); maps lose nodes when spans are dropped (https://docs.datadoghq.com/tracing/services/services_map/); low-traffic edges vanish from rate queries (https://grafana.com/docs/tempo/latest/metrics-from-traces/service_graphs/); maps go stale (https://getunblocked.com/blog/map-blast-radius-incidents-ai-tools/).

### 3.2 `it-change-impact-analyst` -- Marcus, Configuration and Change Manager

- **Background.** ITIL-certified; owns CMDB data quality at a bank and chairs the change advisory board.
- **Tools.** ServiceNow CMDB with Dependency Views and Service Mapping, Event Management impact trees, Excel exports, a Neo4j prototype with Bloom.
- **Data.** Configuration items (applications, services, VMs, hosts, storage, network devices, racks) linked by typed relationship pairs such as "Depends on::Used by" and "Runs on::Runs"; business services, SLAs, customer counts.
- **Goals.** For every change request, list the business services and customers a maintenance window will affect, confirm redundancy, and schedule or reroute (https://linkurious.com/blog/network-management-and-impact-analysis-with-neo4j/).
- **Frustrations.** Stale relationships (a vendor audit of a 14,000-CI CMDB found 34% stale records, https://virima.com/blog/inherited-cmdb-data-quality-audit); maps default to three levels; impact is not calculated for some service classes (https://www.servicenow.com/docs/bundle/xanadu-it-operations-management/page/product/event-management/concept/c_EMImpactCalculation.html).

### 3.3 `cloud-migration-architect` -- Leila, Data Center Migration Architect

- **Background.** Systems integrator architect moving a retailer's 2,400 VMware servers to the cloud; PowerShell, SQL, Power BI, Excel.
- **Tools.** Azure Migrate / Azure Catalyst dependency analysis, AWS Migration Hub network diagram with Application Discovery Service (closed to new customers as of 2025-11-07, replaced by AWS Transform: https://docs.aws.amazon.com/migrationhub/latest/ug/network-diagram.html), Power BI with Microsoft's network connections template, the CMDB, a wave planning runbook and spreadsheet.
- **Data.** Time-sliced connection records between servers with process names and destination ports; resolved (both ends known) versus unresolved (IP-only) endpoints; server inventory (OS, power state, installed software); application owner interviews.
- **Goals.** Turn thousands of servers into move groups -- "a block of servers or applications that should be moved together" -- and order them into waves (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/prescriptive-guidance/latest/large-migration-portfolio-playbook/wave-planning.html); avoid cutting a live dependency between a migrated and an unmigrated server.
- **Frustrations.** Must wait at least 24 hours for enough data; the multi-server view hides unresolved connections; shared services (Active Directory, DNS, monitoring) connect to everything and must be set aside by hand; the map shows connections but does not propose groups.

### 3.4 `network-topology-engineer` -- Sam, Network Engineer

- **Background.** Runs a campus and two data centers; keeps NetBox as the source of truth; Python with Netmiko and Nornir.
- **Tools.** NetBox with netbox-topology-views, NetDoc for discovery, SolarWinds Network Topology Mapper or Auvik for automatic maps, NetBrain-style path tools, Visio and draw.io for deliverables.
- **Data.** SNMP inventory; CDP/LLDP neighbor tables (local device and port, remote device and port); ARP and MAC forwarding tables; VLAN assignments; IP prefixes and VRFs; routing tables; NetBox devices, cables, sites, racks and roles.
- **Goals.** Keep an accurate physical (Layer 1) and logical (Layer 2 VLAN, Layer 3 subnet and routing) picture of each site; during an outage, find the fault domain and the path traffic actually takes.
- **Frustrations.** The same devices need different edge sets per layer; neighbor protocols are less complete than SNMP (Auvik support, https://support.auvik.com/hc/en-us/articles/202956414); interfaces with several LLDP neighbors break cable inference (https://github.com/dainok/netdoc/wiki); diagrams drift unless regenerated.

### 3.5 `software-supply-chain-security-engineer` -- Dana, Application Security Engineer

- **Background.** Five-person AppSec team covering 600 repositories.
- **Tools.** `npm ls --all`, `npm explain`, `mvn dependency:tree`, `gradle dependencyInsight`, Dependabot, Snyk or Endor Labs, OWASP Dependency-Track, CycloneDX Sunshine, cdxgen/Syft, GUAC.
- **Data.** CycloneDX and SPDX SBOMs per build and image; advisories (GitHub Advisory Database, OSV, NVD) with CVSS, EPSS and CISA KEV flags; licenses.
- **Goals.** When a CVE drops, answer "which applications ship the vulnerable component, through which path, and which direct dependency must we bump"; prioritize by reachability and exploitability.
- **Frustrations.** Dependabot shows only direct dependencies and npm audit reports transitive ones "only as a textual, command-line table" (https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09074v1); 52.9% of real SBOMs carry no dependency edges (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22140); nested JARs hide log4j from `mvn dependency:tree` (https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/identify-java-nested-dependencies-with-amazon-inspector-sbom-generator); SBOM graph renderers become unreadable at scale (https://cyclonedx.github.io/Sunshine).

### 3.6 `analytics-engineer-lineage` -- Tomas, Analytics Engineer

- **Background.** About 1,500 dbt models on Snowflake in staging, intermediate and mart layers, plus Airflow and Spark.
- **Tools.** dbt Catalog lineage and column-level lineage, `dbt ls --select`, Slim CI, Recce or Datafold, DataHub, OpenLineage into Marquez.
- **Data.** dbt `manifest.json` (`parent_map`, `child_map`, exposures), `catalog.json`; OpenLineage RunEvents with column-lineage facets; BI dashboards as exposures.
- **Goals.** Before merging, know which downstream models, columns, dashboards and owners are affected; after a test failure, trace the bad column upstream.
- **Frustrations.** Large DAGs "might not render" (https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/explore/explore-projects); column lineage cannot parse Python models or hard-coded table names (https://docs.getdbt.com/docs/explore/column-level-lineage); DataHub defaults to one degree and caps CSV export at 10,000 rows (https://docs.datahub.com/docs/act-on-metadata/impact-analysis).

### 3.7 `seo-site-architecture-analyst` -- Noor, Technical SEO Consultant

- **Background.** Agency consultant auditing e-commerce and publisher sites; Excel, some Python.
- **Tools.** Screaming Frog SEO Spider, Sitebulb, Gephi, NetworkX or Pandas for large exports, Google Analytics and Search Console.
- **Data.** A directed page-to-page link graph from a crawl (section 6) with page attributes: status code, crawl depth, indexability, section, sessions, backlinks.
- **Goals.** Find important pages that receive little internal link value, orphan pages and pages buried too deep; before a redesign or migration, predict which high-traffic pages will lose internal PageRank.
- **Frustrations.** Built-in crawl diagrams stop at 10,000 URLs and "do not scale very well" (https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider-10/); exports must be trimmed by hand before Gephi accepts them; one practitioner's working ceiling in Gephi was about 100,000 nodes and a million edges (https://www.onely.com/blog/plotting-websites-structure-gephi/); exports outgrow Excel.
- **Why a separate persona.** The data and the deliverable differ from the impact personas: the question is how link value flows across the whole graph (PageRank, depth, modularity by section), answered by comparing two versions of the graph, not by a closure from one node.

### 3.8 `mbse-systems-engineer` -- Ken, Systems Engineer at an aerospace prime

- **Background.** Maintains a SysML model of an avionics subsystem.
- **Tools.** Cameo Systems Modeler (MagicDraw) with Teamwork Cloud, Dependency Matrix and Relation Map; Capella on some programs; an auto-layout add-on such as Tom Sawyer Model-Based Engineering.
- **Data.** Requirements linked by derive and containment, blocks by satisfy, tests by verify; blocks nested in blocks with ports and connectors; SysML v2 models through the Systems Modeling API (5.12).
- **Goals.** When a requirement changes, list every derived requirement, satisfying block and verifying test; show coverage gaps (requirements with no satisfy or verify) at a design review; keep structure diagrams readable as the model changes.
- **Frustrations.** "Diagram arrangement is time-consuming and costly", and hand-drawn diagrams "constantly showed the wrong contents" as the model changed (vendor blog, https://blog.tomsawyer.com/growing-pains-learning-from-sysml-v1); generic "Arrange All" layouts are "not adapted to all kinds of diagrams" (https://blog.obeosoft.com/a-picture-is-worth-a-thousand-words); matrices grow very large (https://www.webel.com.au/taxonomy/term/1242).
- **Why a separate persona.** The traceability closure resembles the change-impact persona, but the dominant need does not: orthogonal, layered layouts of nested boxes with ports and typed connectors, regenerated from the model.

### 3.9 `enterprise-architect-portfolio-manager` -- Ingrid, Enterprise Architect and Application Portfolio Manager

- **Background.** Enterprise architect at an insurer, responsible for the application portfolio and its rationalization program; strong in Excel and the repository's meta-model, not a coder.
- **Tools.** SAP LeanIX, whose vendor page promises to "understand the full impact of retiring an application before you make a final decision" and to "see how applications, interfaces, and IT components connect" (https://www.leanix.net/en/products/application-portfolio-management, vendor); Ardoq, whose Dependency Map, Block Diagram and Timeline are the views its rationalization guide uses before removing anything (https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/44015-getting-started-with-application-rationalization); Sparx Enterprise Architect on modeling-heavy programs, whose Traceability window lists every relationship an element takes part in, filtered by relationship type, for impact analysis (https://sparxsystems.com/enterprise_architect_user_guide/15.1/guidebooks/tools_ea_traceability_window.html); Excel for exports and workshops.
- **Data.** A hand-curated, meta-model-typed graph: applications (often in a parent-child hierarchy, such as an ERP with its modules), interfaces with one provider application and many consumer applications, IT components and servers, business capabilities in a hierarchy usually three levels deep (LeanIX ships a capability map to level 3, https://leanix.net/en/sap-leanix-business-capability-map, vendor), organizations or user groups, and people in owner and expert roles. Lifecycle dates, cost and a TIME rating (tolerate, invest, migrate, eliminate) sit on each application.
- **Goals.** Before an application is retired or replaced, list every consumer application and interface that still uses it, every business capability it is the only application to support, the infrastructure it runs on, and the owners who must sign off; sort retirement candidates into quick wins and risky ones.
- **Frustrations.** The repository is only as good as the surveys that fill it; Ardoq's Dependency Map cannot follow a chain that changes direction ("reference chains are strictly directional", https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/44007-dependency-map), so mixed-direction questions need a second view; in Sparx, ArchiMate relationships such as Realization and UsedBy are reported as plain UML dependencies in the Traceability window (Sparx user guide, above), so typed filtering is lost; provider and consumer on a LeanIX interface record ownership and invocation, not data direction (https://community.leanix.net/it-s-all-about-community-7/interface-fact-sheet-category-provider-and-consumer-825), which users confuse.
- **Why a separate persona, and whose variant.** The closure is the same typed upstream closure as the change-impact persona (3.2), and the deliverable -- an affected list with owners, attached to a decision -- is the same shape, so this persona is a variant of `it-change-impact-analyst`, not of `cloud-migration-architect` (no partitioning, no discovered connection data). What differs: the graph is curated by hand rather than discovered, the closure ends at a business capability hierarchy rather than at business services, and the result is read by capability (which level-1 and level-2 capabilities lose their only supporting application), so the view needs grouping and collapsing by the capability hierarchy, as Ardoq's Dependency Map does by default ("Group by Parent All", with a slider to collapse deep hierarchies).

### 3.10 `codebase-dependency-developer` -- Teodor, Senior Developer who owns a monorepo's structure

- **Background.** Senior developer and de facto architect of a TypeScript monorepo of a few hundred packages and a few thousand modules; Java and Python colleagues do the same work with other tools.
- **Tools.** dependency-cruiser, madge and Nx in JavaScript and TypeScript; ArchUnit in Java; import-linter in Python; NDepend or Lattix DSMs in .NET and Java shops; Bazel `rdeps` where a build team runs Bazel. These are among the most installed graph tools in software: npm counted 10,032,839 downloads of dependency-cruiser, 4,252,575 of madge and 15,892,176 of nx in the week of 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-24 (https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/dependency-cruiser, https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/madge, https://api.npmjs.org/downloads/point/last-week/nx). Downloads count installs, mostly by CI machines, not people. All three draw dependency graphs: `depcruise --output-type dot|ddot|archi|mermaid|d2` (https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser/blob/main/doc/cli.md), `madge --image` (https://github.com/pahen/madge) and `nx graph` (https://nx.dev/docs/features/explore-graph).
- **Data.** The module graph (dependency-cruiser or madge JSON or DOT, import-linter DOT), the project graph (`nx graph --file` JSON), the task graph of a CI run, a list of rule violations and a known-violations baseline.
- **Goals.** Keep layers pointing one way and the graph free of cycles, enforced in CI (workflow 5.10); adopt rules on a grown codebase without fixing every old violation first; know what a change affects so CI builds only that and reviewers see the blast radius (workflow 5.9); answer teammates' "why is this installed" questions (workflow 5.6).
- **Frustrations.** The full graph is unreadable: Nx says "Viewing the entire graph can be unmanageable even for smaller repositories" (https://nx.dev/docs/features/explore-graph), and every tool tells him to focus, collapse or exclude before drawing; Graphviz output is a static picture, so each question is a new command; rule failures arrive as a text list of edges, and seeing the cycle they form takes a second tool.
- **One persona or two.** Software architects (5.10) and build or monorepo engineers (5.9) are kept as one persona. In a JavaScript monorepo the same person owns both, with the same tools and data: dependency-cruiser has both the rules and `--affected`, and Nx has both module-boundary rules (https://nx.dev/docs/features/enforce-module-boundaries) and `nx affected --graph`. In larger companies a separate build team runs Bazel `rdeps`; its task shape is the same reverse closure, so it is a variant, not a persona. This persona owns workflows 5.9 and 5.10 and shares 5.6 with the AppSec persona (3.5), who triages vulnerable transitive dependencies and owns 5.3. It does not own the 3D software-city workflow for developers learning an unfamiliar system, whose data is runtime traces rather than static imports (persona `program-comprehension-developer`), nor the graphs of people and code built from git history (persona 3.11).

### 3.11 `version-control-collaboration-analyst` -- Hilde, Engineering Manager who reads who-works-on-what from git history

- **Background.** Engineering manager and former staff engineer over six teams in one large repository. Variants with the same data and computations: an OSPO or community manager tracking an open-source project's health, and a mining-software-repositories researcher. Kept as one persona because all three start from the same git log, merge the same author aliases and project the same developer-file graph; they differ in the report, not the graph.
- **Tools.** CodeScene's social analysis: knowledge distribution, knowledge islands (code written mostly by one developer), knowledge loss (code dominated by developers marked as Ex-Developers), and coordination needs, rolled up to teams (https://codescene.io/docs/guides/social/knowledge-distribution.html, vendor). Its change coupling treats two modules as coupled "if they are modified in the same commit", "by the same programmer within a specific period of time", or under "the same Ticket ID" (https://codescene.io/docs/guides/technical/change-coupling.html, vendor). code-maat, the open-source predecessor by the same author, reads `git log --numstat` and runs coupling, communication, main-developer and ownership analyses (https://github.com/adamtornhill/code-maat). Open-source communities use CHAOSS metrics such as the Contributor Absence Factor, "the smallest number of contributors responsible for 50% of total contributions", formerly the bus factor (https://chaoss.community/kb/metric-contributor-absence-factor/), with GrimoireLab's SortingHat merging identities (https://2019.icse-conferences.org/details/icse-2019-Demonstrations/13/SortingHat-Wizardry-on-Software-Project-Members). Researchers use git2net for line-level co-editing networks analyzed in pathpy (https://github.com/gotec/git2net) and TNM for assignment, co-commit and coordination-needs matrices (https://github.com/JetBrains-Research/tnm). Gource animates the history as a tree whose root is the repository, branches are directories and leaves are files (https://www.mankier.com/1/gource), which shows activity but computes no network measure.
- **Data.** A git log (commit, author, date, files, lines added and deleted, message with ticket IDs); a developer-to-team table and a list of people who left; a file-to-component mapping; optionally issue, pull request and mailing-list interactions, and the static import graph.
- **Goals.** Find code only one active person knows before they leave (workflow 5.14, first part); find files and teams that keep changing together, and coupling that no import explains (second part); map who collaborates with whom over time, core and periphery, and newcomers' first ties (third part).
- **Frustrations.** Authors appear under several names and emails, and CodeScene computes knowledge per committer name, so aliases must be merged first; results change with the time window, which CodeScene says to start at the last reorganization for team analyses; a few large commits couple everything until changeset size is capped; mining tools write JSON and tables, and drawing them is a separate step; commercial tools keep the graphs in their own views.
- **Not this persona.** `codebase-dependency-developer` (3.10) reads the static import and build graph; `program-comprehension-developer` reads runtime traces. Both graphs are about code alone. This persona's nodes include people, and its edges come from history.

### 3.12 `ros-graph-robotics-developer` -- Rui, Robotics Software Engineer who debugs a robot's node-and-topic wiring

- **Background.** Robotics software engineer on a mobile-robot team that runs ROS 2. The robot's software is dozens of nodes (drivers, perception, localization, planning, control) that talk over named topics, services and actions, started by launch files that remap names and push nodes into namespaces. Variants with the same data and task: a student following the ROS 2 tutorials, and an integrator bringing up a large stack such as Autoware, whose docs say to "launch Autoware and run `rqt_graph`" and type `/perception` in the namespace filter to see one component (https://docs.autoware.org/main/tutorials/others/determining-component-dependencies/).
- **Tools.** rqt_graph, opened with `ros2 run rqt_graph rqt_graph` or Plugins > Introspection > Node Graph in rqt. Its options, read from its UI definition (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/blob/rolling/resource/RosGraph.ui): three graph types (Nodes only, Nodes/Topics (active), Nodes/Topics (all)); node and topic filters as comma-separated names or regular expressions with `-` to exclude; namespace boxes nested to a chosen depth; Hide checkboxes for dead sinks, leaf topics, common debugging nodes, tf nodes, unreachable nodes and parameter topics; grouping of action, image and tf topics into one virtual topic node each; highlight of a node's incoming and outgoing connections on mouse over; a manual Refresh button; save and load as DOT, SVG or image. The ROS 2 tutorial says the highlighting "is very helpful when examining more complex systems with many nodes and topics", and tells beginners to uncheck the Hide boxes to find topics that seem missing (https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Tutorials/Beginner-CLI-Tools/Understanding-ROS2-Topics/Understanding-ROS2-Topics.rst). Foxglove's Topic Graph panel draws nodes, topics and services in a browser, with a toggle for services and a switch between topic groups; it needs "a live data source" (native ROS, Foxglove Bridge or Rosbridge, or a custom source that publishes a connection graph update) (https://docs.foxglove.dev/docs/visualization/panels/topic-graph, vendor). On the command line: `ros2 node list`, `ros2 topic list -t`, and `ros2 topic info --verbose`, which prints every publisher's and subscriber's QoS profile.
- **Data.** The live ROS graph: nodes, topics with their message types, and publisher and subscriber endpoints with QoS profiles, read from the running system; services and actions. Less often a DOT file saved from rqt_graph, or the workspace's source code (ros2grapher builds the graph by parsing Python and C++ files, "No running system needed", because "Every existing ROS2 visualization tool requires a live running system", https://github.com/Supull/ros2grapher). No source found for a viewer that builds the graph from launch files or from a recorded bag.
- **Goals.** Confirm that a node publishes and subscribes where the launch file meant it to, after remapping and namespacing; find why a subscriber gets nothing (no publisher, wrong name, wrong namespace, wrong type, or a QoS mismatch); see what a new node or a teleoperation command adds to the graph; hand a teammate or a report a picture of one subsystem.
- **Frustrations.** The graph does not refresh by itself: an issue asking for auto-refresh has been open since 2016 (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/issues/5), and the tutorial tells users to press refresh to see changes. A large graph was hard to pan when zoomed (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/issues/1, 2013, since closed), and the node details sidebar of the older rxgraph (publishers, subscribers, services, connections) is an open request (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/issues/2). The graph cannot be drawn from the command line for a script or CI (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/issues/68, open). A QoS mismatch is invisible in the drawing: when a requested and an offered profile are incompatible "a connection will still not be made" and "no messages will be passed" (https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.rst), yet rqt_graph's options include no QoS or type check, and separate tools exist only to mark QoS-incompatible, type-mismatched and dead (0 Hz) links (connection_inspector, https://index.ros.org/r/connection_inspector/).
- **Variant or own persona.** Kept as its own persona, not a variant of `observability-service-map-engineer`. Both read a live, directed runtime topology and both need positions that survive a refresh, but the service-map engineer embeds a map in a product for on-call engineers and reads request rates and error ratios, while this persona reads a tool's view of its own robot, the graph is two-mode (nodes and topics), and the question is whether an edge exists and is compatible, not how much traffic fails over it. It is also not `model-graph-and-interpretability-users`: a model graph is a static file of operators, a ROS graph is a running system that changes as nodes start and stop.

## 5. Step-by-step workflows

No source found for per-step durations in any of these workflows; none are given.

### 5.1 Incident triage on a service map (SRE)

Sources: Datadog Service Map and Dependency Map Navigator (section 3.1), AWS X-Ray trace map (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonCloudWatch/latest/monitoring/ServiceLens.html), Dynatrace Smartscape Classic (https://docs.dynatrace.com/docs/analyze-explore-automate/smartscape-classic) and Visual Resolution Path (https://docs.dynatrace.com/docs/analyze-explore-automate/smartscape/smartscape-in-context-views/visual-resolution-path), Jack Henry (section 2), PagerDuty (https://www.pagerduty.com/resources/incident-management-response/learn/dependency-mapping-faster-incident-resolution/).

1. **Page arrives**; the SRE opens the service from the alert.
2. **Scope the map.** Set environment and a time window covering onset; Datadog maps per scope, and nodes appear only with data in the window. In X-Ray, choose a group (saved filter); the trace map displays up to 10,000 nodes.
3. **Read health overlays.** Datadog colors node borders red or yellow from Service Health. X-Ray outlines red for 5xx, yellow for 4xx, purple for 429 throttling; node size can be health or traffic.
4. **Isolate the alerting node.** Datadog's inspection view puts callers left ("closer to your customers") and callees right ("more likely root causes").
5. **Walk toward candidate causes.** Step to callees that are themselves unhealthy; this is the core loop. In Smartscape Classic's example, 31 unhealthy services all ran processes on one Windows host, which narrowed the cause to that host (vertical stack: application, service, process, host, data center).
6. **Walk the other way for blast radius.** From the faulty node, follow only dependency edges to affected callers; including ownership or correlation edges pulls in nearly every service within two hops (Edge Delta).
7. **Filter to the affected population.** Jack Henry filters to services carrying one institution ID, isolates the failing path, highlights HTTP 500s, then drills into spans.
8. **Correlate with change and time.** Check last-deploy facets and historical topology (Netflix supports time-window queries "to correlate dependency changes with when an incident began").
9. **Pivot** to traces, logs and monitors from the node menu.
10. **Hand off.** Page the owner from the catalog; tell the commander which customer-facing services are affected.

Decisions: cause versus victim, which neighbor next, whom to page. The graph navigates between alert and evidence; it is never the deliverable.

### 5.2 "What breaks if X fails or changes" in a graph CMDB (IT operations)

Sources: Neo4j IT Service Graph (https://neo4j.com/developer/industry-use-cases/agnostic/it-service-graph/it-service-graph), GraphAware Hume (https://graphaware.com/blog/insightful-it-operations-with-hume/), Linkurious (above), ServiceNow relationships (https://www.servicenow.com/docs/r/yokohama/servicenow-platform/configuration-management-database-cmdb/c_CIRelationships.html) and impact calculation (section 3.2).

1. **Load the model.** Neo4j: `(Software)-[:DELIVERS]->(Service)-[:USED_BY]->(Customer)`, `(Software)-[:INSTALLED_ON]->(Server)`. ServiceNow: typed pairs such as "Depends on::Used by".
2. **Identify the target CI**, for example a server going offline for an upgrade.
3. **Bottom-up impact query.** Follow `DEPENDS_ON` in reverse: `MATCH p=(resource)<-[:DEPENDS_ON*]-(dependency) WHERE ID(resource)=$id RETURN p` (GraphAware).
4. **Single point of failure.** `count(DISTINCT dependent)` per component. Neo4j adds a redundancy check: "whether the most important customers have redundancy throughout the network."
5. **Weigh severity.** ServiceNow Event Management propagates severity up the tree (100% down, 60%, 40%, 20% impaired), lowers it one level for redundant network components, and excludes CIs in maintenance.
6. **View the map.** Dependency Views shows three levels each way by default, clusters collapsed, the root CI framed, indicators for open issues, and lets users draw new relationships.
7. **Decide and hand off.** Schedule or reroute; attach the impacted-service list to the change request.

### 5.3 Transitive vulnerability triage across a portfolio (AppSec)

Sources: Google on log4j (section 2), npm, Gradle and Maven documentation (https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v10/commands/npm-explain, https://docs.gradle.org/current/userguide/viewing_debugging_dependencies.html), Dependency-Track (https://dependencytrack.github.io/docs/next/guides/user/triaging-policy-violations/), Sunshine, V-Achilles (section 3.5), InfoWorld (https://www.infoworld.com/article/3644492/how-to-detect-the-log4j-vulnerability-in-your-applications.amp.html), Veracode (https://veracode.com/blog/managing-appsec/review-log4shell-detection-methods).

1. **Advisory arrives.** Note exact coordinates and version range (log4j-core, not every "log4j" artifact).
2. **Portfolio search.** Query the SBOM store (Dependency-Track, GUAC GraphQL) for every project containing the component; this "is much faster than scanning every Java application" (InfoWorld). Caveat: SBOMs do not show what is deployed (https://www.scworld.com/analysis/asset-management/sbom-is-an-ingredient-not-the-whole-recipe-in-preventing-the-next-log4j-breakdown), and many omit edges, so a missing path is not proof of absence.
3. **Per-application path.** `npm explain <pkg>` prints each installed copy with its chain to the root; `gradle dependencyInsight` and `mvn dependency:tree -Dverbose -Dincludes=<g>:<a>` show selection and conflicts.
4. **Choose the fix point.** For a transitive package the developer usually bumps a direct dependency (V-Achilles). In Maven, fixes propagate "starting from the deepest dependencies first", and many artifacts wait on another's fix (Google).
5. **Prioritize.** By CVSS, EPSS, KEV and reachability. Vendor figures: Semgrep found 31 of 1,614 Dependabot alerts reachable in one project (https://semgrep.dev/blog/2024/overrated-and-underperforming-transitive-reachability-analysis).
6. **Visualize when text fails.** Sunshine draws a sunburst colored by severity, one ring per level. V-Achilles colors direct dependencies orange, vulnerable nodes vermillion and others by hop count; 7 of 10 participants changed fix priority after seeing it versus 3 of 10 with npm audit.
7. **Hand off.** Tickets with the path and the direct dependency to bump; Dependency-Track policy violations can fail CI.

### 5.4 Pre-merge impact on a lineage graph (data engineer)

Sources: dbt graph operators (https://docs.getdbt.com/reference/node-selection/graph-operators), dbt Catalog and column lineage (section 3.6), DataHub (section 3.6), dbt-guard (https://pypi.org/project/dbt-guard/), Atlan (https://docs.atlan.com/product/capabilities/lineage/how-tos/download-and-export-lineage).

1. **Open lineage.** dbt Catalog: model page, then a focus option (upstream and downstream, only downstream, only upstream), or the column card for column lineage.
2. **Select the downstream closure.** `dbt ls --select "my_model+"` (all descendants), `"my_model+1"` (one degree), `"3+my_model+4"` (bounded both ways); `@my_model` adds ancestors of all descendants. Combine with selectors such as `tag:nightly`.
3. **Apply lenses** (resource type, materialization, layer, test status).
4. **Narrow to columns.** Column evolution marks passthrough, rename and transformed columns; only the downstream columns of a changed column are the real impact set.
5. **Check exposures and owners.** DataHub: Lineage tab, upstream/downstream toggle, degree (default 1), facet filters, CSV export with owners, capped at 10,000 rows.
6. **Run CI on the closure.** Slim CI builds `state:modified+`; dbt-guard prunes unaffected descendants with a breadth-first traversal of `child_map`; Recce or Datafold diff values.
7. **Decide.** Breaking, partial or non-breaking; split the PR; notify owners with an impact list.

### 5.5 Root cause after a data break (data engineer)

Sources: dbt column lineage (section 3.6), OpenLineage (https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/object-model, https://openlineage.io/docs/spec/facets/dataset-facets/column_lineage_facet), Dataplex (https://docs.cloud.google.com/dataplex/docs/lineage-views), DataHub framing (https://datahub.com/?p=14889, vendor).

1. **Symptom.** A dbt test fails on a column, or a stakeholder reports a wrong number.
2. **Trace upstream.** dbt's documentation gives the rationale: "a failing data test on a particular column in your dbt model might've stemmed from an untested column upstream." Use status lenses to find the red region.
3. **Inspect transformations on edges.** In Dataplex, processes sit on edges; clicking one shows the SQL and run history; "Visualize Path" highlights the path back to the root. OpenLineage separates DIRECT from INDIRECT (join, filter) column inputs.
4. **Check runs.** In Marquez, a failing or late upstream run (FAIL state, `errorMessage` facet) is often the cause.
5. **Cross tools.** Analysts trace "upstream through three or four candidate sources, then switch between tools" (vendor framing).
6. **Fix and backfill.** Repair upstream, rebuild `my_model+`, notify downstream owners.

### 5.6 Why is this installed, and which version wins (developer)

Sources: npm explain and package.json overrides (https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v10/commands/npm-explain, https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v10/configuring-npm/package-json#overrides), pnpm overrides (https://pnpm.io/settings/dependency-resolution), Yarn 1 resolutions (https://classic.yarnpkg.com/lang/en/docs/selective-version-resolutions/), Gradle dependency debugging and constraints (https://docs.gradle.org/current/userguide/viewing_debugging_dependencies.html, https://docs.gradle.org/current/userguide/dependency_constraints.html), Maven dependency mechanism and tree goal (https://maven.apache.org/guides/introduction/introduction-to-dependency-mechanism.html, https://maven.apache.org/plugins/maven-dependency-plugin/tree-mojo.html), Maven Enforcer dependency convergence (https://maven.apache.org/enforcer/enforcer-rules/dependencyConvergence.html), dependency-tree-diff (https://github.com/JakeWharton/dependency-tree-diff).

1. **Explain presence.** `npm explain <pkg>` "will print the chain of dependencies causing a given package to be installed"; `gradle dependencyInsight` lists "Selection reasons" such as "By conflict resolution: between versions 1.7 and 1.6" and marks repeated subtrees `(*)`; `mvn dependency:tree -Dverbose -Dincludes=<g>:<a>` shows omitted nodes ("omitted for conflict with 2.0"). Maven picks the "nearest definition" in the tree, and the first declaration at equal depth.
2. **Decide.** Pin, exclude or upgrade the parent. For bloat, DepClean found 2.7% of direct, 15.4% of inherited and 57% of transitive dependencies bloated (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-020-09914-8).
3. **Apply the override.** Each ecosystem has its own mechanism:
    - npm: `overrides` in the root `package.json` only, everywhere (`"foo": "1.0.0"`), under one parent (`"bar": { "foo": "1.0.0" }`) or under one parent version (`"bar@2.0.0"`); a direct dependency can be overridden only with the same spec, or `"$foo"`.
    - pnpm: `overrides` at the project root (in `pnpm-workspace.yaml` in current docs), with version selectors (`"bar@^2.1.0": "3.0.0"`), parent selectors (`"qar@1>zoo": "2"`) and `"-"` to remove a dependency.
    - Yarn 1: `resolutions` in `package.json` keyed by path (`"d2/left-pad"`, `"c/**/left-pad"`); Yarn warns when a resolution is invalid or incompatible with the original range and says "nested packages may not work properly".
    - Gradle: a `constraints {}` block (by default "at least this version", with a `because` reason) or a `strictly` rich version that "no other dependency can upgrade past"; a constraint does nothing if the module is never in the graph, and `resolutionStrategy.force` can override `strictly`.
    - Maven: `dependencyManagement`, which "takes precedence over dependency mediation for transitive dependencies", or an `<exclusion>` on the parent that pulls the unwanted copy.
4. **Verify on every path.** Rerun the explain step and check that the chosen version now wins on each path, not just the one that prompted the change:
    - npm: `npm ls <pkg>` labels a copy `overridden` (added in npm 8.18, https://github.com/nodejs/node/commit/bce8041d67) or `invalid`; overrides have been reported as undone by `npm dedupe` and shown as invalid under workspaces or the linked install strategy (https://github.com/npm/cli/issues/5443, https://github.com/npm/cli/issues/9197).
    - Gradle: `dependencyInsight` now gives "By constraint", "By ancestor" (a `strictly` enforced it) or "Forced"; the `dependencies` tree marks constraints `(c)` and upgrades as `1.6 -> 1.7`.
    - Maven: rerun `dependency:tree -Dverbose`; the Enforcer `dependencyConvergence` rule fails the build and prints every "Path to dependency" while two versions remain.
    - Before and after: engineers save the tree as text before and after and diff it. dependency-tree-diff does this for Gradle `dependencies` output and keeps the unchanged ancestors of each changed line, so each change can be traced to the root dependency that introduced it. No source was found for a tool that draws the before and after path sets for one package side by side.
5. **Visualize when needed.** `npm ls --json | npm2dot`, `depcruise --output-type dot src | dot -T svg`, madge, npmgraph.

**Maven verbose mode.** maven-dependency-plugin 3.0 removed `-Dverbose`; MDEP-644 ("Reintroduce the verbose option for dependency:tree") is closed as fixed (https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/MDEP-644), and MDEP-761 reports `-Dverbose` running, very slowly, on plugin 3.2.0, fixed in 3.3.0 (https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/MDEP-761). The current tree goal (3.11.0) documents `verbose` as "Whether to include omitted nodes in the serialized dependency tree". The 3.1.2 to 3.3.0 tree goal pages still carried the old warning that the feature "actually uses Maven 2 algorithm and may give wrong results when used with Maven 3" (https://maven.apache.org/plugins-archives/maven-dependency-plugin-3.3.0/tree-mojo.html); the 3.6.1 page no longer does (https://maven.apache.org/plugins-archives/maven-dependency-plugin-3.6.1/tree-mojo.html). No source states which algorithm the restored option uses. An open report against 3.7.0 says verbose output does not say why an entry was left out, and the JSON output lacks the "omitted for duplicate" notes (https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/MDEP-962), and a request to document the option is still open (https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/MDEP-721).

### 5.7 Discover dependencies and form move groups (migration architect)

Sources: Azure Migrate / Azure Catalyst agentless dependency analysis (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/migrate/how-to-create-group-machine-dependencies-agentless), AWS Migration Hub network diagram (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/migrationhub/latest/ug/network-diagram-how-to.html) and grouping (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/application-discovery/latest/userguide/applications.html), AWS wave planning playbook (section 2), Cloudamize move groups (https://support.cloudamize.com/kb/ai-augmented-move-groups-waves).

1. **Deploy the collector.** An appliance with vCenter, Hyper-V or server credentials polls connections every 5 minutes and sends a combined data point every six hours.
2. **Wait** "at least 24 hours"; each server shows a status (credentials missing, validation failed, over the limit, or View dependencies).
3. **Open the multi-server map.** "Explore Applications" shows "logically grouped server nodes and their connections" since collection began, only resolvable connections, with an overview inset.
4. **Filter** by server name, destination port, OS, connection count and appliance. Selecting a node outlines its immediate dependents; the Connections tab lists incoming and outgoing connections with counts.
5. **Spot and tag a cluster.** Ctrl plus a drag box multi-selects interconnected servers; "Add or edit tags" groups them "as an application". In AWS, shift-click selects, hovering a port "highlights all the connections that open that port", and "Group as application" creates or extends an application.
6. **Drill to processes and ports.** Expanding a connection shows process-to-process connections with destination port (last 24 hours by default, up to 30 days; "Unknown process" otherwise).
7. **Export** a zipped CSV (7 to 30 days), one row per dependency per six-hour slot (columns in section 6); "All" adds unresolved, IP-only connections.
8. **Finish outside the tool.** Microsoft ships a Power BI template whose network connections chart is filtered by source and destination server and process slicers.
9. **Form move groups and waves.** Set aside shared dependencies: AWS says Active Directory "should not be considered when defining move groups". Apply move group rules (shared database, same owner, same patch window). Combine groups into waves (early waves under 10 servers). Cloudamize automates the split with "graph cleaning and clustering" and "deterministic community detection" that avoids overly large clusters; users then move servers between groups by hand.

Decisions: which connections are noise, where a cluster boundary falls, which cut dependencies are acceptable. The core analysis is graph partitioning, done today mostly by eye.

### 5.8 Network troubleshooting and site documentation (network engineer)

Sources: netbox-topology-views (https://github.com/netbox-community/netbox-topology-views), NextBox UI (https://github.com/iDebugAll/nextbox-ui-plugin), NetDoc (https://github.com/dainok/netdoc/wiki), SolarWinds NTM (https://www.solarwinds.com/network-topology-mapper/use-cases/network-mapping), Auvik support (section 3.4), NetBrain A/B path (https://www.netbraintech.com/docs/12ac1ue0to/help/HTML/a-b-path.html, https://www.netbraintech.com/blog/the-science-of-a-b-path-calculation).

Documenting a site:

1. **Discover.** Poll devices by SNMP and collect neighbor, ARP, forwarding, IP and VLAN tables (SolarWinds NTM, Auvik).
2. **Infer cabling.** NetDoc: "L2 neighbors (LLDP/CDP) are used to guess L1 cabling", then "populates NetBox (devices, cables, IP addresses and prefixes)". For Layer 3 it groups IP addresses into prefixes within a VRF.
3. **Render.** netbox-topology-views draws connections "based on the cables you created in NetBox", filtered by name, site, tag and device role; a physics layout runs until the engineer drags devices, whose positions are saved in named coordinate groups. NextBox UI orders device layers by role (layered or auto layout).
4. **Deliver.** Export to draw.io XML or PNG (netbox-topology-views); NetDoc exports L2 and L3 diagrams to draw.io.

Troubleshooting:

1. **Locate the fault domain.** On the Layer 2 view (VLANs, switch links), find the down link or device and the hosts behind it. Auvik describes switching between physical, Layer 2 and Layer 3 views (vendor marketing).
2. **Trace the path.** NetBrain's A/B path emulates forwarding using routing tables, ACLs and policies, in Layer 2, Layer 3 or Layer 3 Active mode; its documented flow baselines a healthy path and, during an incident, compares the live path with the cached one.
3. **Hand off** the path and the failing hop to the device owner or carrier.

### 5.9 What does this change affect in the build graph (developers, build and IaC engineers)

Sources: Bazel query guide (https://bazel.build/query/guide) and language reference (https://bazel.build/query/language), Nx affected (https://nx.dev/docs/features/ci-features/affected), Terraform graph (https://developer.hashicorp.com/terraform/cli/commands/graph), Spacelift (https://spacelift.io/blog/terraform-graph).

1. **Get changed files** from git (Nx: base and head refs).
2. **Compute reverse dependencies within a universe.** Bazel: `rdeps(u, x)` finds "the reverse dependencies of the targets in x within the transitive closure of u", and `rdeps(u, x, depth)` bounds it.
3. **Avoid the universe trap.** `--infer_universe_scope` uses the target patterns in the query, "but this might not be what you want": `allrdeps(//my:target)` can return only the target itself. Sky Query also "cannot order its output according to graph order", so `--output graph` is unavailable with it.
4. **Visualize when needed.** `bazel query "allpaths(//foo, third_party/...)" --notool_deps --output graph | dot -Tsvg`. Nx: `nx graph --affected`, or `nx affected -t build --graph`; `--explain=stdout` writes why each task is affected as JSON. At module level, `depcruise src --affected main` keeps "modules changed since the revision + all modules that can reach them", and with `--output-type mermaid` a GitHub Actions step writes that graph into the pull request summary with the changed modules highlighted (https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser/blob/main/doc/cli.md). For a big Bazel graph the guide's advice is to "start with a single path" (`somepath`).
5. **Infrastructure as code.** `terraform graph` emits DOT of "the dependency ordering of the resources"; `-draw-cycles` colors cycle edges. Large graphs "become dense and hard to read", so practitioners `grep "module.networking"` the DOT before rendering, or switch to Rover, Blast Radius or Inframap.

### 5.10 Find and remove dependency cycles and layer violations (developers and architects)

Sources: dependency-cruiser CLI (https://github.com/sverweij/dependency-cruiser/blob/main/doc/cli.md); madge (https://github.com/pahen/madge); Nx (https://nx.dev/docs/features/explore-graph, https://nx.dev/docs/features/enforce-module-boundaries); ArchUnit user guide (https://www.archunit.org/userguide/html/000_Index.html); import-linter (https://import-linter.readthedocs.io/en/stable/contract_types/, https://import-linter.readthedocs.io/en/stable/ui/); NDepend DSM (https://www.ndepend.com/docs/dependency-structure-matrix-dsm), dependency graph (https://www.ndepend.com/docs/visual-studio-dependency-graph), cycles (https://www.ndepend.com/features/dependency-cycles); Lattix DSM paper (https://groups.csail.mit.edu/sdg/pubs/2005/oopsla05-dsm.pdf).

In JavaScript and TypeScript, where the install counts in 3.10 point, the work starts from a rule file and a CI failure, not from a picture:

1. **Write the rules.** dependency-cruiser `forbidden` rules in its config, such as no circular imports, no import from a higher layer, or no orphans. In Nx, projects carry tags and `depConstraints` say which tags may depend on which; "If you violate the constraints, you will get an error when linting".
2. **Baseline what exists.** `depcruise src --baseline` writes today's violations to `.dependency-cruiser-known-violations.json` and prints how many are new, the same and stale; `--baseline-mode shrink-only` later "only remove[s] existing violations, and not add[s] any new ones".
3. **Enforce in CI.** With the `err` reporter dependency-cruiser prints nothing and exits 0 when there are no violations; otherwise it prints the violating dependencies and exits with "number of violations with severity `error` found". With `--ignore-known`, baselined violations are hidden and counted ("20 known violations ignored").
4. **Draw the overview.** `ddot` summarizes modules on folder level; `archi` collapses "to folders of your own choosing"; `--collapse` takes a folder depth or a pattern; `mermaid` and `d2` output render without Graphviz. `nx graph` draws projects, with group-by-folder in the sidebar. `madge --circular --image graph.svg` draws only the circular part, cyclic modules in red.
5. **Drill into one module.** `--focus` shows a module's "direct dependencies and ... direct dependents" (`--focus-depth` widens it, 0 means unlimited); `--reaches` shows everything that can reach a module; `--highlight` marks matching modules in the full graph; `madge --depends` lists dependents.
6. **Decide and fix.** Choose the edge to cut (usually the lightest in the cycle), refactor, and rerun `--baseline` so the fixed violation leaves the baseline.

Other ecosystems follow the same rule-baseline-draw sequence:

- **Java.** ArchUnit rules such as `slices().matching("..myapp.(*)..").should().beFreeOfCycles()` and `layeredArchitecture()` run as unit tests. Because grown projects have "hundreds or even thousands of violations", `FreezingArchRule` records them so "Consecutive runs will then only report new violations", and shrinks the store as they are fixed.
- **Python.** import-linter contracts (forbidden, layers, independence, acyclic siblings) run with `lint-imports`. `import-linter explore mypackage` opens a browser graph of "the dependencies between all the immediate children of the package", with click-to-drill-down, import totals on arrows, module counts on nodes, and "Cycle breakers": "a minimal set of dependencies that, if removed, would make the graph acyclic", drawn dashed.
- **.NET and Java with a DSM (NDepend, Lattix).** Rows and columns are assemblies, namespaces, classes and methods with coupling counts, and a parent row opens to its children. Triangularizing keeps "non-empty cells as close as possible to the diagonal", which reveals layers or exposes cycles. Black cells "represent mutual direct usage"; "If a dependency cycle involves N components, these N components represent a single super-component." Clicking a cell "shows the path graph", drawn as nested boxes with expand and collapse, callers green, callees blue, entangled elements red. NDepend cycle rules (ND1400, ND1401) fail CI.

Graph sizes: no source gives a typical size. dependency-cruiser's sample `err` output reports "454 modules, 1078 dependencies cruised"; Nx's benchmark monorepo has 253 packages and 5,566 TypeScript files (https://github.com/nrwl/large-ts-monorepo), and the Nx team writes of "large monorepos with thousands of projects" (https://dev.to/nx/the-butterfly-effect-how-we-gave-the-linter-a-100x-boost-4nl9).

### 5.11 Internal-link audit and migration simulation (SEO analyst)

Sources: Onely Gephi tutorial (https://www.onely.com/blog/plotting-websites-structure-gephi/), Dave Davies on simulating a change (https://searchengineland.com/visualizing-site-structure-advance-major-change-286510, mirrored at beanstalkim.com), Screaming Frog link position (https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/how-to-analyse-link-position/), Link Score (https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/link-score/), visualizations guide (section 2), Sitebulb (https://sitebulb.com/resources/guides/crawl-map-examples/, https://support.sitebulb.com/en/articles/12839707-auditing-internal-linking-with-sitebulb), Briggsby (section 2).

Audit:

1. **Crawl.** Crawl the site from the home page. Screaming Frog computes crawl depth, inlink counts and Link Score, a 0-100 internal PageRank-like value calculated in Crawl Analysis for internal, non-redirected, non-canonicalized URLs.
2. **Read in the crawler first.** Sort by Link Score and crawl depth; find orphans by comparing crawled URLs with sitemap, Analytics and Search Console URLs (Sitebulb flags pages found only in those sources). Glance at the force-directed crawl map: Sitebulb colors by depth and exposes flat architecture, pagination chains and "content explosion"; Screaming Frog sizes and colors by depth or path and grays out nodes truncated at 10,000 URLs.
3. **Export the edges.** Bulk Export, All Inlinks or All Outlinks (the vendor warns the file "can be huge"), or Response Codes, Success (2xx) Inlinks.
4. **Clean.** Keep only hyperlinks (link type AHREF, dropping CSS, JS and images); remove nofollow links because "Bots do not follow those links"; decide about navigation and footer links (filter link position to Content, or keep them and down-weight sitewide links -- Onely uses 0.4); keep Source and Target columns; strip the byte order mark. No source found for a standard rule on URL parameters; practitioners decide per site.
5. **Import into Gephi.** Import Spreadsheet as an Edges table, create missing nodes, merge parallel edges by Sum. Past about 50,000 URLs, filter to the giant component first.
6. **Compute.** Directed PageRank with edge weights; modularity, tuning resolution until classes match the site's sections.
7. **Style and lay out.** Color by modularity class or section, size by in-degree or PageRank; ForceAtlas 2 with Prevent Overlap, stopped by hand. Append node attributes (Analytics sessions, Googlebot hits from logs) as a Nodes table.
8. **Act.** List high-value pages with low internal PageRank, propose links from strong pages, and re-crawl to confirm.

Migration or redesign simulation:

1. Build a staging copy of only the new structure (the example adds one hub page of text links in place of a drop-down menu).
2. Crawl live and staging; replace the staging host with the live domain so node ids match.
3. Build both graphs identically, joining organic sessions per landing page.
4. Compare PageRank per page between graphs (the example product page rose from 0.016629 to 0.02171), look for outliers with far fewer inlinks, and flag high-traffic pages that lose value.
5. Deliver side-by-side graph images with a table of per-page PageRank and sessions; adjust the plan before launch.

Code-first variant: read the Source and Target CSV into NetworkX as a directed graph, tag page type from URL patterns, run `pagerank(alpha=0.85)`, and compare score histograms across scenarios (Briggsby).

### 5.12 Requirement traceability and automatic diagram layout (systems engineer)

Sources: MagicDraw relation map criteria, layout and depth (https://docs.nomagic.com/x/OwcmC), predefined maps (https://docs.nomagic.com/MT/latest/magic-cyber-systems-engineer---cameo-systems-modeler/predefined-relation-maps-303992535.html), traceability and matrices (https://docs.nomagic.com/x/OS8mC, https://docs.nomagic.com/x/NS8mC, https://docs.nomagic.com/x/NC8mC), Tom Sawyer Model-Based Engineering (https://tomsawyer.com/model-based-engineering), capellambse auto-layout (https://capellambse-context-diagrams.readthedocs.io/en/stable/diagram_view/), Capella ELK plugin notes (https://capella.rail-research.europa.eu/docs/user/tools/capella/custom-plugins/elk/), SysML v2 API (https://www.omg.org/news/releases/pr2025/07-21-25.htm, https://github.com/systems-modeling/sysml-v2-release).

Change impact on a requirement:

1. **Start at the changed requirement.** Its traceability properties list linked elements; Cameo calls tracing "fundamental to performing impact analysis when requirements change".
2. **Build a relation map.** Choose the context element, the criteria (relationship types), element type and scope; choose Tree or Radial layout; set Depth, the number of branch levels expanded automatically; older versions can also show implied relationships computed from inherited or inner elements (https://docs.nomagic.com/x/ySFUAw). Predefined requirement containment and derivation maps re-render as the model changes.
3. **Check coverage in a matrix.** Satisfy, Refine and Verify matrices put requirements on one axis and design elements or tests on the other; empty rows are coverage gaps. Missing relationships can be added and redundant ones removed in the matrix.
4. **Decide and record.** Update the derived requirements and design, then bring the matrix and maps to the design review.

Keeping structure diagrams readable:

1. **Generate rather than draw.** Tom Sawyer's add-on, a MagicDraw and Cameo plug-in or a Teamwork Cloud web app, lays out block definition, internal block and parametric diagrams; it claims to handle "thousands of elements and relationships" and lets users group elements "into a nested drawing structure to control the level of detail shown" (vendor).
2. **In Capella,** run the ELK layout with snap-to-grid off; it does not support sequence, activity or state machine diagrams. capellambse regenerates architecture diagrams with ELK, keeps nesting at "arbitrary depth", and can leave "large areas of white space" with large subgraphs.
3. **Hand-tune.** Users still lay out by hand: one wants to avoid "spending hours on aligning and adjusting lines, ports and the positions of elements", another calls layout "very time consuming" and not "the most value added of activities", and ELK fails on architecture diagrams that show allocated functions (https://forum.mbse-capella.org/t/effectively-beatifying-diagrams-with-automatic-functions-how/6516).

Data source trend: OMG adopted the Systems Modeling API and Services 1.0 ("SysML v2 API") in 2025, a standard interface "to navigate, query, and update the model" with an OpenAPI description; Tom Sawyer's SysML v2 Viewer reads "any SysML v2 API-compliant repository". It is an emerging standard source; no source found for how many production repositories expose it.

### 5.13 Who still uses this application before we retire it (enterprise architect)

Sources: Ardoq rationalization guide and Dependency Map (section 3.9), Ardoq introduction to rationalization (https://help.ardoq.com/en/articles/43927-an-introduction-to-application-rationalization), LeanIX Interface Circle Map (https://docs-eam.leanix.net/docs/circle-map-report) and its differential view (https://updates.leanix.net/announcements/differential-view-in-interface-circle-map), LeanIX GraphQL and table export (https://docs-eam.leanix.net/reference/graphql-basics), LeanIX community on modeling (https://community.leanix.net/application-portfolio-management-42/l1-applications-leave-empty-bcs-and-orgs-705), Sparx Traceability window (section 3.9). Most of this evidence is vendor documentation; no source describes the whole sequence in one place.

1. **Pick candidates.** Score applications on business value and technical fit and place them on a TIME chart. Ardoq's builder dashboard computes a structural complexity score from realized capabilities, integrations and connected infrastructure, flags "capabilities at risk" (the application is the only one realizing a capability), and lists quick wins: applications reaching end of life within a year with no capability at risk, sorted by rising complexity.
2. **Open the application in context.** Ardoq: the Dependency Map with the application as the context component and the incoming-degree slider raised. LeanIX: the Interface Circle Map, which needs provider and consumer applications documented on each interface. Sparx: the Traceability window, with only the wanted relationship types checked.
3. **Follow incoming typed references.** From the application, through the interfaces it provides, to every consumer application; then any application that depends on those, to the depth needed. Only usage-type references count; ownership and hierarchy links do not. A chain that changes direction needs a different view (Ardoq's Block Diagram).
4. **Roll up to capabilities.** Collect the business capabilities the application realizes, usually mapped at the lowest application level (a module, not the whole ERP), and roll them up to level 2 and level 1. For each, check whether another application also realizes it; a capability with no other realizer blocks retirement or needs a replacement first.
5. **Add infrastructure and organizations.** Ardoq's rationalization viewpoints show connected servers and services, and the owning organizational units and experts for each realized capability.
6. **List owners.** Every consumer application, interface and capability carries an owner and an expert; the output is who must agree, per affected item.
7. **Hand off and plan.** Export the table (LeanIX table view to Excel, or the GraphQL API for a full extract), a PNG of the map or a presentation slide (Ardoq), and a roadmap on the Timeline. Ardoq's 5Rs (retain, retire, replace, renovate, re-engineer) or TIME labels are written back to each application. For a tool-neutral extract, every ArchiMate-certified tool must read and write the ArchiMate Model Exchange File Format, required for certification since June 1, 2018 (https://www.opengroup.org/open-group-archimate-model-exchange-file-format); Sparx's ArchiMate guide covers generating and importing it (https://sparxsystems.com/resources/user-guides/17.0/model-domains/languages/archimate.pdf). LeanIX's differential view then shows which provider and consumer links a planned change adds or removes.

### 5.14 Who knows this code, what changes together, and who collaborates (engineering manager, OSPO, researcher)

Sources: CodeScene knowledge distribution (https://codescene.io/docs/guides/social/knowledge-distribution.html, vendor) and change coupling (https://codescene.io/docs/guides/technical/change-coupling.html, vendor); code-maat (https://github.com/adamtornhill/code-maat); gitmailmap (https://git-scm.com/docs/gitmailmap); gambit (https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05666v1); CHAOSS (https://chaoss.community/kb/metric-contributor-absence-factor/); git2net (https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09484, https://github.com/gotec/git2net); TNM (https://github.com/JetBrains-Research/tnm, https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09766v1); Joblin et al. on core and peripheral developers (https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00830).

Shared preparation:

1. **Export the history.** code-maat's recommended command is `git log --all --numstat --date=short --pretty=format:'--%h--%ad--%aN' --no-renames --after=YYYY-MM-DD`, with pathspecs such as `":(exclude)vendor/*"` to drop vendored code. Each line becomes an edge from an author to a file, carrying the commit, date and lines added and deleted: a time-stamped two-mode graph.
2. **Merge identities.** One person commits under several names and emails. Git's `.mailmap` maps them to one canonical identity for `git log`, `shortlog` and `blame`; GrimoireLab's SortingHat merges identities by matching name, email or username and records organization affiliations; gambit, a rule-based tool using only names and emails, reports an F1 score of 0.985 on hand-disambiguated GNOME GTK data. CodeScene computes knowledge per committer name, so unmerged aliases split one person's knowledge.
3. **Choose the window.** For team analyses CodeScene advises starting at the last organizational change, and a longer span for technical analyses; files whose original author falls before the window are "Inconclusive".
4. **Map people and files to groups.** Developers to teams, departed developers marked as Ex-Developers; files to components with rules such as code-maat's `regex => group` lines.

Then one of three analyses:

- **Knowledge risk (W3078).** Per file, the main developer and each author's share; knowledge islands (one developer); knowledge loss (departed developers); rolled up to components and teams and drawn over the folder hierarchy colored by author or team. The CHAOSS Contributor Absence Factor and the research truck factor summarize the same concentration as one number.
- **Change coupling and coordination (W3079).** Project the developer-file graph onto files: two files are coupled when they change in the same commit, by the same author within a time window, or under the same ticket ID. code-maat's thresholds are minimum revisions 5, minimum shared revisions 5, minimum coupling 30% and maximum changeset size 30 files, so sweeping commits do not couple everything; its temporal period treats a day's commits as one logical commit. CodeScene draws coupling over the folder hierarchy, colors it by trend (stronger, weaker, stable), and overlays architectural components and teams. Multiplying the developer-file assignment matrix by the file coupling matrix gives coordination needs between developers (TNM), which rolls up to teams and can be compared with actual communication (socio-technical congruence).
- **Collaboration network (W3080).** Project onto developers. git2net links one developer to another when they edit code the other wrote, at line level, giving directed, weighted, time-stamped co-editing networks rather than the coarser "same file" co-authorship; it hands them to pathpy for analysis. Joblin et al. classified core and peripheral developers from such networks in 10 open-source projects and checked the result with 166 developers; network-based and count-based classifications were "largely consistent", and core developers held stable, upper positions and coordinated with each other.

Evidence on what the networks predict is mixed: a study of 25 large open-source projects found socio-technical motif congruence unrelated to bugs or churn "in any temporal scenario" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.08198).

Graph sizes: see section 2; no source gives a typical company repository's developer and file counts.

### 5.15 Debug a robot's node-and-topic wiring on the live ROS graph (robotics developer)

Sources: the ROS 2 topics tutorial (https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Tutorials/Beginner-CLI-Tools/Understanding-ROS2-Topics/Understanding-ROS2-Topics.rst), the ROS 2 QoS concept page (https://github.com/ros2/ros2_documentation/blob/jazzy/source/Concepts/Intermediate/About-Quality-of-Service-Settings.rst), rqt_graph's UI definition (https://github.com/ros-visualization/rqt_graph/blob/rolling/resource/RosGraph.ui), Autoware's guide (https://docs.autoware.org/main/tutorials/others/determining-component-dependencies/) and connection_inspector (https://index.ros.org/r/connection_inspector/). Workflow W3096.

1. **Launch and open the graph.** Start the stack with its launch file, then open rqt_graph (or Foxglove's Topic Graph panel over a bridge). Pick Nodes/Topics (active) so topics appear as their own boxes between publishers and subscribers.
2. **Cut it down.** Filter by namespace (`/perception`) or by topic name, with `-` patterns to exclude; keep the Hide boxes for debugging nodes, parameter topics, tf and dead sinks checked; group action, image and tf topics into one node each; draw namespace boxes to a chosen depth.
3. **Trace one wire.** Hover a node to highlight its incoming and outgoing connections, and follow the topic from its publisher to each subscriber. A topic with a publisher and no subscriber, or the reverse, is the usual first clue.
4. **When something looks missing, unhide.** Uncheck the Hide boxes; a remapped or misspelled name shows up as two separate topics, one on each side.
5. **Check compatibility.** For a topic that is connected on the drawing but silent, run `ros2 topic info --verbose` and compare publisher and subscriber QoS (reliability, durability, deadline, liveliness); an incompatible pair makes no connection. Tools such as connection_inspector mark such links amber and dead (0 Hz) links red; rqt_graph does not.
6. **Change and refresh.** Fix the launch file or code, restart the node, and press Refresh; with tools that do not refresh on their own, compare the new picture with the old one by eye.
7. **Share.** Save the filtered view as DOT, SVG or an image for a pull request, a bug report or a design note.

Graph sizes: on the ROS Discourse, one team reports 80 nodes with 1,505 graph objects (198 service clients, 636 services, 236 publishers, 173 subscribers) and another 61 nodes, 129 topics and 627 services (https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/scalability-issues-with-large-number-of-nodes/36399). RTI, a DDS vendor, says every ROS 2 node carries a set of 14 parameter-related DDS topics even if it uses no parameters (https://community.rti.com/page/ros-2-why-are-there-so-many-topics, vendor), which is why parameter topics are hidden by default. No source gives a typical size for a production robot.

## 7. Analysis methods

- **Directed reachability with depth limits.** dbt `+`, `n+`, `@`; Cypher `[:DEPENDS_ON*]`; Bazel `rdeps(u, x, depth)`; DataHub degrees; ServiceNow levels; Ardoq degree sliders. This is the dominant computation, and it is always relative to a universe or loaded subgraph.
- **Edge-type-restricted traversal.** Only dependency edges count for blast radius (Edge Delta); Ardoq chains cannot change direction.
- **Path explanation.** npm explain, Gradle insight, Bazel `allpaths`, Dataplex "Visualize Path", NDepend path graph, NetBrain A/B path.
- **Counting dependents.** Distinct transitive dependents as criticality (Neo4j `count(DISTINCT dependent)`; npm reach). Connection counts as edge weights (Azure).
- **Partitioning into groups with few edges between them.** Migration move groups (5.7), done by eye with lasso selection or by tools such as Cloudamize's community detection; after removing shared hubs (Active Directory).
- **Cycle detection, strongly connected components and layering.** A cycle of N components is one "super-component" (NDepend); DSM triangularization; dependency-cruiser and Terraform `-draw-cycles`.
- **Severity propagation with redundancy.** ServiceNow percentage levels.
- **Root-cause ranking.** Practitioners rarely run centrality by hand, but personalized PageRank over an anomaly-weighted dependency graph is an established research method. MicroRCA reports 89% precision on a Kubernetes benchmark (https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02441640); eIRWR reports MRR 0.78 to 0.95 on 12K-25K-node Alibaba graphs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08073). No source found for which commercial products use it.
- **Decomposition by clustering.** IBM Mono2Micro clusters Java classes by use case and runtime calls to propose microservices (https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09698).
- **Link-value ranking and version comparison.** SEO analysts run weighted directed PageRank and modularity on the whole link graph and compare scores between live and staging crawls (5.11).
- **History-derived networks.** Project a developer-file history onto files (change coupling within a commit, author window or ticket, with thresholds) or onto developers (co-editing), compute ownership, knowledge islands, truck factor and Contributor Absence Factor, core and periphery, and coordination needs compared with communication (socio-technical congruence); always per time window and after merging identities (5.14).
- **Traceability coverage.** Systems engineers look for requirements with no satisfy or verify edge -- empty matrix rows (5.12).
- **Set operations and diffs.** Union of closures, `state:modified`, data diffs, topology at a past time (Netflix), live versus cached network path (NetBrain).
- **Aggregation into higher-level nodes.** Kiali groups versions into an app box; process edges roll up into server edges (Azure); DSM rows roll up to namespaces; ServiceNow collapses CMDB clusters; dbt and Dataplex open tables to columns.
- **Validation habits.** Count confirmations (an edge seen in 400 discovery runs outranks one seen once, Edge Delta); wait for enough collection time (24 hours); treat missing SBOM edges and unresolved connections as unknown, not absent; suspect parsing gaps in column lineage.

## 8. Outputs and collaboration

- **Incident artifacts:** affected-service list and severity; problem timeline; postmortem with the dependency chain. Map screenshots go into postmortems; no source found for export conventions.
- **Change records:** impacted CI lists attached to change requests.
- **Migration plans:** a wave planning runbook recording dependencies, move group rules in priority order and wave criteria, plus a dashboard spreadsheet (AWS playbook); tags on servers marking applications; CSV and Power BI reports for application owners.
- **Network documentation:** draw.io or PNG diagrams per site and layer; NetBox as the source of truth.
- **Security and build:** tickets with path and fix point; VEX statements; CI failures from Dependency-Track policies, dependency-cruiser and NDepend rules; Nx `--explain` JSON.
- **Data impact:** PR descriptions listing affected models and exposures; DataHub/Atlan CSV exports.
- **SEO:** an audit or pre-launch report with side-by-side live and staging graph images, per-page PageRank and sessions, and a list of links to add.
- **MBSE:** traceability matrices, relation maps and regenerated structure diagrams brought to design reviews.
- **Reproducibility:** everything is regenerated from source (manifest, SBOM, traces, discovery); a link to a filtered view, a saved selector or a saved query is the shareable unit. The exceptions are network diagrams with hand-saved coordinates and migration groupings stored as tags.

## 9. Tools landscape and pain points

Tools and frustrations per persona are in section 3. For version-control history the split is sharp: CodeScene computes and draws knowledge and coupling maps inside its own product, while the open tools that mine the networks (code-maat, git2net, TNM, GrimoireLab) write CSV, JSON or database tables that must be drawn elsewhere, and Gource animates activity without network measures (5.14). Users value automatic discovery, status overlays, exact scriptable closures, a pivot from node to evidence, and CSV export. They are failed by hairballs at scale and hard node caps (X-Ray 10,000 nodes, Screaming Frog diagrams 10,000 URLs, dbt DAGs that "might not render"), one-to-three-level default depths, missing or stale edges shown as if complete, grouping done by hand, and analysis finished in Excel, Power BI or Gephi because the product view cannot do it. The Lattix authors report that a DSM "appears to overcome scaling problems" that node-link views hit (section 5.10).

**Layout conventions.** Lineage and build graphs: layered left-to-right. Infrastructure stacks: top-to-bottom (Smartscape). Service maps: force-directed, with a three-column isolation view (callers, focus, callees). Network maps: physics layouts with saved positions, role-ordered layers, grouping by site and rack. Packages: indented trees, sunbursts, force graphs. Architecture: DSMs and nested boxes. SEO crawl maps: force-directed from the home page, colored by depth. MBSE: nested boxes with ports on their borders and connectors (Tom Sawyer offers orthogonal layout for them); relation maps as trees or radial.
