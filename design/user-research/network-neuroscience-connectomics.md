# Network Neuroscientists: Structural, Functional and Cellular Connectomics

## Summary

Network neuroscientists model the nervous system as a graph. In human and animal neuroimaging, the nodes are brain regions taken from a parcellation atlas (typically 68 to 1,000 regions), each with a fixed place in a standard brain space, and the edges are a weighted region-by-region matrix computed per subject from functional MRI time series, diffusion MRI tractography, or EEG/MEG signals. Their graph work is mostly matrix handling and statistics (thresholding, density sweeps, metrics, group comparison) and ends in two kinds of publication figure: a ball-and-stick drawing with nodes at their anatomical positions inside a translucent brain, and a map of nodal results painted onto the atlas regions themselves. Structural researchers also draw edges as real white-matter pathways rather than straight lines. Smaller groups work with intracranial electrodes placed in individual patients, with whole-brain simulation, and with tract-tracing matrices in which only some regions were measured. A distinct sub-population, cellular connectomics researchers, explores electron-microscopy wiring diagrams (139,255 neurons in the FlyWire fly brain; about 120,000 neurons and 523 million synapses in the MICrONS mouse cortex volume) as directed, synapse-weighted graphs, neuron by neuron, from versioned datasets whose identifiers change. All need things no current Graphty workflow assumes: data that arrives as a matrix or a folder of matrices, node positions that never move, nodes drawn as anatomical shapes, and many graphs (one per subject) analyzed as a batch.

## 2. Population

**Who they are.** Graduate students, postdocs, research scientists and principal investigators in cognitive neuroscience, psychiatry, neurology, radiology, biomedical engineering and physics, plus hospital-based clinical researchers. Connectomics postdoc postings nearly always require MATLAB or Python and neuroimaging experience (often diffusion MRI) and list graph theory as a plus, not a requirement (https://mail.nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/archives/list/freesurfer@nmr.mgh.harvard.edu/thread/J2GJUQEONV7WH64T3EEE3XUGWB2TUFMQ/; https://engineering.purdue.edu/ConnplexityLab/news/connplexity-lab-is-hiring/2022_PostDocPosition_CONNplexityLab_final.pdf). These people are imaging specialists first and graph users second.

**Size (evidence only).** No direct count exists. Proxies: the Organization for Human Brain Mapping annual meeting draws roughly 2,500-3,000 attendees (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Human_Brain_Mapping); Rubinov and Sporns (2010), which introduced the Brain Connectivity Toolbox measures, has roughly 8,000 citations in the snapshots found (https://centre.santafe.edu/thermocomp/Complex_network_measures_of_brain_connectivity:_Uses_and_interpretations); the FlyWire Consortium counts members from more than 146 labs at 122 institutions (https://engineering.princeton.edu/news/2024/10/02/mapping-entire-fly-brain-step-toward-understanding-diseases-human-brain).

**How often they touch graph tools.** Bursty: weeks of preprocessing (FreeSurfer reconstruction alone "takes a few hours" per subject: https://andysbrainbook.readthedocs.io/en/latest/MRtrix/MRtrix_Course/MRtrix_08_Connectome.html), then intense days of threshold sweeps, statistics and figure iteration, then revisions months later when reviewers ask for a different threshold or atlas. Cellular connectomics users query Codex, neuPrint or CAVE daily while designing experiments.

**Skill profile.**

- Graph literacy: high for metrics (small-worldness, efficiency, rich club, participation coefficient are routine vocabulary), low for graph-drawing concepts (force layouts, edge bundling) because node positions are anatomical.
- Coding: bimodal. A large group runs MATLAB GUIs (GRETNA, CONN, GraphVar, NBS, BrainNet Viewer) and edits text files in Excel (BrainNet Viewer files "can be generated/edited with text editors or Excel": https://nitrc.org/docman/view.php/504/1280/brainnet). A growing group scripts in Python (nilearn, bctpy, netneurotools, netplotbrain, MNE, neuprint-python, caveclient, navis) or R (brainGraph, ggseg).
- Statistics: strong (GLMs, permutation tests, FWE and FDR).

**Team context.** A lab of 3-15 people around one PI, with an imaging methods person and clinical collaborators. Cellular connectomics is consortium-based (FlyWire: about 33 person-years of proofreading: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11446842).

## 3. Personas

### Persona A: Dr. Lin Zhao -- clinical connectome researcher (id: clinical-connectome-researcher)

- **Role.** Postdoc in a psychiatry imaging lab studying depression or schizophrenia with resting-state fMRI and diffusion MRI.
- **Tools today.** fMRIPrep then XCP-D, or SPM/DPABI, for preprocessing; GRETNA for network construction, metrics and AUC; NBS Connectome for edge-level group statistics; BrainNet Viewer for figures; Excel for node files. GRETNA "works well with" BrainNet Viewer (https://helab.bnu.edu.cn/DOWNLOADS/Gretna/index.htm) and the NBS manual recommends BrainNet Viewer for figures.
- **Data.** 30-200 subjects; one 90x90 (AAL) to 400x400 (Schaefer) Fisher-z matrix per subject as text or BIDS TSV; a covariate table (age, sex, motion, diagnosis); a node file with MNI coordinates.
- **Goals.** Show that patients differ from controls in global efficiency, nodal hubness or a connected subnetwork; produce a 3D figure of the subnetwork and a cortical map of nodal effects; survive reviewer questions about thresholds.
- **Frustrations.** Results change with threshold choice (Garrison et al. 2015 found group differences can flip direction across absolute thresholds: https://esfinn.github.io/files/Garrison_NeuroImage2015.pdf); proportional thresholding can turn a difference in overall connectivity into a fake difference in topology (van den Heuvel et al. 2017: https://people.csail.mit.edu/ythomas/publications/2017Threshold-NeuroImage.pdf); file-order mistakes silently mis-assign subjects (the NBS manual warns that "Subject11.txt comes before Subject1.txt"); MATLAB license dependence; figure tools that require re-entering every setting.

### Persona B: Dr. Maya Okafor -- computational network neuroscientist (id: computational-network-neuroscientist)

- **Role.** Research scientist or junior PI who develops methods (null models, consensus networks, community detection, multilayer dynamics) and applies them to large open datasets.
- **Tools today.** nilearn (connectivity, `find_parcellation_cut_coords`, `plot_connectome`, `view_connectome`), bctpy, netneurotools, GenLouvain, NetworkX, netplotbrain, Connectome Workbench for CIFTI, MRtrix/QSIPrep for structural connectomes, Jupyter.
- **Data.** Hundreds to tens of thousands of subjects (UK Biobank group ICA used more than 4,000 participants: https://www.fmrib.ox.ac.uk/ukbiobank/); several parcellation resolutions (Schaefer 100 to 1,000; Glasser 360); structural matrices with a fiber-length matrix; CIFTI pconn files; sliding-window stacks.
- **Goals.** Group-representative structural networks that preserve long-range connections (Betzel et al. 2019: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6444521/); stable modules via repeated Louvain and a gamma sweep; hub roles; reproducible figure code.
- **Frustrations.** "Visual clutter" of dense 3D node-link drawings; linked anatomical and topological views exist only in separate web viewers outside her Python workflow: NeuroCave opens "two side-by-side rendering views" whose "node selection and the edge display are synchronized" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6145855/), and NeuroMArVL offers "different anatomical and topological projections" (https://immersive.erc.monash.edu/neuromarvl/), but both are point-and-click apps that load their own uploaded files (NeuroCave: per-subject CSV matrices and topology files listed in an index.txt, https://github.com/CreativeCodingLab/NeuroCave) and neither paper or page describes a scripting or Python interface, so results computed in a notebook have to be exported to them by hand; template mismatch between node coordinates and background brain (NetPlotBrain made explicit template naming a design goal: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10312269/); figure libraries that cannot render GIfTI/CIFTI surfaces.

### Persona C: Dr. Tomas Varga -- EEG/MEG connectivity researcher (id: eeg-meg-connectivity-researcher)

- **Role.** Cognitive or clinical neurophysiology researcher using 30-256 channel EEG or whole-head MEG.
- **Tools today.** MNE-Python and mne-connectivity, Brainstorm, FieldTrip, EEGLAB plugins, BCT.
- **Data.** A stack, not a matrix: subjects x conditions x frequency bands (or every frequency bin) x, for time-resolved measures, windows, each cell an N x N matrix over sensors (electrode or MEG sensor positions) or source labels (68 Desikan-Killiany regions in the MNE example). Measures are undirected (coherence, PLV, PLI, wPLI, ciPLV, amplitude envelope correlation) or directed (Granger, spectral Granger, phase transfer entropy) (Workflow 10).
- **Goals.** Compare band-specific networks between groups or conditions; avoid volume-conduction artifacts; avoid threshold bias with minimum spanning trees (Tewarie et al. 2015, cited via https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11076461/).
- **Frustrations.** Sensor-level connectivity has "limited and ambiguous" anatomical interpretation because of field spread and volume conduction, while unconstrained whole-brain source connectomes can reach about 754 GB per participant and condition (Brainstorm: https://neuroimage.usc.edu/brainstorm/Tutorials/Connectivity); leakage-sensitive measures look reliable at group level because of a few spurious strong edges, while leakage-robust phase measures (PLI, wPLI) showed poor test-retest reliability (Colclough et al. 2016: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5056955); each band, method or condition multiplies the figures.

### Persona D: Dr. Aiko Brandt -- cellular connectomics neuroscientist (id: cellular-connectomics-neuroscientist)

- **Role.** Circuit neuroscientist who uses an EM connectome to find the inputs and outputs of neurons studied in the lab. Three sub-groups work differently: fly researchers on FlyWire Codex and neuPrint; mouse and human cortex researchers on MICrONS or H01 through CAVE; and tracing labs (larval Drosophila and many smaller EM circuits) who reconstruct their own circuits in CATMAID.
- **Tools today.** FlyWire Codex (search, cell details, network graphs, pathways, Neuroglancer 3D), neuPrint Explorer and neuprint-python, CAVEclient and MICrONS Explorer, CATMAID and its Graph Widget, navis/natverse for skeletons, NetworkX, Cypher.
- **Data.** Directed, weighted multigraphs: neurons as nodes (root ID or body ID, cell type, hemisphere, predicted neurotransmitter, soma location), synapse counts as edge weights, one edge per neuropil region in fly data. In MICrONS, nodes can also carry calcium-imaging responses for co-registered cells.
- **Goals.** "What are the strongest upstream partners of my neuron, what transmitter do they use, and which pathways reach it from sensory input?" Plan genetic experiments from that list; relate wiring to function where both were measured.
- **Frustrations.** Identifiers change after proofreading (Codex: root IDs "can be replaced when proofreading alters a cell": https://codex.flywire.ai/faq); full-graph drawings are useless at this size, so views must be neuron-centric and filtered; connection tables repeat a pair once per neuropil; CATMAID users must leave the tool to lay out a graph (Workflow 9).

**A distinct sub-population.** Their graphs are directed, sparse, 100-1,000 times larger, explored one neighborhood at a time, single-specimen, versioned, and queried from databases (neuPrint is built on Neo4j: https://neuprint.janelia.org/public/neuprintuserguide.pdf).

### Persona E: Dr. Rosa Iglesias -- intracranial EEG epilepsy network researcher (id: ieeg-epilepsy-network-researcher)

- **Role.** Epileptologist or engineer in a surgical epilepsy center analyzing stereo-EEG (depth electrodes) or ECoG (grids and strips) recorded before surgery.
- **Data.** 10 minutes to hours of interictal or ictal recordings over 100+ contacts per patient. Nodes are contacts at patient-specific positions (localized by fusing CT with MRI), not atlas regions; every patient has a different node set. Edges are undirected (coherence, cross-correlation) or directed (Granger causality, DTF, PDC).
- **Workflow.** (1) Localize contacts and mark gray-matter contacts; (2) compute a per-patient adjacency matrix per band or segment; (3) rank nodes by out-strength, strength or betweenness; (4) compare top-ranked nodes with the clinician-marked seizure-onset zone and the resected or ablated contacts; (5) relate to outcome (Engel class). Stone et al. ranked each contact "based on the sum of outward connection weights" from time-domain Granger causality on 10 minutes of interictal SEEG in 20 patients, and found favorable outcome associated with more highly ranked nodes inside the resection (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10553068). Bernabei et al. ranked nodes by strength in 33 patients and proposed a correction for internodal distance specific to electrode type (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8361393). A review lists betweenness and out-degree as the measures tied to the epileptogenic zone and notes that binarization threshold choice strongly affects results (https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fneur.2013.00175/full).
- **What differs.** Patient-native coordinates, a resection-region overlay, directed edges, and high clinical stakes with small samples.

### Persona F: Dr. Felix Brandauer -- whole-brain modeler (id: whole-brain-modeler)

- **Role.** Computational neuroscientist building simulations on a structural connectome in The Virtual Brain (TVB).
- **Data.** Two matrices per graph: weights and tract lengths. Given a conduction speed, TVB translates "the distances ... into time delays."
- **Workflow (TVB connectivity editor: https://docs.thevirtualbrain.org/manuals/UserGuide/UserGuide-UI_Connectivity.html).** View nodes and edges in 3D and in left, top and right 2D projections; view the matrix one quadrant at a time (intra- and inter-hemispheric); select a subset of nodes and apply set, add, subtract, multiply or divide to edges between selected and unselected groups (virtual lesions); use the space-time view, which slices the weights matrix by delay range (for example 0 to 2.84 ms at 9 mm/ms); save the result as a new connectivity for the Simulator and save named node selections.
- **What differs.** Edits the graph rather than only viewing it; needs weight and length matrices kept in step.

### Persona G: Dr. Hana Sato -- mesoscale tract-tracing connectomics researcher (id: tract-tracing-connectomics-researcher)

- **Role.** Anatomist or modeler working with tracer-injection connectomes of mouse or macaque.
- **Data.** Directed, weighted region-by-region matrices where only injected regions are sources. The Allen Mouse Brain Connectivity Atlas (Oh et al. 2014) used viral tracer injections in 469 mice spanning 295 regions (https://dx.doi.org/10.1038/nature13186). Markov et al. (Cerebral Cortex, 2014 issue) injected retrograde tracer in 29 of 91 macaque cortical areas, giving a 29 x 91 matrix weighted by fraction of labeled neurons, with 1,615 pathways and many nonreciprocal links (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3862262). Rows for uninjected areas are unknown, not zero.
- **What differs.** A partially observed matrix: "not measured" must stay distinct from "measured and absent," and density or reciprocity statistics are computed only on the fully observed subgraph (the 29 x 29 block in Markov).

## 4. Use cases (ranked by frequency times stakes)

1. **Inspect a connectivity matrix** -- the first look at every subject and group result: a heatmap reordered by network or module, expecting two hemisphere blocks and bright homologous diagonals (Andy's Brain Book `imagesc` check); the main view in TVB's editor.
2. **Draw a connectome or a result subnetwork in anatomical 3D for a paper** -- every paper; the main figure. BrainNet Viewer, nilearn, netplotbrain, NBSview, mrview.
3. **Map nodal results onto the cortex** -- degree, participation, hub scores, nodal t-values painted on parcels in lateral and medial views (Workflow 8). Most papers with nodal results.
4. **Compute metrics across densities and summarize by AUC** -- every graph-theory study; reviewers attack threshold choices.
5. **Compare patient and control groups** at global, nodal and edge level (t-tests/ANCOVA with FDR; network-based statistic) -- most clinical studies.
6. **Build a group-representative network** (average for functional, consensus or "backbone" for structural) -- most structural studies.
7. **Find modules and hubs** (Louvain with consensus, participation coefficient, rich club) -- the interpretive core of many papers.
8. **Explore a neuron's partners and pathways in a cellular connectome** -- daily for EM users; drives experiment design.
9. **Rank intracranial contacts to localize the epileptogenic zone** -- few users, highest clinical stakes.
10. **Relate connectivity to behavior** (connectome-based predictive modeling) and show predictive edges as circle plots and glass brains (Shen et al. 2017: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5526681).
11. **Compare frequency-band networks in EEG/MEG** across bands, methods and conditions, as circle plots, sensor-layout plots and matrices, often with MSTs or percentile thresholds (Workflow 10).
12. **Track dynamic connectivity** with multilayer community detection and node flexibility (Workflow 11); structure-function coupling.

## 5. Step-by-step workflows

### Workflow 1: From a matrix and atlas coordinates to a 3D ball-and-stick figure (BrainNet Viewer)

Sources: BrainNet Viewer manual v1.42 (https://nitrc.org/docman/view.php/504/1280/brainnet); Xia et al. 2013 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3701683).

1. **Node file** (`.node`): ASCII, tab-delimited, no header, six columns: x, y, z (MNI mm), color value, size value, label ("-" for none). Column 4 often holds module numbers; column 5 "nodal degree, centrality, T-value, etc." Coordinates come from atlas centroids (nilearn `find_parcellation_cut_coords`, or shipped files for AAL90, Brodmann82, HOA112, Dos160, Fair34, LPBA40).
2. **Edge file** (`.edge`): N x N ASCII matrix, rows in node-file order. Zero means no edge; in undirected mode only the upper triangle is read.
3. **Start** in MATLAB with `BrainNet` (or the compiled runtime). File > Load File: a surface (ICBM152, 81,924 vertices, or Colin27), the node file and the edge file; valid combinations are fixed.
4. **Options**: layout (single, medium = lateral and medial per hemisphere, or full); surface opacity lowered so nodes show; nodes thresholded, labeled, sized and colored by colormap or "Modular"; edges thresholded by value or sparsity and colored by value, Euclidean length or "Nodal module" (node color when both ends share it, gray otherwise). The manual warns: "Always remember to change the threshold when a weighted matrix is loaded, or it will draw the full connection among the nodes."
5. **Export**: pixel or physical size and DPI; TIFF, BMP, EPS, JPEG, PNG; a 12-second rotating AVI (about 10 minutes to render). Save Option writes all settings to a `.mat` file.
6. **Batch**: `BrainNet_MapCfg('surf.nv','net.node','net.edge','opt.mat','out.jpg')` renders without the GUI for many subjects or windows.

**Pain.** Node size and color are baked into the file, so analysis changes mean regenerating files; "thousands" of nodes slow rendering (Xia 2013). Nilearn's equivalents are `plot_connectome(matrix, coords, edge_threshold="80%")` for static glass brains and `view_connectome` for interactive 3D saved with `save_as_html` (https://nilearn.github.io/dev/modules/generated/nilearn.plotting.view_connectome.html). netplotbrain takes nodes as a DataFrame, a named TemplateFlow template and view strings such as 'LSR' for multi-panel figures, or presets: 'preset-3' gives Left, Right and Superior panels and 'preset-4' the lateral and medial of each hemisphere (https://netplotbrain.org/preset/). nilearn's `plot_connectome` puts several glass-brain views in one figure through `display_mode` ('ortho', 'lzry', 'lyrz' and others; https://nilearn.github.io/stable/modules/generated/nilearn.plotting.plot_connectome.html). The panel is sized for a journal column: Elsevier asks for 300 DPI color images at 90, 140 or 190 mm wide (https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/author/artwork-and-media-instructions/artwork-sizing).

### Workflow 2: Threshold sweep and metrics across densities (GRETNA)

Sources: GRETNA manual v2.0 (https://www.nitrc.org/docman/view.php/668/2262/manual_v2.0.0.pdf); Wang et al. 2015 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4485071).

1. **Construct matrices** after preprocessing: choose a parcellation (AAL-90, HOA-112, Dos-160, Power-264, custom), correlate regional time series, write per-subject matrices before and after Fisher z, optionally sliding-window stacks.
2. **Network Analysis panel**: add matrix files, set group IDs.
3. **Sign**: positive only, negative only, or absolute. The manual calls negative correlations from global signal removal "currently controversial."
4. **Threshold method**: "Network Sparsity" (equal edge counts per subject) or "Value of Matrix Element" (absolute cutoff, which "could confound between-group comparisons"); enter a threshold sequence, for example sparsity 0.05 to 0.5 in 0.05 steps (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.13822).
5. **Binary or weighted; random networks** by degree-preserving rewiring for normalized clustering, path length and small-worldness.
6. **Metrics**: global (efficiency, rich club, assortativity), nodal (clustering, efficiency, degree, betweenness), modular (modularity, participation coefficient).
7. **Run and read**: one row per subject, one column per threshold (`Eg_All_Thres`), AUC scalars (`aEg`, `aSigma`); plot metric versus density per group ("Shade" plot), check sigma above 1, carry AUC to statistics.

**Variants.** CONN recommends a "fixed network cost level (e.g. keeping the strongest 10% of connections)" (https://web.conn-toolbox.org/fmri-methods/connectivity-measures/graphs-roi-level). GraphVar offers "Significant, Relative, or Absolute" thresholds and refuses to show results for a single threshold (https://nitrc.org/docman/view.php/844/1824/GraphVar%20Manual).

**Traps.** Absolute thresholds make metrics unstable (Garrison 2015); proportional thresholds inject weak edges in low-connectivity subjects (van den Heuvel 2017); MSTs avoid the choice; path metrics need weights converted to lengths (BCT `weight_conversion(W,'lengths')`: https://brainconn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/brainconn.utils.matrix.weight_conversion.html), and `efficiency_wei` needs weights in 0-1 (https://brainconn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/brainconn.distance.efficiency_wei.html).

### Workflow 3: Group comparison with the network-based statistic

Source: NBS Connectome v1.2 manual (https://NITRC.org/docman/view.php/629/1249/Reference_Manual_NBS_v1.2.pdf).

1. **Inputs**: one symmetric N x N text matrix per subject in a folder (alphanumeric order, so zero-pad names) or a `.mat` N x N x subjects array; design matrix; contrast (e.g., `[1 -1]`); test (one sample, t-test, F-test). Node coordinates are "required to display any significant networks."
2. **Primary threshold** on the t-statistic, "ultimately a matter of experimentation"; liberal thresholds find "subtle yet topologically extended" effects, conservative ones "strong, topologically focal" effects. In the bundled schizophrenia example (71 AAL regions, 12 patients, 15 controls), threshold 2.5 gives a component "too dense to enable inference" and 3.5 finds nothing.
3. **Advanced**: 5,000 permutations by default, FWER 0.05, NBS or FDR, component size by "Extent" or "Intensity."
4. **Read** in NBSview (only if a component is significant). 5. **Figure**: write the component as `.edge` and its nodes as `.node`, then Workflow 1.

**Hand-off.** Component p-value, an edge table with region labels, a 3D figure, often a circular "connectogram" grouped by lobe or network.

### Workflow 4: Group-representative network, modules and hubs

Sources: Betzel et al. 2019; community detection review (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.06723); netneurotools consensus example (https://netneurotools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/auto_examples/plot_consensus_clustering.html).

1. **Functional**: average Fisher-z matrices.
2. **Structural**: keep edges present in at least a fraction of subjects (GRETNA "Backbone (Structural)"). Betzel et al. show uniform consensus under-represents long connections; their distance-dependent method bins candidate edges by fiber length and keeps the highest-consensus edge per bin, within and between hemispheres (70 subjects, 68 to 1,000 regions). This requires an edge-length matrix alongside weights.
3. **Modules**: run Louvain many times, build an agreement matrix, threshold and recluster (`consensus_und`); sweep the resolution gamma (one study: 0 to 4 in 0.05 steps).
4. **Hubs**: participation coefficient and within-module degree z-score; rich club against random networks.
5. **Visualize**: nodes colored by module in 3D, a matrix reordered by module with block outlines, parcels colored by module (Workflow 8), and sometimes a 2D topological layout (NeuroMArVL switches "physical (anatomical) and topological layouts": https://immersive.erc.monash.edu/neuromarvl/; netplotbrain has a spring-layout view).

### Workflow 5: Neuron-centric exploration of a fly connectome (FlyWire Codex and neuPrint)

Sources: https://codex.flywire.ai/faq; neuprint-python tutorial (https://connectome-neuprint.github.io/neuprint-python/docs/notebooks/QueryTutorial.html).

1. **Find the neuron**: Codex search (`cell_type {like} Pm2`, `nt_type`) or neuPrint `NeuronCriteria(type=...)`.
2. **Partners**: upstream and downstream tables, counted only at or above the dataset minimum (5 synapses in FAFB and male CNS, 3 in BANC, 1 in MANC); users may raise but not lower it; one row per pair per neuropil. `fetch_adjacencies(sources, targets)` returns a neuron table and a connection table (pre, post, ROI, weight).
3. **Network graph** in Codex: labels, grouping by cell type, color by hemilineage, minimum synapse threshold, a cap on top connections, edge filters.
4. **Pathways**: shortest paths, downloadable as a path-length matrix CSV.
5. **Export and analyze**: CSV/JSON tables, Feather releases on Zenodo (https://zenodo.org/records/10676866), NetworkX or navis, or Cypher in neuPrint (`MATCH (a:Neuron)-[w:ConnectsTo]->(b:Neuron) WHERE a.bodyId = ... RETURN b.bodyId, w.weight`).

### Workflow 6: Querying a versioned mouse cortex connectome (MICrONS through CAVE)

Sources: MICrONS quickstart (https://tutorial.microns-explorer.org/quickstart_notebooks/00_cave_quickstart.html); synapse query tutorial (https://tutorial.microns-explorer.org/quickstart_notebooks/04-cave-query-synapses.html); CAVEclient materialization guide (https://caveclient.readthedocs.io/en/latest/guide/materialization.html).

1. **Connect and pin a version**: `CAVEclient('minnie65_public')`, then `client.version = ...`. Each materialization version is a timestamped snapshot with an expiry date. Public releases run from 117 to 1300 (https://www.microns-explorer.org/cortical-mm3), and the tutorials used 1926 in September 2026. The guide says code that uses specific IDs should use "a specific version that is tied to a timepoint where those IDs are valid."
2. **List tables**: `client.materialize.get_tables()` shows synapse tables (`synapses_pni_2`, `synapse_target_predictions_ssa`, `synapse_target_predictions_ssa_v2`), cell-type tables (`aibs_metamodel_celltypes_v661`, others) and functional co-registration tables (`coregistration_manual_v4`, `coregistration_auto_phase3_fwd_v2`).
3. **Query synapses** without downloading the whole table ("hundreds of millions of rows"): `client.materialize.synapse_query(pre_ids=..., post_ids=..., remove_autapses=True)`, keyed by `pre_pt_root_id` and `post_pt_root_id`.
4. **Aggregate to a graph**: `groupby(['pre_pt_root_id','post_pt_root_id']).count()` for a weighted edge list, or `pivot_table` by `cell_type_pre` and `cell_type_post` for a cell-type matrix.
5. **Join node attributes**: cell type, soma position, proofreading status, and functional properties for co-registered cells.
6. **Handle identifier churn**: `is_latest_roots()` checks validity at a timestamp; `suggest_latest_roots` (added in CAVEclient 5.11: https://caveconnectome.github.io/CAVEclient/changelog) maps an old root ID to its successor; `live_query` runs against a timestamp but repeated live queries "may return inconsistent IDs."

### Workflow 7: Building a structural connectome from diffusion MRI (MRtrix, QSIPrep)

Sources: MRtrix structural connectome guide (https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/doctest/quantitative_structural_connectivity/structural_connectome.html); `tck2connectome` reference (https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/commands/tck2connectome.html); `connectome2tck` reference (https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/commands/connectome2tck.html); Andy's Brain Book MRtrix course. The official 2015 HCP tutorial (100 million streamlines, `tcksift` to 10 million) now says "We do not recommend to use the steps below in connectomics processing for any project" (https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/dev/quantitative_structural_connectivity/ismrm_hcp_tutorial.html).

1. **Preprocess** diffusion and T1 (MRtrix `dwifslpreproc` or QSIPrep), estimate response functions and fiber orientation distributions.
2. **Tissue segmentation for anatomically constrained tractography (ACT)**: `5ttgen` produces a five-tissue-type image; the guide says assignment "is one aspect where ACT really shines."
3. **Parcellate**: run FreeSurfer, then `labelconvert` so node indices run from 1 upward and match matrix rows.
4. **Track**: `tckgen ... -act 5TT.mif -backtrack -crop_at_gmwmi -select <count>` (streamline count is a researcher's choice; Andy's Brain Book uses `-select 10000000` and then `tcksift2 ... -out_mu sift_mu.txt`: https://andysbrainbook.readthedocs.io/en/latest/MRtrix/MRtrix_Course/MRtrix_07_Streamlines.html).
5. **Weight streamlines**: SIFT2 (`tcksift2`) writes a per-streamline weight file; QSIRecon also reports a SIFT2 mu value to adjust weights across response functions (https://qsirecon.readthedocs.io/en/1.2.0/builtin_workflows.html).
6. **Build matrices**: `tck2connectome tracks.tck nodes.mif sc.csv -tck_weights_in sift2.txt -symmetric -zero_diagonal -scale_invnodevol -out_assignments assign.txt`. Endpoints are assigned by a radial search (4 mm default). A second call with `-scale_length -stat_edge mean` writes a mean edge-length matrix, used by distance-dependent consensus (Workflow 4) and whole-brain models (Persona F). QSIRecon writes all atlases to one `*_connectivity.mat` (format varies by version).
7. **Inspect**: heatmap of the matrix (expect hemisphere blocks).
8. **Figure with real pathways**: `connectome2tck tracks.tck assign.txt exemplars.tck -files single -exemplars nodes.mif` makes one mean streamline per edge; in `mrview` (`-connectome.init nodes.mif -connectome.load sc.csv`) the connectome tool sets Edge visualization > Geometry to "Streamlines / Streamtubes" instead of the default straight line, and Node visualization > Geometry to "Mesh" so nodes are drawn as the parcels themselves (https://mrtrix.readthedocs.io/en/0.3.16/tutorials/connectome_tool.html). `connectome2tck` also extracts "specific connections of interest" for inspection.

### Workflow 8: Nodal metric map on the cortex

Sources: NetPlotBrain paper (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10312269/); ggseg (https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/ggseg/readme/README.html, https://github.com/LCBC-UiO/ggseg); Xia et al. 2013.

1. **Compute a per-node value**: degree, participation coefficient, hub score, NBS node involvement, a nodal t-value, or module label.
2. **Join to atlas labels**: by row order or by region name. ggseg takes a data frame with a `region` or `label` column matching the atlas and "geom_brain() handles the join"; it ships `dk` (Desikan-Killiany), `aseg` (subcortical) and `tracula` (tracts), with more atlases such as Yeo 2011 in add-on packages.
3. **Render parcels colored by the value**: netplotbrain's node style 'parcels' "generates a surface per parcel" from a NIfTI image in which "a unique integer label identifies each node" (its other styles are 'circles' and 'spheres'); ggseg draws 2D lateral/medial atlas outlines and ggseg3d draws 3D meshes; BrainNet Viewer maps NIfTI volumes onto the surface with eight mapping algorithms and can combine surface, nodes, edges and a volume in one figure; Connectome Workbench and PySurfer-style tools color dlabel parcels on fs_LR or fsaverage surfaces.
4. **Lay out** a grid of lateral and medial views per hemisphere, one shared color bar (diverging for t-values, categorical for modules), often next to the ball-and-stick view of the same nodes with the same colors.
5. **Export** at publication size.

**Pain.** The metric is computed in one tool and painted in another; netplotbrain cannot yet take GIfTI or CIFTI surfaces.

### Workflow 9: Laying out a traced circuit outside CATMAID and bringing it back

Source: CATMAID Graph Widget docs (https://catmaid.readthedocs.io/en/stable/widgets/graph-widget.html).

1. Build the circuit graph in CATMAID's Graph Widget from reconstructed neurons.
2. Export it as GML ("the default export options (linearize IDs) are fine").
3. Lay it out in Gephi, because "layouting is easier done in other tools."
4. Export GraphML from Gephi and re-import it with the widget's "Open..." button, because "the coloring is likely easier to do in CATMAID."
5. Pain: when node filters are in use, subgraphs (axon/dendrite splits, synapse clusters) cannot be used and "grouping isn't available either." The round trip depends on node IDs and attributes surviving both formats.

### Workflow 10: EEG/MEG band-specific and spectral networks (MNE-Connectivity, Brainstorm)

Sources: MNE-Connectivity label example (https://mne.tools/mne-connectivity/stable/auto_examples/mne_inverse_label_connectivity.html) and sensor example (https://mne.tools/mne-connectivity/stable/auto_examples/sensor_connectivity.html); `plot_sensors_connectivity` reference (https://mne.tools/mne-connectivity/stable/generated/mne_connectivity.viz.plot_sensors_connectivity.html); Brainstorm connectivity tutorial (https://neuroimage.usc.edu/brainstorm/Tutorials/Connectivity) and graph tutorial (https://neuroimage.usc.edu/brainstorm/Tutorials/ConnectivityGraph); FieldTrip help for `ft_topoplotCC` and `ft_connectivityplot` (https://github.com/fieldtrip/fieldtrip/blob/release/ft_topoplotCC.m, https://github.com/fieldtrip/fieldtrip/blob/release/ft_connectivityplot.m).

1. **Decide sensor or source level.** Brainstorm warns sensor results have "limited and ambiguous" interpretation and recommends source-level analysis, which is "neuroanatomically interpretable" and comparable across participants; but 15,000 cortical vertices give about 1.6 GB per connectome, and unconstrained sources with coherence over 50 frequency bins about 754 GB per participant, condition and trial, so researchers reduce N to a parcellation or ROIs.
2. **Decide the pattern.** NxN (all pairs), 1xN (one seed region, or a non-brain signal such as a muscle channel, against all others), or AxB (two lists). A seed analysis produces a star graph or a value per node, not a full matrix.
3. **Epoch with rejection.** MNE label example: event 1, -0.2 to 0.5 s, baseline (None, 0), reject at mag 4e-12, grad 4000e-13, EOG 150e-6; 72 events found, some dropped. The sensor example uses -0.2 to 1.5 s for enough low-frequency cycles and kept 49 of 73 epochs.
4. **Source level only: invert and extract labels.** Read the inverse operator, apply dSPM to each epoch (lambda2 = 1, normal orientation), read the 68 `aparc` labels and keep each label's color for later node coloring, and extract one time course per label with `mode="mean_flip"` (sign flipping reduces cancellation).
5. **Compute several measures at once.** `spectral_connectivity_epochs(..., method=["pli","wpli2_debiased","ciplv"], mode="multitaper", fmin=8, fmax=13, faverage=True)` returns one 68 x 68 lower-triangle result per method (2,278 pairs); without `faverage` there is one value per frequency bin. The sensor example (PLI, 4-9 Hz, gradiometers) gives 20,503 pairs, the lower triangle of 203 sensors. Brainstorm's menu adds directed measures (Granger, spectral Granger, phase transfer entropy) and envelope correlation with optional orthogonalization; correlation, coherence, envelope correlation and PLV can be time-resolved in windows (an example uses 5 s windows with 50% overlap), while Granger and PTE cannot. Brainstorm stores [pairs x time x frequency], lower triangle only for symmetric measures.
6. **Choose a band summary.** Average within bands (one 256-channel study used theta 4-8, alpha1 8-10, alpha2 10-13 and beta 13-30 Hz over 214 channels: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4186758) or keep the spectrum. Brainstorm's spectrum view plots a measure against frequency and clicking it changes the bin shown in the graph and matrix; its graph view has a frequency slider that reloads the edges. FieldTrip's `ft_connectivityplot` draws a square grid of small plots, one spectrum per channel pair.
7. **Threshold or summarize.** Brainstorm's "Threshold by percentile" keeps the top n% within one file (the example keeps 5%); MNE draws only the strongest lines (`n_lines=300` on the label circle, `n_con=20` by default on the sensor helmet); MSTs avoid the choice (Persona C).
8. **Order and lay out nodes.** Source level: MNE sorts left-hemisphere labels by mean y-position (anterior-posterior), reverses them, mirrors the right-hemisphere labels onto the same order, and calls `circular_layout(..., start_pos=90, group_boundaries=[0, N/2])` so the two hemispheres face each other; Brainstorm groups circle nodes by hemisphere and lobe from the atlas (DKT in its example) and also offers "Display fibers," which draws source links as virtual white-matter tracts. Sensor level: `plot_sensors_connectivity` draws lines in 3D on the MEG helmet with a diverging `RdBu` colormap; FieldTrip's `ft_topoplotCC` draws lines between channel positions on a 2D layout, width and color each mapped to a named field, at one chosen frequency (`cfg.foi`), with optional arrowheads ('stop', 'start', 'both') for directed measures.
9. **Filter interactively.** Brainstorm's display panel filters by intensity (a value slider and a percentile slider kept in step), by minimum distance between the two nodes, by direction for directed graphs (In, Out, Bi, All, with arrowheads), and by anatomy ("Between Lobes" or between hemispheres); it hides self-connections and shows absolute values by default, with "Absolute values" unchecked to show signed values. MNE's sensor plot drops pairs closer than `min_distance` (default 0.05 m) because near neighbors are dominated by field spread.
10. **Draw method or band comparisons as small multiples.** The MNE example ends with three circle plots in one row, one per method (labels blanked, 300 lines each); per-band and per-condition figures are built the same way.
11. **Group statistics.** Brainstorm derives thresholds from non-parametric paired permutation tests (condition versus condition or versus baseline). Cluster-permutation statistics over connection pairs were introduced for coherence spectra by Maris, Schoffelen and Fries 2007 (J Neurosci Methods 163:161-175; FieldTrip list: https://mailman.science.ru.nl/pipermail/fieldtrip/2007-August/001321.html) and extended to high-dimensional source-space MEG, finding delta and high-theta clusters in 30 MCI patients versus 30 controls (Sanchez-Bornot et al. 2021: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/849554.full.pdf). Simpler designs run an uncorrected t-test per band and draw the MST on a scalp projection (Chen et al. 2018, 30 electrodes, 15 subjects: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6233332). An EEG/MEG tutorial running the network-based statistic separately per band: no source found.

**Typical sizes.** 30 to 256 EEG channels; about 200 MEG gradiometers (203 in MNE's example); 39 to 72 source regions (Colclough 2016: 39 regions, 61 subjects, six bands, 12 measures); 4-6 bands or tens of frequency bins; windows when time-resolved.

**What this sets.** One figure is a slice through a subjects x conditions x methods x frequency (x time) stack of matrices, so the product needs a slider or small multiples over that stack, a symmetric hemisphere circle layout with atlas colors, sensor-position layouts (2D scalp and 3D helmet), directed edges with arrowheads and in/out filtering, a choice between absolute and signed color, and a filter on inter-node distance.

### Workflow 11: Dynamic connectivity with multilayer communities

Sources: Bassett et al. 2011 (https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/1010.3775); Garcia et al. 2018 tutorial (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6294140); Telesford et al. 2016 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5133201); Puxeddu et al. 2021 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7956967/); review (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.02325).

1. **Window** the time series and compute one matrix per window (N x N x T).
2. **Link layers**: each node is tied to itself in adjacent windows with weight omega, the "temporal resolution parameter"; small omega gives "greater independence of partitions across neighboring layers."
3. **Maximize multilayer modularity** (Mucha et al.) with a Louvain-style heuristic, usually GenLouvain; run many times and search the gamma-omega plane for a stable partition. Puxeddu et al. tuned omega over 0.1 to 10 and found GenLouvain outperformed alternatives "in most conditions."
4. **Node measures**: flexibility, the number of community changes divided by T-1 (Bassett 2011 found flexibility in one session predicted later learning); module allegiance (fraction of windows two nodes share a community); core-periphery labels from flexibility.
5. **Check window length**: Telesford et al. found 75-100 s windows gave the widest range of flexibility, while long windows "are dominated by nodes with flexibility values equal to zero" and short windows "do a poor job of stratifying brain regions."
6. **Visualize**: a node-by-window community heatmap or alluvial diagram, an allegiance matrix reordered by system, and flexibility painted on parcels (Workflow 8).

**BIDS derivatives as an index.** In XCP-D output the folder tree and file names (`sub-`, `ses-`, `space-`, `atlas-`) are the subject-by-session-by-atlas table; a researcher selects "all subjects, atlas 4S456Parcels" by pattern, not by a list. Node identity comes from the dseg TSV, not from row position.

**Coordinate-space problems.** "MNI" is several templates (FSL: MNI152NLin6Asym; fMRIPrep: MNI152NLin2009cAsym); neurostars answers advise "avoiding using them interchangeably" (https://neurostars.org/t/what-is-the-difference-between-mni152nlin2009casym-and-mni152nlin6asym/30951; https://www.lead-dbs.org/about-the-mni-spaces/). Node coordinates must match the background surface's space or nodes float outside the brain. Intracranial EEG contacts live in each patient's own MRI space.

**Typical sizes.**

- Atlas graphs: 34 to 1,000 nodes; common sizes 68/84 (Desikan-Killiany), 90 (AAL), 200, 264 (Power), 268 (Shen), 360 (Glasser), 400 (Schaefer). Complete graphs have N(N-1)/2 edges (79,800 for 400 nodes, 499,500 for 1,000), thresholded to 5-50% density.
- Many graphs per study: one per subject (tens to tens of thousands), times 10-25 thresholds, times bands or windows.
- Fly: 139,255 neurons, about 54.5 million synapses and 2,700,513 connections at a 5-synapse threshold (FAFB v783); max degree 6,399; median in/out degree 11 and 13; about 25,000 neurons in the hemibrain (https://elifesciences.org/articles/57443).
- Mouse (MICrONS cubic millimeter): more than 200,000 cells, about 120,000 neurons, more than 523 million synapses, four kilometers of axons, 1.6 PB (https://alleninstitute.org/news/scientists-complete-largest-wiring-diagram-and-functional-map-of-the-brain-to-date); functional imaging of about 75,900 estimated neurons, with 19,181 manual matches covering 15,439 neurons co-registered between function and structure (https://www.microns-explorer.org/cortical-mm3).
- Tract tracing: 29 x 91 macaque matrix (Markov 2014); 295 mouse regions (Oh 2014).
- EEG/MEG: 30-256 sensors or 39-72 source regions, times 4-6 bands or tens of frequency bins, times methods, conditions and windows (Workflow 10).

**Graph properties.**

- Functional: undirected, weighted, signed, complete before thresholding.
- Structural: undirected, weighted by streamline count or SIFT2 weights, sparse, with a companion length matrix; edges have real 3D pathway geometry.
- EEG/MEG: undirected or directed, with a value per frequency bin and optionally per time window; sensor nodes sit at electrode or helmet positions.
- Effective, intracranial Granger, tract-tracing and cellular: directed. Tract-tracing matrices are partially observed. Cellular graphs are multigraphs with typed nodes and versioned IDs.
- Hierarchical attributes (region, lobe, hemisphere, network; neuron, cell type, class); a fixed 3D position and, for atlas data, a region shape; sliding-window stacks.

**Sensitivity.** Patient imaging data is protected health information; open datasets require data use agreements (UK Biobank, HCP restricted). Derived matrices are usually treated as de-identified but kept on institutional servers (policy citation: no source found). Intracranial EEG comes only from surgical patients. Fly and mouse connectomes are open; CAVE requires a token and Codex a Google sign-in for server computations.

## 7. Analysis methods

- **Edge estimation**: Pearson correlation with Fisher z, partial correlation and sparse inverse covariance (https://nilearn.github.io/dev/auto_examples/03_connectivity/plot_inverse_covariance_connectome.html), SIFT2-weighted streamline counts scaled by node volume, PLI/wPLI/AEC, Granger causality/DTF/PDC for intracranial EEG, fraction of labeled neurons for tract tracing, synapse counts for EM.
- **Thresholding**: absolute, proportional, significance-based, consensus, distance-dependent consensus, MST, orthogonal MSTs (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5405139), none.
- **Metrics**: strength, clustering, path length, global and local efficiency, small-worldness against degree-preserving nulls, modularity, participation coefficient, betweenness, rich club, assortativity (Rubinov and Sporns 2010).
- **Summaries across thresholds**: AUC; metric-versus-density curves.
- **Group statistics**: t-tests, ANCOVA, correlation with behavior, FDR/Bonferroni; NBS; CPM with cross-validation and 100-10,000 permutations.
- **Multilayer and temporal communities**: windows coupled by omega, multilayer modularity via GenLouvain, flexibility, allegiance, core-periphery from flexibility; results depend on window length, gamma and omega (Workflow 11).
- **Node ranking for clinical localization**: out-strength, strength, betweenness, compared with resection and outcome (Persona E).
- **Validation habits**: null networks; repeat across thresholds and atlases (https://nilearn.github.io/dev/auto_examples/03_connectivity/plot_atlas_comparison.html); motion checks (CPM excludes mean framewise displacement above 0.15 mm).

## 8. Outputs and collaboration

- **Deliverables**: matrix heatmaps ordered by network or module with block outlines; multi-view 3D ball-and-stick figures; parcel maps of nodal values on lateral and medial surfaces; streamline or streamtube renderings of significant edges; circular connectograms (CPM tool circle plot with positive edges red and negative cyan); metric-versus-density plots; bar/violin plots of AUC by group; community-by-time heatmaps; supplementary edge tables; rotating movies.
- **Reproducibility**: saved option files (BrainNet `.mat`), GRETNA configs, scripted figures with named templates, versioned connectome snapshots (FlyWire v783, MICrONS materialization versions) cited in papers.

**Cross-cutting pain points.**

- Analysis and figure tools are separate programs joined by hand-written text files; the same nodes are styled again in each.
- Synchronized views of two connectomes exist only in dedicated web viewers: NeuroCave was built because, with general tools, "the user needs to open multiple instances of the application," which "usually requires the use of multiple monitors," and its own default is two synchronized side-by-side views (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6145855/). - Dense node-link clutter; edge bundling or real pathway geometry are the fixes users reach for.
- Template mismatches between nodes and background; per-patient spaces for intracranial data; in EEG/MEG, every figure is one slice of a band x method x condition stack.
