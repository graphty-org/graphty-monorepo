# Population to Designloom Persona Index

This index maps every user population and persona id proposed in the research documents in `design/user-research/` to
the Designloom records in `design/designloom/` (16 personas, workflows W01 to W25), deciding for each whether an
existing persona covers it, it becomes a new persona, it folds in as a variant, or it is out of scope, with the
evidence. The documents propose about 160 persona ids across roughly 28 populations; Designloom covers about one
fifth. The recommendation is 42 persona records (the 16 existing, several corrected or split, plus 26 new), every
other id recorded as a variant, 32 new workflows and rewrites of eight existing ones, including W12 Anomaly Detection,
which has no evidenced practitioner workflow (section 7). A census of the fields citing eight general network tools
(section 2.1) confirms the Tier A biology and bibliometrics judgments, enlarges the microbial ecology population, and
finds a large unresearched population, network pharmacology (section 9). The Designloom records have since grown to about 215 persona and 430 workflow files, most of them variants. They do not follow the roster by merging files; instead every persona record carries `roster_persona`, `tier` and `scope` fields that place it in the roster (section 3.3), and the documents researched after the scoring have provisional roster rows there.

`node --test tools/user-research-index.test.mjs` fails when a research document is missing from section 2.

## 1. Method: how personas were merged, kept, tiered or excluded

**Merge rule.** Two proposed personas merge when they share the three things that drive design: the shape of the data
they load, the sequence of steps they take in the tool, and the deliverable they hand off. Differences in domain
vocabulary, sector or seniority alone make a variant, not a persona. This follows the goal-directed method of clustering people on
behavioral variables ([IUS notes on Cooper user modeling](https://enter77.ius.edu/cjkimmer/user-modeling/)).

**Set size.** Cooper-style guidance is three to seven personas per project
([Birzeit course companion](https://ihshaish.github.io/birzeit/comp322/personas_companion.html)), but graphty-element
serves many products and domains, and NN/g says personas "should be one-to-one" with a design challenge
([NN/g persona scope](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona-scope)). So the set is larger, split into a design-target
tier (Tier A: workflows written first) and a coverage tier (Tier B: checked so no population is silently dropped).

**Tier rule.** Each persona is scored on four columns in section 3.1, each taken from the research documents:

- _Size_: **large** when a source shows 10,000 or more users (or citing works, section 2.1), **medium** for 1,000 to
  10,000 or when no count exists, **small** below 1,000 or when the research document calls the group minor.
- _Frequency_ of graph-tool use: **daily or weekly**, **per project** (study, case, engagement, course), or
  **occasional**.
- _Centrality_: **core** (a main screen of the task), **supporting** (a check or figure beside other analysis),
  **peripheral**.
- _Gap Graphty can close_: **yes**, **partly** (needs a capability that is planned but not built), **no**.

A persona is **Tier A** when centrality is core, the gap is yes or partly, and either size is large, or size is medium
and frequency is daily or weekly. Every other persona is Tier B. A workflow in section 5.2 is Tier A when any persona it
serves is Tier A. Size is the weakest column: most counts are tool downloads or members of a society, not people doing
the task, so a tier is a ranking aid, not a market estimate.

**Out-of-scope rule.** A population is out of scope when its graph work needs a simulation solver, a raster or GIS
engine, a disassembler or a safety-certified real-time system, or when a free domain portal already owns the data and
the graph view is minor. The rule applies to the simulation step, not to the people: a population that also builds,
styles and reads the network (the signaling modelers in section 9) is in scope for that work. "Deferred" means in scope but not worth a persona record until higher-tier gaps close.

**Durations rule.** A workflow phase carries a duration only when a research document gives that step that time
(the Gephi class project, the AP-MS merge, the network pharmacology pipeline, the ONA survey, the mobility and
psychometric pipelines and a few others), or when the workflow's own sources state it (the vendor estimate of about
three hours per AML case in W118). No other source gives per-step minutes: the CATMAID Graph Widget page gives no time
for its layout round trip ([CATMAID](https://catmaid.readthedocs.io/en/stable/widgets/graph-widget.html)), and the
EpiContactTrace paper says only that a single-farm search "is a rapid process", with one measured run time, 2.1
seconds for NetworkSummary over all herds on a Windows XP desktop with 3.25 GB of RAM
([PMC3974595](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3974595/)). So W74 Livestock Movement Tracing carries no phase
durations, and W32, W45 and W68 none either. A time target in `success_criteria` cites its source or is labeled
"design goal; no source measures it". `tools/designloom-links.test.mjs` ("workflow durations and time targets are
sourced or labeled") fails on a phase duration that no research document or workflow source gives, and on an
unlabeled time target.

**What counts as "covered".** A persona record names the population's data and tools, and a workflow walks through its main
task. A generic workflow (W02, W04) does not count: its step sequence is often wrong for the population (section 7).

### 3.3 How the records carry the roster

The persona files are not merged into the 42 roster ids: the variant files hold sourced detail (their own data, steps
and frustrations) that a merge would throw away. Instead every file in `design/designloom/personas/` states its place in
the roster with three fields, so a reader or a script can filter to the personas that drive requirements first:

- `roster_persona`: the roster id in section 3.1 it belongs to (its own id when it is the roster persona), or `none`
  when out of scope.
- `tier`: `A` or `B` from the roster persona's row; `none` for deferred and out-of-scope files; `facet` for
  publication-figure-author. A tier belongs to the roster persona, never to a variant: every in-scope file with one
  `roster_persona` carries that row's tier, so a filter on Tier A keeps the whole population (the plant scientists of
  crop-coexpression-biologist stay with genomics-cytoscape-user, Tier A, whose citing fields include Plant Science, 9.0%
  of Cytoscape citers in section 2.1). A variant that would score lower on its own is ranked inside its population, not
  by a lower tier; if its data, steps or deliverable differ, the merge rule in section 1 makes it a roster persona of
  its own with its own scored row. `tools/designloom-links.test.mjs` ("in-scope personas share their roster persona's
  tier") fails on a mismatch.
- `scope`: `in-scope`, `deferred` or `out-of-scope`, following section 9.

A workflow whose personas are all deferred or all out of scope carries `scope: deferred` or `scope: out-of-scope`;
W12 carries `scope: retired` (section 7). Requirements are drawn first from Tier A in-scope personas and their
workflows; a capability required only by deferred or out-of-scope workflows (today `code-text-nodes`,
`activity-balance-roles`, `fcm-scenario` and `shared-risk-diversity`) is not built for them.

## 6. Data: what the merged roster loads, and how big it is

### 6.1 Data shapes

The populations differ less in domain than in data shape:

- **Two tables joined by id** (node list plus edge list, roster plus nominations): explorer-elena, journalists, survey
  researchers, ONA, digital humanists (data-preparation-and-import.md).
- **Two-mode tables** (person by event, plant by pollinator, author by paper): survey, ecology, fraud, bibliometrics.
- **Event streams** (sender, receiver, time, weight): calls, transactions, emails, animal associations, logons.
- **Event logs (case, activity, timestamp)**: process mining; the graph is derived by counting "A directly followed by
  B", so it depends on the activity definition, filters and slider thresholds (process-mining-and-process-maps.md 6).
- **Live, continuously edited sources**: a Markdown vault whose notes and links change on every keystroke, with links
  to unwritten notes (personal-knowledge 5).
- **Square matrices per subject** on fixed atlas positions (connectomics).
- **Correlation, distance or similarity matrices thresholded into networks**: microbial co-occurrence, genetic
  distance, co-expression, psychometric partial correlations.
- **Bibliographic exports** (Web of Science, Scopus, OpenAlex) that need parsing, not CSV.
- **Live databases and services** (Neo4j, SPARQL, APM back ends, CMDBs, dbt manifests): never fully loaded.
- **Directed, typed DAG-like graphs**: dependency, lineage, attack paths, supply chains.
- **Geographic node positions**: infrastructure, supply chain, outbreaks, places, flows.
- **Hand-authored maps** with no source file: systems and stakeholder maps.
- **Rooted trees with up to millions of tips**: phylogenies read beside a map and a timeline. A state's few hundred
  genomes are read against a public SARS-CoV-2 tree of about 5.6 million tips (public-health-and-molecular-epidemiology.md
  5.9); Taxonium's browser-only mode reached about 6 million terminal nodes "on some systems" and its desktop app 12.5
  million sequences on an 8 GB laptop ([Taxonium, PMC9704803](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9704803/)).
- **Collections of thousands of small graphs**: one graph per molecule, protein network or syntax tree, each with a
  label and a split; ogbg-molhiv holds 41,127 graphs and ogbg-molpcba 437,929, both of about 26 nodes, and ogbg-ppa
  158,100 graphs of 243 nodes (code-first-data-scientists-and-graph-ml.md, typical sizes table). Connectomes are the same
  shape: one matrix per subject, tens of thousands of subjects.

Sensitive data (health, case files, classified or CEII, leaks, ONA privacy, private journals) runs through most
populations, so local-only processing is a shared must-have; MicrobeTrace's local-only design is the reference.

**W12 Anomaly Detection has no practitioner behind it.** Its six phases (baseline, statistical detection, structural
detection, triage, investigation, classification) cite no tutorial or practitioner report, yet drive the
`anomaly-detection` capability and the `anomaly-panel` component. No tool manual or practitioner report documenting a
domain-free "find the anomalies" workflow was found. What was found:

- _Rule-based alerts._ In Linkurious, "The models of an alert are the detection patterns which users set": read queries
  in Cypher or Gremlin with domain thresholds (one flags a distance over 300 km), a target entity "used to deduplicate
  and group pattern occurences", run "every hour" by default; analysts sort the case list, assign, open each case's
  graph, and confirm or dismiss ([manual](https://doc.linkurious.com/user-manual/4.2/alert-setup/);
  [blog](https://linkurious.com/blog/linkurious-enterprise-anomaly-detection/)).
- _Label-seeded ranking._ Neo4j's fraud series starts from flagged accounts, ranks others by weighted degree over shared
  cards, devices and IPs, has analysts triage "to label more fraud accounts", expands with Node Similarity (cutoff
  0.01) and reviews the top account in Bloom
  ([Neo4j](https://neo4j.com/blog/developer/exploring-fraud-detection-neo4j-graph-data-science-part-3/));
  financial-crime 5.2 matches.
- _Domain-free scores, no workflow._ PyGOD returns a score per node, "the higher, the more abnormal"
  ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pygod/0.2.0/)); its authors' benchmark found "Most methods evaluated fail to detect
  organic outliers" as opposed to injected ones ([BOND, NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.10071)). OddBall
  ([Akoglu et al.](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/lakoglu/pubs/oddball_TR.pdf)) is a research method. UMAP's docs
  pair it with Local Outlier Factor and urge wariness even of the outlier set
  ([UMAP](https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/outliers.html)); no Graphistry outlier tutorial was found.
- _Domain sequences already researched_: cybersecurity 5.2, 5.4 and 5.5; the study-bias check (network-scientists).

Decision (applied): W12's generic phases are dropped and it points at those sequences; it is linked to no persona and carries `scope: retired`. Replace `anomaly-panel` with a **ranked case
list**: rows from a saved pattern query or a seeded score, each opening its subgraph, with assign, comment and confirm
or dismiss. Where rows are ranked by a heavy-tailed centrality, use log, rank or within-module scores, not z-scores.
`anomaly-detection` survives only as the scoring step feeding that list.

No existing workflow is made obsolete outright except W07 as written; W04, W06, W08 and W11 encode a method the field
does not use, and W12 encodes no evidenced method; all must be rewritten before capabilities are built from them. No existing workflow covers a source that
changes while it is drawn (personal knowledge) or a graph derived from an event log by parameters (process mining).

**Links pruned to match the rewritten phases.** A workflow's `requires_capabilities` feed each capability's
`used_by_workflows`, and so its priority. Where a rewritten workflow no longer uses a method, the link is gone from both
sides: W08 no longer requires betweenness, closeness, degree centrality or community detection (its module comes from
seed connectivity and expansion, its ranking from proximity against degree-matched sets); W11 no longer requires
betweenness (exposure is time to recover and time to survive); W06 no longer requires anomaly or community detection
and suggests `case-list-panel` (the ranked case list, capability `case-list-triage`, sourced from the
[Linkurious alert manual](https://doc.linkurious.com/user-manual/4.2/alert-setup/) and its
[case manual](https://doc.linkurious.com/user-manual/4.2/alert-investigate/)) in place of `anomaly-panel` and
`community-panel`. A scan of every workflow's phases and success criteria for each method's terms removed the same
unused links elsewhere: betweenness from W03, W125, W190, W234, W245, W26, W307, W334, W35, W413, W438, W53 and W90;
closeness from W03, W05, W104, W220 and W26; PageRank from W03, W127, W128, W138, W176, W365 and W438; eigenvector from
W03, W10, W104, W26, W311 and W438; anomaly detection from W112, W113, W282, W312, W345, W52, W65 and W72 (and
`anomaly-panel` from W312, W345, W65 and W72); community detection from W128, W285 and W344 (and `community-panel` from
W128). Degree centrality and the looser community terms (group, cluster, module) were left alone, since a keyword scan
cannot tell their uses apart; the links test does not check phase use for the same reason.

**What "implemented" means.** A capability is `implemented` only when every requirement it lists ships. The research
added unbuilt requirements to all 46 records that were marked implemented, so each is now `in-progress`, and its
`notes` say what ships (with the file) and name the main planned items; any requirement the note does not name as
shipping counts as planned. Algorithm lists mark each method shipped or planned. Examples checked in
graphty-element/src/algorithms: link prediction ships Adamic-Adar and common neighbors only; clustering coefficient reads
no weights and no direction; betweenness reads no weights, runs undirected and has only a sampled-sources option;
shortest paths have no k-shortest (Yen) method in graphty-element or algorithms.

### 10.1 Where each capability comes from in the research

A capability record in `design/designloom/capabilities/` that says only "Proposed from the user research" cannot be
traced: a reader cannot tell which population needs it, at what size, or what done means. The table below names, for
each capability file that had no sources or no requirements on 2026-11-25, the requirement in a research document it
was written from. Every record in the table now cites that document and requirement in `sources` (the document path as
the url, the requirement number in the title, the priority and the coverage note found at research time in the
summary), copies the requirement line, prefixed with its priority, into `requirements`, and names the trace in
`notes`. The requirement line carries the size target where the research gave one.

The match was made by comparing each capability's description with the requirement lines of the research documents
behind its workflows; most descriptions repeat their requirement nearly word for word. "Table row N" means the Nth
row of a requirement table that has no number column. "Workflow only" means the need appears in a document's
workflow but in no numbered requirement, so the record can cite the workflow but has no requirement line to copy.
Counts at the time of the match: 224 Must, 145 Should, 10 Could, 9 workflow only; six of the nine workflow-only rows have since been traced to a Should row.

**Every requirement line carries a priority.** Each line in a capability's `requirements` starts with `Must:`, `Should:` or
`Could:`, the priority of the research row it restates (the highest one when a line merges several rows), or with
`Library parity:` when no research row backs it (a formula, a NetworkX convention, a panel detail), so a Must from the
research can be told from a library-parity detail and ranked first. Lines were matched to rows by comparing their text with
the requirement tables of every research document; on 2026-11-25 the 1,607 lines were 837 Must, 311 Should, 14 Could and
445 library parity. `tools/designloom-links.test.mjs` ("capability requirements carry a priority") fails on a line with
no prefix.
