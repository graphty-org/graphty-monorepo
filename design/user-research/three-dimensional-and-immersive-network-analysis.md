# Analysts Who Work With Networks in 3D and in Immersive VR or AR

## 1. Summary

Most people who use a 3D network view do it on an ordinary monitor, not in a headset. The largest groups are: note-takers who browse their personal knowledge base as a 3D graph (Obsidian's 3D Graph plugin alone has about 84k downloads); expression and omics biologists who build correlation networks in Graphia, where 3D is the default view; multilayer and multi-omics biologists in browser tools (Arena3Dweb, OmicsNet); connectome researchers, whose node positions are anatomical; and investigators and fraud analysts using Kineviz GraphXR, a 3D-first graph tool over Neo4j or CSV, adopted by at least one multinational law-enforcement agency (vendor-published evidence). Developers embed 3D views widely (`react-force-graph-3d`: about 408k npm downloads in a week).

Headset use is smaller and mostly in research: network-medicine labs exploring an interactome of about 16,000 proteins and 300,000 interactions in VR (VRNetzer, DataDiVR) on a GPU PC; single-cell biologists in CellexalVR; software engineers exploring running systems as 3D "cities" in ExplorViz; linked-data users querying and expanding a knowledge graph in Graph2VR on a standalone Quest 2; and military cyber-defense research on HoloLens 2. Every published immersive workflow found is a native or PC-tethered application; graphty-element's path is WebXR in a browser, and the only published standalone-headset scale figure is Graph2VR's: about 5,000 triples at about five frames per second. Controlled user studies agree on a pattern: 3D and stereo help with tracing paths and reading connectivity, but are slower and worse for spatial memory, side-by-side comparison and judging density; labels on distant nodes are hard to read in a headset; and study graphs are far smaller than practitioners' graphs.

- Cytoscape's 3D renderer app Cy3D: 27,036 downloads (read 2026-11-25) [S15], against 436,862 for stringApp in the same store (systems-biology doc). 3D is a minority interest among Cytoscape users.
- Developers: `react-force-graph-3d` 408,145 npm downloads in the week 2026-11-18 to 2026-11-24 [S65]; `3d-force-graph` about 851K weekly and `3d-force-graph-vr` about 26K (developers doc). These measure developers embedding a 3D view, not analysts using one.
- Headset reach: no source found for how many analysts own a standalone headset. The Steam hardware survey (1.35% of Steam users with a headset in September 2026 [S27]) counts only PC VR on Steam and says nothing about standalone Quest owners who never use Steam, so it is not a measure of headset reach.
- OmicsNet, a WebGL-3D-first multi-omics tool, added 2D in version 2.0 because "many users are more accustomed to the traditional 2D perspectives" [S10].

Reading (inference): tens of thousands of people touch a 3D network view on a monitor (note-takers, biologists in Graphia, Arena3Dweb and OmicsNet, GraphXR investigators); headset users number in the tens of labs and study cohorts. Investigator use of 3D in production is documented only by vendor-published material (section 9).

**How often.** Note-takers: daily to occasional. Biologists and investigators: per project or case. Cyber: exercises and studies only.

**Skill profile.** Graph literacy is intermediate to high for the VR labs and connectome researchers (random walk with restart, z-scores of connected components, modularity are routine), low to intermediate for Graphia and Arena3Dweb users who follow tutorials. Coding: Graphia, Arena3Dweb and NeuroCave are no-code; VRNetzer and DataDiVR require a Python backend, a Windows VR PC and precomputed layouts, so a lab engineer sets them up; CellexalVR needs an R conversion step (cellexalvrR). The survey reports a median of 18 participants per study (range 3 to 60) but not who they were [S14]; individual studies used undergraduates (20 in Huang, Pfister and Yang [S21]) or cadets (Ask et al. [S22]), and only a few (Sorger's two experts [S29], Graph2VR's 34 participants, 26 aged 30 to 50 [S49]) report working-age or expert users. GraphXR and Obsidian users are no-code.

**Team context.** VR sessions are social and results leave the headset through a desktop or web module (section 8).

### 3.1 Dr. Fiona Reid -- correlation-network biologist using Graphia (`correlation-network-3d-biologist`)

- **Role.** Senior postdoc in a livestock and immunology genomics group; runs expression atlases and single-cell projects.
- **Background.** Learned BioLayout Express3D from its 2009 protocol [S3], moved to Graphia; scripts in R but explores in Graphia.
- **Tools today.** Graphia desktop (3D smooth-shaded by default [S9]); R for normalization; Excel for the input table; occasionally Cytoscape for a figure.

- **Goals.** Find co-expression modules, name them by enrichment, and show one figure per module with its expression profile.
- **Frustrations.** The threshold changes everything and she has to choose it by eye on a "knee" plot [S18]; 3D rotation is needed to see clusters, but the paper figure is a flat image and Graphia's guide documents no camera save [S19]; no web sharing of a live 3D view from the desktop app (Graphia has a separate web build [S20]).

### 3.3 Dr. Eleni Kostas -- multilayer-network biologist (`multilayer-network-3d-biologist`)

### 3.4 Cadet Ingrid Solberg -- cyber-defense analyst in mixed reality (`immersive-cyber-defense-analyst`)

- **Role.** Cyber cadet in a defense academy working a live-fire exercise dataset with a teammate.
- **Tools today.** Arkime packet capture with a 2D graph view and a paper network schematic; in the study condition, Virtual Data Explorer (VDE) on HoloLens 2 with a shared multi-user server [S22].
- **Data.** Blue-team network topology from NATO Locked Shields 2022; up to 958 nodes and groups and 789 edges in view; edge brightness for traffic volume [S22].

### 3.6 Jonas Weber -- developer learning an unfamiliar system in a software city (`program-comprehension-developer`)

- **Role.** Backend developer who has just joined a team owning a multi-service Java system.
- **Background.** Program comprehension is most of the job: a field study of 78 professional developers found it takes about 58 percent of their time [S68].
- **Tools today.** ExplorViz in a browser: OpenTelemetry traces drawn as a city of services, packages and classes, with desktop, VR and mobile AR multi-user modes [S59][S69].
- **Data.** Live or recorded traces plus static structure; size not reported in the sources.

## 5. Step-by-step workflows

### 5.1 Correlation network from a table in Graphia (3D by default)

Sources: Graphia user guide [S18][S19][S9][S20][S30]; Freeman et al. 2022 [S1]; BioLayout Express3D [S2][S3].

1. **Prepare the table outside Graphia.** Normalize in R or a pipeline; CSV, TSV or Excel with a unique row ID, row attributes, then numeric columns [S18]. The single-cell example used 48 principal components [S1].
2. **Open the file; the correlation wizard starts.** It detects where numbers begin; the user can adjust or transpose [S18].
3. **Choose metric and thresholds.** Pearson (Jaccard for discrete data); minimum threshold default 0.7; initial threshold near the "knee" of the predicted node/edge plot; optional log scaling, normalization, imputation [S18]. Published thresholds: 0.85 for genes, 0.75 for SNV profiles [S1]. BioLayout at r 0.9 gave 5,410 nodes and 447,467 edges; at 0.8 or lower graphs were "(too) large" [S2].
4. **Pick a template and finish**; the summary goes to the provenance log [S18].
5. **Read the 3D layout as it settles.** Live force layout, pausable; default smooth-shaded 3D, with flat 3D, orthographic 3D and 2D in the View menu; left-drag rotates, right-drag pans, double-click focuses; Overview and Component modes [S19].
6. **Tune.** Slide the threshold; singletons removed automatically; k-NN keeps the top k edges per node (paper used 15, 10 and 3, cutting a genome graph from 400.4k to 5,996 edges) [S20][S1].
7. **Cluster.** MCL with a granularity slider (1.7 for genes), or Louvain [S20][S1].
8. **Inspect.** Step through clusters; the data plot shows member profiles; Find Rows of Interest; color, size and text mappings [S20][S9]; cluster enrichment [S1].
9. **Export.** GraphML, GML, JSON Graph or pairwise text; tables; "high-resolution, publication-ready" images [S30]; layouts saved and reloaded [S19]. No documented way to save a camera viewpoint (no source found).

**Decision points.** Threshold and k decide what the graph is; the user judges by watching the 3D layout separate. **Hand-off.** Image, cluster table, enrichment table.

### 5.2 Candidate-gene prioritization in VR (VRNetzer)

Sources: Pirch et al. 2021 [S4]; blog [S5]; DataDiVR README [S6].

1. **Narrow variants outside VR**: sequencing, filtering and pedigree comparison leave 15 candidate genes [S5].
2. **Upload in the web module**: candidates (13 of 15 map to the interactome) and 276 curated seeds (247 map) onto HIPPIE (16,376 proteins, 309,355 interactions) with GO, DisGeNET, OMIM, HPO, pathway and tissue features [S4].
3. **Precompute layouts**: force-directed, random-walk, and t-SNE "functional landscapes" from node-feature matrices [S4]; DataDiVR packs positions into image textures [S6].
4. **Enter VR** on a GPU PC (Quest tethered or HTC Vive; builds about 1,300 to 6,300 euros); over 100 Hz reported for hundreds of thousands of nodes and links [S4].
5. **Explore**: two-handed grab to move, rotate and scale; point to select; a hand-held clipboard panel with a stylus for search (up to four AND/OR clauses), node sets, settings and analytics; animated layout switches; "dynamic link fade" shows random link subsets about 1 s each [S4].
6. **Analyze in VR**: seed connected component 152 of 247 (z = 5.8); random walk with restart ranks DOK3, DOCK2, DDX31; DOCK2's immune neighborhood makes it the lead [S4][S5].
7. **Leave VR**: results go to the web module for collaborators to "review, annotate, download, and share"; Unreal renders screenshots and videos [S4].

### 5.3 Multilayer network from Cytoscape to 3D layers (Arena3Dweb)

Sources: Kokoli et al. 2023 [S8]; Karatzas et al. 2021 [S7].

1. **Build in Cytoscape** (example: a stringApp STITCH query for aspirin plus enriched diseases, tissues and pathways) [S8].
2. **Send to Arena3Dweb**: choose the column that defines layers (up to 18 groups), directionality and whether to keep unassigned nodes; styles and coordinates carry over [S8]. Or upload a tab-delimited file: node, layer, connected node, connected layer, weight [S7].
3. **Arrange layers**: side by side, zig-zag, star or cube; rotate, translate, scale and space each layer [S8][S7].
4. **Lay out** per layer or across layers (one layout over several layers aligns nodes and reduces inter-layer crossings) [S7].
5. **Cluster** (Louvain, Walktrap, Fast-Greedy, label propagation) and **encode**: up to nine edge channels as curves, separate inter-layer edge styling, size by centrality, highlight paths across layers [S8][S7].
6. **Optional VR view** in a new tab, static, on a headset or a phone with a gyroscope [S8].
7. **Export**: light theme for "publication-ready figures"; session JSON with 3D coordinates [S8][S7].

### 5.4 Group comparison of connectomes in 3D and VR (NeuroCave)

Source: Keiriz et al. 2018 [S11]; NeuroMArVL [S12]. Anatomical figures: connectomics document.

1. **Load a study folder**: one adjacency matrix per subject, an index mapping subjects to files, topology and clustering files, and an atlas (82, 177 or 2,514 regions built in) [S11].
2. **Choose a topology**: anatomical positions or an abstract space such as an isomap-based "intrinsic" space; available topologies are detected automatically [S11].
3. **Cluster**: modules placed on faces of a Platonic solid; users enter the layout through an empty face [S11].
4. **Reveal edges from a root node**: edges are hidden by default and bundled when shown [S11].
5. **Threshold** by edge strength (example: absolute correlation above 0.1) [S11].
6. **Compare two groups side by side** in synchronized 3D views; in VR, Oculus Touch thumbsticks navigate, grip highlights, index trigger selects [S11].
7. **Share** (NeuroMArVL, desktop web): "Generate Link" stores the view for others; export JPEG or SVG [S12].

### 5.5 Team cyber analysis on HoloLens 2 (Virtual Data Explorer)

Source: Ask et al. 2023 [S22]; Army news [S24]; Kullman et al. 2019 [S23].

1. **Layout from analysts' mental models**, not a force layout: interviews grouped entities into logical and functional groups placed in space; "These visualizations are not geospatial" [S24].
2. **Load the exercise data**: Locked Shields 2022 blue-team topology as cubes, edges for session counts, brightness for traffic [S22].
3. **One partner learns the topology and explains it** to the other (no time limit) [S22].
4. **Pair finds the top five red-team hosts by traffic** within 40 minutes, walking through the space and grabbing nodes [S22].
5. **Pair finds blue hosts abused for lateral movement** [S22].
6. **Result**: VDE pairs found more attackers and compromised segments, reported lower communication demands and higher confidence; the Arkime (2D) pairs did better on a 2D topology recognition test; decision-making did not differ [S22].

### 5.6 Single-cell session with networks and an automatic report (CellexalVR)

Source: Legetth et al. 2021 [S13]. Included because it shows how a VR session becomes a reproducible record.

1. Process data in Seurat or Scanpy; convert with the R package cellexalvrR (expression plus at least one 2D or 3D embedding).
2. In VR (any SteamVR headset; Vive and Index in development), grab embeddings as objects, select cells freehand, search genes on a virtual keyboard.
3. Compute transcription-factor networks for selected groups (top 130 interactions by default) and heatmaps.
4. Record a "fly-by" camera path to MP4; save networks as images or adjacency lists.
5. On save or exit an HTML report of every action, with figures, p-values and FDRs, is generated.
6. Multi-user: up to 16 participants, each with a local copy of the data; desktop users join as spectators.

### 5.7 From a 3D session to a shareable 2D deliverable (cross-cutting)

Connectome figure tools have a documented one-step flattening route: a canonical view set. For the general 3D graph tools examined (Graphia, Arena3Dweb, CellexalVR, VRNetzer, GraphXR), the sources read describe a capture of the current camera or a video, and none describes exporting several fixed views at once (no source found). What practitioners do:

- Export a canonical view set as one panel (connectome tools). BrainNet Viewer offers three layouts: a single view, which can be "one of the three standard (sagittal, axial or coronal) views or a custom camera view"; a medium view that "shows the lateral and medial sides of each hemisphere"; and a full view that "shows all sides of the brain surface". Figures save as TIFF, BMP, EPS, JPEG or PNG at a chosen DPI [S75]. nilearn's `plot_connectome` draws glass-brain projections chosen by a `display_mode` string: 'ortho' gives three orthogonal cuts, and combinations such as 'lzry' or 'lyrz' put left and right sagittal, axial and coronal views in one figure [S76]. netplotbrain's `view='preset-3'` gives one figure with Left, Right and Superior panels, and 'preset-4' gives the lateral and medial side of each hemisphere [S77]. The connectomics document's figure requirement asks for these grids at a set DPI and physical size (connectomics doc, R15). Journals size such panels by column: Elsevier asks for 300 DPI for color images, 500 DPI for combination art and 1000 DPI for line art, at 90, 140 or 190 mm wide [S78].
- Save a high-resolution image of the current camera (Graphia [S30]; Arena3Dweb light theme [S8]; CellexalVR screenshots [S13]).
- Render a video: Unreal screenshots and videos (VRNetzer [S4]), CellexalVR fly-by MP4 [S13], and BrainNet Viewer's turntable, "a 12 second long, 30 FPS, 735x534, AVI file" in which the model "rotates clockwise in a circle at one degree per frame" [S75].
- Save a session file (Arena3Dweb JSON [S8], Graphia layout [S19]) or a link (NeuroMArVL [S12], OmicsNet bookmarks [S10]).
- Carry results out to a web or desktop module for annotation (VRNetzer [S4]) or an automatic report (CellexalVR [S13]).
- Switch to 2D for the figure [S10][S19].

Standard views depend on axes. A brain has anatomical axes, so left, right, superior and the rest name the same views in every paper. A force-directed or embedding layout has no such axes, and no source read defines a standard view set for one (no source found). The nearest work is Joos et al., whose viewpoint study measures "the perceivability of a graph's principal axes" (Isometric Viewpoint Deviation) and finds it among the key indicators of which viewpoints users prefer [S79]. Views along a layout's principal axes are therefore a reasonable default set when the space has no anatomy; that is an inference from [S79], not a documented practice.

### 5.8 A browser WebXR session on a standalone headset (graphty-element's real XR setting)

No published network-analysis workflow runs in a browser on a standalone headset; this sequence is assembled from platform documentation, the closest standalone tool (Graph2VR, a native Unity app on Quest 2 [S49]) and browser VR graph libraries.

1. **Open the page in the headset's browser.** Quest Browser on Meta Quest, or Safari on Apple Vision Pro, where WebXR was behind a feature flag (Settings > Apps > Safari > Advanced > Feature Flags > WebXR Device API) when natural input shipped in visionOS 1.1 [S47]. The same page also runs on a desktop.
2. **Find the enter button.** A page shows "Enter VR" or "Enter AR" only when the browser supports that mode (A-Frame's rule [S51]; a page checks `isSessionSupported('immersive-ar')` before offering AR [S50]).
3. **Grant permissions.** Hand tracking is an extra feature the page must request and the user must grant; on Vision Pro, hand poses then appear as tracked pointers [S47]. Meta's help center describes approving or declining a site's request for hand pose data (read through search summary) [S67].
4. **Choose an input.** Quest: controllers with a ray and trigger, or hands. Vision Pro: gaze-and-pinch "transient-pointer" input; no input source exists until the user pinches, the ray starts at the gaze and then follows the hand [S47]. 3d-force-graph-vr exposes node and link hover and click callbacks for the pointer [S38].
5. **Optionally switch to passthrough mixed reality.** The page requests `immersive-ar` and must draw on a transparent background "otherwise the passthrough environment won't be visible"; it can ask for detected planes (horizontal or vertical rectangles on Quest) to place the graph on a table or wall, and anchors that persist, at most 8 per site [S50]. The Meta page read lists Quest 2 (grayscale passthrough) and Quest Pro (color) [S50]; no source read covers later Quest models.
6. **Work within the standalone budget.** Graph2VR loaded "about 5000 triples at once ... but the framerate dropped to around five frames per second", and its authors advise querying an endpoint rather than loading large files [S49]. 3d-force-graph-vr's large example is "~4k elements" [S38]. graphty-element's own VR guide advises under 1,000 nodes. VRNetzer's 16,376-node figure is for Unreal on a GPU PC [S4].
7. **Read labels and keep sessions short.** Labels on distant nodes were the most-mentioned problem in Graph2VR's 34-person evaluation, caused by headset pixel resolution; sessions ran 40 to 60 minutes and some participants reported dizziness or tiredness; the authors note manufacturers advise a break every 30 minutes [S49].
8. **Leave and continue on the 2D page.** No source describes how a browser network tool carries the selection, camera or findings back to the page after the session ends (no source found). Native tools hand off to a web module or an HTML report (sections 5.2 and 5.6).

### 5.9 Investigator in 3D on a desktop (GraphXR)

Sources: user tutorial [S55]; GraphXR API reference [S56]; Neo4j blog [S40]; GraphXR CSV tutorial (search summary) [S66]. Vendor-published or single-user evidence throughout.

1. **Connect data.** Install GraphXR as a Neo4j Desktop graph app or open it in the browser and connect to Neo4j [S40][S55]; or drag a CSV into a workspace, where each row becomes a node [S66].
2. **Shape the graph.** The Connector panel's Extract tab creates new nodes from an existing label's properties, applied only to the selection when one exists [S66]. Set property tags and colors per type [S55].
3. **Lay out in 3D.** Run the force layout; switch to a parametric view, which maps a property to an axis (API example `parametric({ x: 'price' })`), or distribute nodes along an axis by binning a property (`distributionBy`, `dimension: 'y', bin: 'seasonNumber'`), or a line sorted by a property [S55][S56].
4. **Place on a map and timeline.** Geospatial views and timeline filtering are named as the main value for desktop users [S40].
5. **Inspect.** An enhanced table of properties and aggregations of neighbor values [S55]; images on nodes [S40].
6. **Present.** Kineviz positions this as "visualization-first" analysis ending in data-driven presentations [S54]. Export and case-file steps: no source found.

### 5.10 Query-and-expand exploration of a knowledge graph in VR (Graph2VR)

Source: Graph2VR paper [S49]. See also graph-database-query-and-expand-exploration.md and knowledge-graph-engineers-and-graphrag.md.

1. Put on a Quest 2 (standalone, through OpenXR, no PC) or a PC-tethered HTC Vive; the app opens on the SPARQL endpoint set in a configuration file (a local Virtuoso or public DBpedia), with others to switch to.
2. Start from a search result or load an N-Triples file, which adds a graph without clearing the scene; a saved state replaces the scene.
3. Expand a node: a menu lists its incoming or outgoing predicates, grouped, each with its triple count; choosing one adds up to 25 linked nodes by default, adjustable with a slider in fixed steps.
4. Pin nodes to take them out of the layout while still dragging them by hand.
5. Switch among 3D force-directed, a simple 2D arrangement, a hierarchical view and a class hierarchy.
6. Fly through the scene (testers found flying too fast) and build queries visually; participants asked for undo, a query-building wizard, OPTIONAL and UNION, and export of the query to a browser.
7. A 2025 follow-up tried three life-science graphs (cohort metadata, synthetic rare-disease variants, drug ontologies). Small filtered graphs showed no benefit from VR; a malformed RDF export had to be fixed first; the authors report about 5,000 nodes on a GTX 1060 and about 17,000 at 14 frames per second on an RTX 4090 (PC-tethered) [S74].

### 5.11 Exploring a running software system as a 3D city (ExplorViz)

Sources: ExplorViz project page and 2022 study (search summary) [S59]; survey [S14]. See also dependency-and-impact-analysis-engineers.md.

1. Instrument the application (for Java, an agent such as inspectIT Ocelot) and export traces to an OpenTelemetry collector [S59].
2. Open the web view: applications are foundations, packages stacked districts, classes buildings, method calls curved pipes between them [S59].
3. Explore alone on a desktop or phone, or join others: in VR, users share one space and see each other as avatars [S59].
4. The 2022 study found VR and AR suitable for collaborative program comprehension but reported readability of visualized entities and performance problems [S59]. A separate study in the survey found AR helped novices, not experts, identify dependencies in large systems [S14].

### 5.12 Browsing a note vault in 3D (personal knowledge graphs)

Sources: plugin pages and forums [S60][S61][S62][S63][S64]; personal-knowledge-graphs-and-note-networks.md.

1. Install a 3D graph plugin (Obsidian 3D Graph, desktop and mobile [S61]) or switch org-roam-ui to 3D.
2. Rotate, zoom and highlight to see links, clusters and node details [S61]; in org-roam-ui, double-click opens the note (companion doc).
3. Graph Styler's experimental 3D takes a snapshot of the nodes visible in Obsidian's global graph, so after changing filters the user closes and reopens the 3D view; it offers force, "orbital hop shells" and globe layouts [S63].
4. Performance: org-roam-ui's README advises "Turn off labels ... Favor 2D over 3D" because 3D is "very slow" [S64].
5. Opinion splits: an Obsidian forum poster cites comments calling such plugins "useless" and defends a 3D graph as "a fun way to keep boredom at bay" [S62].

**Identifier problems.** The same as their 2D siblings: candidate genes that do not map to the interactome (2 of 15, 29 of 276 in VRNetzer [S4]), layer assignment from an attribute with nodes left "unassigned" [S8].

**Positions as data.** In many of these workflows the 3D position is not computed by a force layout. Three kinds occur: (1) positions produced by an analysis (t-SNE functional landscapes [S4], isomap intrinsic space [S11], analyst mental-model layouts [S24]); (2) positions mapped from attributes, as in GraphXR's parametric layout that puts a property on the x, y or z axis, its binned distribution along an axis and its sorted line [S56] -- in effect a 3D scatter of nodes with links drawn between them; (3) real geometry from imaging, where nodes carry measured coordinates and edges carry radius and a curved centerline (vascular graphs [S57], anatomical connectome regions and neuron skeletons). All three must be imported or derived, kept fixed and switched between.

**Sensitivity.** Patient variant data (VRNetzer) and military network data (VDE) are sensitive; VRNetzer's public server is the default configuration [S4]; CellexalVR deliberately does not transmit data between multi-user participants [S13].

## 7. Analysis methods

- **Construction:** Pearson (and Jaccard) correlation with minimum and display thresholds, kNN pruning, component-size filters [S1][S18][S20].
- **Clustering:** MCL (granularity or inflation 1.5 to 2.2), Louvain, Walktrap, Fast-Greedy, label propagation [S1][S2][S8].
- **Network medicine:** largest connected component of seeds with a z-score against 100,000 random draws, random walk with restart, neighborhood enrichment [S4].
- **Embeddings as layouts:** t-SNE on node-feature matrices, isomap [S4][S11].
- **Centralities for sizing:** degree, betweenness, clustering coefficient, PageRank, eccentricity [S1][S7].
- **Validation habits:** enrichment statistics with multiple-testing correction [S2][S4]; independent clinical confirmation in the rare-disease case [S4]. No 3D-specific validation (for example checking a finding from several viewpoints) is described in any workflow; the GD 2025 viewpoint study shows 3D drawings are "highly viewpoint-dependent" [S31].

**Synthesis.** Evidence favors 3D/VR for (a) tracing paths and reading connectivity, especially at hundreds to thousands of nodes, (b) separating overlapping clusters, and (c) whole-graph counting tasks with embodied rotation. It favors 2D for (d) speed, (e) spatial memory and change detection, (f) side-by-side comparison and (g) judging density or comparing layers. Label legibility in a headset is a recurring problem [S49][S59]. Study graphs ran to about 5,000 nodes at most, with a median of 120; the survey's 76 size-class counts lean small (45 of 76 at 120 nodes or fewer) [S14]; none tested genome-scale graphs like VRNetzer's, and none tested attribute-mapped axes or imaging-derived geometry.

## 8. Outputs and collaboration

- **Deliverables.** Flat images of a chosen viewpoint, videos, session files, links, enrichment and ranking tables, HTML action reports (section 5.7). Paper and court have no 3D medium (deliverables doc).
- **Collaboration patterns reported.** Onlookers watching a mirrored display [S16]; hosted multiplayer sessions (DataDiVR [S6]); up to 16 users plus desktop spectators (CellexalVR [S13]); co-located mixed-reality dyads (VDE [S22]); avatars in shared VR alongside desktop and mobile users (ExplorViz [S59]); web-module review after the session [S4].
- **Note-taking gap.** In the multilayer VR study, three participants asked for something to write notes on [S17]; VRNetzer and CellexalVR put a virtual keyboard or stylus in the scene [S4][S13].
- **Reproducibility.** Graphia's provenance log [S18], CellexalVR's automatic report [S13], session JSON [S8]; VRNetzer session-trace recording was only a prototype [S4].
