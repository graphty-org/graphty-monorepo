**Prototype 12: Lean In** -- Distance is the mode: how close your hand is to the graph, a cluster or a node decides which verbs exist, and handles grow on that object at the scale you are working at.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away inside a faint bounding box about 45 cm across. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Primary input is tracked hands (fingertip position, pinch, open palm, fist). Controllers work as a stand-in hand on Quest and Galaxy XR. Eyes are not used. Voice is optional and only types text into an open text field (a note card or the Find field). Every input works with either hand, so the whole scheme can be used one-handed and seated. Band sizes and hysteresis are settings, so a user with a tremor can widen them.

The two open-palm gestures cannot be mistaken for each other: gather needs the palm facing down (within 45 degrees) and held still inside the box; undo needs the palm facing away from you (within 35 degrees), fingers up, a push faster than about 0.8 m/s, and is ignored for half a second after any handle release. ### The handle kit

Every verb on every object is one of six handle shapes. The shape tells you how to drag it and what kind of thing it does.

Once a pinch grabs a handle the scope is locked until release, so reaching across the graph to a handle never changes what it acts on. Handles grow on the side of the object facing you, offset from the fingertip so the hand does not cover them, and only the object in scope and pinned objects show handles (at most nine on a set's hull, spread around it).

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on and stand, or sit, facing the graph.
    - **What you see:** The 15 families floating at chest height inside a faint box. Your hands are down, so nothing else shows: no menus, no handles. The readout plate on the box's front edge reads "Florentine families".
    - **Controls:** none; rest band.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Raise a hand toward the box, stopping short of it. Poke the readout plate to pin it open. Lean left and right to see the graph's depth. If seated, make a fist near the box and pull it to the arm's-reach detent, then turn the base ring a detent or two.
    - **What you see:** As your hand crosses into the graph band, the box outline brightens and its handles grow along its edges. The pinned readout lists "15 families, 20 marriages, 1 piece, undirected" and the node attributes the sample carries. Labels are shown for the largest nodes only. A reeled box clicks into its arm's-reach detent, close enough that its far side is within reach.
    - **Controls:** hand distance (graph band), fingertip poke (pin the readout), body lean, fist reel, turntable ring.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Move your hand into the box. Labels bloom on the nodes near your hand. Slide toward the node labeled Medici until your fingertip is beside it, and poke it. (In a graph too big to scan, you would pull the Find arrow at graph band up through letters and sideways through names and release on "Medici", or pinch its field and say or type "Medici"; either pins it.)
    - **What you see:** Labels appear on the region under your hand as you enter the box. Medici swells as your fingertip nears it, gets an outline, and its handles grow: a Neighbors arrow, a Path thread, a Hide tab, a note flag. A readout beside it says "Medici, 6 ties". The poke pins it, so its handles stay when your hand moves.
    - **Controls:** hand distance (neighborhood, then element band), fingertip hover, fingertip poke.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Medici's Neighbors arrow and pull it outward to the first detent. Release.
    - **What you see:** While you pull, the six neighbors light up and the arrow's tip reads "1 hop: 6 families, 7 marriages among them". Pulling on to the second detent would read "2 hops: 11 families"; you stop at one. On release the six (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni) and Medici become a pinned set with a soft hull around them, and the hull carries its own handles.
    - **Controls:** pinch and drag an arrow, detent, release to commit.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pull your hand back out of the box to the graph band. Pinch the Run arrow on top of the box and pull it up: the algorithm families pass as detents (Centrality, Community, Paths, ...). Stop at Centrality, slide sideways through its members to PageRank, and release.
    - **What you see:** The arrow's readout follows the pull: "Centrality", then "PageRank on 15 families". On release, PageRank runs on the whole graph (the graph band means graph scope, even though a set is pinned) and a result ladder stands up beside the box's right side: one rung per family, ranked, with the run's name plate on top.
    - **Controls:** hand distance (graph band), pinch and drag a list arrow along its two legs, release to run.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Bring your hand to the ladder. Run your fingertip down its rungs.
    - **What you see:** From the graph band the ladder shows the top three names. As your fingertip nears it, the rungs spread and show values: Medici 0.146, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, and so on down to Acciaiuoli 0.031. The rung under your fingertip lights its family in the graph (hover is selection, here too).
    - **Controls:** hand distance (element band on the ladder), fingertip hover.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Near the ladder, pinch its Size arrow and pull it out until the largest family is about three times the smallest; release. Then pinch its Color ring and turn it two detents to the sequential palette; release.
    - **What you see:** Node sizes follow the pull live; the arrow reads "Size by PageRank, 1x to 3x, 15 families". The ring's readout names each palette as it passes and recolors the graph live. On release, a key appears on the box ("Size: PageRank", "Color: PageRank") and a new layer slat appears on the box's left side.
    - **Controls:** pinch and drag an arrow, pinch and turn a ring, release to commit.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the Blade at the top of the ladder and slide it down the rungs to the detent at 0.070. Release.
    - **What you see:** Rungs below the blade dim, and those families fade out of the graph as you slide. The blade reads "PageRank at least 0.070: keeps 5 of 15 families, 4 marriages". After release, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain, and the box's readout says "Showing 5 of 15 -- 1 filter". The blade stays where you left it, as the filter step's own handle.
    - **Controls:** pinch and slide a blade, detents, release to commit.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Lean in to Medici until your fingertip is beside it, pinch its note flag and pull it out. Dictate "Married into six families; the hub of the network", or type it on the small keyboard that unfolds under the card. Push the flag back into its socket to close it.
    - **What you see:** A note card on a short stalk from Medici, with your text. Closed, it becomes a small flag marker on the node.
    - **Controls:** hand distance (element band), pinch and pull a flag, voice (optional) or in-scene keyboard.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You reach back to the note flag to fix a word, but your pinch lands on the Hide tab beside it; you pull, glance past the readout "Hide Medici: 1 family, 4 marriages hidden", and release. Medici vanishes. Keep your hand where Medici was and push a "stop" palm away, short and quick.
    - **What you see:** A hidden node leaves a ghost outline until your next change, so the spot is still there to reach and it is outlined as the undo's target. The push undoes Medici's last change only: Medici reappears with its note intact, and a ghost Hide tab hangs beside it (pinch it to redo). Here a push from the graph band would have done the same, because the hide was the latest change. The difference shows when the mistake is older: had you also written the graph note since, the push at Medici would still restore Medici without touching the graph note.
    - **Controls:** "stop" palm pushed away (scoped undo), ghost handle (redo).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Pull back to the graph band. Pinch the Save tab on the box's front edge, pull it down past its detent, and release.
    - **What you see:** The tab reads "Save project" while pulled; on release the readout says "Saved Florentine families". The Save as recipe tab beside it is not used here.
    - **Controls:** hand distance (graph band), pinch and pull a tab, release to commit.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Blades stack, and each one is a filter step: a blade on the PageRank ladder plus a blade on a Degree ladder means both must hold (AND). For "strong families among the Medici in-laws", leave the in-law set pinned and use the Blade on the set's hull instead of the graph's ladder: it filters inside that set only. OR is made by gathering both results' sets under one palm and closing it (a union), which is honest about being a set, not a saved rule. Saved rules with OR and NOT are weak in this scheme. A blade's Remove tab takes its filter step out, with the count it will bring back shown before release.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Bring the fingertip to the ladder's name plate (the run's object). Its options grow as arrows and rings: a damping arrow at 0.85 with detents every 0.05, an iterations arrow, a "weighted" ring. Dragging an option reruns live as a preview, with the ladder's rungs shifting and a count "3 families change rank". Release commits a new run beside the old one; drag back past rest to keep the original.
- **Comparing two runs.** Pull the Compare thread from one ladder's name plate to the other ladder. The two ladders join rung to rung by family, so crossing lines show who moved, and the thread's readout says how many changed rank and by how much. Drop the thread on empty space to cancel.
- **Layers on subsets.** Gather a region with the palm and close it to pin the set; its hull's Size arrow and Color ring make a style layer that paints only that set. Each layer is a slat on the box's left side; fingertip on a slat grows the handle that made it (drag it again to edit the layer), its order ring (turn to move it up or down the stack), its Hide tab and its Remove tab.
- **Removing a run.** The name plate's Remove tab reads, before release, what goes with it: "Remove PageRank: also removes 1 style layer and 1 filter step that use it".
- **Dense or large graphs.** Gather a rough region with the palm (it needs no precise touch), close it, and pull the hull's Fit tab: the graph magnifies inside the box until that set fills it, and what falls outside the box is clipped. The base's Zoom arrow dragged back to 1x returns to the whole graph. Navigation and zoom are view state, not in the undo history.
- **Recipes.** The Save as recipe tab saves the runs, style layers and filter steps without the data. On another graph, the recipes appear as one more family on the Run arrow's pull; releasing on one replays it, with a count of what it changed. Editing a recipe step by step stays on the 2D page.
- **Joining tables.** Stays on the 2D page. Handles act on what is drawn; choosing files and matching columns is reading and typing, which this scheme has no good handle for.

### How it scales

- **The 60+ algorithm catalog.** The Run arrow is a two-leg list: up through about ten families, then sideways through each family's members, every one a detent with its name, at a fixed 2.5 cm spacing so the reach to a favorite is learnable. The longest pull is about 25 cm each way. The readout always names the detent under your hand, so nothing must be memorized. Past about 120 entries the pull gets too long; the Run arrow would then need the same text field Find has (dictate or type a name), which this design leaves for later.
- **New object types.** Each object type declares its handle set from the six shapes and the band it lives in. A new type (a path, a time slice, a group of groups) brings handles, never a panel, and users already know how each shape drags.
- **Plugins.** A plugin algorithm appears as a detent in its family on the Run arrow and gets an options plate built from its option list (numbers become arrows, choices become rings, flags become tabs). A plugin object type ships its handle set.
- **The limit.** Density. The element band needs nodes farther apart than the fingertip's 3 cm radius and the tracking jitter (about 1 cm with hands). Reeling the box closer does not help, because it moves the box without spreading the nodes. A few hundred nodes at 45 cm is fine; beyond that you magnify with the Zoom arrow or a set's Fit tab, so node spacing in the box grows. Handle crowding is capped: only the object in scope and pinned objects show handles, at most about nine on a hull and four on an element.

### Why it should work

- Physical navigation helps people read large displays: in Ball, North and Bowman's "Move to improve" (CHI 2007), people who walked up to a large display found and compared targets faster than people who panned and zoomed. Here stepping in is both the zoom and the scope change.
- Semantic zoom (Pad++, Bederson and Hollan, 1994) showed that changing what is shown, not just its size, with distance keeps detail out of the way until it is wanted. Lean In applies it to verbs.
- Proxemic interaction (Greenberg, Marquardt and colleagues, 2011) uses distance as implicit input so that people do not have to switch modes by hand; their proxemic media player changed its controls as the viewer approached.
- Gizmos in Blender, Maya and Unity have taught millions of people that a handle on the object means "drag this to change that property", with the value visible while dragging. Direct manipulation (Shneiderman, 1983): continuous feedback and reversible actions.
- Hover before touch is shipped: visionOS shows a hover state before a direct touch, and Meta's poke interactions show hover as the fingertip approaches a button. People already expect things to react as a finger nears them.
- Drag back to cancel is shipped and familiar: pull-to-refresh, swipe actions on phones, and the "drag off the button to cancel" rule of desktop buttons.
- Speed-dependent gain is how every desktop pointer already gets both reach and precision from one hand (Casiez and colleagues, 2008, on control-display gain), so fine control needs no extra gesture.
- Selective and regional undo are studied: Berlage's selective undo (1994) and Seifried and colleagues' regional undo for large surfaces (CHI 2012) found people can understand undoing one region's changes without the global history.

### What it would teach us

- Do people experience distance bands as zoom (natural) or as hidden modes (mode errors)? Can they predict which verbs they will find before they reach in?
- What band sizes and hysteresis keep handles stable under real hand tracking on each device, and does a 3 cm element band work at 15, 77 and 300 nodes?
- Is hover-is-selection a Midas touch in 3D: does scope jump while people reach for a handle, even with scope locked on pinch, and does the poke rule stop accidental pins as a hand passes through the graph?
- Can handles alone carry a 60+ algorithm catalog, or does the two-leg Run pull break down without search?
- Do people understand scoped undo, and do they prefer it to a single global undo once they have made an older mistake?
- How tiring is it? How long can people work with a hand raised near the graph, and how much do the arm's-reach detent and seated turntable help?
- How many handle drags does the basic journey take compared with the desktop app's clicks?

### Known risks

- **Arm fatigue.** Every verb needs a raised hand. Mitigation: graph-band handles sit on the box's lower edge; the arm's-reach detent brings the box close, and reeling while looking down brings it lower; rest band shows nothing, so lowering a hand is the natural pause, and global undo still works from there.
- **Seated reach.** At 75 cm the far half of the box is out of a seated arm's reach. Mitigation: the arm's-reach detent at 50 cm, offered on the readout plate's first open when the headset reports a seated height.
- **Accidental scope changes.** A drifting hand crosses a band and the verbs change under it. Mitigation: hysteresis on every band edge (and band sizes as a setting), the outline that always shows the object in scope, scope locked while a pinch holds a handle, and pinning for anything you want to keep.
- **Accidental pins.** A hand moving through the box touches nodes on the way. Mitigation: only a quick in-and-back poke toward an element pins it; sliding past or through does not.
- **Scoped undo with dependents.** Undoing an old change on one object may break later changes built on it (a style layer on a run). Mitigation: the undo previews the later changes it would also undo, by count, and needs a second push within two seconds to go ahead.
- **Occlusion.** Your hand covers the node you are working on. Mitigation: handles grow outward on the side facing you and offset from the fingertip; readouts float above the hand.
- **No haptics on hands.** Detents can be felt only with controllers. Mitigation: a click sound and a tick mark at every detent, and a readout that names the value.
- **False undo.** A quick move after a drag reads as a push-away. Mitigation: require a palm facing away with fingers up and a minimum speed, ignore pushes within half a second of releasing a handle, and leave a ghost handle so redo is one pinch.
- **Handle crowding on a set.** A hull carries up to nine handles. Mitigation: they spread around the hull, and those on the far side grow as you move your hand around it.
- **Dense graphs.** The element band fails when nodes are closer than the tracking jitter. Mitigation: magnify with the Zoom arrow or a set's Fit tab; the readout says when nodes are too close to pick one by touch.
- **Text entry.** Notes need typing, and an in-scene keyboard is slow. Mitigation: optional dictation where the browser supports it; short notes; long writing stays on the 2D page.
- **Gaze pinch on Vision Pro.** Safari reports every pinch as a pointer select too, and without hand permission the scheme cannot run. Mitigation: filter those events whenever hand joints are on a handle, ask for hand permission at Enter VR with the reason, and test on the device early.
