**Prototype 13: Hand of Cards** -- A fan of cards in the off hand, each a ready-to-run verb with frozen settings; play a card onto a target and it stays on that object as the record of what was done to it, and lifting it off undoes just that.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away, sitting on a thin round base about 45 cm across whose front edge is about 50 cm from you, inside easy reach. The front edge carries the graph's name plate and the row of cards played onto the graph. Just below and in front of the base, about 40 cm from you, is a ledge where the fan rests when it is not on your hand. Primary input is bare hands (thumb-index pinch, open palm, index poke, palm orientation). Controllers work where the device has them (trigger for pinch, grip on the rim, buzz on each step). Voice is optional and used only to search the draw pile or dictate a note. Eyes are not used, except through Apple Vision Pro's system look-and-pinch.

### Key structures

**Card kinds**

| Kind           | What it is                                                                                                                                                          | Played onto                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Verb card      | One command with its settings frozen and printed on its face ("PageRank, damping 0.85", "Neighbors, 1 hop", "Keep top 5"); the face lists the object types it takes | Any object of those types; others do not light                                                                                                               |
| Modifier card  | One setting ("damping 0.7", "top 10", "at least 0.07", "weighted by marriages", "or", "off")                                                                        | A played verb card; it replaces that card's setting and the effect is redone, with a ghost first                                                             |
| Selection card | The nodes and edges you pinched, listed on its face; always in the first slot of the fan. Playing it plays a copy; your selection stays in the slot                 | A verb card in the fan or a played one (scopes it to the selection), or the graph's name plate (keeps it as a named group)                                   |
| Result face    | What a played run card turns into: its top values and counts                                                                                                        | It is an object, so other cards play onto it (Color by, Size by, Keep where, Compare)                                                                        |
| Recipe card    | A set of played cards copied into one card by the Copy card                                                                                                         | Any target the first card of the set accepts; it previews the whole set as one ghost, then lays the cards down one by one, so each can still be lifted alone |
| Note card      | Text you type or dictate into it                                                                                                                                    | Any object, including another played card                                                                                                                    |
| Version card   | "Save version": a named point you can return to; the project itself is kept after every play                                                                        | The graph's name plate                                                                                                                                       |

**The deal: what the fan holds, by working object.** Your working object is the last object you pinched; a played run makes its result your working object. The deal changes only when your dominant hand is empty and away from the fan. Cards you drew from the pile stay in the fan until you discard them.

| Working object       | Dealt cards (besides selection, Note, Copy)                                              |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Graph                | Summary, PageRank, Communities, Degree, Find path, Save version                          |
| Node                 | Inspect, Neighbors 1 hop, Neighbors 2 hops, Path to selection, Hide, Pin                 |
| Edge                 | Inspect, Hide, Path through here, Weight by                                              |
| Selection or group   | Neighbors of all, Inspect all, Hide, Path between (when it holds two nodes)              |
| Run result           | Color by, Size by, Keep top 5, Keep above mean, Compare with previous, Label top 5       |
| Style or filter card | Off, and its modifier cards (palette copies, reverse, top 10, top quarter, at least ...) |
| Note                 | Edit (reopens its text panel)                                                            |

Project verbs (Export, Open another project, Rename) live in the draw pile under "Project".

**Where played cards sit.** A card played onto a node or edge sits on a small tab beside it; a hidden node leaves its tab floating where it was, as a faint ring, so the card that hid it can still be lifted. A card played onto the graph sits in the row on the base's front edge, in the order played; that row is the analysis: runs, filter steps and style layers, first to last. Cards played onto a played card hang off it as a short shingled strip, in the order played. The row also shows a small count chip for every node that carries cards; opening a chip (open hand near it) shows that node's cards so you can lift one without reaching into the graph.

**What a lift takes with it.** Lifting a card removes its effect and the effects of the cards played onto it; they travel with it as a small stack in your hand. Cards later in the row that used its output (a filter on its values, a run on a graph it filtered) are redone without it. Everything that would change lights while you pinch, before you pull. Lifting a card and playing it again puts it last, which is how you reorder style layers and filter steps.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
   **What you do:** Open the Florentine families in graphty on the headset, press Enter VR, and raise your off hand palm up.
   **What you see:** The graph floats at chest height on its base, with "Florentine families" on the name plate. A fan opens above your palm, dealt for the graph: an empty selection card, Note, Copy, Summary, PageRank, Communities, Degree, Find path, Save version.
   **Controls:** Off hand palm up.

2. **Get oriented.**
   **What you do:** Pinch the Summary card out of the fan, hold it over the name plate until its border fills, then release. Push the base rim with an open hand to turn the graph.
   **What you see:** While you hold the card there, a ghost of its face appears on the base: 15 families, 20 marriages, one connected piece. On release the Summary card takes the first place in the row with those facts. The graph turns as you push and stops when you let go.
   **Controls:** Pinch (take), move (aim), release (play), open hand on the rim (turn).

3. **Find the Medici.**
   **What you do:** Pinch the draw pile at the end of the fan. A search panel opens on the ledge; say or poke "Medici". Pinch the Medici card that rises, hold it over the graph, then release it away from the graph.
   **What you see:** The search matches family names as well as commands, so the Medici come up as a node card. Held over the graph, it turns the graph (slowly, about the vertical axis only) until the Medici node faces you. Released over nothing, the card joins your selection card: the Medici are now selected and your working object, and the fan is re-dealt for a node.
   **Controls:** Pinch (open search), voice or poke keys (search), pinch, move (the graph brings the named node to the front), release over nothing (into the selection).

4. **Who they married into.**
   **What you do:** Take "Neighbors, 1 hop" and hold it over the Medici node until the border fills; release.
   **What you see:** Before release, a ghost lights the six families the Medici married into and the six marriages. After release the card sits on a tab beside the Medici, its face listing Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni, and those families stay highlighted.
   **Controls:** Pinch, move, release.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
   **What you do:** Pinch the name plate to make the graph your working object, take "PageRank, damping 0.85" from the re-dealt fan, hold it over the name plate, release.
   **What you see:** Only the name plate lights for this card; nodes do not, because PageRank runs on a graph. The ghost previews the ranking as node sizes (on a large graph, where the run would be slow, the ghost shows the element's estimate of how long it will take instead). On release the card joins the row and turns into its result face. The fan is re-dealt for the result: Color by, Size by, Keep top 5, Keep above mean, Compare with previous, Label top 5.
   **Controls:** Pinch (graph as working object), pinch, move, release.

6. **Read the result.**
   **What you do:** Hold your open hand near the PageRank card in the row. Then take Inspect and hold it over the Pazzi without letting the border fill; move it back to the fan.
   **What you see:** The card's face opens toward you: Medici about 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071. Inspect over the Pazzi shows a ghost label "PageRank 0.036, 1 marriage"; released back over the fan, nothing is pinned.
   **Controls:** Open hand near (read), pinch, move (the ghost is the reading), release over nothing.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
   **What you do:** Take "Color by", hold it over the PageRank card in the row, release. Do the same with "Size by".
   **What you see:** Color by lights only cards that produced values (the PageRank card; not Summary). While held, the graph shows the coloring as a ghost. On release the colors stay and Color by hangs off the PageRank card. Size by hangs after it, and the families grow by rank. Each is a style layer; its place in the strip is its place in the layer order.
   **Controls:** Pinch, move, release (twice).

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
   **What you do:** Take "Keep top 5", hold it over the PageRank card, release. Then take the "top 10" modifier from the re-dealt fan, hold it over the Keep card, look at the ghost, and put it back.
   **What you see:** Held over the PageRank card, Keep top 5 outlines the ten families it would remove. On release they go, and the card reads "Keep top 5: 5 of 15". The top 10 modifier previews ten kept; you decline it. For an exact cut you would open the draw pile and type "0.07": the pile offers an "at least 0.07" modifier, which you would play onto the Keep card.
   **Controls:** Pinch, move, release; release over nothing to decline.

9. **Write a note.**
   **What you do:** Take the Note card. Its text panel opens on the ledge; poke or dictate "Kept 5: the Medici lead by a wide margin". Pinch the finished note and play it onto the Keep card.
   **What you see:** The note hangs off the filter step it explains, so anyone who opens the project finds the reason next to the rule.
   **Controls:** Pinch, poke keys or voice (only while the Note panel is open), pinch, move, release.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    **What you do:** You meant to Inspect the Strozzi but played Hide; the Strozzi vanished, leaving a faint ring with its tab. Pinch the Hide card on that tab (or the "last play" chip on the fan) and pull it away.
    **What you see:** As you pinch, only the Strozzi's ring lights: that is all the lift would change. As you pull, the Strozzi return, and the Hide card is in your hand to discard. The filter, the colors, the sizes and the note stay as they were: undo removed only that card's effect, even though the Hide came after them. Had you pinched the PageRank card instead, Color by, Size by, Keep and the note would have lit as dependents; releasing before the pull leaves everything in place, and lifting it would bring all of them into your hand as one stack that a single play puts back.
    **Controls:** Pinch on a played card, pull (lift).

11. **Save.**
    **What you do:** Nothing is needed to keep the work: the project is kept after every play. To mark this point, pinch the name plate, take "Save version", and play it onto the name plate.
    **What you see:** A version card joins the row reading "Kept 5, colored by PageRank", the text taken from the cards before it (open it to rename it by poking). Reopening the project lays every played card back on its object. Lifting the version card removes that named point, not the work; it comes back to your hand, and playing it again restores it, like any other card.
    **Controls:** Pinch, move, release.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Two filter cards in the row are an AND: play Keep top 5 onto the PageRank card and "Keep at least 3 marriages" onto a Degree result card, and the graph shows families that pass both. For an OR, play the "or" modifier onto the second Keep card: it joins that card to the filter just before it in the row, a bracket links the two, and the second card's face reads "or at least 3 marriages". Any clause is removed by lifting its card, and the ghost shows the families that would come back before you pull.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Play "damping 0.7" onto the played PageRank card: the ghost shows the new ranking, and on release the run is redone, its face updates, and the cards that hang off it are redone in turn. Values not in the deal come from typing in the draw pile ("damping 0.72" mints that modifier). Options with a fixed set of choices come as one modifier per choice ("weighted by marriages", "personalized from selection").
- **Comparing two runs.** Play a second PageRank card with damping 0.5 onto the name plate, then play "Compare with previous" onto it. Its face lists the families whose rank changed, with arrows, and while it is held the ghost colors the graph by the difference. Lifting the Compare card ends the comparison and leaves both runs.
- **Layers on subsets.** Pinch the Medici and their six partners into the selection card, then play the selection card onto Color by in the fan (or onto a played Color by). The style layer paints only those seven families; the copy of the selection card hangs off the style card, so the scope is visible and liftable. Play "off" onto a style card to hide the layer without losing it; lift "off" to show it again.
- **Recipes.** Play Copy onto the PageRank card: a recipe card "PageRank 0.85, color by, size by, keep top 5" comes into your hand and into the draw pile's recipes. Played onto another project's name plate, it ghosts the whole set first, then lays the cards down one by one, so each part can still be lifted alone.
- **Joining tables.** Stays on the 2D page: choosing a file and matching its columns to node ids is form work. The joined columns then come into VR as new "Color by <column>", "Size by <column>" and "Keep where <column>" cards in the deal for the graph.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog.** Only six or so are dealt at once; the rest live in the draw pile, found by typing, by a spoken word, or by a category shown when the pile opens. A drawn card stays in your fan until you discard it, so a session's own working set builds up there. Each algorithm's card plays only onto the object types it takes (a graph, a node, a selection holding a pair of nodes), so the lighting tells you where it can go before you learn it.
- **New object types.** A new object (a layout run, an imported table, a saved view) brings a row in the deal table and a list of target types on each card that accepts it. The vocabulary does not grow: it is taken, aimed and played onto like everything else, and a card played on it sits on it.
- **Plugins.** A plugin algorithm or layout becomes a verb card from its catalog entry, with its default settings frozen on the face, plus one modifier card per option choice and a handful of preset values per number; any other value is minted by typing in the draw pile. Plugins ship cards; they never ship new gestures.
- **Bigger graphs.** Hundreds of nodes make each node a smaller drop target. The armed-target border and ghost show exactly which node a card will land on, and a search or selection card brings a named node to the front; past a few hundred nodes, scoping by selection card replaces aiming at single nodes.
- **The limit.** Continuous tuning: anything you want to drag until it looks right (layout forces, a color ramp's midpoint, a time slider) becomes a run of separate plays, each redoing the effect. A busy node with many cards is read through its count chip in the row, and an analysis of 40 plays makes a long row. Past roughly a dozen cards on one object, a 2D page or a dial (Palette Hand, Tool Belt) does the job better.

### Why it should work

- **Card games already taught verb-first targeting to millions.** Hearthstone and Slay the Spire both have you pick a card, drag it, see the valid targets light and the effect previewed on the target (Slay the Spire shows the damage number on the enemy before you release), and release to commit. The skill transfers directly.
- **The record on the object has a shipped analytics precedent.** Tableau's shelves hold the whole analysis as visible pills; dragging a pill off its shelf removes just that part of the view. People learn that without instruction. Here the shelf is the object itself.
- **Live preview before commit is proven to cut trial and error.** Office's Live Preview and the hover previews in Figma and Photoshop let people see a style before applying it; the ghost on hover is the same thing on the graph.
- **Selective undo matches how analysts talk about mistakes.** People say "take that filter off", not "undo three steps". Research on selective and regional undo (Berlage 1994; later work on regional undo in editors) found that being able to undo one earlier action while keeping later ones is wanted and understandable when the dependencies are visible, which the lighting on pinch provides. Because a lifted card carries its dependents and a single play restores them, a lift is never a loss.
- **An off-hand palette is a settled XR pattern.** Tilt Brush's off-hand palette and the palm-up hand menus of MRTK and Quest's own system UI show that holding a command surface in the non-dominant hand while the dominant hand works is comfortable and fast for short sessions; the ledge covers the longer ones.
- **Discrete settings are easier to read back than continuous ones.** A card that says "damping 0.7" can be read by anyone later; a dial position cannot. HyperCard and recipe cards showed that a stored action on a card is a unit people can collect, copy and share.

### What it would teach us

- Will analysts accept choosing the verb before the target, once the object they pinched has dealt the verbs? Does the ghost preview make choosing the target last feel natural?
- Does leaving cards on their objects make the state of an analysis readable at a glance, or does it clutter the graph past usefulness? At what count of cards per object does it break?
- Is per-object undo understood when effects depend on each other (lifting PageRank takes its colors with it)? Do people read the lighting before they pull, and do they discover that playing the lifted stack again restores it?
- Can parameter entry live without any continuous control? How often do people want a slider, and for which settings?
- Is a search or selection card turning the graph to its node helpful, or does an object turning on its own disorient people?
- Is a deal by working object predictable, or do people lose a card when the fan re-deals?
- Can card faces be read at arm's length on each headset, and how short must the text be? Do people use the open-hand read, or try to pinch a played card to read it?
- How long do people keep the fan on the hand before moving it to the ledge?

### Known risks

- **Clutter of played cards.** Many cards on one node hide the node. Mitigation: a played card on a node collapses to a small tab showing a count; it opens only when your open hand comes near it, and every node's cards can also be opened and lifted from its count chip in the row.
- **The graph turning on its own causes discomfort.** Mitigation: only a card that names a node turns the graph, only after it has been held still for a moment, only about the vertical axis, at a capped speed; it never moves the graph toward or away from you and can be switched off. The rim always works.
- **Accidental plays when a pinch drops.** Hand tracking sometimes reports a release you did not make. Mitigation: a play needs the card held over a lit target until its border fills; a release before that, or elsewhere, returns the card; and any play is undone by lifting the card or the "last play" chip.
- **Reaching the back of the graph and far tabs.** At 75 cm, the far side of the graph is out of reach. Mitigation: turn the rim, bring a node forward with a search or selection card, lift node cards from the row's count chips, and on Vision Pro take far nodes with look and pinch.
- **Exact numbers are awkward.** Typing a value into the draw pile is slower than a slider for exploration. Mitigation: deal relative modifiers (top 5, top quarter, above mean) that cover most needs; mint exact values by typing; measure how often people need them.
- **Slow previews.** A ghost of a run means running it before you commit, which is slow on a big graph. Mitigation: style and filter cards over existing values preview live; a run card ghosts its result only when it is fast, and otherwise shows the estimated time, and a run started by the ghost is kept so the play is instant.
- **Lifting a lower card surprises people.** Taking off PageRank removes everything built on it. Mitigation: everything that would change lights the moment you pinch, the lift happens only after a 4 cm pull, letting go earlier cancels, and the lifted card brings its dependents into your hand so one play puts them all back.
- **The fan re-deals while you reach.** A card can move from under your fingers. Mitigation: the deal changes only when your dominant hand is empty and away from the fan.
- **Gorilla arm, one-handed and seated use.** Holding the off hand up is tiring, and some people have one usable hand. Mitigation: palm down puts the fan on the ledge in front of you, within reach seated or standing, and one-handed mode keeps it there. Turning needs one hand; the only two-handed act is scaling, which a "Focus" card from the draw pile replaces (played onto a node or selection, it frames it; lifting it restores the view).
- **Text panels need a free hand.** You cannot type while pinching a card. Mitigation: taking the Note card or opening the draw pile puts a panel on the ledge and leaves your hand empty to poke; you pinch the finished note to carry it.
