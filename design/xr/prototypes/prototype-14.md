**Prototype 14: Voodoo Dolls** -- Frame part of the graph between thumb and finger to pull a hand-size copy of it into your hand, then work by touching copies together and by how you hold and move each one, while the real graph stays put.

A doll is not a picture of the graph: it is a live handle on the part of the real graph it stands for. Whatever you do to a doll happens to that part of the real graph, and the real graph is never grabbed, turned or flown through. Moving dolls only proposes; nothing changes in the real graph until a fingertip presses a card.

**Setup.** Solid colored background in full VR. The real graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away and never moves. A whole-graph doll waits at a fixed home spot below the real graph's near edge on your off-hand side, at lap height when seated, in reach without leaning. Hands are the primary input on every device. Controllers on Quest and Galaxy XR map to the same vocabulary, and so do PS VR2 Sense controllers on Vision Pro if Safari exposes them. Eyes help only on Vision Pro, where looking at a node and pinching picks it. Voice is optional and only fills text fields; the page's own floating keyboard always works.

### Key structures

**Kinds of doll.** Every object in graphty can become a doll; the figure shows what it is.

| Doll                   | Pulled from                                                                                               | What it looks like in the hand                                                                                                |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Whole graph            | Always out, at its home spot; also by framing the whole graph or pinching another whole-graph doll's base | The whole graph, scaled to about 30 cm, on a base labeled with its counts and its ranking measure                             |
| Region (any selection) | Framing the real graph, or from another doll                                                              | The nodes and edges of the region on a small base labeled with its count and its ranking measure                              |
| Node or edge           | Framing nearly closed over it, or a pinch on it                                                           | The element with stubs of its edges                                                                                           |
| Group                  | Folding a region doll                                                                                     | One group node, with its count                                                                                                |
| Run (a result)         | Running an algorithm                                                                                      | What the algorithm returns: ranked values as a column of names highest at the top, communities as clusters, a path as a chain |
| Step                   | Pinching a line out of a doll's Steps list                                                                | A short label of the action ("Filter: PageRank 0.075 or more")                                                                |
| Style layer            | Pinching a line out of a doll's Layers list                                                               | The layer's name and a swatch of what it paints                                                                               |
| Option                 | Pinching an option off a run's Options list                                                               | The option's name and number on a bar about 20 cm long                                                                        |
| Recipe                 | Pinching the header of a Steps list                                                                       | The list of steps as a strip                                                                                                  |
| Note                   | Pinching a note off an element                                                                            | The note's text                                                                                                               |

**Verbs in the gap (two dolls).** Algorithms and actions that take two inputs appear here, chosen by the kinds of the two dolls, most likely first.

| Two dolls                      | Verbs in the gap                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Node + node                    | Shortest path; all paths up to k hops (tether); common neighbors; maximum flow                            |
| Node + region                  | Neighbors in the region within k hops (tether); path into the region                                      |
| Region + region                | New region: either, both, first but not second; edges between them                                        |
| Two proposed stretches (rules) | Keep both; keep either; keep first but not second (one filter step)                                       |
| Run + region                   | Color by; size by; color and size by; rank by; apply layout (layout runs); select (a community or a path) |
| Run + run                      | Compare (who moved, by how much); correlation                                                             |
| Option + run                   | Rerun with this value; run a copy with this value                                                         |
| Layer + layer                  | Put above; put below                                                                                      |
| Layer + region                 | Apply only here                                                                                           |
| Recipe + region                | Replay here                                                                                               |
| Note + anything                | Attach the note here                                                                                      |

Running an algorithm never changes the graph; a run's result reaches the graph only through a gap verb you press.

**Tiles on the base (one doll).** Every verb of the doll, including a tile for each motion (Grow for shake, Group for fold, Discard for the throw) for anyone who cannot make the motion. Each motion tile shows its gesture.

| Doll         | Tiles                                                                                                           |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Region       | Run (the catalog of one-input algorithms); Rank by; Find; Grow; Group; Note; Steps; Layers; Undo; Redo; Discard |
| Whole graph  | The region tiles, plus Save and Load. Its Undo, Redo and Steps cover the whole project.                         |
| Node or edge | Run from here; Grow; Note; Pin; Steps; Undo; Redo; Discard                                                      |
| Group        | Ungroup; Note; Steps; Undo; Redo; Discard                                                                       |
| Run          | Options; Rerun; Note; Steps; Discard                                                                            |
| Step         | Edit (stretch again or retype its value); Note; Discard                                                         |
| Style layer  | Hide or show; Note; Discard                                                                                     |

Discard does the same as the throw, with the same preview while your fingertip rests on it. Undone steps stay in the Steps list, crossed out, until something new replaces them; Redo or pressing one brings it back. Load saves the current project first.

### The journey

1. **Enter**
    - **What you do:** Put on the headset, open graphty with the Florentine families loaded, press Enter VR, and stand or sit facing the graph. Frame a single dot once so the page learns your dominant eye.
    - **What you see:** The 15 families as nodes with names, at chest height about 75 cm away, on a solid background. Your hands appear, and so does the whole-graph doll at its home spot by your off hand. As your hand rises between your eye and the graph with thumb and finger apart, an outline on the real graph shows what lies between them.
    - **Controls:** One calibration frame; the outline teaches framing before you do it.

2. **Get oriented**
    - **What you do:** Pick up the whole-graph doll in your off hand. Turn it to see the graph from the side and from behind; bring it toward your eyes to read the crowded middle.
    - **What you see:** The real graph does not move. While you hold the doll, the real graph is outlined to show that the doll stands for all of it. The base reads "15 families, 20 marriages".
    - **Controls:** Hold; turn; bring close.

3. **Find the Medici**
    - **What you do:** Hold thumb and finger nearly closed over the Medici on the real graph and close them, or pinch the Medici inside the whole-graph doll and pull. On a large graph, turn the whole-graph doll over, press Find and say or type "Medici"; the found node's doll drops into your free hand.
    - **What you see:** A Medici doll in your dominant hand: the node with six edge stubs. The Medici node lights on the real graph.
    - **Controls:** Frame and close, or pinch and pull out; or turn over and press Find.

4. **Who they married into**
    - **What you do:** Shake the Medici doll once.
    - **What you see:** The doll grows a ring as Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni join it. The real graph lights those six and the Medici. The base reads "Medici and 6 neighbors, 1 hop". A second shake would add the second ring (the Medici and 11 other families within 2 hops); the doll's Undo tile takes a ring back.
    - **Controls:** Shake.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank)**
    - **What you do:** Toss the Medici doll toward the real graph to put it away. Turn the whole-graph doll over, press Run, and press PageRank. PageRank sits near the top of the list as a common choice; the list scrolls with a fingertip and has search.
    - **What you see:** The run finishes and a PageRank run doll appears in your free hand: the 15 families as a ranked column. The run is also listed under Steps on the whole-graph doll's base. Nothing on the real graph has changed yet.
    - **Controls:** Toss toward the graph; turn over; press tiles.

6. **Read the result**
    - **What you do:** Bring the run doll toward your eyes. Rest a fingertip on the top entries.
    - **What you see:** Medici first (about 0.145), then Guadagni (about 0.098) and Strozzi (about 0.088). Each entry you touch lights on the real graph, so you see where the top families sit.
    - **Controls:** Bring close; fingertip touch.

7. **Color and size by PageRank**
    - **What you do:** Touch the run doll to the whole-graph doll. Cards for "Color by", "Size by", "Color and size by" and "Rank by" appear in the gap. Open the hand holding the run doll so the two stay docked, rest a fingertip on "Color and size by", then press it.
    - **What you see:** While your fingertip rests on the card, the whole-graph doll and the real graph preview the new colors and sizes. After you press, they stay, and the Medici are largest. The whole-graph doll's base now reads "ranked by PageRank": a doll with no ranking measure takes the first measure it is colored or sized by, and Rank by changes it. A new style layer is listed under Layers.
    - **Controls:** Touch two dolls; open a hand; rest a fingertip; press a card.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter)**
    - **What you do:** Take the whole-graph doll in both hands and pull them apart. At the count you want, open one hand and press the card that appears.
    - **What you see:** The doll lines its families up by PageRank along the stretch, and the lowest drop out one at a time. Each step snaps to the gap between two families' values, so the cut never sits on a meaningless number. The real graph fades the dropped families as a preview. At "PageRank 0.075 or more: 4 of 15" (Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi) you open one hand; the stretch holds and a "Keep 4 of 15" card shows on the base. Pressing it makes the filter step.
    - **Controls:** Stretch; open one hand; press the card (returning to rest length cancels).

9. **Write a note**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the Medici out of the whole-graph doll. Turn the Medici doll over, press Note, and dictate "Bridge between the oligarch and new families", or type it on the floating keyboard. Toss the doll toward the real graph.
    - **What you see:** The note appears beside the Medici on the real graph and in every doll that contains the Medici.
    - **Controls:** Pinch and pull out; turn over; press Note; voice or keyboard; toss toward the graph.

10. **Undo a mistake**
    - **What you do:** Suppose your stretch in step 8 went too far and kept only Medici and Guadagni. Turn the whole-graph doll over and press Steps. Pinch the filter step out of the list, raise it above your shoulder, read what it will undo, and throw it back. Then stretch again to 4 of 15 and press Keep. (Seated or with limited shoulder movement, rest a fingertip on the step doll's Discard tile to see the same list, then press it; or press Undo on the whole-graph doll.)
    - **What you see:** Above your shoulder the step doll reads "Undo: Filter PageRank 0.12 or more. Nothing depends on it." After the throw the filter is gone and all 15 families are back; the color and size stay, because they were a separate step. The thrown step stays in the Steps list, crossed out, so Redo or pressing it would bring it back. The new stretch replaces it.
    - **Controls:** Turn over; press Steps; pinch and pull out; raise and throw, or Discard; stretch; press.

11. **Save**
    - **What you do:** Turn the whole-graph doll over and press Save.
    - **What you see:** A short confirmation on the base: "Saved: Florentine families, 3 steps, 1 note". Toss the doll toward the real graph and it returns to its home spot, clearing your hands.
    - **Controls:** Turn over; press Save; toss toward the graph.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** A stretched doll you have not pressed Keep on is a rule. Pinch the whole-graph doll's base to pull a copy, so you have two. Stretch one, ranked by PageRank, to keep the top 5, and open a hand without pressing Keep; park it. On the second, press Rank by, choose degree, and stretch to keep families with 3 or more marriages. Touch the two rule dolls together and the gap offers "Keep both", "Keep either" and "Keep first but not second". Rest a fingertip on Keep both to preview it on the real graph, then press it: one filter step keeps only the families that pass both rules.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Turn the PageRank run doll over and press Options. Pinch "damping 0.85" off the list into an option doll and stretch it to 0.70; it moves in detents of 0.05, or steps of 0.01 when you bring it toward your eyes. Touch the option doll to the run doll and the gap offers "Rerun with 0.70" and "Run a copy with 0.70". Resting on Rerun previews what the colors and sizes built on this run would become.
- **Comparing two runs.** Run a copy with the new damping, then touch the two run dolls together. The first card in the gap is Compare. Its preview shows which families moved up or down and by how much. Press it to keep the comparison as its own run doll, ranked by how far each family moved.
- **Layers on subsets.** Frame the Medici's neighborhood, or shake a Medici doll once, and touch the PageRank run doll to that region doll. "Color by" now makes a style layer that applies only to those seven families. Folding the region doll and pressing Group collapses them into one group node, and the group carries the layer. To change the order of layers, pinch two lines out of the Layers list and touch them together: "Put above" or "Put below".
- **Recipes.** Turn a doll over, press Steps, and pinch the list's header: the whole sequence comes out as a recipe doll. Touch it to a whole-graph doll of another dataset and the gap offers "Replay here", previewing each step before you press. A recipe doll saved with the project can be pulled again later from Load.
- **Joining tables.** This stays on the 2D page, because choosing a file and the matching columns needs reading and typing that dolls do not help with. Once a table is joined, its columns appear under Rank by and in Run, and they can drive stretches and colors in VR.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog.** An algorithm's inputs decide where it appears, so no menu is built by hand. One-input algorithms are listed under Run on the base of the matching kind of doll: whole-graph and region dolls get centrality, community and layout, and node dolls get "from here" algorithms such as single-source shortest paths. Two-input algorithms appear in the gap of the matching pair, such as node + node for paths, flow and similarity. The Run list shows recent and pinned algorithms first, then search by voice or keyboard. Options are pinched off as option dolls. What an algorithm returns (scores, groups, a path, positions) decides which gap verbs its run doll offers.
- **New object types.** A new kind of object, such as a new kind of filter step, a table or a comparison, needs a doll figure, its base tiles, and the list of pairs it joins. Shake, stretch, fold, turn over, put away and throw keep their meanings on it, and a motion that has no meaning for it does nothing visibly.
- **Plugins.** A plugin algorithm declares its inputs and outputs, as every catalog entry does, and lands on the right base or in the right gap automatically. A plugin's two-object action, for example "align these two graphs", appears in the gap of its pair.
- **The limit.** A 30 cm doll of thousands of nodes is unreadable, so on a large graph you pull dolls from dolls, two or three levels deep. A verb with three or more inputs needs two dolls combined first. More than about six parked dolls becomes clutter. Many steps take two presses (open a hand, then press a card) where releasing could have been one. A region base carries a dozen tiles. Typing anything longer than a search term is slow without voice.

### Why it should work

- In Pierce, Stearns and Pausch's Voodoo Dolls (1999), people worked on distant objects by holding hand-size copies in two hands, with one copy as the reference frame for the other. They could act on objects of very different sizes without moving the view.
- Image-plane selection (Pierce et al., 1997) picks distant objects by framing them as you see them, with no ray to aim. Framing a region between thumb and finger is that work's "head crusher" technique.
- Worlds in Miniature (Stoakley, Conway and Pausch, 1995) showed that people readily understand that acting on a hand-held copy changes the full-size world.
- Mine, Brooks and Sequin (1997) proposed actions relative to the body, including deleting an object by throwing it over your shoulder, because proprioception finds the space around your own body without looking. Half-Life: Alyx (2020) taught a large audience to reach over the shoulder for an inventory.
- Many graph questions are relational: a path between two families, a comparison of two runs, the overlap of two groups. Bringing the two things together is the question itself, and the gap shows only verbs that fit that pair.
- Direct touch on a doll held at 35 to 45 cm is steadier than a ray aimed at small targets 75 cm away, where hand-tracking jitter is magnified. Enlarging the doll as it nears the eyes adds precision without a finer gesture and without focusing on something too close.
- Asymmetric two-handed work (Guiard, 1987) fits the doll: one hand holds the frame of reference while the other touches and pinches inside it. A parked doll can take the holding hand's place, so the same verbs work one-handed.
- Motions only propose and a fingertip press does, so trying a stretch, a pairing or a fold is safe, a dropped hand or a tracking loss never commits anything, and the common VR habit of pulling an object apart to enlarge it costs nothing.

### What it would teach us

- Do people understand a doll as a live handle on the real graph, rather than a copy they are editing on the side?
- Does placing verbs by how many objects they take (one on the base, two in the gap) make algorithms easier to find than a catalog organized by family?
- How accurate is image-plane framing with consumer hand tracking at 75 cm, compared with a ray or a lasso, and how much does eye dominance throw it off? How often do people frame at all once the whole-graph doll is always at hand?
- Is per-doll history clearer than one global undo stack, or do people lose track of which doll did what?
- Is stretching a ranked doll an accurate way to set a threshold, and do the snaps between values help or annoy?
- Does pressing a card after a motion feel slow next to committing on release, and do people miss the one-motion version?
- Do people miss moving the view, or does pulling dolls cover every reason to navigate?
- How often do shakes, stretches and throws fire by accident during ordinary gesturing?
- How many dolls do people keep parked, and where do they put them?

### Known risks

- **Framing parallax.** What lies between thumb and finger depends on which eye you use. Mitigation: show the framed region on the real graph before closing, learn eye dominance from one frame at entry, pick inside the whole-graph doll instead, and on Vision Pro pick single nodes by looking and pinching.
- **System gestures.** A pinch with the palm toward your face opens the system menu on Quest and Galaxy XR, and an upturned palm brings up Home on Vision Pro; pages cannot block either. Mitigation: framing is palm away, turning over is palm down, and holding a doll in a fist is always allowed.
- **Accidental shakes.** Ordinary hand waving could add neighbors. Mitigation: require two quick reversals above a speed threshold, let users with a tremor turn shaking off and use Grow, and make the doll's Undo tile take a hop back. A shake never changes the graph, only what the doll stands for.
- **Throwing undoes more than intended, and is hard to track and to make.** A hand behind the head leaves tracking, and seated people or those with limited shoulder movement cannot throw. Mitigation: the raised doll lists every step it will undo, including dependents, before the throw; the throw counts while the hand is still tracked; undone steps stay crossed out and Redo brings them back; Discard and Undo tiles do the same with a fingertip.
- **Hands hide each other** when two dolls touch or a doll is folded. Mitigation: dolls touch at their edges, so the hands holding them stay about 30 cm apart; folding stops short of the hands touching; opening a hand never commits, so a lost frame is harmless; controllers for long sessions.
- **Clutter.** Many parked dolls crowd the space. Mitigation: when more than four are parked they shrink and line up to one side, tossing a doll toward the graph puts it away, and the whole-graph doll shows how many dolls are out.
- **Which measure a stretch uses.** Mitigation: the base always names its ranking measure, and the stretch shows the cut value and the count kept.
- **Dense regions are unreadable at 30 cm.** Mitigation: pull dolls from dolls; labels appear only as the doll nears the eyes.
- **Eye strain from close objects.** Text held closer than about 35 cm is tiring to focus on in a headset. Mitigation: the doll enlarges as it approaches, so labels are readable at arm's half-length.
- **Arm fatigue** from holding dolls up and framing at arm's length. Mitigation: parked dolls stay where they are left, so the arms can drop; the whole-graph doll sits at lap height; everything except framing and the throw works with elbows at the sides.
- **Shaking means undo on phones.** People may expect a shake to undo. Mitigation: the first shake shows its effect clearly, and undo is never a shake.
