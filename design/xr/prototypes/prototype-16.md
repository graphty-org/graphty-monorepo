**Prototype 16: Proofreader's Slate** -- A stylus on a slate that shows the graph flat and unoccluded; you edit it with proofreader's marks, and the ink is a script you can run again.

**Setup:** solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height 70-80 cm away. The slate is about 40 by 30 cm, landscape, like a large drawing tablet, so margin text stays legible on current headsets. It lies on a real desk, or on a rigid board (a clipboard, a tray, a large book) on the lap, which gives real touch under the nib. Standing, or with no surface, it floats fixed in place at waist height, tilted toward you like a lectern; it can also ride in the off hand like a clipboard, at the cost of arm fatigue and no real touch. The nib is a tracked stylus (Logitech MX Ink on Quest), a controller held like a pen, or the dominant index fingertip. No voice. No air strokes. Pen buttons and pressure are deliberately unused, so the vocabulary is the same on every device and every task works with one hand.

### The control vocabulary

One meaning per input, everywhere. The dominant hand's nib inks, unless the stroke starts on the frame, where it moves the view. The off hand never inks.

| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

Marks and notes go here. |
| Margin (right third; mirrored to the left for left-handed users)                            | One reading per step, numbered top to bottom: the script. Every object has its verbs through the same path: circle it or start a leader on it, and the margin word completes only from that object's verbs. Nodes, edges and selections live on the plate; runs and results are galleys; filter steps, style layers and notes are readings; the project is the title line.

### The journey

1. **Enter**
    - **What you do:** Put on the headset and pick up the pen. At a desk, touch the nib to three points on it once; the slate lies down there. On the lap, do the same on a board. Standing, the slate appears as a lectern at waist height.
    - **What you see:** The Florentine families graph floating in front of you; the same 15 families drawn flat on the slate's plate; an empty margin with the page title "Untitled".
    - **Controls:** None beyond picking up the pen; the three-tap calibration if there is a surface.

2. **Get oriented**
    - **What you do:** Start a stroke on the left ruler and pull it into the plate to pan. Slide along the bottom ruler to turn the big graph a quarter turn. Hover the nib over a few nodes.
    - **What you see:** The big graph pans and turns to match the plate's framing; the plate re-flattens after the turn. Each hovered family lights in the big graph, and its name, degree and wealth appear in the margin.
    - **Controls:** Pull from a ruler (pan), slide along the bottom ruler (turn), hover.

3. **Find the Medici**
    - **What you do:** Circle the Medici node on the plate.
    - **What you see:** The circle is redrawn as a neat ring; the margin reads "1 select: Medici (1 node)"; the Medici light in the big graph.
    - **Controls:** Circle. (In a large graph: write "find" in the margin, then "Medici"; completion offers node labels after "find", and the found node is circled for you.)

4. **Who they married into**
    - **What you do:** Underline the Medici label.
    - **What you see:** Six families join the selection on the plate and in the big graph; the margin reads "2 + neighbors: 6 (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni)".
    - **Controls:** Underline.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank)**
    - **What you do:** Write "pa" in the margin with no leader line, so it acts on the whole graph. Tap "pagerank" among the completions.
    - **What you see:** The reading "3 PageRank: whole graph, damping 0.85" and, under it, a galley: the 15 families ranked with their scores and a small histogram.
    - **Controls:** Margin word, tap a completion.

6. **Read the result**
    - **What you do:** Hover down the galley; pull along the margin's outer edge to scroll if it is long.
    - **What you see:** Medici first at 0.146, then Guadagni 0.098 and Strozzi 0.088. Each hovered row lights its family in the big graph and on the plate.
    - **Controls:** Hover, margin-edge scroll.

7. **Color and size by PageRank**
    - **What you do:** Draw a leader line from the galley's PageRank column header out into empty margin and write "color" (completion offers only what a result column can do). Draw a second leader from the same header and write "size".
    - **What you see:** "4 color by PageRank" and "5 size by PageRank"; the big graph and the plate recolor and resize, Medici largest.
    - **Controls:** Leader line, margin word.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter)**
    - **What you do:** Draw a vertical cut line across the galley's histogram near the low end. It reads 0.060, which would keep Salviati (0.061). Keep the nib down and slide it up along the cut line, away from the histogram; the cut now creeps at a quarter speed, and you ease it to 0.065. Lift, then draw a delete loop through the bars left of the cut. (Or strike "0.060" in the cut's label and write "0.065", or tap it and pick 0.065 from the nearby values.)
    - **What you see:** The reading "6 hide: PageRank below 0.065 (6 nodes)". Salviati, Barbadori, Pazzi, Ginori, Lamberteschi and Acciaiuoli fade out of both views; nine families remain.
    - **Controls:** Cut line, slide along it for fine movement, delete loop (or strike and write a value, or tap a nearby value).

9. **Write a note**
    - **What you do:** Write "marriage hub; bank" in open space on the plate and draw a short leader from it to the Medici.
    - **What you see:** Your handwriting is set neatly beside the Medici on the plate and pinned to the Medici in the big graph; the margin reads "7 note on Medici".
    - **Controls:** Words on the plate, leader line.

10. **Undo a mistake**
    - **What you do:** Trying a thought, you loop out Strozzi, and the margin reads "8 hide: Strozzi (1 node)". You want Strozzi back. Strike through that reading. (Struck the wrong one? Write "stet" beside it and it comes back.)
    - **What you see:** The reading and the loop on the plate are both shown struck, Strozzi returns in both views, and every later step replays without it.
    - **Controls:** Strike through a reading; "stet" to restore.

11. **Save**
    - **What you do:** Write "Florentine power" on the title line, then write "save" in the margin.
    - **What you see:** "saved: Florentine power, 8 steps, 1 struck". The page, its ink and the graph state are the project. If a project by that name already exists, the reading stays gray with "replace saved Florentine power?" and an "apply" to tap.
    - **Controls:** Title line, margin word, tap to confirm a replace.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule:** write "wealth" in the margin to get its histogram, then cut it and loop out the low side. Two hide steps in a row are AND. For OR, bracket the two readings in the margin and write "or" on the bracket; the reading becomes "hide: PageRank below 0.065 or wealth below 40".
- **Tuning algorithm options:** the PageRank reading lists its options in the margin ("damping 0.85, iterations 100"). Strike 0.85 and write 0.7 beside it; the step reruns and everything after it replays. To set an option that is not shown, write its name and a number on the reading's line; completion offers only that algorithm's option names.
- **Comparing two runs:** with the caret, insert a second "pagerank" step with damping 0.7. Draw a line from one galley's header to the other's, then write "compare" on that line (it lies in the margin, so the word is a command). A third galley lists each family's two ranks and the change, largest change first.
- **Path between two nodes:** draw a line from one node to another on the plate, then a leader from that line out to the margin, and write "path". A word written on the plate itself would be a note.
- **Layers on subsets:** circle a group, lead it to the margin and write "color", then write the color name or a number on the same line. The layer applies only to that circle. Swap marks between two style readings reorder which paints on top.
- **Recipes:** bracket a run of readings (say, PageRank, color, size, cut) and write a name on the bracket. On any other project, write that name in the margin, with a leader for a scope or none for the whole graph. The steps arrive as gray, proposed readings and the big graph previews their result; tap "apply" under them to run them. Each step then stands as its own reading, which you can strike, renumber or retune like any other. Steps that picked nodes by hand stay out of a recipe; a "find" step with a rule travels.
- **Joining tables:** stays on the 2D page of the graphty app. Matching columns by handwriting is slower and riskier than a table on a screen. Once saved as a recipe, the join can be run from the slate by its name.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog:** there is no menu to outgrow. Any verb is a word, and completion after two letters narrows it to five candidates. Writing "?" in the margin opens the catalog as a galley; how it is grouped and ordered is the app's choice, and tapping a row writes the word for you, so a whole session can be done with circles, leaders and taps.
- **New object types:** anything that shows up on the plate or in the margin can be circled and led to a margin word. Completion offers only the verbs valid for the object at the start of the leader, so new types add words, not marks.
- **Plugins:** a plugin's algorithms, layouts and formats arrive with their plain names and options, so they can be written and completed at once. Plugins add words, never marks: the set of about ten marks stays fixed.
- **The limit:** the plate is flat. Graphs of thousands of nodes need the plate to show clusters that you circle and zoom into. Tasks that are truly about depth (3D layout, spatial structure) belong to the big graph, which this scheme only lets you watch. Writing is slower than pointing for one-off picks, so a session of many small unrelated commands favors a pointing scheme.

### Why it should work

- **Real touch makes strokes accurate.** Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn (1999) found that a physical surface under virtual widgets made manipulation faster and more accurate. Arora et al. (CHI 2017) measured drawing in VR and found strokes much more accurate against a physical surface than in mid-air. The slate is built to sit on one.
- **Pen and pad in VR is old and sound.** The Personal Interaction Panel (Szalavari and Gervautz, 1997) and the Virtual Notepad (Poupyrev et al., 1998) put a tracked pen and pad in VR for commands and handwriting.
- **Editing marks have shipped and people use them.** Microsoft 365's Ink Editor (strike through to delete, caret to insert, circle to select) and Apple's Scribble on iPad (scratch out to delete, circle to select, a vertical line to split or join) teach almost this exact mark set.
- **Pen acts, the other hand moves.** Hinckley et al., "Pen + Touch = New Tools" (UIST 2010), showed that people naturally write with the pen and manipulate with the other hand. Here the off hand only moves the view, and the frame lets the pen hand move it too when the off hand is busy or absent.
- **Sliding away for finer control is familiar.** Media scrubbing on iOS slowed the scrubber to half, quarter and fine speed as the finger slid away from the bar; the cut line works the same way.
- **Ink as a command that stays.** InkSeine (Hinckley et al., 2007) kept search ink as part of the notes. Scriboli (Hinckley et al., 2005) used a drawn delimiter to separate scope from command, as the leader line does here. Sketchpad (Sutherland, 1963) put the pen on the model itself.
- **A flat view removes occlusion.** Picking in a dense 3D node-link drawing fights overlap and depth. On the plate every node has its own spot, and the big graph keeps the 3D view for looking.
- **A shipped stylus exists for the target headset.** Logitech MX Ink works with Quest 3 and 3S and is used by design apps on that headset, so a pen is not a lab prop.
- **The record is readable.** A marked-up page shows what was done, in order, to whoever reads it next, the way a proof does for an editor.

### What it would teach us

- Does working on a flat, unoccluded view while watching a 3D mirror beat acting on the 3D graph directly for analysis tasks, in time and in errors?
- How much does the real surface matter? Test the same slate on a desk, on a board on the lap, and as a lectern with nothing under it.
- Is handwriting with completion fast enough for verbs compared with picking from a menu, and after how many letters do people stop writing and tap?
- Do people go back and edit earlier steps on the page (strike, retune, reorder), or only add at the bottom?
- Can about ten marks plus words carry a whole session, and what share of commands are marks rather than words?
- How often do people look up at the big graph, and does working head-down on the slate cost them the 3D structure?
- Is sliding along the cut line for finer movement learnable, or do people strike and write the number instead?
- Do frame-start strokes for the view ever get mistaken for ink, or ink for a view move?
- Will analysts reuse their pages as recipes, and can someone else read a page they did not write?

### Known risks

- **Recognition errors.** Mitigation: a small mark set chosen to be distinct in shape and in place (an underline is below a label and only on the plate, a strike is through a reading in the margin, a swap mark must span two lines); gray ink with up to three candidate readings to tap; marks redrawn neatly so a misreading is visible at once.
- **Marks read out of handwriting** (a looped letter in a note crossing a node and reading as a delete loop). Mitigation: a stroke that starts within a short time and distance of the previous stroke is read as part of the same word, never as a mark; a delete loop must be one stroke with a pigtail at its end; any hide is a filter step, undone by one strike.
- **Neck and arm fatigue from working head-down, or from holding the slate up.** Mitigation: rest the slate on a desk or a board, or use the lectern; let the slate tilt toward the face; at a desk the slate and the big graph both fit in one view, so a glance up is an eye movement more than a head movement; holding the slate in the hand is optional, never required.
- **Drift between the virtual slate and the real surface,** so the nib "touches" a little early or late. Mitigation: MX Ink's tip sensor reports true contact and re-anchors the plane on every touch; a hover band with a tick at contact; recalibrate with three taps.
- **No pen on Galaxy XR and Vision Pro.** Mitigation: a fingertip or a controller as the nib, a larger margin writing area, completion after two letters, taps on nearby values instead of written numbers, and a vocabulary that never needed pressure or pen buttons.
- **Handwriting is hard for some people** (tremor, limited grip, an injured writing hand). Mitigation: every verb can be reached by "?" and a tap, every number by tapping nearby values, and the whole scheme works with one hand; the slate mirrors for either hand.
- **Text too small to read on a headset.** Mitigation: a 40 by 30 cm slate, readings that wrap, and text never smaller than about 0.6 degrees of view; zooming the plate never shrinks the margin.
- **Two views to reconcile.** People may lose track of which plate node is which in the big graph, especially where the plate fans overlapping nodes apart. Mitigation: hover lights the same object in both; the big graph always follows the plate's framing and turn.
- **People who do not know proofreader's marks.** Mitigation: the mark card on the back of the slate and in the "?" galley; most marks match Word's and iPad's ink editing; anything can be written as a word instead.
- **Dense graphs on a flat plate.** Mitigation: clusters drawn as single marks until zoomed; overlapping nodes fanned apart; zoom with the ruler.
- **Removing an early step breaks later ones** (striking PageRank leaves color and size with nothing to read). Mitigation: those readings turn gray and say what they are missing; "stet" brings the struck step back and they run again.
