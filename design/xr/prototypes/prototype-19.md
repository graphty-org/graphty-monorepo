**Prototype 19: Show Me** -- Do it by hand to one or two examples; the system guesses the rule, previews it on everything, and you correct it, before or after accepting, with counter-examples.

**Setup.** Solid colored background, full VR. The graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. The rule strip sits about 45 cm away at lower-chest height, tilted up toward you and shifted slightly to the dominant side, so it sits below the graph in your view instead of in front of it, and you reach it with forearms low (left-handed users get it mirrored). Voice is optional: it only mirrors the three strip buttons and fills in text.

- the controller ray is point

**Views and data changes.** A guess that only changes the view (bring to front, frame, move or zoom the graph) applies live as you move, so you see it while you do it; letting go keeps it, and No puts the view back. Every other guess (selection, run, style layer, filter, note, node move) is only a preview until you Accept, and a preview you leave alone is dropped, never applied, when you start another demonstration.

**The set-aside ledge.** A strip under the graph that holds whatever filters have hidden, as small ghosts, with the borderline ones first. While a hide guess is pending, the things it would hide appear there as outlines. Drag a ghost back up to say "this should not have been hidden".

**The recorder.** Tap Record, work, tap Record again. A tape is the demonstrations together with the guesses you accepted. It has input slots for the examples it started from, so you replay it by dragging it from the shelf onto new examples. Each recorded guess replays as a pending guess with its preview. Tapes are for reuse only; undo never plays a tape backward.

## The journey

1. **Enter.**
   **What you do:** Put on the headset and enter VR from the graphty page.
   **What you see:** The 15 Florentine families and their 20 marriages float in front of you. The rule strip says "Show me: pinch one or two families". The tray is empty.
   **Controls:** None.

2. **Get oriented.**
   **What you do:** Look around the graph. To see the families at the back, pinch-drag Lamberteschi out of the graph toward you and let go slowly.
   **What you see:** Because it landed outside the graph, toward you, the guess is "bring Lamberteschi to the front". It is a view change, so the graph turns and slides as you drag, over about half a second, until Lamberteschi faces you; Next guess would be "move just this node", and No would put the view back. To see the whole graph again, pinch tap empty space inside the graph's outline: the graph becomes the example and its first guess, "frame everything", applies. To frame part of it, pinch tap two families and then pinch the air with both hands and pull apart: "frame these".
   **Controls:** Pinch-drag ("put it here"), pinch tap, two-hand pull ("this much"), Next guess, No.

3. **Find the Medici.**
   **What you do:** Point across the graph; each family you aim at is outlined and shows its name (on Vision Pro the name appears when you pinch). Pinch tap the Medici.
   **What you see:** The Medici join the tray. The strip shows their facts (6 marriages), the first guess, "families married to the Medici", and beside it the next two: "families like the Medici", "frame the Medici". The six in-laws are outlined as a preview. Nothing is applied yet.
   **Controls:** Point, pinch tap.

4. **Who they married into.**
   **What you do:** With the Medici in the tray, pinch tap one in-law, Ridolfi.
   **What you see:** The guess stays "families married to the Medici", now with the reason "Ridolfi married a Medici; so did 5 others". Six families are outlined: Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni. You Accept, the selection becomes a row in the rule list, and the tray clears.
   **Controls:** Pinch tap, Accept.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
   **What you do:** Pinch tap the Medici and Pazzi. In the tray, drag the Medici's thumbnail above Pazzi's: this one matters more than that one.
   **What you see:** The first guess is [rank] [families] [by degree], with the reason "the Medici have 6 marriages, Pazzi 1". As a preview, each family rises to a height set by its degree. You tap the [by degree] chip. Its alternatives are the measures that agree with your order, each with a small preview: betweenness, PageRank, closeness, eigenvector, and "more measures" (searchable). You pick PageRank, the preview re-ranks, and you Accept. The run is added to the rule list and stays current.
   **Controls:** Pinch tap, drag in the tray, tap a chip, Accept.

6. **Read the result.**
   **What you do:** Point at families one after another.
   **What you see:** While the run is current, each family stays lifted to its PageRank height, and the top ten carry value labels. The family you aim at shows its value and place: the Medici are about 0.14, first of 15; Guadagni second; Strozzi third; Pazzi near the bottom. The count line reads "top 3: Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi". On Vision Pro, which has no hover, pinch tap a family to see its value and place in the tray, and tap it again to drop it. To make the run current again later, pinch tap its row in the rule list.
   **Controls:** Point (pinch tap on Vision Pro).

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
   **What you do:** Pinch the Medici with one hand, pinch the air with the other and pull apart; then pinch Pazzi and push your hands together. (With one hand: drag the corner handles of their tray thumbnails.) Drag the red swatch onto the Medici and the blue onto Pazzi.
   **What you see:** After the stretch, the guess is "size by PageRank", with the reason "you made the first ranked bigger and the last smaller; the PageRank run is current". Every family previews at its new size. After the swatches, the chips grow to [size and color] [blue to red] [by PageRank]. You Accept, and it becomes a style layer.
   **Controls:** Two-hand pull (or tray handle), drag a material, Accept.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
   **What you do:** Flick Pazzi away, then Ginori. (Or drag each onto the ledge.)
   **What you see:**
    - The first flick has nothing pending, so the guess is "hide families like Pazzi", and Pazzi appears as an outline on the ledge.
    - The second flick refines it to [hide] [families] [PageRank] [below 0.04]. The reason line says "your 2 examples are among the 4 lowest; also fits: fewer than 2 marriages, which hides the same 4 today".
    - The 4 previewed on the ledge are Acciaiuoli, Ginori, Lamberteschi and Pazzi. Barbadori, at 0.050, is named as borderline.
    - You Accept, and the 4 move to the set-aside ledge.
      **Controls:** Flick (or drag to the ledge), Accept.

9. **Write a note.**
   **What you do:** Drag the blank note from the shelf onto the Medici, then type "center of every path" on the keyboard that appears in the scene, or dictate it where the browser allows.
   **What you see:** The first guess is "a note on this one", previewed attached to the Medici; Next guess would offer "the same note on the Medici's in-laws". The keyboard's Done key is Accept. The note becomes a row in the rule list.
   **Controls:** Drag a material, in-scene keyboard or dictation, Accept.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    **What you do:**
    - Before accepting: you pinch-drag the Medici a little by accident and let go inside the graph. The guess "move just this node" previews the Medici in their new place. Tap No and they go back. If you simply carry on, the preview is dropped when you start the next demonstration; it is never applied.
    - After accepting: you pinch tap the filter row to make it current and flick Barbadori to tighten the filter. You accept the guess without reading it, then notice that Salviati has gone to the ledge too. You drag Salviati up off the ledge.
      **What you see:** The guess you accepted was [fewer than 3 marriages], which hid both Barbadori and Salviati. Dragging Salviati up makes it re-infer: [PageRank] [below 0.055], with the reason "keeps Salviati (0.061), still hides Barbadori (0.050)". The preview shows Salviati coming back and nothing else changing. You Accept, and the new rule replaces the old row in the list. To undo the tightening as a whole instead, tap No with nothing pending: the button reads "Take back: hide fewer than 3 marriages", the preview shows Barbadori and Salviati returning, and Accept confirms. Tapping No again with nothing pending offers the change before that.
      **Controls:** No; pinch tap a row; flick; pinch-drag off the ledge; Take back; Accept.

11. **Save.**
    **What you do:** There is nothing to demonstrate: every Accept is already saved. Pinch tap the project chip at the strip's left end. Its verb chip lists rename, save a copy, export and settings; pick rename, type "Medici study", and Accept. To keep the whole method for another graph, you would have tapped Record before step 5 and again after step 8.
    **What you see:** The project chip shows the new name and "saved". The rule list is the project's history of layers, filters, runs, selections and notes.
    **Controls:** Pinch tap, tap a chip, in-scene keyboard or dictation, Accept.

## Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Pinch tap Strozzi and Guadagni, then flick the Medici. The guess is [families] [PageRank above 0.08] [and] [not married to the Medici], with the reason "both examples are high and outside the Medici marriages; the counter-example is high and inside". Because a selection guess was pending, the Medici are only crossed out in the tray, not hidden. Next guess offers the [or] form. To drop a clause, flick its chip off the strip.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Pinch tap the PageRank row, then Strozzi and Guadagni, and drag Strozzi's thumbnail above Guadagni's to show you disagree with the order. The guesses are "PageRank with damping 0.6 puts Strozzi above Guadagni", "betweenness" and "closeness". The search over options is honest about failure: when no setting fits, the strip says "no PageRank setting orders these this way; closest: damping 0.5". When you already know what you want, tap the PageRank chip to see its options as value chips.
- **Comparing two runs.** Pinch tap two run rows in the rule list (PageRank and betweenness). The guesses are "compare ranks", "difference" and "where they disagree". The preview lifts each family by how much its rank changed and outlines the biggest movers, with the reason "the Medici and Guadagni are 1st and 2nd by both; Salviati is 10th by PageRank and 4th by betweenness".
- **Layers on subsets.** Pinch tap the "married to the Medici" selection row, then drop a swatch on two of its members. Because both examples are inside that selection, the first guess's scope chip reads [in: families married to the Medici]; tap the chip to change it to [in: whole graph]. The layer touches only its scope.
- **Recipes.** Tap Record, do steps 5, 7 and 8, tap Record again. The tape lands on the shelf with one slot, "the graph". On another dataset, drag the tape onto the graph. Each recorded guess replays as a pending guess with its preview, so you accept or correct each step instead of trusting the whole recipe blind.
- **Joining tables.** This stays on the 2D page. The natural extension is a Flash Fill for keys: drag one table row onto the node it describes, and the system guesses the key column and the match. That needs a table view in VR, which this prototype does not have.

## How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog.** You never browse the catalog first. A demonstration shows the outcome you expect (an order, a size, a group), and the system ranks the algorithms whose results agree with it, previewing at most three. Cheap measures run for the preview. Expensive ones appear by name with their cost estimate and run only when you pick them. A chip's alternatives list is searchable, so a known algorithm is two taps away.
- **New object types.** Anything you can pinch can be an example: a node, an edge, a group, a run, a style layer row, a filter row, a note, the project. Inference needs only that the object type describes its attributes, relations and verbs; the strip then guesses over those, and the verb chip lists the verbs.
- **Plugins.** A plugin algorithm that returns a value per node or edge automatically becomes a candidate for ranking, sizing, coloring and filtering guesses. A plugin can add a reason template (a code with parameters) so its guesses explain themselves.
- **The limit.** The limit is the set of rules the system can guess. Show Me works for thresholds, conjunctions, structural relations (neighbors, paths, communities) and measures with a visible effect on each element. It runs out in three places:
    - It cannot infer verbs that have no visible effect on an example: export, settings, save a copy. Those are reached by tapping the object (often the project chip) and picking from its verb chip, which is a short list, not a menu tree.
    - It needs more examples when many measures agree; centrality measures are highly correlated on small graphs.
    - On a 10,000-node graph, two examples tell it almost nothing, so the borderline list and counter-examples do most of the work.

## Why it should work

- **Flash Fill** (Excel 2013, built on Gulwani's program synthesis from examples) showed that ordinary users accept a rule inferred from one or two examples and fix it with one more. It is one of the most used inference features in shipped software.
- **Trifacta Wrangler and "predictive interaction"** (Kandel et al., CHI 2011; Heer, Hellerstein and Kandel, CIDR 2015) showed that this loop works for data transformation: select an example, see ranked suggestions with previews, pick one. The preview is what earns the user's trust.
- **Tableau's "keep only" and "exclude", and Gmail's "filter messages like these",** show that many analysts filter by acting on examples, not by writing a condition.
- **Pygmalion (Smith 1975) and Eager (Cypher 1991)** established demonstrate-then-generalize. Eager's lesson -- highlight the prediction and let the user keep going or confirm -- is exactly the pending-guess preview.
- **Pinch is the native select gesture on all three headsets,** so the core loop needs no new gesture. The less common inputs map to physical meanings people already know: a flick throws something away, a two-hand pull makes it bigger, putting one card above another ranks it.
- **The 60+ catalog sits behind "show me the outcome".** That removes the hardest part of VR menus: reading long lists at arm's length.

## What it would teach us

- How many examples people give before the first guess is right, and whether that number is small enough for work done in the air.
- Whether people read the reason line or accept on the preview alone (automation bias), and whether naming borderline cases changes that.
- Whether ordering two thumbnails in the tray reads as "this ranks above that" without being taught, and whether that can replace running an algorithm by name.
- Whether flicking counter-examples feels natural and precise in the air, or whether people fall back to dragging onto the ledge.
- Whether people reach for Take back or for correcting by counter-example after accepting, and which they trust.
- Whether views that apply live while every other guess waits for Accept feels consistent or confusing.
- Whether recorded tapes are reused, and whether replaying them as pending guesses is trusted more than a recipe that runs in one go.
- Which verbs resist demonstration (save, export, settings), and how often people need the verb chip.

## Known risks

- **Wrong guesses accepted without reading.** Mitigation: the count and the borderline names sit next to Accept. When a guess changes many more elements than the examples imply, its count is shown larger. Take back is always one tap plus Accept, and correcting by counter-example is one drag.
- **Ambiguity: many rules fit two examples.** Mitigation: prefer the simplest rule, and say in the reason line what else fits ("also fits: fewer than 2 marriages"). Next guess cycles the alternatives, and the chips are editable when you know exactly what you mean.
- **Experts want to name the verb.** Mitigation: tap the thing, then the verb chip lists every verb the object has, with search; a known algorithm takes one tap on the thing and two on chips, never a hunt.
- **Inference latency on large graphs and big catalogs.** Mitigation: preview only the current guess and name the next two, run cheap measures first, and show expensive candidates with their cost estimate, running them only on request.
- **Re-inference after accepting changes more than intended.** Mitigation: the re-inferred rule is never applied automatically. It is a new pending guess with its own preview and a diff ("Salviati back, nothing else changes").
- **"Put it here" is overloaded.** Mitigation: it has one meaning, and where the thing lands says why, by fixed regions: the tray (order), outside the graph toward you (view), inside the graph (move the node), the ledge (out). When a drag is ambiguous, the first guess is the most literal one, and No costs nothing.
- **Tap, drag, flick and pull start the same way.** Mitigation: fixed thresholds, each tunable: under about 2 cm of movement is a tap, a fast release away from you or down is a flick, a slow release is a drop, and a second pinch during a drag makes it a pull.
- **Flick misfires from tracking noise, and fast throws lose hand tracking.** Mitigation: a speed threshold, drag-to-ledge as the slow equivalent, and nothing is hidden before Accept; a stray counter-example is one pinch tap to drop from the tray.
- **Small targets at 75 cm.** Mitigation: the ray snaps to the nearest node within a few degrees, and the tray thumbnails are large, so ordering and sizing happen on the thumbnails, not on the tiny nodes.
- **Arm fatigue.** Mitigation: system rays work with hands resting, the strip is low and close, and no input needs held arms or a held pose.
- **Hidden things are out of reach for correction.** Mitigation: the set-aside ledge holds them, borderline first. On a large graph it shows a sample of the borderline ones and a count.
- **Vision Pro has no hover.** Mitigation: the outline appears when you pinch, values are read by tapping into the tray, and a pinch tap on the wrong thing is undone by tapping it again.
