**Prototype 20: Pilot and Navigator** -- Two people with split jobs: the driver drives the view and points at objects, the navigator holds that object's verbs as tokens and hands one across; the driver pulling it free is the second key.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: a solid colored background in full VR, the graph at chest height 70-80 cm in front of the driver. Two headsets, any mix of Meta Quest, Samsung Galaxy XR and Apple Vision Pro, joined to one shared session over the network. Remote by default: each person stands or sits in their own room and sees the other as head and hands, the navigator placed at the driver's off-hand side and slightly forward, at the near corner of the graph, so the driver sees her and the graph in one glance. The pass (where tokens change hands) is anchored to each person's own body, a forearm's length in front of their off-hand hip, at the lower edge of their view; the other person's avatar hand is drawn arriving there. Nobody reaches behind or across their body. Co-located is optional (one room, a one-time alignment step, and then the pass is a real shared point between them). Driver: hands or controllers. Navigator: hands or controllers, or a laptop browser. A voice channel between the two people is always open (that is talking, not a command input). Dictation into a note token is optional. Both roles work seated.

To re-aim, the driver stops pulling, points elsewhere, and pulls again.

### The journey

**1. Enter**

- **What you do:** On the 2D page, one person opens the Florentine families and shares the session link; the other joins. Each picks a role on the join page and presses Enter VR.
- **What you see:** The graph at chest height in front of the driver. The navigator sees the same graph from the driver's off-hand side, her kit at her waist, and the driver's head, hands and ray. The driver sees the navigator's head and hands and an empty string at the off wrist.
- **Controls:** None in VR. Roles can be swapped later by handing the string's knot across.

**2. Get oriented**

- **What you do:** The driver closes both hands on the graph, turns it and pulls it a little closer. The navigator reads the readout aloud: "fifteen families, twenty marriages".
- **What you see:** The graph turns for both, as an object in front of a still background; neither person's viewpoint moves.
- **Controls:** Driver: point at the graph and close both hands. Navigator: none.

**3. Find the Medici**

- **What you do:** The driver points at empty space; the front row shows the whole graph's verbs. The navigator picks up Find and twists its ring to "Medici"; the matching node glows faintly for both. She holds it out. The driver takes it and pulls.
- **What you see:** During the joint hold the ghost rings the Medici node with "1 match"; on commit the view frames it and the Medici become the selection. (If the driver had spotted the label first, a pinch on it would select it directly; the navigator never points.)
- **Controls:** Navigator: close, twist, hold out. Driver: point at empty space, close the off hand, pull.

**4. Who they married into**

- **What you do:** The driver points at the Medici (selected); Neighbors appears in the front row. The navigator picks it up (ring at 1 hop) and holds it out. The driver takes it.
- **What you see:** The ghost outlines six families (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni) with "+6". On the pull they join the selection; the spent Neighbors token hangs on the driver's string.
- **Controls:** Same handoff. Twisting the ring to 2 during the joint hold would have grown the ghost before commit.

**5. Who matters most (PageRank)**

- **What you do:** While the driver looks around, the navigator opens the Catalog drawer, the centrality compartment, picks up PageRank (ring: damping 0.85) and offers it. The driver points at empty space and takes it.
- **What you see:** Joint hold shows "will run on 15 nodes, instant". On commit the run finishes; the readout lists the top families. The navigator says "Medici first, about 0.15, then Guadagni and Strozzi".
- **Controls:** Navigator: close on the drawer, close on PageRank, hold out. Driver: point, take, pull.

**6. Read the result**

- **What you do:** The driver sweeps the pointer across the graph. The navigator reads the ranked list and calls out anything the driver passes, or holds the top-five line out at the pass so the driver can read it.
- **What you see:** Each hovered family shows its score at the pointer. The navigator's readout shows the full ranking. To pull the top five into the selection, the navigator offers Top N (ring: 5) and the driver takes it at empty space.
- **Controls:** Driver: point. Navigator: optionally hold a readout line out. Optional Top N handoff.

**7. Color and size by PageRank**

- **What you do:** The navigator picks up Color by (its attribute defaults to the latest run, PageRank) in one hand and Size by in the other. She offers Color by; the driver, pointing at empty space, takes it and pulls. Then Size by.
- **What you see:** The ghost recolors the whole graph during each joint hold; each commit adds a style layer, and each layer is a spent token on the string.
- **Controls:** Two handoffs. The driver twists Size by's ring (largest size) while holding, to make the Medici less dominant, then pulls.

**8. Keep only strong families (filter)**

- **What you do:** The navigator twists Keep where's ring to 0.07 (attribute: PageRank, by default) and offers it. The driver takes it at empty space, sees too few left, twists the ring down a notch while still holding, then pulls.
- **What you see:** The ghost fades the families that would go and shows the count that stays; the count updates as the ring turns. On commit the faded families leave and a filter step hangs on the string.
- **Controls:** Handoff with an adjusted value: the navigator set the first value, the driver adjusted it before commit.

**9. Write a note**

- **What you do:** The navigator holds a Note token to her mouth and says "Medici: center of the marriage network" (or types it on the pinch keyboard), then offers it. The driver points at what he believes is the Medici and takes it.
- **What you see:** The text is written on the token during the hold, and the ghost shows the note pinned to the pointed node with that node's name. On commit it pins to the node.
- **Controls:** Navigator: hold to mouth (or pinch keyboard), hold out. Driver: point, take, pull.

**10. Undo a mistake**

- **What you do:** The note landed on Tornabuoni, which sits beside the Medici: the driver pulled without reading the ghost's name. The driver closes the dominant hand on the top token of the string (the note), pulls it off and opens the hand. The navigator picks the note out of the Return slot, offers it again, and the driver points at the Medici, checks the ghost and pulls.
- **What you see:** The moment the driver lets go, the note lifts off Tornabuoni and drops into the Return slot with its text intact. Deeper undo takes tokens off the top in order; the string shows how many steps back you are. The navigator can undo the same way from her copy of the string if the driver's hands are busy.
- **Controls:** Driver: close on the string's top token, pull, open. Navigator: take from the Return slot, hold out. Driver: point, take, pull.

**11. Save**

- **What you do:** The navigator offers Save; the driver points at empty space and takes it.
- **What you see:** The ghost shows the project name; on commit the readout shows "saved" and the string's tokens dim slightly to mark the saved point.
- **Controls:** One handoff.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule:** The navigator opens Where (attribute PageRank, at least 0.07), snaps it end to end to Neighbors (1 hop), and offers the compound token. The driver takes it pointing at the Medici: "strong families married to the Medici" becomes the selection. The faint match preview lets the navigator check the rule before offering.
- **Tuning algorithm options:** The navigator pulls an algorithm token open; it lengthens into one ring per option (PageRank: damping, iterations, tolerance, weight attribute). She sets them and closes it. Only the main ring stays visible to the driver, who can twist it during the joint hold.
- **Comparing two runs:** The navigator closes her hand on the PageRank step in her copy of the string to get a copy of the token, sets its damping to 0.5 and offers it; the driver takes it, and there are now two runs. Then she offers Compare (its two rings pick the runs); the driver takes it at empty space and the graph shows the difference per node, with the biggest movers in the readout.
- **Layers on subsets:** The driver selects the Medici and their neighbors, then takes Color by while pointing at a selected node: the layer applies to the selection only. To change it later, the driver pinches that layer's spent token so it floats beside the pass; the front row now shows the layer's verbs (Edit, Move up, Move down, Hide, Remove), and the navigator offers Edit with new values. The ghost shows the layer's new look before the pull.
- **Recipes:** The navigator closes one hand on the first and the other on the last of a run of steps in her copy of the string and pulls; a copy of those steps becomes one bundled token, which goes into her kit. Offered later on another graph, it replays all its steps at the driver's target, ghosting the final result first; it lands as one step on the string, so one undo removes it.
- **Joining tables:** Stays on the 2D page. Neither role has a good way to match columns by handing objects.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog:** The Catalog drawer has one compartment per family; the six most recent sit in the Analyze row. Browsing is the navigator's job and happens while the driver keeps exploring, so the catalog's depth costs the driver nothing. Where speech recognition exists, saying a name into the open drawer lifts the matching token.
- **New object types:** A new object type brings its verbs (they fill the front row when the driver points at one) and a rule for what the pointer can land on; the five landing rules above stay the same. Verbs on history objects (re-run, edit a layer, remove a filter step) land on a spent token the driver has pinched out of the string.
- **Plugins:** A plugin registers tokens: a name, its rings (its options, with defaults), the object types it applies to, and a preview. They appear in the drawer's family compartment and in the front row for those object types. Nothing about the handoff changes.
- **The limit:** Every command costs one handoff, about 1.5 to 3 seconds and two people's attention. It suits deliberate analysis and teaching; it is slow for fast exploratory sweeps, which stay with the driver's pointer and selection. The kit's fixed rows hold about two dozen tokens before the navigator stops finding them by place, and the string grows long after 30 or so steps (older tokens coil up and are reached by count, not by eye).

### Why it should work

- The surgical instrument pass and the rally co-driver are trained divisions of labor that keep the operator's eyes on the work while someone else prepares the next tool. The pass itself has a protocol: the receiver's grip is felt before the giver lets go. This prototype copies that protocol, with the receiver's pull as the commit.
- Two-person rules (launch keys, bank vaults, change review in software) exist because a second deliberate act catches single-person slips. Here the second act is the physical pull, with the effect previewed on the target at the moment of taking. Undo is kept one-person on purpose, because a safety net that needs two people is not always there.
- Pair programming studies (Williams, Kessler and others, around 2000) found pairs produced fewer defects for modestly more person-time; one person steering while the other plans ahead is the same split.
- Pairs analyzing networks in head-mounted displays did as well as or better than in a CAVE (Cordeil and others, "Immersive collaborative analysis of network connectivity", IEEE TVCG 2017), so co-analysis of graphs in headsets is not a stretch.
- Handing objects between avatars is something social VR users do without instruction (passing items in Rec Room or VRChat), so the handoff needs little teaching.
- Removing every command surface from the driver leaves them with three inputs (point, pinch, hold), which removes mode errors on that side; Spaceteam and Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes show that splitting what each person can see drives useful talk.

### What it would teach us

- Does the pull catch mistakes, or do pairs rubber-stamp (the driver pulls without reading the ghost)?
- What does a handoff cost per command, and does it fall as the navigator learns to anticipate the next verb from the front row?
- Is the navigator's private readout good for the pair, or does the driver feel blind?
- Does letting the driver adjust a value during the joint hold help, or does the navigator feel overruled?
- Does the handoff feel solid remotely, with only avatar hands, a body-anchored pass and network delay, or does it need co-location?
- Is the value in the second person or in the two-key structure? If a solo user would want a self-handoff, the structure is worth borrowing for destructive verbs elsewhere.
- Can the kit's fixed rows plus a drawer reach a 60-algorithm catalog without the driver waiting?
- Is a hanging string a readable history and a usable undo?

### Known risks

- **Needs two people and two headsets.** Mitigation: the navigator can join from a laptop browser, where the kit is a page and the handoff is dragging a token onto the pass. If the navigator drops out mid-session, the kit moves to the driver's waist and the driver hands tokens from one hand to the other (the same states, one person). Solo users use another prototype.
- **Rubber-stamping.** Mitigation: a commit needs the ghost to have been on that target for at least 300 ms; a token pulled faster returns to the navigator. The ghost always names the target and counts what changes.
- **Pointer slip at commit.** Mitigation: the target lock above freezes the target when the pull starts. Aiming at the wrong neighbor (journey step 10) is still possible; the ghost names the target, and one-person undo covers the rest.
- **Ownership conflicts over the network.** Mitigation: the server owns who holds each token; tugs resolve as refusals; a commit is shown only after the server confirms it, with the glow held until then.
- **Undo blocked by the other person.** Mitigation: undo needs no handoff; either person undoes from their own string, and redo is an ordinary offer from the Return slot.
- **Physical collisions when co-located.** Mitigation: remote is the default; co-located pairs see each other's full avatar and stand on fixed marks with the pass between them.
- **Arm and neck fatigue.** Mitigation: the pass is anchored to each person's own hip, forward and low, and the reach is short; the driver points with the dominant hand resting low; fine values come from slow twists, not from holding the arm out; the kit tilts up so the navigator does not bend her neck far.
- **Motion sickness.** Mitigation: only the graph moves; neither person's viewpoint is ever moved by the other, and a called view (Peek) eases the graph into place as an object.
- **One-handed use.** Mitigation: the driver can point, rest 300 ms to lock the target (it is outlined), then take and pull with the same hand; the navigator opens tokens by pinching one end and drawing it out.
- **Role imbalance or boredom.** Mitigation: roles swap by handing the string's knot across, at any time, in one move.
- **Hand-tracking grasp of small tokens.** Mitigation: tokens are fist-sized at the pass and snap into a closing hand within 5 cm.
- **Speech-dependent steps.** Mitigation: the pinch keyboard covers dictation; readout lines can be held out as text for a driver who cannot hear the navigator well.
