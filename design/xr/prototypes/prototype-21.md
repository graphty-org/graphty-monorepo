**Prototype 21: Mixing Desk** -- A mixing console with one channel strip per style layer, filter and selection: nothing is ever applied or committed, every effect is a live fader, two algorithm runs sit on two decks with a crossfader between them, and you can hear any channel.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height 70-80 cm away. The desk floats about 35 cm in front of you, about 15 cm below the elbows, tilted toward you like a real console and about 90 cm wide, below and in front of the graph so it never hides it. It is placed from your head height when you enter, and PLACE on the master section re-seats it: press PLACE, then rest both palms flat for a second on a real table, your lap, or the air where you want it, and the desk snaps to that plane. Resting it on a real table gives the fingers a surface. The desk mirrors for left-handed use, and every control works with either hand: nothing needs two hands. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR with hands (direct touch) or controllers (tip touch and trigger); Apple Vision Pro with hands for the desk and look-and-pinch to aim at the graph. Sound through the headset's speakers or headphones. Voice is optional and used only for TALKBACK (dictating notes).

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** Put on the headset and open the Florentine families project.
    - **What you see:** The graph at chest height and the desk below it, placed from your height. Decks are empty, one empty strip waits in the center, and the master meter reads 15 of 15 nodes, 20 of 20 edges. Holding a fingertip just above any control shows its name.
    - **Controls:** none yet; hover for names. PLACE if you are seated at a table.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the jog platter and turn it to spin the graph, poke it to stop, slide the nudge strip to bring the graph closer, tip it with TILT. Tap hot cue pad 1 to store this view.
    - **What you see:** The graph turning and stopping with the platter, coming closer, tipping; you stay still. Pad 1 lights.
    - **Controls:** jog platter, nudge strip, TILT, hot cue pad.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Either aim at the node labeled Medici and pinch, or turn the browse encoder to Nodes, scroll to Medici and press the encoder.
    - **What you see:** Medici ghost-highlighted on the cue bus. Found through the browser, where you could not see it, the turntable eases round to bring Medici to the front; aimed at directly, the graph stays put. The master meter is unchanged: nothing has reached the graph yet.
    - **Controls:** aim and pinch, or browse encoder.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Press SEND. A new strip appears, "Selection: Medici", already selected, with OUTLINE assigned and its fader up. In the selected channel, turn HIGH from 0 to 1 hop, then LOW to 1 to leave Medici itself out. Press LABEL.
    - **What you see:** The outline spreads from Medici to its six marriage partners, Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni, with their names; the strip's meter goes 1, 7, 6. The cue bus is empty again.
    - **Controls:** SEND, HIGH and LOW knobs, LABEL.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Tap deck A's LOAD to arm it, turn the browse encoder to Algorithms, Centrality, PageRank, and press the encoder.
    - **What you see:** Deck A shows PageRank with its option knobs (damping 0.85, iterations). The run is cheap, so it starts at once, and the progress ring fills and closes. The ranked strip appears and the run is listed under Runs. Nothing on the graph changes, because no strip uses it yet.
    - **Controls:** LOAD, browse encoder turn and press.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Press deck A's CUE, then hover a fingertip along its ranked strip.
    - **What you see and hear:** The graph shows the PageRank sizes as ghosts, and you hear a quick falling run, one note per family. Under the fingertip each family lights in turn with its name and value: Medici 0.146, then Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, down to Acciaiuoli at 0.031; the note steps down with the value. Medici sits clearly apart at the top. Press CUE again to take it off.
    - **Controls:** CUE, hover along the ranked strip.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Poke the empty strip's name to select it, tap INSERT, and press the encoder on "PageRank (deck A)" at the top of Runs. Press COLOR and SIZE, then push the strip's fader up slowly.
    - **What you see and hear:** As the fader rises, every node eases from its plain style toward the PageRank colors and sizes; at the top it snaps to the full mapping. The meter reads 15. Press SOLO and sweep your aim ray (no pinch) across the graph: the pitch is high over Medici and drops over Acciaiuoli. Press SOLO again to release.
    - **Controls:** strip name, INSERT, browse encoder, COLOR, SIZE, fader, SOLO, aim sweep.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Make another strip from "PageRank (deck A)" the same way, press KEEP, push its fader to the top, and turn LOW up. Near the end, keep holding the knob and lift the hand off the desk to slow it, and land on 0.070 (or hold the readout and type it).
    - **What you see and hear:** Families drop out as the knob passes their value, each with one click, and the meter counts down. At 0.070 five families remain: Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni. Ridolfi, at 0.0696, just missed, which is why fine mode is there. Pulling the fader halfway down would leave the dropped families as faint ghosts instead of hiding them.
    - **Controls:** strip name, INSERT, KEEP, fader, LOW knob, fine mode.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Aim at Medici and pinch to put it on cue. The master display reads "Note to: Medici". Hold TALKBACK and say "Medici lead PageRank by half again over Guadagni." Without voice, tap TALKBACK and type.
    - **What you see:** The text appears on the master display as you speak, then pins to Medici as a note. With nothing on cue the note goes to the project; with a strip or deck cued last, it goes to that strip or run.
    - **Controls:** aim and pinch, TALKBACK.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** Reaching for the color strip's fader, you pinch the filter strip's LOW knob instead and drag it up to 0.090 before you notice. Press UNDO.
    - **What you see:** The knob turns back to 0.070 by itself and blinks, so you can see which control moved; the meter climbs back to 5. REDO would put it back to 0.090. For a bigger retreat, tap a scene pad you stored earlier and the whole desk, every fader, knob and deck, returns to that moment; that recall is itself one UNDO step.
    - **Controls:** UNDO, REDO, scene pads.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the empty scene pad 1 to store this mix as a scene, then press SAVE.
    - **What you see:** The pad lights and SAVE flashes once. The project holds the selection, style and filter strips in their order, the PageRank run with its options, the scene and the note. A muted strip is saved but changes nothing. The cue bus is never saved, exported or shown in a shared view.
    - **Controls:** scene pad tap, SAVE.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Strips with KEEP combine with AND: a node stays only if every KEEP strip passes it, and a node dropped by several fades as far as the strongest fader says. "Strong families that did not marry a Medici" is the PageRank filter strip plus a KEEP strip whose INSERT is the "Selection: Medici" strip's membership, with INV pressed: Medici, Guadagni and Strozzi remain (the Medici are not their own in-laws). For OR, load a Group from Presets into an empty strip, press its ANY button, and turn ROUTE on two KEEP strips to that group. The group strip's fader and mute then act on the whole rule at once.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Deck knobs come from the algorithm's option schema: numbers are knobs, choices are detented knobs, on and off are buttons, and an attribute choice (such as edge weight) is a slot loaded from the browser. Turning damping reruns PageRank live when the run is cheap, and every strip using that run follows it, so you watch the colors and the filter move as you turn. When the element's cost estimate says the run is costly, the deck shows PLAY: knob changes wait, the deck says the result is stale, and PLAY runs it.
- **Comparing two runs.** Press SYNC on deck B to load PageRank with deck A's options, then turn B's damping to 0.5. Point the style strip's INSERT at MIX and sweep the crossfader: Medici and Guadagni hold their size, Salviati swells from tenth to fifth, and Albizzi and Strozzi trade places; the agreement meter shows how close the two rankings are overall. Load betweenness into B instead and Medici pulls even further ahead. Cue deck B alone to preview it as a ghost. Loading a new algorithm into a deck never takes a run away from a strip: the old run stays in Runs and the strips keep it. Only MIX follows whatever the decks hold.
- **Layers on subsets.** Turn a style strip's SUBSET knob to "Selection: Medici", and that layer paints only the Medici neighborhood. A quick algorithm loaded into a strip's INSERT with SUBSET set runs only over that subset, for example communities within the neighborhood.
- **Recipes.** A scene can be exported as a recipe, and a single strip can be stored in the browser's Presets folder. Loading a recipe on another graph rebuilds the strips and decks. Any source the new graph lacks shows as an unpatched INSERT slot with a blinking light, ready to be repointed with the encoder.
- **Joining tables.** Stays on the 2D page. Once a table is joined there, its columns appear under Attributes in the browser and can be loaded into any strip.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog.** The browser lists algorithms by family folder, sortable by name or cost, with type-to-search from the desk keyboard. The decks never grow: whatever has more than four options pages its knobs, as DJ effect units do.
- **New object types.** A new kind of result becomes a new source in the browser; a new style property becomes a new assign button in the selected channel. Edges get their own bank of strips with the same anatomy. A layout can sit on a deck as well, and the crossfader then morphs node positions between two layouts.
- **Plugins.** A registered algorithm appears in its browser folder, and its options become deck knobs automatically from its declared schema. A plugin needs no desk code.
- **The limit.** Anything that is not a value over nodes or edges fits badly. Paths, matchings and flows reduce to membership, so their range knobs do little. Editing data (adding nodes, changing attributes) is not a mix and stays on the 2D page. Past about 16 live strips, banks hide what is shaping the graph; the master meter and SOLO help, but a heavy analysis outgrows one desk.

### Why it should work

- **Dynamic queries.** Sliders that update the view continuously (Ahlberg, Williamson and Shneiderman, 1992, and the FilmFinder that followed) let people explore faster and with fewer errors than forms with an apply step. The desk applies that result to everything: style, filter, selection and algorithm options.
- **Shipped VR precedent.** Tribe XR (a DJ trainer on Quest with virtual decks, jog wheels and a crossfader) and Electronauts show that people can work mid-air faders, knobs and jog wheels in a headset for long sessions.
- **One kind of control.** Every value is a fader or knob with the same feel and the same fine mode, so learning one strip teaches them all. Console users already move across 32-channel desks this way, because the strips repeat.
- **Selected channel.** Digital consoles keep the fader strips slim and show one selected channel's full settings in a single section at full size. That is what lets eight strips fit at a size bare fingers can hit.
- **Fine mode has a known precedent.** Moving the finger away from a slider to scrub more finely is how iOS media scrubbing has worked for years. Scaling motion for precision is also the idea behind PRISM (Frees and Kessler, 2005) in VR.
- **Real surfaces help.** Lindeman et al. (1999) found that a physical surface under virtual controls improves speed and accuracy. PLACE puts the desk on any table you have.
- **Scene recall and pre-fader cue.** Every digital mixing console ships scene recall and pre-fader listening; operators rely on them because they make bold changes safe and previews harmless.
- **Sonification of values.** Apple's Audio Graphs (VoiceOver, iOS 15 and later) ship pitch-mapped charts; The Sonification Handbook (Hermann, Hunt and Neuhoff, 2011) reports that pitch reads magnitude and trends well. Hearing a soloed strip adds a second channel for reading a result without looking away from the graph.
- **Comparison by blending.** Gleicher et al. (2011) name superposition as one of three basic comparison designs; a crossfader is superposition you control over time, and change blindness research suggests motion under your own hand makes differences pop out.

### What it would teach us

- Is "no apply step" calmer or more anxious? Do people trust a mix that is never committed, or do they keep storing scenes as a substitute for an apply button?
- Can a style layer, a filter, a selection and an algorithm option really share one control, or do users want different controls for different kinds of things?
- Does a filter as a fader, with dropped nodes fading by degrees, help people see what a threshold costs before they commit to it?
- Is crossfading two runs a better way to compare than side by side or a difference view? Do people find stable and unstable nodes faster?
- Does sonification add information in a working session, or is it a novelty that is switched off after a minute?
- How precise are mid-air faders with fine mode compared to typed values, and does a real table under the desk close the gap?
- Does keeping the hands off the graph (desk only) tire people less than grabbing the graph, or does it make the graph feel remote?
- Does selecting a strip and editing it in one section feel object-first, or do people reach for controls on the strip itself?

### Known risks

- **Mid-air faders with no touch.** Bare-hand faders overshoot. Mitigations: fine mode by lifting the hand, detents with a click sound and a controller pulse, PLACE onto a real table, a typed value by holding any readout.
- **Everything live means accidental changes.** A grab on the wrong control changes the graph at once, as in step 10. Mitigations: UNDO and REDO; the undone control blinks; a fader or knob only moves on a pinch or held trigger, and a button only presses on a push from the front, never on a brush; the desk can be moved out of the arm's path.
- **Costly runs fight the live model.** A slow algorithm cannot rerun on every knob turn. Mitigations: the deck shows PLAY when the cost estimate is high; knob changes then wait until PLAY, and the deck says the result is stale.
- **Strip overload.** With many strips it is hard to tell what shapes the graph. Mitigations: the master meter, the per-strip meters, SOLO, banks, and scribble strips that name each source.
- **Partial faders are hard to read.** A color at 40 percent is neither style. Mitigations: faders snap at 0 and 100 percent; the readout warns when a strip sits between them; saving with a strip in between asks once.
- **Sound in a shared office.** Mitigations: sound is never the only signal (meters and labels carry everything); the SOUND button; headphones recommended.
- **Console vocabulary is foreign to most analysts.** Mitigations: hover names on every control; plain labels (KEEP, not "gate"; UNDO, not "recall"); one strip anatomy everywhere; the first strip of a new project is made for you.
- **Desk below, graph above.** Looking down to the desk and up to the graph tires the neck. Mitigations: the desk sits just below the graph's lower edge rather than at the hips, it can be raised and pulled closer, the meters and master display can be read in peripheral view, and the hot cue pads and CUE bring the graph to you instead of you hunting through it. The graph moves, never the viewpoint, which keeps motion sickness low; spin speed is capped.
- **The element has to grow first.** A style layer's strength, a filter's fade, a source blended from two results and the agreement between two rankings are not things graphty-element offers today.
