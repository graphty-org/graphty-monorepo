**Prototype 22: Command Card** -- Real-time-strategy controls: box select in the air, control groups for selections and for camera views, a fixed grid of commands laid on your forearm, and orders queued to run in sequence.

**Setup:** solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height, 70-80 cm away. A column of object tiles (runs, style layers, filter steps, notes, recipes) stands just left of the graph, so objects that have no place in the graph can be clicked like anything else. Built for controllers (Quest, Galaxy XR, PS VR2 Sense controllers on Vision Pro where supported); hands work as the alternative and are the default on Vision Pro. The dominant hand points, clicks and presses cells; the off forearm carries the grid. Eyes are used only on Vision Pro, where a pinch aims where you look. Voice is optional and off until turned on: saying a cell's name presses it, and a note can be dictated. The off hand never pinches: on all three headsets, a pinch with the palm facing you is the system's own menu gesture.

### Key structures

**The command card.** A 3 by 5 grid on the inside of the off forearm, rows from wrist to elbow, columns from the thumb side (A) to the little-finger side (C). The grid starts 4 cm up from the wrist crease (so your eyes land on the forearm, not the palm) and is scaled to the calibrated forearm, about 9 cm wide and 20 cm long; the outer columns land on the sides of the forearm. Each cell keeps its verb for every object type; a verb that does not apply to the selection is dark, and pressing it gives a dull tick and does nothing. A selection holds graph elements (nodes and edges together) or objects of one type; append-clicking an object of another type gives a dull tick and changes nothing.

**Preview before change.** Hovering over a cell that changes the graph shows the change faintly: Remove outlines what would be hidden or deleted (and names any layers that depend on a run), Keep only fades what would go, Color by and Size by tint and scale faintly. The status line names it with a count, for example "Remove: hide 3 nodes". Lifting the finger away without contact changes nothing.

**How orders and the selection fit together.** Every order acts on the selection as it stands when that order runs. Grow is the only order that changes the selection; orders that make an object (a run, a layer, a filter step, a note) add a tile to the column and leave the selection alone. Values chosen on the stepper while queueing are kept with the chip. Storing into a group or camera cell counts as an order, so Cancel last restores what the cell held before. Cancel last is never queued: pressed while append is on, it drops the newest queued chip.

**Cards that replace the command card for a moment.** The elbow corner is always Cancel last; on these cards it backs out of the card.

Selection group cells show their counts; a lit latch shows "append" or "subtract" on the strip too.

**The queue strip.** A row of chips floating 3 cm above the thumb-side edge of the forearm, next order at the wrist end. While append is on, each press adds a chip and nothing runs. Turning append off runs them in order; the running chip fills as it works, and a one-line status under the graph repeats it so you can watch the graph instead. After a queue finishes, its chips stay grey on the strip until the next order, so you can drag the whole run onto the object column to keep it as a recipe. An order undone with Cancel last leaves a hollow chip at the end of the strip until the next new order; dragging it back onto the strip redoes it.

**Seated and one-handed.** The card follows the forearm wherever it rests, so a seated person can lay it on a thigh or an armrest. In Tune on the graph, "card placement: in the air" pins the card and the group bar side by side, 1.5 times larger, low and to the left of the graph, pressed by fingertip or by ray click; append and subtract are then the latches. The card is pinned in the air automatically when only one hand or controller is tracked.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** From the 2D page, enter VR. The first time, touch your off wrist crease and then your off elbow crease with the right fingertip (or controller tip) so the grid fits your forearm. Turn your off palm up.
    - **What you see:** The Florentine families at chest height. The command card lights on your forearm with the graph's verbs; dark cells are the ones that need a selection.
    - **Controls:** Two calibration touches, once per person; palm up.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Grab the graph and turn it. Press Label (C4) to turn all names on, and Inspect (A1) for a summary. Turn your palm down and hold camera view 1 to keep this overview.
    - **What you see:** Family names on the nodes; a readout beside the graph: 15 families, 20 marriages, and their attributes. Camera cell 1 shows a stored mark.
    - **Controls:** Grab (fist / grip); touch C4, A1; palm down, hold camera 1 (A3 of the bar) for 0.6 s.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Spot the name near the middle and click it. With the palm still down, hold selection group 1 to store it.
    - **What you see:** The Medici node selected; turning the palm up shows node verbs (Grow and Link to light up). Group 1 on the bar shows "1".
    - **Controls:** Click (pinch / trigger); hold group 1 for 0.6 s (controllers: off stick flicked up and held).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Palm up. Hold the append modifier, press Grow (C1), then Frame (B1), and let go. Store the result as group 2.
    - **What you see:** Two chips appear on the queue strip, Grow then Frame. On release they run in order: the six families married to the Medici join the selection with their marriage edges, then the view frames the grown selection. Group 2 shows "7".
    - **Controls:** Off fist (off grip) held; touch C1, B1; release; palm down, hold group 2 (off stick flicked right and held).

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Click empty air to clear the selection. Palm up, press Analyze (A3); the sub-grid opens on page 1, centrality. Press PageRank at its wrist corner (A1).
    - **What you see:** A PageRank chip on the queue strip fills and clears; a "PageRank 1" tile appears in the object column. The card returns to the graph's verbs.
    - **Controls:** Click empty air; touch A3, then A1. Voice alternative: "PageRank".

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Click the "PageRank 1" tile. Press Inspect (A1).
    - **What you see:** The card shows run verbs. A ranked readout opens beside the graph, Medici first, then Guadagni and Strozzi, with values; pointing at a row lights that family in the graph, and clicking a row selects it.
    - **Controls:** Click the tile; touch A1.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** With the run still selected, hold the append modifier, press Color by (A4) and Size by (B4), and let go.
    - **What you see:** Two chips, then the graph recolors and resizes by PageRank in that order. Both orders act on the run, which stays selected; two layer tiles appear in the object column.
    - **Controls:** Off fist (off grip) held; touch A4, B4; release.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** With the run selected, press Keep only (C2). Touch the value cell (B3) and slide toward the elbow to raise the threshold, or tap minus (A3) and plus (C3); then tap the value once to accept.
    - **What you see:** All cells but row 3 and Cancel last dim. Families below the value fade as a live preview, with "kept: n of 15" beside the value. On accept, the rest are hidden and a filter step tile appears.
    - **Controls:** Touch C2; slide from B3 along the arm (controllers: slide the controller tip); tap A3 / C3 for single steps; tap B3.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Palm down and tap group 1 to recall the Medici. Palm up, press Note (A5). Speak the note, or spell it on the keypad. Press A5 again to finish.
    - **What you see:** The card becomes a phone keypad; the text grows on a note card next to the Medici. On finish, the note pins to the Medici and a note tile appears in the column.
    - **Controls:** Tap group 1 (off stick flicked up); touch A5; dictation or keypad; touch A5.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** With the Medici selected, you queue Label (C4) and Frame (B1), but your finger lands on Remove (B2) between them. Before letting go, pinch the Remove chip on the queue strip and pull it away. Let go. Then you decide the labels clutter the view. Hover over Cancel last (C5): the status line says "Cancel: Frame". Press it twice.
    - **What you see:** Three chips, the middle one Remove; pulled away, it vanishes and the other two close up. On release, labels show and the view frames the Medici. The first Cancel last undoes Frame and the view returns; the second undoes Label. Two hollow chips sit at the end of the strip; dragging the Frame chip back would redo it.
    - **Controls:** Off fist held; touch C4, B2, B1; pinch-drag the chip away (trigger-drag); release; hover, then touch C5 twice.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Press Save (B5). It is lit on the command card for every object type.
    - **What you see:** A "Saved: Florentine families, Oct 9" line under the graph; a double tick on controllers. To rename later, select the graph, press Tune (C3), press the name option and use the keypad.
    - **Controls:** Touch B5.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Control groups are the set algebra. "Strong families married into the Medici, plus the Strozzi": recall group 2, hold append and tap group 3 (the Strozzi, stored earlier) to take the union, let go, then press Keep only (C2). Select the PageRank run and press Keep only again with a threshold. Filter steps in the column apply in order, so two steps are AND; append is OR; subtract is AND NOT. Inspect on a filter step names what it removes.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Click the run tile, press Tune (C3): the option list shows damping, iterations and weight attribute, one per cell. Press damping, step it from 0.85 to 0.7 on the stepper, accept; the tile now reads "options changed". Press Analyze (A3) to run it as "PageRank 2". PageRank 1 keeps its results and the options that made them.
- **Comparing two runs.** Select "PageRank 1", press Compare (B3): the pointer becomes a targeting cursor. Click the "PageRank 2" tile. A comparison run appears whose values are the rank changes; Inspect reads it, Color by paints it, Keep only shows only families that moved more than a step size.
- **Layers on subsets.** Select a style layer, press Link to (A2): targeting cursor. Turn the palm down and tap group 2 (a group cell is a valid target), and the layer now paints only those seven families. Painting a subset directly works too: recall group 2, press Color by (A4), and step through the palette on the stepper.
- **Recipes.** A queue is a recipe in waiting. After a queue runs (for example Grow, Grow, Analyze PageRank on these, Frame: rank the two-hop neighborhood and look at it), its grey chips stay on the strip; drag them onto the object column to keep them as a recipe tile. Later, select the recipe tile and press Link to (A2), then click a target node, group or the graph: the orders replay there, as a queue you can still pull chips from before it runs. Inspect lists its steps; Tune exposes the numbers inside it.
- **Joining tables.** Stays on the 2D page. Choosing join keys and columns is a form, and a 15-cell arm grid is the wrong place for it. The joined columns appear in VR as attributes that Color by, Size by and Keep only can use.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog.** Twelve per page in fixed categories gives six pages; every algorithm has a permanent address such as "Analyze, page 2, B3", the same for every user and every session, so practice transfers the way grid hotkeys do. A page indicator runs along the side of the sub-grid. Saying a name (optional) presses the cell and briefly shows its address, which teaches it. Nothing is ever reordered by recency, because that would undo what the arm has learned.
- **New object types.** A new type maps its actions onto the 15 existing verbs and leaves the rest dark. Actions that fit no verb go behind Tune as option cells. A type may not move a verb or claim a cell for a new verb.
- **Plugins.** A plugin's algorithms take new pages after the built-in ones, in the order the plugins were installed; built-in addresses never change. A plugin's object types follow the rule above.
- **The limit.** Fifteen primary verbs, and one level behind Analyze and Tune. The grid cannot grow: a fingertip on a forearm, under current hand tracking, cannot reliably hit cells smaller than about 3 by 4 cm, so 3 by 5 is near the ceiling (and on a short forearm, the pinned card in the air is the way to keep cells that size). Past about ten algorithm pages, paging gets slow and a search on the 2D page (or voice) has to carry the long tail.

### Why it should work

- Strategy games have tested this control set for decades with players who act hundreds of times a minute. Box select, numbered control groups, camera hotkeys, shift-queued orders and a fixed command grid (StarCraft II's "grid" hotkey layout) exist because a fixed spatial layout plus stored sets lets experts act without looking.
- Spatially stable command layouts beat changing ones for experienced users: CommandMaps (Scarr, Cockburn, Gutwin and Bunt, CHI 2012) were faster than ribbons and menus once people knew them, because position is remembered.
- Touching a real surface helps in VR. Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn (CHI 1999) found that a hand-held physical panel under virtual buttons made presses faster and more accurate than floating panels. Mine, Brooks and Sequin (SIGGRAPH 1997) showed body-relative controls use proprioception so they can be found without looking.
- The body itself works as that surface. Skinput (Harrison, Tan and Morris, CHI 2010) put buttons on the forearm; Gustafson, Rabe and Baudisch (CHI 2013) found the tactile cue of touching your own palm mattered more than seeing it for palm-based interfaces.
- Shipped precedent for off-hand surfaces: MRTK's hand menus on HoloLens 2. (The palm-up gestures of Quest and visionOS are precedent too, and also the reason this design keeps the off hand from pinching.)
- Analysis has natural sequences (select, grow, run, color, filter), and a queue lets you state the whole sequence once and watch it run, with a chance to fix it before it does.

### What it would teach us

- Whether hand tracking is precise enough for a fingertip on your own forearm, or whether this only works with a controller tip as the stylus.
- Whether people learn cell positions by feel within one session, and how many presses it takes before they stop looking at the arm. The corner cells have landmarks (wrist and elbow creases); the middle ones may not.
- Whether queuing is used in analysis, where most orders finish at once, or whether it only pays for slow algorithms and recipes.
- Whether numbered selection groups map to how analysts think about subsets (the family, its in-laws, the top ten), and whether four are enough.
- Whether stored camera views matter in a graph that fits in front of you, and whether "tap to see, tap twice to move" prevents surprise motion.
- Whether a 3D box is a useful selection shape in a node-link layout, or whether people want a lasso.
- Whether hover previews on the arm are seen at all, since the eyes are on the arm, not the graph, at the moment of hovering.
- How long people hold the off forearm up before resting it, seated and standing, and how often they switch to the card in the air.

### Known risks

- **Arm fatigue from holding the forearm up, palm up (supination is tiring to hold), and from reaching across the body.** Mitigation: the grid follows the forearm wherever it rests, so a seated user can lay it on the thigh or armrest; the graph does not move when the arm does; the card can be pinned in the air; voice spares the arm for common actions.
- **Hand tracking loses a hand that touches the other forearm.** Mitigation: the press commits on lift, using the last good fingertip position; cells are about 3 by 4 cm and the hover light shows the aim before contact; controllers, whose tip is tracked by the controller, are the primary build.
- **The forearm is not tracked, so the grid can drift off the real arm.** Mitigation: per-person calibration of wrist and elbow, a grid anchored at the wrist and laid along the measured direction, and a quick recalibration by touching the elbow crease again.
- **System gestures on the off hand.** A pinch with the palm toward you opens the system menu on Quest and Android XR and the Home view on visionOS. Mitigation: the off hand never pinches (no thumb-to-fingertip shortcuts, no pinch modifier); off-hand select events are ignored; the card starts up the forearm, away from the palm. Open item: on Vision Pro, confirm the off fist never registers as the Home tap.
- **Accidental presses when the arm brushes the body or the other hand.** Mitigation: only the side facing you takes presses, only the dominant index tip or controller tip counts, and a press needs contact then lift without a slide.
- **Adjacent-cell slips (Remove next to Keep only).** Mitigation: hover previews show what a cell would do before contact; destructive verbs sit away from the wrist corner where the eyes first land; every order is undone by Cancel last; queued orders can be pulled off before they run.
- **Hands lose the subtract modifier as a pose.** Subtract is a latch on the group bar on hands, which costs a palm flip. Mitigation: subtract is rarer than append, and group algebra (where it is used most) already happens on the group bar.
- **Queue confusion for people who never played strategy games.** Mitigation: without append, every order runs at once, like a button; the queue only exists while append is on.
- **Many-meaning Cancel last.** Mitigation: it always acts on the newest order, whatever its state, and hovering over it names what it is about to cancel.
- **Temporary cards reuse cells (B3 is Compare on the card, the value on the stepper).** Mitigation: every cell a temporary card does not use goes dark, and the elbow corner always backs out.
- **Motion when views change.** Mitigation: you never move; recalling a camera view or sliding with the stick moves the graph against a solid background, at a steady speed, with no acceleration, after a preview outline.
- **No haptics on Vision Pro with hands.** Mitigation: the touch on the arm is real; the lit cell and a short sound confirm the press.
- **Clicking empty air by mistake clears the selection.** Not solved here: the stored groups are the recovery. If testing shows it matters, the previous selection becomes a fifth, automatic group.
