**Prototype 23: Cutting Room** -- A film editor's timeline under the graph holds every change as a clip on a track; you edit the history, not the state, and compare runs by branching. Looking is free: pressing anything shows it in the source monitor and records nothing; only a verb lands a clip. Every object's verbs come from the command catalog, and every clip says which graph it read.

### Key structures

**Three orders, three places.** The timeline's horizontal axis is only the order the work was done, which is what depends on what. The order style layers paint is the layer stack, a list in the source monitor. The order a walk-through is shown is the Story track. Reordering paint or story never moves a clip and never replays analysis.

**What graph a clip reads.** One rule, everywhere: a clip reads the graph that the enabled clips to its left on its own branch produce. A filter clip has one switch, "Later steps see: the filtered graph / the whole graph" (default: the filtered graph; "the whole graph" makes it a view-only hide). Every run, route, Neighbors and selection clip prints what it read on its face and at the top of its monitor: "on 212 of 4,000 nodes, after: Last 90 days, Except brokers". When a new clip changes the graph that an earlier run on the same branch measured (a filter, an Except, a merge, a join, a removal), the new clip lands with a yellow corner and "Measured before this: Betweenness, PageRank -- Re-measure here"; one press lands fresh copies of those runs after it, with their style and filter clips pointed at the new copies. A filter made from a run ("Filter by this") never flags that run: it reads those values on purpose. Until re-measured, every reader of the old run (ranked strip, histogram, diff lane, Branches table, legend) carries "measured before 'Except brokers'". A route or slot whose node is not in the graph it reads says so in the slot ("ACC-10077 is hidden by 'Last 30 days'") with "Route on the whole graph" and "Pick another".

**Verbs are generated, not listed.** Every graphty-element command declares which object kinds it accepts: a node, an edge, a selection or set, a run, a clip, the graph, a value range (a histogram band, a legend entry, a group-table row, a span on the ranked strip), a pair of runs, a load in progress. An object's verb row in the source monitor is generated from those declarations: the eight verbs graphty's defaults rank first for that kind (a fixed order, so positions never shift under the hand; a person can pin a verb into the eight from More), then "More", which opens the drawer filtered to that object. A plugin command that accepts a selection appears under that selection's "More" without any XR code. Clip housekeeping (Disable, Duplicate, Rename, Variant of this, Save as recipe, Delete) sits behind a "Clip" fold so an object's own verbs lead. The lists in the table below are the default eight.

**A drawer row and a peek.** The object's verbs at the top of the drawer act at once, as in the monitor. Pressing an Analysis, Style or Filter row (anything with options) is a peek: the monitor shows the algorithm's one-line description, its options as generated controls, its cost for this graph and a Run verb, so options can be set before the first run. A row in the recent list carries a small "Run now" button for the fast path.

The node list and every table have a search field at the top (in-world keyboard or dictation). **Two ways to fork, each saying where it forks.** "Variant of this" on any clip forks just BEFORE that clip, copies the clip and everything after it onto the new branch, puts the playhead on the copy and opens its controls: the original stays untouched on the old branch, so turning the copy's knob can only change the copy. "Branch here" on the ruler starts an empty branch at that point. Both name what they do in the verb ("Variant: copies Louvain and 2 later clips"). Right after any edit to an old clip lands, the ripple bar keeps a "Keep the old one too" button until the next edit: it turns that edit into a variant, so the obvious move (press the run, turn its knob) never has to destroy the first result.

Trim handles are tabs outside the clip edge; the clip body never trims. A clip with one main number (a filter's threshold) has one tab, on its right; a clip with a range (a time window, a rank range) has one on each side. Where two clips' tabs would meet in one gap, the left clip's tab stands high and the right clip's stands low, and each shows its clip's value name while it grows ("PageRank above", "degree at least"). Tabs grow to 2.5 deg as the ray or fingertip approaches; on Vision Pro, which has no approach, they are 2.5 deg at rest and clips keep a 1.5 cm gap. The clip's grip tab sits on its top edge and is the only way to move a clip. Two style bands that split one range share a handle: pulling it moves the cut for both.

**The clip drawer** has a search field at the top, a recent row, the verbs of the object in the source monitor, then sections by track (Selection, Analysis, Filter, Style, Notes) and, inside Analysis, by category and by the kind of value an algorithm returns. Each algorithm row shows its cost estimate for this graph. "Run several" puts check boxes on the rows and lands one batch clip. Run (in the monitor, after the peek) appends the clip at the end; dragging a row out of the drawer places it anywhere. Placing a clip before an existing one is an edit to the past and raises the ripple bar. Search matches plain and technical names and also finds every project verb, so the drawer is the command palette. A row whose option kind the controls cannot render says "Set this on the 2D page".

**The laptop pairing.** The 2D page shows a 6-digit code; entering it once in the headset pairs the two. Files, pasted text and drafts go both ways: From laptop lists the laptop's chosen folder (Inbox), and every Export lands there (Outbox). It needs a relay graphty does not run today (see Known risks). Without it, files reach the headset only by leaving VR for the headset browser's file picker or download folder; samples, Generate, Recent and a web address are the in-session start.

**The ripple bar** appears before any edit that replays or changes what earlier clips measured. It names, in this order: the estimated replay time (the sum of the element's estimates for every clip it will replay: "about 4 s -- Betweenness on 3,200 nodes"), the notes and sets whose members will change, the clips that will break, and the count of clips it replays.

### The journey

**1. Enter**

- **What you do:** press Enter VR on the 2D page.
- **What you see:** the Florentine families graph at chest height; below it the timeline with the data clip at its head, one "Start" keyframe on the camera track, the other tracks folded to thin bars, and the playhead at the end. The four-line legend (pinch empty space to move the graph; press anything to look; act with its verbs or "Add"; Undo) shows once at the graph's lower edge, stays until you first grab the graph or press something, then folds into the "?" chip. With no data loaded, the source monitor shows the start card instead (Samples, Recent, From laptop, Generate).
- **Controls:** none. The "?" chip reopens the legend.

**2. Get oriented**

- **What you do:** grip (or pinch empty space) and turn the graph, add the second hand to scale it up (or the Scale chips); press A or the "Mark view" chip to keep this view.
- **What you see:** the graph moves with your hand; a "View 1" keyframe clip appears on the camera track.
- **Controls:** grip or empty-space pinch; A or the "Mark view" chip.

**3. Find the Medici**

- **What you do:** in the source monitor's node list, press its search field and type "med" (or say it), or scroll; press the "Medici" row. Or point at the Medici node and press it. Then press "Select" in the verb row.
- **What you see:** hovering lights the node and draws a leader line to it; at the ray tip a name tag shows before you press. Pressing rings the Medici node and shows its attributes and six marriage edges in the source monitor, with "Looking -- press Select to keep it" beside the node the first few times; nothing lands. "Select" lands a "Medici" clip on the Selection track and the node is selected on the graph.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch on a row or node, then on Select.

**4. Who they married into**

- **What you do:** press "Neighbors" in the Medici selection's verb row; or press B (or the "Add" handle), whose header reads "On: Medici -- the selection", and choose "Neighbors" (or type "neigh" in its search).
- **What you see:** a "+ Neighbors, 1 hop" clip lands after "Medici"; the selection grows to the Medici and their six marriage partners (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni). Its knob row shows hops and an edge-type choice, with the count it would add printed under each.
- **Controls:** B or the "Add" handle; trigger / pinch on the drawer row.

**5. Who matters most (PageRank)**

- **What you do:** in the drawer, open Analysis, Centrality (or search "pagerank") and press PageRank; the monitor shows what it does, its options and its cost; press Run.
- **What you see:** a PageRank clip lands, printed "on 16 of 16 families", on the Analysis track and fills with real progress as it runs (a Cancel mark on its face while it does), then shows a small distribution and the words "Ran -- press to read". The graph does not change: a run is a fact, not a style.
- **Controls:** B; trigger / pinch on the row, then on Run.

**6. Read the result**

- **What you do:** press the PageRank clip.
- **What you see:** the source monitor turns into the ranked strip by PageRank: Medici on top, then Guadagni and Strozzi, with each value. Hovering a row lights the node. Damping, iterations and tolerance sit above the strip as generated controls, with the seed and scope ("whole graph") printed. Nothing is recorded by pressing.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch on the clip; hover to read.

**7. Color and size by PageRank**

- **What you do:** press "Color and size by this" in the strip's verb row. (Or, the fast path: drag the PageRank header onto the Style track's bar; the header shows "drag to a track" the first time.)
- **What you see:** the Style track opens with a "Map PageRank" style clip; the graph colors and sizes every node by its value and a legend appears beside it. Its controls (palette from a list by kind, size range, missing-value color) appear in the source monitor; turning one recolors live.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch on the verb, or a drag onto a track.

**8. Keep only strong families (filter)**

- **What you do:** press "Back to PageRank" on the monitor's frame (the monitor moved to the style clip in step 7), then "Filter by this"; a filter clip lands at the median value, so about half the families go at once and the change is visible. Pull its right trim handle (the tab outside the clip edge, with a one-time arrow "pull to set"). Or set the same number with the first knob in the source monitor.
- **What you see:** while you hold the handle, a PageRank scale pops out under it with the count kept; the graph hides weaker families as you pull, with a tick at each value step. Raising the hand above the scale makes each centimeter count for less, and the value freezes after a brief stillness so the release does not jitter it. Release at about 0.07; the clip prints "above 0.07". The banner at the graph reads "Showing X of Y" with the counts.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch drag on the trim handle, or the knob in the source monitor.

**9. Write a note**

- **What you do:** press the "+ Neighbors, 1 hop" clip to show the group, then press "Note" in its verb row (it reads "Note on: Medici and 6 partners"); type on the in-world keyboard or dictate "Central by marriage, not by wealth".
- **What you see:** a note clip on the Notes track, attached to that group and frozen to its seven members, a pin on that group in the graph, and the text on the clip face. The note editor has a caret: press between two words to put it there.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch; in-world keyboard or its microphone key.

**10. Undo a mistake**

- **What you do:** first, a fresh mistake: you just dropped a stray "Hide labels" clip. Press Y (or the Undo chip). Second, an older one: the selection clip says "+ Neighbors, 2 hops" because you chose the wrong row from the drawer's recent list, so your note covers too many families. Press that clip and turn its hops knob back to 1.
- **What you see:** the stray clip leaves and the labels come back; nothing else replays. While you turn the hops knob, the ripple bar reads "under a second -- the note 'Central by marriage' would change from 12 families to 7 -- replays 5 clips", and on release every dependent clip fills in order as it replays (flashing instead is a Settings choice). The selection shrinks; PageRank, style and filter stay as they were. The note clip shows "members changed: -5"; press it and it asks "Update this note to the 7 families now selected?"; press Update. A small tick on the Neighbors clip records the edit, and "Keep the old one too" on the ripple bar would have kept the 2-hop version on a branch instead. If the result is wrong, Y puts the knob back. This half of the step needs graphty-element's replay (the first stage under Known risks); before it lands, the only path to the old mistake is Y back through the note, filter, style and PageRank, losing them, and the file does not pretend otherwise.
- **Controls:** Y or the Undo chip; trigger / pinch on the clip and its knob (on hands, pinch the knob and move the hand down); one press on Update. To look back first: shuttle back on the off stick (or pinch the ruler and slide); the graph's banner reads "Looking at step 6 of 11 on Main -- new steps go to the end"; stick click or the "End" chip returns. A verb pressed while looking back jumps the playhead to the end first and shows the result there; if its target is not in the end state, the verb row says so before you press ("lands at step 12; Strozzi is hidden there by 'above 0.07'").

**11. Save**

- **What you do:** press the project chip, then Save. The first time, it asks for a name (suggested: "Florentine families -- Medici").
- **What you see:** a "Saved" marker on the ruler (a marker, not a step). The whole timeline is saved: every clip, disabled ones, branches, camera keyframes, the story, and each clip's computed result, so a later walk-through reads results instead of recomputing. The project is saved where the 2D page saves on that device, and the 2D page opens it: until the 2D page has a timeline view of its own, it shows the enabled clips of the branch you saved on as its ordinary linear history, and keeps the other branches, disabled clips and the Story in the file untouched for the next time the headset opens it. The project page says this on Save the first time a project has a second branch.
- **Controls:** trigger / pinch on the project chip and Save.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule:** two filter clips side by side are AND ("PageRank above 0.07", then "degree at least 3"). For OR, drag the second onto the first and drop on "Any of"; they nest into a group clip. For NOT, drop on "Except", or turn the filter's Invert toggle. The razor splits a group back into its steps. Several categories are one filter with the Choices control ("type is account, phone or device"), not one clip each. A rule that selects without hiding is "Select where" in the drawer's Selection section: it opens the same rule builder and lands a rule clip directly on the Selection track; "Select where" on a filter clip copies that filter's rule there. Each filter clip has a description line, carried into recipes and exports.
- **Tuning algorithm options:** press the run and turn damping or iterations; dependent clips replay. Live replay while the hand moves runs only when the element's estimate for the whole downstream replay is under about 8 ms a frame; otherwise the knob shows a ghost value and the count it would give, and replays on release, in a worker, with the clip filling as it goes.
- **Comparing two runs (keep both):** three paths, all ending in a comparison. (1) The obvious one: press the Louvain run, turn resolution to 1.5, and press "Keep the old one too" on the ripple bar: the edit becomes a variant on a new branch and the first run stays. (2) Press "Variant of this" on the Louvain clip first, then turn the copy's knob. (3) Duplicate the clip on the same branch and turn the copy; then "Compare with" on one run (or drop one on the other's "Compare" zone) opens the two-run view: a group-overlap table for community runs, rank in each side by side for value runs. Only the tracks that differ are doubled after a fork. The diff lane appears between the branches; dragging the playhead into it marks the graph by added (plus badge), removed (ghost outline) and changed (delta mark), each also colored, and the ranked strip shows each node's rank in both branches side by side. New clips land on the branch the playhead is on; the other is dimmed, and the graph's banner names the branch ("on: Resolution 1.5"). For more than two branches (a sweep of four resolutions), the Branches list on the project page is a table: one row per branch, one column per chosen result (modularity, number of groups).
- **Showing one run's colors while keeping another:** a style clip's column slot names which run it reads ("Communities from: Louvain 1.0"). On one branch, with two runs side by side, press the style clip and pick the other run in its slot. Across branches this is not possible: a style on one branch cannot read a run on another, and the file does not offer it. Undo always takes back the edit together with its consequences; to keep a consequence of an edit without the edit, keep both runs on one branch.
- **What-if removal (remove and re-measure):** select the nodes to remove (two brokers by In and Out at ranks 1 and 2), then "Remove and re-measure" in the selection's More. It shows the runs that measured the graph before (Betweenness, Louvain) with check boxes, all checked, and lands an "Except these" filter followed by fresh copies of the checked runs, as a variant on a new branch. The diff lane then compares old and new measurements: who gained brokerage, which components split, and the summary row (components, diameter, largest group). If you build the same thing by hand (fork, Except), the new filter lands with "Measured before this: Betweenness -- Re-measure here", and every reader of the old betweenness says "measured before 'Except these'" until you do.
- **Two networks:** "Add table" or "Open" on the data clip with "as a second network" lands a second data clip; a branch can start from it. The diff lane then matches elements by a key column you choose (gene id), and elements only in one network show as added or removed. "Merge with" on the data clip joins the two by that key into one graph with a membership column (only in A, only in B, both) to style or filter by; columns with the same name get each data clip's name as a suffix (logFC_control, logFC_disease), shown in the merge preview.
- **Side by side (Show both):** "Show both" on the diff lane head, or on a second data clip, draws the two branch or network states as two graphs side by side, each about 60 percent of the single graph's width, sharing one set of positions (the seeded layout of their union) and one grab: moving or turning one moves both. The selection in effect is drawn in both; pressing a node in either peeks it with its values in both states in one row ("degree: 4 control, 11 disease"). A diff mark (added, removed, changed) shows on the side that has it. Show both doubles the scene's draw cost, so above the element's frame estimate it draws the second graph without labels, and above twice that it refuses with the node count and offers the diff lane.
- **Entity resolution in bulk (Merge by):** "Merge by" on the data clip (or "Merge all with same column" on a selection's More) takes a key column slot (phone number, normalized name), shows how many groups it finds ("41 groups of 2 to 4"), takes the rule rows once (keep first, last, join, sum, per conflicting attribute) and lands one batch merge clip. Its monitor lists every group with an Exclude check box; the razor splits it into one merge clip per group.
- **Paths, several routes:** with k above 1, the route clip's monitor is a route table: one row per route with length, hop count and its intermediate nodes as chips; pressing a row lights that route; Next and Previous step along it, and each hop shows its edge type and attributes with Note in its verb row.
- **Layers on subsets:** make the subset as a selection or set, then drop a style clip on its "Paint only these" (or "Paint all but these", to fade the rest) zone; a chain mark shows the link. Paint order is set in the Style track head's layer stack, never by moving clips.
- **Paths:** press a node and choose "Route from here"; a route clip lands with From filled and To armed. Press the target in the graph, a row or a search result. Its controls: k routes (up to 20), weight column, direction, edge types. The route's source monitor steps along the path with Next and Previous. Flow and cut use the same From and To slots.
- **Data time:** drag a time column onto the Filter track: a time-window clip with two trim handles (start and end), and step chips and Play on its own face, so the transport stays under the hand when a peek takes the monitor. Its source monitor shows a data-time transport with a bar of edge counts per window along it and change points as ticks, plus a "Metric over time" tab (any run, re-measured per window, as a line), colored and labeled "Data time" to tell it from the history ruler: step back and forward chips (by window, day or week), Play with speed, and a window-length knob. Stepping changes the window clip's value (a run of steps is one undo entry); dependent clips replay. Press "Hold result" on an expensive run to keep it from replaying while you step; it shows "stale" until you press Refresh. Data time is never on the off stick.
- **Recipes:** mark In and Out on the ruler around a span and press "Save as recipe" on the span's bar. The source monitor lists the span's clips as a checklist (camera keyframes, notes and disabled clips unchecked) and turns every node pick into a named input slot ("the flagged account") and every column into a column slot. The recipe goes into the drawer. Dropping it into another project shows its slots; it runs only when each required slot is filled (by the same pick-a-node control) or skipped. Recipes export through the Outbox.
- **Presenting:** drag camera keyframes and notes onto the Story track in the order you want (the track holds references, so reordering moves nothing on the other tracks). A keyframe stores only the pose and the step it was marked at. Each story step has a state choice, "at the end" (the default), "as marked" or "at step N", so views marked early while exploring play over the finished analysis. "Present" on the project page plays the story: the graph eases between poses, and each step shows its chosen state, read from saved results, not recomputed. "Mark view to story" on the camera track head marks and adds in one press. "Record" on the Story track head writes the walk-through as a video to the Outbox.
- **Joining tables:** "Add table" on the data clip: pick the file from the Inbox, pick the key column on each side, see the live match count, Join. The data clip updates and dependent clips replay; clips that no longer apply turn red, are skipped, and say why.
- **Asking the assistant:** "Ask" on the project page takes typed or dictated text. A proposed action arrives as ghost clips at the end of the timeline, shown in the source monitor; "Keep" lands them, "Discard" removes them. The provider key is set on the 2D page.

### How it scales

- **60+ algorithm catalog:** the drawer is searched by typing or dictation, browsed by category and by the kind of value returned, and has a recent row. Each row shows its cost estimate. The timeline never grows a menu per algorithm: every run is one clip kind on the Analysis track.
- **New object types:** a new object either becomes a clip kind on an existing track (a group is a selection clip, a time window is a filter clip) or, if it changes state over time and stacks, a new track. Its facts and verbs appear in the source monitor, which is where every object already is inspected, so no new surface is designed for it.
- **Plugins:** a registered algorithm, layout, reader or writer appears in the drawer, Open or Export; a command appears on the verb row (under More) of every object kind it declares; its controls are generated from its declared options, and its trim handle maps to its marked or first number. A plugin needs no timeline code.
- **Verb rows:** capped at eight per object in a fixed order, with More opening the drawer filtered to that object, so a selection with thirty applicable commands still shows one row.
- **The limit:** long sessions and replay cost. A few hundred clips need ruler zoom and folded tracks; past four open tracks the band scrolls vertically, and during a drag it scrolls itself when the hand nears its top or bottom edge, so a drop target is never out of reach. Editing an early clip on a large graph with slow algorithms downstream is not live: it replays on release, and Hold result trades freshness for speed. More than two branches are a table, not lanes.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options:** a drawer row in its category, with its cost; its options as generated controls; its result read by kind in the source monitor; Color and size by this, Filter by this and Select from work on any node value. A gene-list input is a set slot.
- **A new layout:** a Style drawer row and a style clip with generated controls; Stop on a running one.
- **A new reader or writer:** a format in Open and Add table, or in Export, with its options generated.
- **A new object type:** graphty's own team declares its facts, the object kind its commands accept and the track its clips go on; its verb row is generated. No gesture is designed for it. An object with continuous behavior (play, scrub) gets the one shared transport (step chips, Play with speed, a bar of counts) that the data-time window uses, declared rather than designed.
- **A new option kind** beyond the eight listed needs design work once; until then the option says "Set this on the 2D page".
- **A third-party plugin** never gets a button or a stick direction; its verbs join the drawer and the source monitor's verb row after the built-in ones.

### Why it should work

- Shuttle, step and In/Out marks are the standard of Avid, Premiere, Final Cut and DaVinci Resolve (the J/K/L keys and the shuttle ring on editing consoles); a spring-loaded stick, or a pinch held on the ruler and slid, is the same shuttle ring.
- Editors separate the source monitor (look at a clip, mark it, nothing changes) from the timeline (what you commit). This design uses the same split: looking happens in the monitor, committing lands a clip.
- "Edit the history, not the state" ships in parametric CAD: Autodesk Fusion 360's design timeline sits under the model, you roll back to a feature, change it, and every later feature recomputes; SolidWorks has its rollback bar. Houdini and Blender's modifier stack do the same for procedural models.
- Visualization provenance research supports it: VisTrails (Bavoil et al., 2005) keeps a version tree of visualization pipelines and compares versions; Heer et al. (2008), "Graphical Histories for Visualization", found analysts used a browsable history in Tableau to revisit and communicate work; Kurlander and Feiner's editable graphical histories date from 1988.
- VR animation tools (Quill, Tvori) already put a timeline under a 3D scene and edit it by hand in a headset.
- Undo is weak in most XR apps (no Ctrl+Z, invisible stacks). Here the last edit is one press away, every older step is visible, and a fix in place says before it lands which notes and sets it will change.
- The timeline sits low and close, so hands work near the lap instead of held up at shoulder height.
- A diff answers "what did changing this change?" directly, the way git diff does, instead of asking the reader to compare two pictures from memory.

### What it would teach us

- Do people who have never used an editor understand that changing an old clip changes everything after it, and does naming the notes and sets that will change let them predict the result before it replays?
- Do people grasp "press to look, verb to act", or do they expect a press on a node to select it?
- Is "the clip's handle is its value" readable, or do people still expect clip width to mean time, and use the monitor knob instead?
- Do people keep data time and history apart when both have a transport?
- Do people reach for Undo or for fixing the old clip in place, and does having both confuse them?
- How large a graph and how long a history still feel live when an early clip changes, and how often do people use Hold result?
- Is a diff lane better than two graphs side by side for comparing two runs?
- Do people build a Story, or only replay the history?
- Do people notice and act on "measured before this -- Re-measure here", or do they read the stale numbers anyway?
- Does "Keep the old one too" right after an edit get used, or do people fork before editing once they know about variants?
- Selecting by rank range on the ranked strip versus searching versus pointing at nodes: which do people choose, and for which questions?

### Known risks

- **The concept is heavy for non-editors.** Mitigation: the playhead stays at the end and new clips always append there, so the timeline reads as a log; Undo works the way people expect; editing the past is there when wanted; empty tracks stay folded.
- **The replay engine does not exist yet, and it is the idea.** graphty-element today keeps a journal of commands and a linear undo history; nothing re-executes from an edited middle step. All of what follows belongs in graphty-element, since any consumer wants it. What the person can do at each stage:

| Stage of graphty-element's replay                                                   | What works                                                                                                                                                                                        | What does not                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Today: journal and linear Undo                                                      | Steps 1-9 and 11 of the journey; the fresh mistake in step 10; peeking, verbs, the drawer, the monitor, keyframes, notes, recipes as a saved list of commands replayed on new data from the start | The old mistake in step 10: the only path is Undo back through every later step, losing them. No variants, no branches, no diff lane, no disabling an old clip (disabling is a replay), no ripple bar for the past. The design is then an append-only log with Undo, and is no better at history than the plainest prototype |

| Stage 4: dependency tracking                                                        | Replays only what depends on the edit; the 8 ms live gate becomes common                                                                                                                          | --                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

Stage 1 is the first milestone and the measure of whether the idea holds; the file's history claims (coverage area 18) are made for stage 3.

- **The 2D page has no timeline.** The history model (branches, disabled clips, frozen members, the Story) is graphty-element's, but graphty's 2D page shows a linear history. Until the 2D page gets a timeline view of its own, a project made in the headset opens on the desktop as the saved branch's enabled clips; the rest is kept in the file and comes back in the headset. Analysts who clean data and write reports at the desk see half the project there; that is a real split, not solved here.
- **"Measured before" marks need the element to know what each run read.** The rule that a clip reads the graph to its left is simple; detecting that a later clip changed that graph needs each run to record its input (node and edge counts and the filters it saw). Without it, the what-if removal is silently wrong, which is the most harmful failure in this design.
- **Show both costs frame rate.** Two graphs double the draw cost; above the element's estimate it drops labels, and above twice that it refuses and points at the diff lane.
- **Replay cost at scale cannot be designed away.** Editing the past means re-running what depends on it. On 100,000 nodes, fixing an old filter re-runs betweenness. Mitigations (replay on release in a worker, cost on the ripple bar, Hold result) make it bearable, but a held result is a stale result, and that trade is the person's to make. This is the cost of the core idea.
- **Live preview budget.** Knobs and trims preview live only when the element's estimate for the downstream replay (style repaints and filter hides included, not only runs) is under about 8 ms a frame (one frame at 72-90 Hz is 11-14 ms); otherwise ghost value and replay on release. Runs report real progress from a worker or the GPU. The results of a replay are applied to the scene in chunks across frames behind the ghost value, so a release does not land 10,000 material changes in one frame.
- **Shuttling redraws the whole graph.** During a fast shuttle the graph updates only when the playhead rests or at most four times a second, cross-fading instead of animating positions; reduce motion turns every shuttle redraw into a cut.
- **An edit to the past breaks later clips** (a filter whose column a replaced run no longer produces). Mitigation: a broken clip turns red, is skipped, and says why; the ripple bar names it before the edit lands; Undo takes the edit back.
- **Frozen members ask questions.** A note or set whose members changed asks whether to update it to the new members or keep the old ones (Update is the main button). On a long history one upstream fix can raise several of these. Mitigation: they are listed on the ripple bar before the edit and gathered in the Notes and Selection track heads after it, with "Update all".
- **Two clocks.** The history ruler and data time both have a transport. Mitigation: data time lives only in the time-window clip's monitor, in its own color and label, never on the off stick. The learning cost remains.
- **The laptop pairing needs a relay** graphty does not run today. Without it, files reach the headset only through the headset browser's own download folder, and the Inbox is workable only on devices that expose one.
- **Tool modes cause mistakes.** Mitigation: tools are one-shot, named at the pointer, preview on hover or while pinching, and need a flick and return.
- **Looking down at the timeline strains the neck.** Mitigation: tilt, the one-press Lift, monitor and drawer within 20 deg of center, and the step name and banner mirrored at the graph.
- **Camera playback can cause motion sickness.** Mitigation: keyframes store the graph's pose, so playback moves the graph, not you; flights are eased and speed-capped; reduce motion turns them into cuts.
- **Text entry in a headset.** Immersive WebXR offers no system keyboard. Mitigation: an in-world keyboard with a caret, dictation where available, the laptop's keyboard through the pairing, and a drawer that can always be browsed.
- **Tables are slower than a monitor.** Twelve rows by five columns at a time; wide work goes to the laptop.
- **Branches multiply.** Mitigation: two visible at once, more as a table on the project page.
- **Small targets on clips.** Mitigation: clips at least 2.5 deg, trim handles outside the clip that grow on approach, drift on a clip body does nothing, moving needs the grip tab (a hold never lifts a clip), adjacent trim tabs stand high and low, text and target size in Settings, one-handed ruler zoom.
- **Color-only feedback.** Mitigation: diff marks, member badges and broken clips carry badges and labels as well as color.
