**Prototype 24: Hand Menu and Windows** -- The platform-default app: the graph is a volume wearing a toolbar ornament, an always-visible filter-step ornament and a layer-legend ornament; one tabbed main window renders the same generated panels as the desktop app; a popover of verbs opens beside whatever you pinch; any object or task can be torn off into its own small window; a hand menu (palm up on Quest and Galaxy XR, a lap tray on Vision Pro) holds ten common actions; and every destructive verb takes a second click on a ghosted preview of what it will change.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away inside a volume about 45 cm across (scaled up for large graphs). Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands everywhere; eyes where the browser exposes them (Vision Pro's gaze-and-pinch pointer; Galaxy XR to be confirmed); controllers on Quest and Galaxy XR; graphty's in-scene keyboard everywhere, plus a paired keyboard or the platform's keyboard where the browser passes keys into an immersive page (to be checked per device); dictation where the platform offers a recognizer; files through the headset browser's own file picker (leave VR, pick, re-enter; the page and the session's state stay) or, once the headset and laptop are paired, the laptop's 2D page. Everything works with either hand, with one hand, and seated. The main window sits 1.0 to 1.3 m away, 10 to 15 degrees below eye level and about 30 degrees to the dominant side, so it never covers the graph; a setting puts it below the volume instead, which spares a seated neck the repeated turn. "Bring windows here" (long press on Fit in the hand menu, or on the toolbar) re-centers the volume and every window around your current heading and height.

### Popovers

Each popover shows the object's name and key values, up to six verbs with counts, All actions and Open in window. Pinching the popover's header opens the full Inspector. The six are the object's declared top verbs; All actions reaches the rest. Verbs marked (arms) are destructive and take a second click.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on, standing or seated.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families float inside the volume, with Pucci drifting alone because he has no marriages. Under the volume sit the window bar, the toolbar ornament and the status line ("16 families, 20 marriages"). The filter ornament on the left holds only a "+" chip and "No filters -- 16 of 16"; the layer ornament on the right holds one row, "Default look". The main window, off to your dominant side, opens on Data > Overview. A one-time hint beside your off hand says "Turn this palm up for the hand menu" (on Vision Pro: "Look down for the tray").
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the volume's window bar and drag it a little closer and lower. Pinch both ends of the window bar and spread your hands to 150 percent. Drag the rim a quarter turn. Pinch empty space inside the volume.
    - **What you see:** The scale readout clicks into its 150 percent detent. The graph's popover opens beside the volume: "16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci alone), undirected, density 0.17", with Characterize, Find, Run, Fit, Note and Picture. The main window's Overview page shows the same counts and the attributes the sample carries. If you turned too far, Back on the view ornament returns the previous view.
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag (window bar, rim), two-hand pinch on the frame, quick pinch on empty volume.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Look at the node labeled Medici and pinch with your resting hand (on Quest 3, point the ray at it and pinch). In a graph too big to scan, you would poke Find on the hand menu and type "Med" on the in-scene keyboard, or dictate it.
    - **What you see:** Medici is selected as the pinch lands, and its popover opens beside it: "Medici -- 6 marriages", with Neighbors (1 hop), Route from here, Note, Hide, Pin and Style this. Find would have listed "Medici" as its one result; pinching the result frames the node and opens the same popover.
    - **Controls:** look-and-pinch (or ray and pinch); Find with the in-scene keyboard or dictation.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Neighbors.
    - **What you see:** The button reads "Neighbors -- 6 families" (on Quest, resting the ray on it first also lights them). The pinch selects Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni with the Medici, a soft hull appears around the seven, and the status line reads "Selected 7 families, 7 marriages among them". The hull's popover takes over: Name and keep, Run on this, Filter to this, Style this, Lay out this, Note. Neighbors' "..." would have offered 2 hops ("11 families") and edge-type checkboxes; Undo reads "Undo Select neighbors".
    - **Controls:** quick pinch on a verb.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Analyze on the toolbar. In the main window's Catalog, pinch "Influence (PageRank)", the first entry under Suggested for this graph (or type or say "pagerank" in the catalog search). Leave Run on at "Whole graph" and pinch Run.
    - **What you see:** The window jumps to Analyze > Catalog. Pinching the entry unfolds its generated form in place: damping 0.85, maximum iterations 100, tolerance 1e-6, weight "none", with Advanced folded, and a cost line "under a second". After Run, the status line reads "PageRank done", a PageRank chip appears in the toolbar's runs well, and a Run window opens beside the volume with a leader line to the chip (the next run's result replaces it unless you pin it).
    - **Controls:** toolbar button, list pinch, generated form, Run button.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read the Run window. Pinch-drag its list to scroll. Pinch the row for Guadagni.
    - **What you see:** The reader is chosen from the result field's kind (a number per node), so it is a ranked list with a histogram tab: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, and so on down to Pucci, lowest at about 0.01. A line at the bottom says how it was produced: "PageRank, damping 0.85, whole graph, 16 families". Pinching Guadagni's row frames that family in the volume and outlines it.
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag to scroll, quick pinch on a row.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the PageRank chip and choose Paint. Then pinch the new layer row in the layer ornament, choose Edit, and on the layer page drag the size range's upper handle to 3x.
    - **What you see:** The Paint button reads "Paint 16 families: color and size by PageRank". After the pinch, a layer row "PageRank: color and size" appears at the top of the layer ornament, its row showing the color ramp from about 0.01 to 0.145 and a size key. The layer page opens in the main window, generated from the style schema; as you drag the handle, sizes follow live, and the field beside it reads "1x to 3x".
    - **Controls:** popover verb, popover Edit, two-handle range slider.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the PageRank chip again and choose Filter from. In the small form that opens, drag the threshold slider down to its 0.070 detent (or type 0.07), then pinch Add step.
    - **What you see:** While you drag, the families below the threshold ghost out of the volume and the form reads "PageRank at least 0.070: keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 of 20 marriages". After Add step, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain; a chip "PageRank at least 0.070 -- 5 of 16" appears in the filter ornament; the status line reads "Showing 5 of 16 families -- 1 filter".
    - **Controls:** popover verb, slider with detents or typed field, button.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Look at the Medici and pinch; choose Note. Pinch the field's microphone and say "Married into six families; the hub of the network" (where a recognizer runs in the session), or type it on the in-scene keyboard that opens under the field. Pinch Done.
    - **What you see:** A one-sentence note field opens in the popover with the dictated text, editable before Done. After Done, a small note marker sits on the Medici, and the note appears in Notes and History > Notes with a Go to target link.
    - **Controls:** look-and-pinch, popover verb, dictation or the in-scene keyboard.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You reach for the PageRank layer row to check its palette, but your pinch lands on the row's grip and drags it below "Default look". The graph turns back to one color and one size, and the layer ornament now shows "Default look" on top, so it wins. Turn your off palm up and poke Undo, which reads "Undo Move layer".
    - **What you see:** The layer row returns to the top and the colors and sizes come back. Redo now reads "Redo Move layer". The same step is the top entry in Notes and History > History; had the mistake been several steps back, you would pinch its entry to preview that state, then choose Revert only this step (which keeps every later step) or Go back to here. On Vision Pro the same Undo is on the lap tray or the toolbar. Turning and scaling the graph are not in this list; they belong to Back on the view ornament.
    - **Controls:** hand menu Undo (named), or the History tab.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Turn your off palm up and poke Save (or Save on the toolbar's Project tab).
    - **What you see:** Because the project is still the unsaved sample, a small save sheet asks for a name and suggests "Florentine families -- PageRank"; pinch Save. The status line reads "Saved Florentine families -- PageRank". Later saves go straight through.
    - **Controls:** hand menu Save, sheet with a suggested name.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Pinch "+" in the filter ornament, then Rule. The rule sheet is the desktop's rule editor: rows of attribute, comparison and value, where the value list shows how many elements have each value; rows group under AND, OR and NOT with indentation, and a live count shows what the whole rule keeps. "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" is four rows and one group. A week later, pinch its chip, Edit rule, and the same sheet reopens with the rule as built. The same editor serves Select by rule, rule sets and layer targets.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Pinch a run chip, Re-run with options: the run's form opens with its current values. Change damping from 0.85 to 0.75 with the stepper and pinch Run as new; both runs stay. For Louvain, Sweep beside resolution opens from, to and step steppers (0.5 to 2.0 by 0.5, no typing) and makes four runs, shown in one Run window with a small result per run. Logarithmic values (tolerance 1e-6) are an exponent stepper with a typed field. ForceAtlas2's 13 options are long, so Open in window lowers the form to waist height; Advanced folds half of them; Reset restores defaults.
- **Comparing two runs.** In Analyze > Results, tick two runs and pinch Compare, or use Compare with on one run chip and pinch the other. The Compare page is generated by field kind: two rankings give a rank-change table sorted by how far each node moved; two community results give a cross table of who moved between groups and an agreement score. For two networks (disease and control), Project > Add network, then Merge networks (union, intersection or difference, by a key) and Split view: a second volume beside the first, with a Sync cameras switch and linked selection.
- **Layers on subsets.** Select a set (a rectangle, a rule, Select from on a run, or a group in the Groups table), then Style this on its hull: a new layer targeting that set appears at the top of the layer ornament and its page opens. The W20 case (diverging logFC centered on zero with gray for missing, size by p-value on a log scale, thick outline on six named genes) is three layers built this way, then put in order by dragging rows in the ornament. Why on any node's popover lists the layers that painted it, top first, with the value each used.
- **Recipes.** In Notes and History > History, tick the steps to keep (two runs, a layer, a filter), pinch Save as recipe and accept or type a name. On another dataset, Analyze > Recipes > Replay opens an input-mapping form ("weight in the recipe is which column here?") and shows what each step will change before it runs.
- **Undoing an old step among look-alikes.** Every History entry names its object and values ("Filter: PageRank at least 0.070", "Style: PageRank layer, size 1x to 3x"), not just "Filter" or "Style". Pinch the entry eight steps back: the volume previews that state. Its popover offers Revert only this step when no later step depends on it (armed, it ghosts the result with the seven later steps kept), or Go back to here, which arms with "drops 7 later steps" and keeps them in Redo.
- **Typing an id (fraud ring).** Pinch Find; the in-scene keyboard opens. Find matches any part of a name or id, so typing 48213 on the digit row is enough for ACC-48213, and the results list appears after three characters. Pinching a result frames the node and opens its popover; Route from here, then Find again for ACC-10077 while the status line says "Pick the target", sets B without ever pointing at it.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** The file is usually on the laptop. Today's path: leave VR, pick the file in the headset browser's own file picker (from its downloads or a cloud drive), re-enter; the session resumes where it was. Once the headset and laptop are paired (a short code shown on both), the file dropped on graphty's 2D page there appears in Project > Open > Laptop inbox in the headset. The import sheet shows the detected format (with an override list), the format's options form, a column-mapping list where each of the 10 to 40 source columns is a row (its name, first values, inferred type and a role dropdown: id, source, target, attribute, skip), and the load report with a fix per issue. Add data > Join picks the key column on each side and shows a live match count ("412 of 430 rows matched") before Confirm.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor puts it in its family in the Catalog (or a new family group), in search under both names, in the command palette and in Run on this. Its options render in the generated form; its result fields choose their readers; its run chip gets the standard run verbs. The "Biology: enrichment" plugin with a gene-list input and two numbers is a new family group, a form whose gene-list input is a set picker (any set or the selection; a long pasted list comes from the paired laptop, never typed in the headset) plus two sliders, and a ranked reader.
- **A new layout.** A row in the Layout tab's list, its engine in the dropdown, its options in the generated form, and Lay out this on any set's popover.
- **A new data source or format writer.** A reader appears in Project > Open with its options form (a service that turns a gene list into a network takes the same set picker as its list input); a writer appears in Export with its options form.
- **A new object type.** These come from graphty itself, not from plugins (the six public calls add catalog entries, not object kinds). It declares its verbs with a priority, its key values and the tab its page lives in. The popover shows its top six, All actions reaches the rest, its page is generated from the same panel description, Open in window works on it, and it can carry notes and be a step in history.
- **A third-party plugin.** If it registers through the six public calls with descriptors, it reaches the headset at the same moment it reaches the desktop, with no headset code. An option or result kind the headset renderer does not know yet shows "Set this on the 2D page" or a plain value table, so nothing silently disappears. A plugin that ships its own hand-written panel instead of descriptors is shown in the headset as "Open on the 2D page".
- **The practical limit.** This scheme is exactly as complete as the desktop's panels, and no better than they are at work inside the graph. Every new feature arrives as a form or a list, which keeps coverage but adds no spatial advantage; a feature that wants a spatial gesture (drawing a pattern in 3D, flying a camera path) gets the desktop's form unless someone designs more. The other limit is space and attention: three torn-off windows plus the main window plus a second volume is the most a person can track, and the scheme caps it there.

### Why it should work

- It is the pattern both platform makers tell developers to use. Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for visionOS put toolbars and tab bars in ornaments attached to a window, objects' actions in popovers, multi-step tasks in sheets, and input on look and pinch (developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ornaments; .../windows; .../eyes). Meta's Horizon OS UI Set gives panels, tabs and buttons with standard sizes for hands and controllers. People who use either headset already know these controls.
- Hand menus are shipped and studied: Microsoft's Mixed Reality Toolkit hand menu (palm up, buttons on the hand's edge, poked by the other hand) was the standard quick-access menu on HoloLens 2 (learn.microsoft.com/windows/mixed-reality/design/hand-menu).
- Gaze-shifting was shown to let one pinch act directly on near targets and indirectly on far ones depending only on where you look (Pfeuffer, Alexander, Chong and Gellersen, "Gaze-shifting", UIST 2015). Gaze plus pinch in VR was faster and less tiring than reaching for distant objects (Pfeuffer, Mayer, Mardanbegi and Gellersen, "Gaze + Pinch Interaction in Virtual Reality", SUI 2017), and Apple then built visionOS on it.
- A magnified list for ambiguous picks follows progressive refinement: SQUAD (Kopper, Bacim and Bowman, IEEE 3DUI 2011) selected cluttered small targets more accurately than ray casting by turning one hard pick into easy picks from a list.
- Windows placed around the body at chosen distances were measured in the Personal Cockpit (Ens, Finnegan and Irani, CHI 2014): world-fixed and body-fixed layouts near arm's length beat head-fixed ones for switching between several windows, which supports a main window plus a few torn-off ones.
- Tear-off panels and palettes are decades old on the desktop (MacPaint's tear-off menus, 1984; Photoshop's floating panels), so "this one stays open next to me" needs no teaching.
- Previewing a verb's effect before committing is shipped: Microsoft Office's Live Preview (2007) shows a style on the document while you rest on it, and showing counts before destructive actions follows Shneiderman's direct manipulation principle of visible, reversible steps (1983). Arming on a first click and acting on a second follows iOS's swipe-to-delete (the swipe arms a Delete button, a tap confirms), which needs no hover and so works on Vision Pro.
- Named undo ("Undo Typing" in macOS, "Undo Move Layer" in Photoshop) and a history panel are the shared standard every desktop user expects.
- Our own research found the parity floor is a model that renders one panel description on desktop and headset (the Concierge and Cards on the Volume models in design/xr/vr-control-surfaces.md): nothing is maintained twice and every function has a findable home.

### What it would teach us

- How far the plain platform pattern goes: which of the 22 areas reach "natural" with only windows, popovers and ornaments, and which need anything more. This is the baseline every other prototype should be measured against.
- Whether the floated-desktop failure (Unity EditorVR and Unreal VR Mode, which retreated to curated palettes over text density, small targets and trigger overload) is avoided by popovers, ornaments and tear-off, or only postponed.
- Whether gaze-shifting holds on real graphs: does looking at a node and pinching from the lap beat a ray, and how often does the "which one?" list appear at 77, 400 and 3,000 nodes?
- Whether always-visible filter and layer ornaments prevent the "what am I looking at?" errors (a filter forgotten, a layer hidden by one above it), measured by questions people get wrong about the current state.
- Whether six popover verbs are enough, and how often people go to All actions; which verbs belong in the six for each object.
- How many acts the basic journey and the six stress journeys take compared with the desktop app, and where the extra acts are.
- How much a paired keyboard changes the ratings for find, options, notes and naming.
- Whether a hand menu on the palm (or the lap tray on Vision Pro) is found and used, or whether people go to the toolbar every time.
- Whether arming destructive verbs on the first click feels safe or merely slow, and how often a second click confirms something the ghost should have stopped.
- Whether a gaze-hand alignment pick (a fingertip raised into the line of sight, Lystbaek, Rosenberg, Pfeuffer, Gronbaek and Gellersen, ETRA 2022) would beat the "which one?" list for tiny nodes where hand joints are available, without colliding with the pinch.

### Known risks

- **A monitor in a headset.** The scheme can feel like the desktop app floated in front of you, which failed for Unity and Unreal. Mitigation: the graph is the center and the windows sit to the side; the popover handles most single-object work at the object; filters and layers are on the volume, not in the window; tear-off keeps the working form next to the work.
- **Text density and reading speed.** Tables and forms hold fewer rows at readable sizes, and lookups are slower than on a monitor. Mitigation: text size is a setting; tables page by 15 to 25 rows; any table can be torn off and pulled closer; sorting and Select from replace scanning.
- **The panel description must exist.** Today the desktop app's panels are hand-built, and there is no DOM in an immersive VR session, so nothing on the desktop can simply be shown in the headset. What does exist as data -- algorithm and layout option descriptors, result fields, the registries behind the catalogs, the style schema -- is enough to generate the option forms, readers, catalogs and layer pages; everything else (tables, the rule editor, sheets, settings) must be written once as panel descriptions, and the headset needs its own renderer for them in the 3D scene, with its own text fields and keyboard. This is the largest engineering cost of the prototype. Mitigation: convert panels in the order the journeys use them; list any panel not yet converted as "Open on the 2D page" so nothing silently disappears.
- **File and key handoff needs a pairing.** The laptop inbox and Send to laptop assume the headset and the laptop share a project, which graphty does not do today. Mitigation: the headset browser's own file picker and downloads folder work now (leave VR, pick, re-enter); a pairing by short code is the later, better path.
- **No eye tracking on Quest 3.** Gaze-shifting is the scheme's best selection, and the most common headset lacks it on the web. Mitigation: the snapping ray and the "which one?" list are the full scheme there; selection at scale goes through rules, tables and results anyway.
- **Palm-up conflicts.** Quest, Galaxy XR and Vision Pro all use hand gestures for their own menus. Mitigation: the hand menu's buttons are poked by the other hand, never pinched by the palm hand; on Vision Pro, where both palm up and the turned-over hand belong to the system, the menu is a lap tray instead; the same ten actions are always on the toolbar too.
- **Two-hand gestures colliding.** Adding to the selection (a held pinch plus a click) and scaling (two pinches) both use two hands. Mitigation: scaling takes only the volume's frame, and inside the volume two pinches always mean add.
- **Too many windows.** Torn-off windows pile up and frame rate drops. Mitigation: three at most, a dock bar for the rest, "Bring windows here" to re-center, and the second volume of a split view drawn at reduced detail on Quest.
- **Arm and neck fatigue.** Ray pointing at windows for a long session tires the shoulder, and turning 30 degrees to the main window again and again tires a seated neck. Mitigation: look-and-pinch lets the hand rest in the lap where eyes are tracked; long forms open lowered to waist height; controllers allow a resting wrist; the main window can sit below the volume instead; tear-off keeps the working form beside the graph.
- **Text entry.** Names, ids and notes need typing, and in-session keyboards are poor or absent. Mitigation: graphty's own in-scene keyboard on every headset, with a caret and completions from the data; suggested names everywhere a name is needed; matching on any part of an id; dictation where the platform offers it; a paired keyboard when present; and the laptop's 2D page for long text, keys and reports.
- **Accidental drags.** A click that moves a little becomes a drag (as in step 10). Mitigation: a 3-degree drag threshold on rows and nodes, rows that reorder only by their grip, named undo one poke away, and destructive verbs that only act on a second click after their ghost preview.
- **Ornaments crowd the volume.** A long filter list or many layers make the side ornaments tall. Mitigation: the ornaments show the top eight and scroll; Open in window moves either into a full window.
