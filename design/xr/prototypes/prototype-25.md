**Prototype 25: Search Everything** -- A command palette under the graph is the front door to every command, node, attribute, algorithm, view and setting; picking a command turns the field into that command with fill-in slots, and an eight-slice pie at each object gives its seven top verbs plus More (the palette scoped to that object).

No metaphor. It is the desktop's command palette (VS Code, Spotlight, Raycast) made the main surface instead of a shortcut, plus a pie menu at objects for the verbs used every minute.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. Four surfaces sit around it and never move while you work: the field below the graph, the results drawer to the left of the graph, the reader panel to the right, and the stack row of chips between the graph and the field.

- **The field** is a strip about 35 cm wide, about 50 cm from you, its center about 30 degrees below your eye line (just below the graph's lower edge as you see it), tilted to face your eyes. Text is at least 0.6 degrees tall (about 5 mm at that distance); a text size setting scales the field, drawer and readers together.
- **The drawer** (results, a slot's picker, a folded-out form) opens as a panel to the left of the graph, at the graph's height and tilted toward you, joined to the field's left end by a thin line. It shows up to 8 rows and scrolls. It never opens downward, so reading results never pushes your eyes lower than the field, and it never covers the graph, so a row can light its target beside it.
- **Readers** (result panels) open to the right of the graph at the same height.
- **Recenter**: the system recenter, or "recenter" in the field, places all four around your current head pose, seated or standing. A setting moves the field up beside the drawer, to the graph's left at graph height, for people who should not look down at all.

Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR (hands or controllers), Apple Vision Pro (eyes and hands; PS VR2 Sense controllers if Safari exposes them). Pointing is a ray from the dominant hand or controller, or Vision Pro's gaze-and-pinch pointer. Text comes from a paired Bluetooth keyboard, the system keyboard where the browser offers one, an in-scene keyboard the page draws, closed-vocabulary dictation, or suggestion chips (picking instead of typing). Everything is reachable seated and by ray, so nothing needs a long reach.

Which pie: a hold on a node or edge that is part of a selection of two or more opens the Selection pie, as a right-click on a selected item does on the desktop; to get one node's own pie, click it first. A pie verb with every slot defaulted runs at once and leaves its sentence in the field to adjust. A pie never gains a slice when a plugin loads; plugins arrive through More.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D start page, with nothing loaded, choose Enter VR. Click the field. It is empty, so it shows suggestions; click "Open a sample", and in the list of 16 samples click "Florentine families" (or type "flor").
    - **What you see:** Before any data, only the field floats in front of you, reading "Search everything", with three suggestions in the drawer: Open a sample, Reopen a recent project, Open a file. A one-line hint under the field reads "Click a suggestion, or type anything". The sample list shows each dataset with a one-line description and its size ("16 families, 20 marriages"). On click the graph appears at chest height above the field, and the selection chip reads "Nothing selected".
    - **Controls:** click (field, suggestion, result row).

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Click the field. The first suggestion is "Describe this graph"; click it. Then close both fists around the graph and turn it a little to see its depth.
    - **What you see:** A reader opens to the right: "16 families, 20 marriages, undirected, unweighted, density 0.17, 2 pieces: 15 connected families and Pucci alone", and the node attributes the sample carries. While you turn the graph, the field, the drawer and the reader stay put. The field's suggestions now read "Who matters most (Influence)", "Find communities", "Find a family".
    - **Controls:** click (suggestion), grab with both fists.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Click the field and type "med" (or dictate "Medici"). Click the row "Medici -- node, 6 ties".
    - **What you see:** After three letters the Nodes group shows "Medici" first; the ray over the row lights the Medici node in the graph. Clicking the row selects Medici; Medici is already in view, so the graph does not move (a result out of view glides into view over 0.3 s, or cuts with a fade under Reduce motion). The selection chip now reads "Medici". The row's trailing buttons offered Go to (frame without selecting) and Inspect.
    - **Controls:** click (field), type, click (result row).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold on Medici until the pie draws, and move right to "Neighbors". Release.
    - **What you see:** The pie shows Medici's eight verbs. On release the six neighbors (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni) join Medici in the selection, and the field holds the step, adjustable: "Selected neighbors of [Medici] within [1] hop -- 7 families, 7 marriages". Clicking the [1] chip would show a stepper; at 2 the preview line reads "12 families", and releasing on 2 would redo the step in place (a selection is a cheap step). You leave it at 1. The selection chip reads "Selection: 7 families".
    - **Controls:** pinch-hold (pie), move and release on a slice; optional click on a slot chip.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Click the field and type "pager" (or "influence"). Click "Run Influence (PageRank)". Press Go.
    - **What you see:** The field reads "Run Influence (PageRank) on [whole graph] [options: defaults]": a typed command starts on the whole graph even though seven families are selected, and the target chip's picker would offer "Selection (7)". The preview line reads "on 16 of 16 families, under 1 s, no preconditions". Go shows a short progress bar in the field, then "Ran Influence (PageRank) on whole graph".
    - **Controls:** click, type, click (result), Go.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Look at the reader that opened to the right; move the ray down its rows.
    - **What you see:** A ranked list, chosen because the result is a number per node: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.078, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069, Castellani 0.069, Bischeri 0.068, Peruzzi 0.067, Salviati 0.061, and on to Pucci 0.010 at the bottom. A histogram strip sits above the rows. The header names the run: "Influence (PageRank), whole graph, damping 0.85". The ray over a row lights that family in the graph.
    - **Controls:** ray over rows.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Click the field and type "color by pagerank". Click the first result; the palette slot opens; click the sequential palette "Blues"; Go. Then type "size by pagerank", click the result, and in the range slot drag the upper thumb to 3x; Go.
    - **What you see:** The first sentence reads "Color [all nodes] by [PageRank] with [Blues] as [amounts]"; as the ray rests on each palette swatch the graph previews it. After Go a chip "Color: PageRank" joins the stack row and a legend appears beside the reader. The second reads "Size [all nodes] by [PageRank] from [1x] to [3x]", and the nodes grow live as you drag. A second chip "Size: PageRank" joins the stack row. (The faster path: pinch-hold the reader's header and release on Paint, which applies the run's suggested style in one act, then adjust its slots in the field.)
    - **Controls:** type, click (result, swatch), drag a range thumb, Go.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Type "keep pagerank >= 0.07" and press Enter (or Go).
    - **What you see:** As you type, the field turns your words into the sentence "Filter: keep [nodes] where [PageRank] [at least] [0.07]", and the preview line reads "keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages" while the eleven others fade in the graph. After Go, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain; the stack row gains the chip "PageRank at least 0.07" and shows "Showing 5 of 16". (Without typing: the run's pie, Filter from..., with the same number slot over the histogram.)
    - **Controls:** type, Go.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold Medici and release on Note (left). Type "Married into six families; the hub of the network" on the keyboard that comes up, and press Go.
    - **What you see:** The field reads "Note on [Medici]: [text]" with the text slot open. On Quest the system keyboard rises over the scene and hands the text back when you close it; with a paired keyboard you type straight into the field. After Go a small note marker sits on Medici, and "notes" in the field would list it.
    - **Controls:** pinch-hold (pie), release on a slice, type, Go.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You mean to rename the filter step, so you pinch-hold its chip in the stack row, but your flick lands on Remove (lower left) instead of Rename (left). Remove waits for its key, which reads "Remove" in the warning color, with the preview "Remove filter step: brings back 11 families"; you press it out of habit, and all 16 families return. Click the Undo chip.
    - **What you see:** The Undo chip read "Undo: Remove filter PageRank at least 0.07". After the click the filter chip is back, the graph shows 5 families again, and a Redo chip sits beside Undo until your next change. Typing "history" would have listed every step so far, newest first, each with "Go back to here", "Preview" and "Undo only this".
    - **Controls:** pinch-hold (pie on a chip), the Remove key, click (Undo chip).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Type "save" and press Enter.
    - **What you see:** The field reads "Save project as [Florentine families -- PageRank]" with the suggested name already filled; you keep it. The preview line says "new project; autosave on". The journey takes about 29 acts with typing counted as one act per word or pick, against about 25 clicks in the desktop app. Steps 3, 5, 7, 8 and 11 depend on typing; each also has a no-typing path (suggestions, Browse, pies), at two or three acts more.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Type "filter type:account and (degree>20 or risk_score>=0.8) and created in last 30 days". The field parses it into clause chips with a bracket around the OR group, and the date phrase into a date-range chip; a word it cannot place turns red with "Did you mean created_at?" as a chip, and Go stays gray until it is resolved. Clicking the rule slot folds out the clause rows in the drawer (attribute, comparison, value per row; AND / OR / NOT buttons group the ticked rows), for people who would rather build than type. A week later, pinch-hold the filter chip and release on Edit rule: the same sentence returns to the field with every chip editable, and the preview line shows the new count before Go.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Type "louvain resolution 1.5": the field fills the resolution slot directly and shows the rest at their defaults. Click [options] to fold out the generated form: Katz, for instance, shows alpha (stepper and slider), beta, max iterations (integer stepper), tolerance (exponent stepper at 1e-6), normalized (switch), weight attribute (attribute list), with advanced options folded and Reset at the bottom. For a sweep, type "0.5, 1, 1.5, 2" into the resolution slot: Go runs four runs, each its own reader tab. Changing a slot of the run that just finished offers Replace or Keep both; the run's pie Re-run with... puts an older run's sentence back in the field with its values, and its Go always keeps both.
- **Comparing two runs.** Type "compare" and pick "Compare runs [A] with [B]"; A and B start on the two newest runs of the same algorithm. To change one, click its chip and click a reader tab (slot picking lights the tabs that fit), or type "louvain 1.0". The comparison reader shows the two side by side: for communities, a table of which groups split or merged with counts; for rankings, rank changes with arrows, and "3 families change rank".
- **Route to a node you cannot see.** Pinch-hold the flagged node, release on Route from here. The sentence is "Route from [ACC-48213] to [?] by [fewest steps] [1 route]". The [to] slot opens: the ray's tip reads "Pick: target node" and every visible node lights; the far node is behind the cluster, so you type "10077" instead and click the result, which is marked "hidden by filter" and fills the slot anyway. - **Layers on subsets.** Any style command's first slot is its target: "Color [set: Medici in-laws] by [wealth]" or "Border [rule: degree>5] [3 px]" makes a layer that paints only that target. - **Recipes.** Type "save as recipe": the sentence is "Save [steps: last 6] as recipe [name]", and the steps slot opens the history list with checkboxes so you can drop a stray step. On new data, "run recipe Ring triage" shows one slot per input the recipe needs ("[attribute for weight]", "[set to start from]"), each with its picker.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** "join table" asks for [file] and [key]. The file slot offers the headset's files, a URL, and recent files; a file on the laptop has to reach the headset first (a cloud drive the headset browser can see, or a URL). Then the import form folds out in the drawer: the first rows as a table, a role list per column (key, attribute, skip), the inferred type per column, and the preview line "412 of 480 rows match node names; 68 do not (show them)". Go joins it. Opening a new graph file uses the same form with node id, source and target roles.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor's plain name, technical name and aliases become search names ("Run Gene set enrichment (GSEA)"; "gsea", "enrichment" also match). Its options become slots, each rendered by kind; its category places it in Browse, a new category becoming a new family there. A list-of-genes option is a many-targets slot: it takes a set, the selection, ticked search rows or typed names, and reports how many it found. Its result fields choose its readers. "Suggested for this graph" can offer it if its descriptor states the preconditions it needs.
- **A new layout.** Appears in the [layout] slot's list and as "lay out as <name>"; its options fold out from [options].
- **A new object type** (a path, a time slice, a group of groups). It declares its verbs as commands with a typed target slot, so it can fill any slot that accepts it, and names up to seven of them for its pie by direction (read up, take away lower left, note left); with none named, its pie has Inspect up, More down, and its first declared verbs in the other slices. It also gets a row kind in search results. An object with no shape in the scene (a time slice) is reached through search, its reader rows and the target picker; slot picking lights it wherever it is drawn.
- **A third-party plugin.** Registering anything registers its search names and slots, so it is reachable from the field the moment it loads. The pies stay stable: a plugin never adds a slice to an existing object's pie. A user who wants a plugin verb in a pie swaps it into one of the three free slices through "customize pie [node]"; the five fixed directions never change.
- **A new kind of option.** An option whose kind matches none of the slot kinds above renders as a Text slot with the descriptor's description under it, so it is never unreachable, only less convenient, until a slot kind is added for it.
- **The practical limit.** The scheme is only as good as its text entry and its ranking. Without a keyboard, every command costs suggestions or Browse picks. With hundreds of commands, near-duplicate names ("Communities (Louvain)", "Communities by flow") need good ranking and descriptions on each row. Work that is spatial rather than nameable fits it badly: drawing a pattern, outlining a callout by hand, placing a camera path, moving many nodes into a shape. Those get a command that arms one gesture (a box, a click per point, one drag), or go to the 2D page.

### Why it should work

- Command palettes are how desktop power users already reach everything: Sublime Text's Goto Anything and Command Palette (2011), VS Code's command palette (code.visualstudio.com/docs/getstarted/userinterface), Spotlight (2005), Alfred and Raycast; and the Cmd+K bar in Linear, Slack and GitHub. graphty's 2D app already ships a command palette, so the headset version extends something users know.
- Searching a command set scales where menus do not: Ubuntu's HUD (2012) and Microsoft Office's "Tell Me" box (2016) both put search over menu trees too deep to browse. A 60+ algorithm catalog plus plugins is that kind of tree.
- Blender couples a searchable operator menu (F3) with an "Adjust Last Operation" panel that stays open after a command runs, so you change a value after seeing the result instead of guessing it beforehand. That is this scheme's "the last step stays adjustable in the field".
- Pie menus beat linear menus for speed and errors because every item is the same short distance away in its own direction: Callahan, Hopkins, Weiser and Shneiderman, CHI 1988. Marking menus (Kurtenbach and Buxton, 1993-1994) showed experts move from looking to flicking blind when slice positions never change. Blender's pie menus (2014) ship that idea to a large 3D audience.
- In VR, pie menus did well for system control: "An Evaluation of Pie Menus for System Control in Virtual Reality", NordiCHI 2020, doi 10.1145/3419249.3420146.
- Typing in VR is workable with the right input: Speicher, Feit, Ziegler and Krueger (CHI 2018) measured selection-based VR keyboards, with controller pointing the fastest of those tested; Knierim and colleagues (CHI 2018) and Grubert and colleagues (IEEE VR 2018) found that people type on a real keyboard in VR at close to their normal speed when they can see their hands or a stand-in for them.
- Our own research catalog lists a grammar-aware command palette with ghost slots as a paradigm that reaches every command, node and attribute from one line (design/xr/vr-control-surfaces.md, section 6), and the parity matrix there rates the command palette as the function the keyboard-and-desk models do best.

### What it would teach us

- How much of graphty people reach by typing, how much by suggestions and Browse, and how much by pies, on each device; and whether people without a keyboard stop using the field.
- Whether slot sentences read clearly in a headset, and whether people trust the rule "typed commands start on the whole graph, pie commands start on the object", or still run commands on the wrong target.
- Whether "the last step stays adjustable" is used, and whether Replace versus Keep both is understood after a run.
- Whether pie slices with fixed directional meanings (read up, take away lower left) become blind flicks within a session, and how often a flick lands on the neighboring slice.
- Whether slot picking (the ray's tip naming the slot, only fitting objects lit) is noticed, or people click the graph expecting to select.
- Which closed vocabulary dictation needs to cover, and whether a page-shipped recognizer is accurate enough on node names and ids like ACC-48213.
- Whether the drawer to the left of the graph, rather than under the field, costs more head turns than it saves in neck flexion.
- Act counts and times against the 2D app for the six stress journeys.

### Known risks

- **Text entry carries the scheme.** Without a keyboard the field is weaker. Mitigation: every command has a no-typing path (suggestions, Recent, Browse in three clicks, pies, slot pickers); ask for a paired keyboard at Enter VR; the in-scene keyboard puts the graph's own words and id prefixes as chips above it; "combine" replaces most formula typing.
- **Recall instead of recognition.** A blank field asks people to know what to type. Mitigation: the empty field always shows Suggested for this graph, Recent and Browse; the tour teaches one search, one slot, one pie and Undo; every row carries a one-line description.
- **Wrong target.** A command runs on the selection when the user meant the graph, or the reverse. Mitigation: one rule (typed starts on the whole graph, pie and More start on the object); the target is the first chip of every sentence; the preview line gives counts ("on 7 of 16 families") before Go.
- **Slot picking surprises.** A click on the graph fills a slot instead of selecting. Mitigation: the ray's tip names the slot, only fitting objects light, empty space does nothing, and Escape leaves slot picking.
- **Parser surprises.** "keep pagerank >= 0.07" might be read differently than meant. Mitigation: typed text always becomes a sentence of chips before Go, with the count in the preview line; an unknown word turns red with a suggestion and Go stays gray, never a guess that runs.
- **Neck and eye strain.** Mitigation: the field sits about 30 degrees below the eye line, inside the comfortable reading range; the drawer opens beside the graph, not further down; a setting moves the field up beside the drawer; recenter follows seated or standing.
- **Accidental pie on a slow click.** Mitigation: the pie needs a still hand for 0.4 s (adjustable), and releasing in the center does what the click would have done.
- **Flick against scroll and against a near-miss.** Mitigation: blind flicks only on graph elements and chips, never on scrolling panels; a flick needs 4 cm of travel; taking something away always stops at a warning-colored key; blind flicks can be turned off in settings, and are best with controllers.
- **Grab against add.** With hands, add is an off-hand pinch and grab is a fist, so they never meet; on Vision Pro without hand permission, grab is a two-hand pinch and add moves to the Add key.
- **The pie hides the graph under it.** Mitigation: the pie is drawn small (about 12 cm across) and semi-transparent, offset 15 cm toward you from the object, and flicking needs no pie drawn at all.
- **Result ranking drifts.** Frequency-ranked results move as you work, so a learned position stops working. Mitigation: exact name matches always rank first; the top result for a typed prefix you have chosen three times is locked to that prefix (Alfred's learned results).
- **Motion from Go to.** Mitigation: the graph moves only when the target is out of view, glides for 0.3 s without turning, and cuts with a fade under Reduce motion.
- **Vision Pro has no hover and no keyboard in session.** Mitigation: previews on pinch-hold, larger result rows, suggestions first; long text deferred to the 2D page.
- **Dictation on Quest needs a shipped recognizer.** Mitigation: a small model limited to the graph's vocabulary, loaded only when the microphone key is first used; typing never depends on it.
- **Long forms (merge, import, ForceAtlas2).** Mitigation: advanced options folded, the drawer scrolls, and the form detaches beside the drawer so the graph and the form show together.
