**Prototype 26: Hotbox** -- Hold one button anywhere and the app's whole menu bar appears in front of you as readable menu headers, with a center row of the pointed object's verbs and four corner favorites; steer to an item by turning the ray (the arm can stay low), release to run it, and Repeat last runs the last command again on whatever you point at next. On hands the same hotbox opens latched -- a visible Commands chip, a click on any object, or a quick off-hand tap -- and a pinch chooses, so nothing runs because a hand relaxed or left the cameras' view.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR with controllers (the primary input: the scheme is designed around buttons and an analog trigger) or tracked hands; Apple Vision Pro with eyes and hands (and PS VR2 Sense controllers if Safari exposes them). The headset page and graphty's 2D page on the laptop pair once through a 6-digit code shown in the headset. The pair runs over a small relay service graphty must host (a WebSocket relay that only forwards between the two pages of one code, with a size cap per file and no storage); graphty has no backend today, so this is new infrastructure and is listed under Known risks. The pairing is guarded: a code works for one pairing and expires after 5 minutes; the relay allows 5 wrong guesses per code before it retires it; the laptop shows "Headset wants to connect -- Allow?" and nothing is shared until someone clicks Allow; both pages derive an encryption key from the code (a password-authenticated key exchange), so the relay forwards only encrypted bytes; and both sides show a "Laptop: connected, folder shared" or "Headset connected" chip with Disconnect. While paired, the 2D page holds a screen wake lock so the laptop does not sleep; if the laptop goes away anyway, the headset's Laptop chip says so, the file browser says so, and File > Open falls back to headset files. Without the relay (today, or with the laptop asleep), every "Save to laptop" goes to the headset's downloads and into a Send to laptop queue on the Laptop chip that delivers when the pair returns; nothing is lost and no one has to lift the headset to keep working. On the laptop, the 2D page offers Share a folder once (the browser's folder picker in Chrome and Edge; in other browsers, a drop zone that keeps the files dropped this session). From then on File > Open > From laptop browses that folder from inside the headset, so no one has to lift the headset to send a file. The pair also carries pasted text, drafts (Finish on laptop), and every export whose "Save to" is set to the laptop. A paired Bluetooth keyboard is optional: a held Space opens the hotbox, as in Maya, and typing goes straight into fields. Voice is optional and only fills a text field through the system keyboard's own dictation key where it has one. All of it works seated, with the arms low: the hotbox is steered by turning the ray, not by reaching. The dominant hand points and chooses; on controllers the off hand undoes, sizes the brush and holds the add modifier. On hands the off hand only taps to open the hotbox and sizes the brush during a sweep; it never has to stay raised, and the latched hotbox stays open while either hand rests out of view. The status line sits at the graph's lower edge, no more than 15 degrees below eye level, so a door there is a glance, not a bent neck. Button names below assume a right-handed person; Settings > Handedness mirrors every mapping.

**One hand.** Settings > One hand keeps every command and the view reachable with the dominant hand alone. Controllers: A, B and the trigger are unchanged; Undo is the toast or the corner; the "Click adds" switch replaces the add modifier; the brush keeps a size set in Settings, or the right stick sizes it during a sweep; pressing the right stick cycles orbit, zoom and pan (the mode shows at the ray tip), so free orbit needs no off-hand stick. Hands: the Commands chip opens the hotbox latched, and a one-hand pinch-drag orbits wherever it starts, so nothing depends on holding still; the "Drag does: orbit, zoom, pan" row switches that drag to zoom or pan, and the brush keeps the size set in Settings. The dominant pinch held still for 0.4 s also latches the hotbox (a ring fills at the ray tip); its hold time and stillness tolerance are in Settings, and with tremor the person turns it off and uses the chip. View > Orbit has left, right, up and down steppers, and View > Zoom and pan has in, out, left, right, up and down steppers, for anyone who cannot drag.

**Timings in Settings.** Hold time, stillness tolerance, sweep threshold and sweep hold, orbit drag threshold, corner flick speed, toast duration (6 s by default, up to "until dismissed"), hover delay and hotbox gain are all in Settings > Timing; the Tour's gain check suggests a lower gain when it measures a jittery ray. Nothing in the scheme depends on a click landing within a time window.

### The hotbox

The hotbox opens 55 cm in front of your eyes at a fixed pitch, 5 degrees below the horizon, and only turns to follow the direction your head faces left or right. Where you were looking up or down when you pressed does not matter, so a hotbox opened from a chip in the status line still opens at eye level, not down at the chip, and its lower columns never cover the status line you came from. The cursor starts at its center, and moves by how far the ray turns from where it was at the press, one degree for one degree on controllers and 1.5 degrees per degree on hands, whose shoulder-wrist ray needs more arm to turn (Settings changes the gain; the Tour's first step checks it). Because steering is by turning, not reaching, the controller can rest on your lap while you steer; aiming at a node or a chip before the press still means raising it. The graph behind it dims through one overlay plane drawn between the hotbox and the graph, not a restyle of the nodes, so opening the hotbox costs the same at 16 nodes and at 10,000. Idle, the hotbox is about 46 cm wide and 13 cm tall (about 45 by 13 degrees); an open column extends it to about 18 cm above and below its center. Items are 3 cm tall (about 3 degrees), so a hand ray can hit them; an option box is a full row tall. A dimmed item dims only its name: its reason stays at full contrast.

**What the target means to the twelve columns.** A node target fills the command's first node input (a route's or a flow's source, Radial's focus node, Neighbors' center); the command otherwise covers what is showing. A selection or set target fills the command's seeds input when the command has one -- a descriptor marks an input as seeds when the command grows from it: Neighbors and k hops, Ego network, Filter > Add step > k hops, a route's "to any of" -- and the form says "starting from: Selection, 21". Seeded commands and every command that finds routes or hops search the whole graph, not what the filter leaves, and the result marks members outside the current filter ("adds 14 -- 3 outside your filter"), so "the ring and its 1-hop context" really grows beyond the ring and a shortest route through a filtered-out mule is never silently missing. Otherwise a selection or set target becomes the command's scope: Analyze runs on it, Layout lays out only it, Style paints it, Filter filters to it, Export exports it. A run, layer, filter step, network or other object target is used by the commands that take that kind and ignored by the rest. Empty space means what is showing: the whole graph with no filter, or the 5 shown families with one, and the label always says which ("showing 5 -- or all 16"). A node that is the only selected element (as after Find, or a click) is a node target, with its selection verbs under More; a press on one member of a larger selection targets the selection, with "-- or just Medici" on the label. The Commands chip gives the same target as a press on empty space, so the two doors never disagree: "showing 16 -- or the 7 selected", or "showing 16 -- or Medici" when one node is selected. Commands that take no target (File > Open, View > Fit) ignore it. Every form repeats the scope in its title ("PageRank -- on Selection: 7 families"), and every toast names it ("Ran Influence (PageRank) on 5 shown families").

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on and take the controllers.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families float at chest height, labels on the larger ones, Pucci alone off to one side. Under the graph a status line reads "Florentine families -- 16 families, 20 marriages". At the status line's left end sits a Commands chip, always. A hint below it says "Hold A for every command. B repeats the last one. X undoes. Help > Tour shows the rest." and stays until the first hotbox opens. With hands it says instead: "Click Commands for every command. Drag to turn the graph. Click anything for its commands." The off-hand tap is not in the first hint: after the third use of the Commands chip, the hotbox's own footer shows once "Faster: tap your left thumb and finger, palm away". The hint is shown on every first entry, whether the data came from the 2D page or the start form.
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Push the left stick to turn the graph and tilt it; push the right stick up to zoom in a little. Point at empty space beside the graph and hold A. Turn the controller slightly to put the cursor on Statistics in the center row; release.
    - **What you see:** The hotbox opens in front of you with "whole graph, 16 families" at its center, the six upper and six lower headers, and the four corners; the beam hides and a cursor dot sits at the center. Before you release, Statistics shows "under 1 s". A form opens in the form slot beside the graph: "16 families, 20 marriages, density 0.167, average 2.5 marriages per family, undirected, 2 pieces: 15 families and Pucci alone".
    - **Controls:** left stick, right stick, hold A, turn, release.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Hold A and flick to the top-left corner (Find); release. Type "med" on the system keyboard. Click "Medici" in the results.
    - **What you see:** The Find form opens with its field focused and results updating as you type: "Medici -- 6 marriages". Clicking it selects Medici and frames it; the ladder chip at the ray reads "Medici -- node". (When the node is plainly visible, a single trigger click on it does the same.)
    - **Controls:** hold A, corner flick, release, keyboard, click.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Click Medici again (Find just selected it, so the ladder is live and the click climbs; there is no time limit). The ray tip and the ladder chip said "click again: 7 neighbors" before you did.
    - **What you see:** The selection climbs one level. The ladder chip reads "Medici and neighbors -- 7 families, 7 marriages" and Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni light up with Medici. (A third click would skip the community level, which the chip marks "no community result yet", and climb to "component -- 15 families, 20 marriages"; a fourth returns to Medici. For 2 hops, hold A on Medici and release on Neighbors' option box: its form has a hop count and the edge and node type filters.)
    - **Controls:** click the selected node again.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Point at empty space and hold A. The target label reads "whole graph -- or the 7 selected"; leave it on the whole graph. Rest the cursor on Analyze, slide down to Centrality, then across to "Influence (PageRank)" in the side submenu. Release on the name, not its option box.
    - **What you see:** Analyze's column drops over the center row: its search row, Suggested and Recent, the six families, Runs and Plugins. The Centrality submenu lists its 8 measures by plain name with the technical name beside it, each with "under 1 s". (Had you rested on Flow, "Most that can flow" would show dimmed: "needs a source: press on a node".) On release PageRank runs at once with its defaults (damping 0.85), the toast reads "Ran Influence (PageRank) on 16 families", and a "PageRank" chip joins the status line.
    - **Controls:** hold A, slide through a header and a submenu, release on the name.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Point at the PageRank chip in the status line and hold A. Release on Read in the center row.
    - **What you see:** The center row now holds the run's verbs: Read, Paint, Select from, Filter from, Compare, Explain, Re-run with options. Read opens a ranked list in the form slot: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069 ... down to Pucci 0.010, with a histogram tab. Pointing at a row lights that family in the graph; clicking a row selects it.
    - **Controls:** hold A on the chip, release, point at rows.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Hold A on the PageRank chip again and release on Paint.
    - **What you see:** Paint runs at once with PageRank's suggested style: a sequential palette and sizes from 1x to 3x, Medici largest. A legend appears beside the graph ("Color: PageRank", "Size: PageRank") and the toast reads "Painted PageRank: 1 layer". To change the palette or the size range, hold A on the legend title and release on Edit: the layer's form opens with the palette grid and a range slider.
    - **Controls:** hold A, release on a center-row verb.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Hold A on the PageRank chip and release on Filter from. In the form, drag the threshold handle on the histogram (or press its plus stepper) to 0.070. Click Apply.
    - **What you see:** Filter from needs a threshold, so its form opens instead of running: a histogram of PageRank with a "keep at least" handle and the value field. As you drag, the count above Apply reads "keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages" and the dropped families fade; Ridolfi, at 0.069, falls just under. After Apply, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain, and the status line reads "Showing 5 of 16 -- 1 filter step".
    - **Controls:** hold A, release, drag a slider handle or click a stepper, click Apply.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Click Medici, then hold A on it and release on Note. Type "Married into six families; the hub of the network." Click Save.
    - **What you see:** Medici and its 6 neighbors are still selected from step 4, but the ladder ended when PageRank ran, so the ray tip on Medici reads "click: just Medici". The click selects just Medici, as on the 2D page; a lone selected node is a node target, and the label reads "Medici (node)". The note form opens in the form slot with the system keyboard. On Save, a small note marker sits on Medici and the toast reads "Added note to Medici". On Vision Pro the form offers an in-scene keyboard, and a Finish on laptop button that keeps the note as a draft on the 2D page.
    - **Controls:** click, hold A on the node, release, keyboard, click.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You want to look at the filter steps, so you hold A, rest on Filter, and release one row low, on Invert instead of Steps. The five strong families vanish and the other eleven come back. Press X.
    - **What you see:** Invert ran at once, because a step that can be undone does not ask. The toast reads "Inverted filter -- 11 of 16 shown". Pressing X shows "Undid: Invert filter -- 5 of 16 shown" and the five families stand alone again, with Redo on the toast. ### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor names a family, a plain name, a technical name, options and result fields. It appears in Analyze > that family's submenu (and in the search row, Help's command search and Run several), with an option box because it has options, a cost from the estimator and a dimmed reason from its preconditions. Its form is built by the form kit; its results read through Read, Paint, Select from and Filter from by field kind. A new family ("Biology: enrichment") joins Analyze > Plugins, sorted by name: while all plugin algorithms fit in 9 rows they are drawn there directly under a divider per family, so nothing is a third level; past that the family row opens a list form whose rows keep their option box, cost and dimmed reason. A plugin installed today is on screen the first time its author opens Analyze; built-in families never move or fold. An option that takes a list of gene ids renders as "use selection, or paste from the laptop"; an option that takes a file renders as the kit's file browser; one that takes two nodes renders as two Pick slots.
- **A new layout.** It appears in the Layout group its descriptor's category names (a new category becomes a row in Layout > Plugins), with its engines as the first choice in its form; Stop and Reshuffle work if it is iterative.
- **A new reader, writer or data service.** A reader joins File > Open's format list and its options become a page of the open form; a writer joins Export > Data's format list with its options form; a service that turns a gene list into a network appears in File > Open > From a service with its own form.
- **A new object type.** When graphty adds an object kind (a path, a time slice, a group of groups), it declares its verbs. Pressing on it puts those verbs in the center row (7 plus More), and its global commands go into the column its descriptor names when that column has its free row, and otherwise under Data > Objects, without any change to the hotbox. Where to press on it is generated too: every object kind gets a row in Data > Objects and a status-line chip (folding into "+N"), so nobody hand-places a pick target for it.
- **A third-party plugin.** It adds items, never layout: plugins cannot add a thirteenth header, so the twelve positions stay learnable. A verb a plugin adds to an existing object kind joins that center row after the built-in verbs and spills into More. A palette joins the palette grid.
- **The practical limit.** Breadth is not the problem: one press shows everything, and search covers any column. The limits are depth and reading. Two levels is the most the hotbox draws and 9 rows the most in a column; Data, Analyze and Layout each keep one row free for the next built-in family, and after that the next one costs a regrouping of rows that people learned by place; anything beyond becomes a list form with search, one more act, and every plugin family or category is one row deeper than a built-in one (the price of never folding a built-in row away). A center row past about 7 verbs pushes the rest into More, which costs a second slide. And everything is text read in the headset, so the scheme is as fast as your reading at 55 cm, and long forms (13 options, 40-column mapping, 25-row tables) are workable rather than natural.

### Why it should work

- The Hotbox itself: Kurtenbach, Fitzmaurice, Owen and Baudel, "The Hotbox: Efficient Access to a Large Number of Menu-items", CHI 1999. It put Maya's 1,200-plus commands behind one held key, used no screen space while hidden, and has shipped in Autodesk Maya for over 25 years. Graphty's command count (29 to 60+ algorithms, 14 layouts, 40+ style properties, 22 areas) is the same problem.
- The option box (release on the name to run with the last settings, on the square to open the form) is Maya's long-standing convention, and "Repeat last" is Maya's G key and Blender's Shift+R. Both reduce repeated analysis (the same filter on the next dataset, the same note style on the next node) to one button.
- Repeated clicks that widen the selection are familiar from text editors (double-click a word, triple-click a line). Nanome, a shipped VR molecular-modeling tool, climbs atom, residue, chain by repeated clicks (nanome.ai blog, version 2.3.0), the same move applied to structure in a headset.
- Analog trigger pressure as stroke size has shipped in Tilt Brush (Google, 2016) and Open Brush, so VR users already read trigger pressure as "how big".
- Menus placed relative to the body, rather than in the world, use proprioception: Mine, Brooks and Sequin, "Moving objects in space: exploiting proprioception in virtual-environment interaction", SIGGRAPH 1997. Steering by relative turning is the same clutch a mouse gives, and it is how Vision Pro's own pinch-and-drag pointer already behaves.
- Readable menus over memorized gestures: the 3D menu taxonomy of Dachselt and Hubner (Computers and Graphics, 2007) and Bowman and Wingrave's TULIP study (IEEE VR 2001) both point to visible, labeled items for large command sets in VR, and "An Evaluation of Pie Menus for System Control in Virtual Reality" (NordiCHI 2020, doi 10.1145/3419249.3420146) supports the direction-picked corners as the expert path.
- Recognition over recall (Nielsen's heuristics, 1994): a first-time user never needs to know where a command lives, because one press shows all twelve menus.

### What it would teach us

- Does a 46 cm hotbox with 12 headers and 9-row columns fit comfortably in each headset's view and reading distance, or do people turn their heads to find a column?
- Is relative steering (turning the ray) understood at once, or do people try to point straight at items, and which gain suits hands and Vision Pro's transient pointer?
- Do people understand that the target is what the ray was on when they pressed, and do they use the second half of the label ("or the 7 selected", "or all 16", "or just Medici")?
- Do people read the ray tip's "click again: 7 neighbors" against "click: just Medici", or do they expect every click on a selected node to do the same thing?
- On Vision Pro, does look, pinch and hold feel as fast as a plain look and pinch, and how often does the held hand need to nudge a gaze that landed one row off?
- On hands, do people settle on the Commands chip or move to the off-hand tap, and how many extra acts per command does the latch cost against the undos that held release used to cost?
- How fast do people move from reading columns to flicking corners and remembered positions, and how many corner favorites do they actually change?
- How often do people use Repeat last, and how often do they hold it to reopen the form?
- Do the selection ladder and the pressure brush cover most real multi-node selections without the Select column's rules?
- Does the 9-row cap with layout groups and family submenus cost more slides than it saves in reading?
- On hands, is the off-hand tap reliable against two-hand grabs and Quest's palm-facing system gesture, and is the hand ray steady enough for 3 cm items at 1.5 gain?
- Does the candidate cycle in dense spots get used, or do people zoom in instead?
- How many acts the basic journey and the six stress journeys take compared with the desktop app, and where the extra acts are.

### Known risks

- **Too much text at once.** Twelve headers, an open column and a submenu is a lot to read. Mitigation: only the column under the cursor opens, and only after a 0.15 s rest; headers stay in fixed positions so they are learned by place; columns hold at most 9 rows; search rows on long columns; corner favorites for the most used commands.
- **Wrong item on release.** Releasing a row off runs a neighbor (as in step 10). Mitigation: undo is one button (or one pinch on the toast) and names the step; Clear all and Reset sit after a gap at a column's foot; steps that cannot be undone (Remove selected, Delete set, Close with unsaved changes, Merge nodes) open a confirm form with counts instead of running; commands above 5 s ask before they start; the sticky highlight needs the cursor to leave an item by 1 cm before it moves; an item lights only after 100 ms and the lit item is what runs; the center gap is a labeled safe release, and a column closes when the cursor returns to it; latched corners need a 0.2 s rest before they light; on hands the latch removes release-on-relax entirely, and a lost hand never commits. It still happens on controllers when a thumb releases A early; the undo is the answer there.
- **Target confusion.** Users may expect a global command to act on their selection, or on what is showing. Mitigation: the one target rule with no gaps (a lone selected node is a node; a selection seeds a command that grows from it and scopes the rest; empty space and the Commands chip both mean what is showing), the target label with its second half switched only by choosing it, the scope in every form's title and every toast, and a Scope row in every form that covers part of the graph.
- **Relative steering.** People may try to point straight at an item. Mitigation: the beam hides and a cursor dot shows where you are; a Settings option switches to absolute pointing (the hotbox then opens where the ray is, at the cost of reach).
- **Hands: tap versus grab, and the system menu.** An off-hand pinch could be half of a two-hand grab, and a palm-facing pinch opens Quest's own menu. Mitigation: the default door is the Commands chip, and the first hint does not mention the tap, so a learner never needs an off-hand pose; the off-hand tap is a short closed-and-open pinch, ignored while the dominant hand pinches or moves and debounced for flicker, while a held one is half of a grab; the tap is offered once, after the third chip use; a sweep that the off hand joins within 0.5 s becomes a grab and restores the selection.
- **The latch costs an act on hands.** Every command on hands is open, then choose: one more act than a held release. That is the deliberate trade for never running a command because a hand relaxed, drifted or left the cameras' view, and it cannot be removed without bringing back release-to-run on hands. It shows in the publish journey: about 120 acts on hands against about 100 on controllers. Controllers keep hold-and-release on A, and a tap of A latches there too. The latched hotbox now stays open through tracking loss, so the cost is one act per command, not one per dropped hand.
- **Ray jitter on hands.** Items are small for an unsteady hand ray. Mitigation: 3 cm items, the sticky highlight, and Settings options for a larger hotbox and a lower gain.
- **Forms piling up.** Each command can open a form. Mitigation: one form slot beside the graph; unpinned forms close when the next opens; at most four pinned forms, the oldest folding into a row under the status line.
- **Text entry.** The Quest keyboard replaces the field and Vision Pro has none in the session. Mitigation: suggested names first; pasting from the laptop; the token builder for formulas; the in-scene editor with a caret on every headset for editing what is already written; the assistant key carried by the pair; a paired keyboard where it works. Writing a long report section from scratch is still slow in any headset, and Finish on laptop stays the suggested path for it: no control scheme makes a ray keyboard a word processor.
- **Arm fatigue.** Mitigation: steering by turning the wrist, so the arm can rest while steering (aiming at a node or a chip before the press still means raising the controller, so the lap rest covers about half of each act); corner flicks are short; the sticks need no raised arm. On hands the latch lets the off hand drop as soon as the hotbox is open, and the steering hand may drop too while the person reads, because tracking loss pauses rather than closes; 1.5 gain shortens its travel. The hotbox opens at eye level from every door, and the status line sits within 15 degrees of eye level, so commands no longer start with a bent neck.
- **Ladder surprises.** A third click jumps to a whole component. Mitigation: the ray tip and the chip say what the next click gives before you make it ("click again: component, 840,000"), and past 10,000 nodes the climb needs the chip's Climb instead of a click; Back down, Just this node, Previous selection and Restore on the toast bring back the last one. The ladder lives only from a click until the next click or command, so a click on a member of an older selection always narrows to it, as on the 2D page. In a dense spot a click never cycles; the candidates are on the chip, with Next and a ring on the current one.
- **Repeat last surprises.** B runs something you forgot. Mitigation: the Again item names the command before you use it; the toast names the command and its new target, X undoes it; B opens the form instead of running when a required input is missing, and asks first when the command's cost is above 5 s; in a list that reorders, B follows the moved row. On hands and Vision Pro, Again on the toast repeats on the step's own target and Again on the ladder chip repeats on the node just clicked, so which one you pinch says which target you get. Repeat holds one command, not a chain: "find, predict, read, filter" for the next user is a recipe, not B.
- **Status line crowding.** Many runs, filter steps, networks and new object kinds would overflow the chips. Mitigation: the last 3 runs show as chips (each naming the option that differs from its siblings, "Links -- Jaccard") and a "+7 runs" chip opens Results; filter steps beyond 4 fold into a "Steps" chip; every object is also a row in Data > Objects.
- **The laptop pairing is new infrastructure, and it exposes a folder.** Files from the laptop, pasted text, drafts, the saved assistant key and "Save to laptop" all ride on a relay service graphty does not run today. The pairing is guarded as stated in Setup -- one-use codes that expire in 5 minutes, 5 guesses per code, an Allow on the laptop before anything is shared, end-to-end encryption keyed from the code, a visible Disconnect -- and the per-file cap is 200 MB, with "too large for the pair: save it in the headset" when exceeded. Even so, a shared folder of bank or patient data is reachable from the headset for as long as the pair is open, so Share a folder asks for a folder, never a drive, and the 2D page shows what is shared. Until the relay exists, files come only from headset files (the session is saved before leaving and resumed after), exports wait in headset downloads and the Send to laptop queue, and long text comes from the in-scene editor. Share a folder needs the browser's folder picker (Chrome and Edge); elsewhere the laptop page keeps a drop zone, so someone must drop files before entering VR. A laptop that sleeps despite the wake lock drops the pair; the Laptop chip says so and File > Open falls back to headset files.
- **Journey 6 is still 2.5 to 3 times the 2D page.** Publishing an analysis (reopen, restyle, filter, callout, methods, recipe, three exports, report) is about 100 acts on controllers and about 120 on hands, against about 40 clicks on the 2D page. No act is a stop now, but the hotbox reads and steers every command, and that cost does not go away without abandoning the idea.
- **The preview channel is a graphty-element dependency.** Previews, row hover and the live fade need a transient preview channel in graphty-element: composited over the style layers, owned by the element, cleared on commit or cancel, and never a style layer, with the preview drawn on top until it is cleared. It does not exist today. Until it does, every preview shows a count instead of drawing ("would keep 5 of 16"), so frame rate holds and nothing bypasses the layers; what is kept on Apply is always a real style layer or filter step.
- **Frame rate from picking and extra views.** The hover label and row lighting need a pick every frame on Quest and Galaxy XR. Mitigation: the pick runs against a spatial index only when the ray has moved more than a fraction of a degree, and above 50,000 nodes the hover label waits for a click. The overview map renders into a texture a few times a second, not every frame per eye; the split view is the one remaining double render, and Condition on the merged network makes it rarely needed.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: on hands the hotbox opens latched and a press commits.

- The door is a visible Commands chip at the left end of the status line, or, once learned, a quick off-hand pinch tap. The off-hand held pinch is gone.
- While latched, a dominant pinch (or the trigger) chooses the item under the cursor. A pinch on the center gap, a lowered hand or any tracking loss closes the hotbox with nothing done.
- The commit always uses where the cursor was 100 ms before the commit motion began, on every device, so the kick of a release or a pinch no longer moves the choice one row.
- Controllers keep hold-and-release on A, where it works, and a tap of A latches too.
- Vision Pro's default is now look at an item and pinch inside the latched hotbox. The held drag remains as an expert option with the same pre-release sampling.
- The cost is one extra act per command on hands, stated under Known risks.

The red team's second change, adopted: the target rule has no gaps.

- A node that is the only selected element is a node target.
- A press on one member of a larger selection offers "-- or just Medici".
- With a filter on, empty space means what is showing and offers "showing 5 -- or all 16".
- The second half of the label switches only when chosen, never by resting on it.
- Every form that covers part of the graph has a Scope row, and every toast names the scope.
- Step 9 of the journey is rewritten to click Medici first, so the note lands on Medici and not on the 7-family selection.

**Round 2.**

The idea is unchanged: one press shows every menu with the target's verbs at its center. The round's lesson was that the round 1 fixes added meanings to the most common act -- a click on a node -- and doors on hands that did not follow the target rule the rest of the file teaches. Round 2 takes meanings away rather than adding surfaces.

The red team's top change, adopted: a click on a selected node has one meaning again.

- A click on one member of a larger selection selects just that node, as on the 2D page.
- The ladder lives only from a click (or a Find) that selects one node until any other click or command; while it lives, a second click on that node climbs. The ray tip always says which ("click again: 7 neighbors" or "click: just Medici").
- In a dense spot a click never cycles. The candidates sit on the ladder chip with Next, the current one has a ring in the graph, and pointing at a name lights its node.
- Florentine step 9, the fraud ring's route step and the publish journey's callout step now work as written, and their text says why.

The red team's runner-up change, adopted: every door follows the target rule.

- The Commands chip gives the same target as a press on empty space ("showing 16 -- or the 7 selected").
- A click on anything that cannot be selected (a set or group outline, a legend title or entry, a note marker) opens the hotbox on it, as status-line chips do. The ladder chip has a Commands button, so after a click the node's hotbox is one pinch away at the ray.
