**Prototype 27: The Sheet** -- A curved spreadsheet under the graph is the control and the graph is its picture: nodes and edges are rows, every attribute and result is a column, analyses are new columns from a function picker, filters are autofilters, styles are conditional formatting, and runs, layers, filters, sets, notes and history are all sheets.

No metaphor here. It is the spreadsheet nearly every office worker already knows, with two additions: the graph floating above it always draws whatever rows the sheet shows, and the verbs that only make sense in space (neighbors, routes, regions, moving a node) are reached from the graph as well as from the rows. Because everything graphty knows is already a row or a column, a new algorithm, option, result, format or palette arrives as one more entry in a list the sheet already has. Results that are not one value per node or edge (routes, cross-tables, removal impact, a plugin's table) open as a sheet of their own, so even those need no new surface.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. The sheet is a horizontally curved panel below it, about 75 cm wide and 35 cm tall, its center about 40 degrees below eye level and 55 to 65 cm from you, tilted to face your eyes, its top edge just under the graph's box so neither hides the other. A grab bar under the status bar moves it: back to 1.1 m (for rays and gaze), in again, up or down, or to your dominant side at eye height for people who should not look down for long. **Desk mode**: if you sit at a real table, touch two points on it (fingertip or controller) and the sheet lies on that surface, so forearms rest on something real and a fingertip tap lands on something solid; without a real desk the sheet floats and is worked by ray or gaze with the hand resting, with mid-air touch only for short bursts. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: hand rays with pinch, or direct touch when the sheet is within reach; controllers (trigger, grip, thumbstick and its press, two face buttons); look and pinch on Vision Pro; an optional tracked stylus (Logitech MX Ink, Quest only); an optional paired Bluetooth keyboard; optional dictation into the formula bar where the browser offers a recognizer. Everything works one-handed: adding to a selection is a tick box or the status bar's Add toggle, and the graph can be turned and framed with one hand.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** Put the headset on with graphty open and choose Enter VR. The sheet shows the Start sheet: rows of samples (with one-line descriptions), recent projects, Open from inbox, URL, Generate. Tap the Open cell on the "Florentine families" row.
    - **What you see:** The sheet switches to the Nodes tab with 16 rows (10 in view), one per family, and the graph appears above it: 16 nodes, one of them (Pucci) floating alone. A hint in the status bar: "Hold a header, or tap its ..., for its menu."
    - **Controls:** tap (a button cell).

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the Project tab, read the first row, then tap back to Nodes. Look up at the graph; tap Follow if it is off.
    - **What you see:** The Project row reads "Florentine families: 16 nodes, 20 edges, density 0.167, 2 pieces (largest 15), undirected". On the Nodes tab the status bar says "Showing 16 of 16". The graph is framed whole inside its box, with labels on every node because there are only 16; the 10 rows in view are outlined.
    - **Controls:** tap (tabs), Follow.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Either tap the Medici node in the graph, or tap Find on the toolbar and type "med" on the in-scene keyboard (or say it). In a 16-row sheet you can also just see the row.
    - **What you see:** The Medici row gets the cursor and a thin line runs from its row up to its node, which is outlined. The status bar reads "Medici: 6 marriages".
    - **Controls:** tap (a node), or Find plus typing.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Hold Medici's gutter (or hold the Medici node itself) and choose Neighbors, then "1 hop", then Select.
    - **What you see:** The submenu shows its counts before you choose: "1 hop: 6 families, 7 marriages among the 7"; "2 hops: 11 families". After Select, seven rows are selected and pinned under the header: Medici, Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni. The graph eases (about 0.3 s) to frame those seven, outlined. The status bar reads "7 selected".
    - **Controls:** hold (gutter or node menu), tap (menu entries).

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Tap Insert column, then P on the A to Z strip (or open Centrality) and tap "Influence (PageRank)". The formula bar fills with the function and its chips: Scope: whole graph, Damping 0.85, More. Tap Add.
    - **What you see:** The picker's entry reads "Influence (PageRank): who is linked to by well-linked nodes; under a second here". After Add, a PageRank column fills top to bottom with a short highlight, its header histogram appears, and the Runs sheet gains a row "PageRank, whole graph, damping 0.85". The selection is untouched; the scope chip said whole graph, so the run did not use it.
    - **Controls:** tap (toolbar, strip letter, picker entry, Add).

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the PageRank header's sort arrow twice (high to low). Tap Clear on the status bar's selection count. Scroll down with a swipe, then drag in the gutter over the top three rows.
    - **What you see:** Medici 0.144, Guadagni 0.097, Strozzi 0.087, Albizzi 0.078, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069, Castellani 0.069, and down to Acciaiuoli 0.030 and Pucci 0.010 at the bottom. The header histogram shows one bar far to the right (Medici), a cluster between 0.06 and 0.08, and Pucci alone at the left. As you scroll, the graph stays still and the outline moves to the rows in view. With the top three selected the graph frames them and the status bar reads "3 selected; PageRank sum 0.328, mean 0.109, min 0.087, max 0.144".
    - **Controls:** tap (sort arrow, Clear), swipe (scroll), drag in the gutter.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the PageRank header, choose Color by, and tap a sequential palette from the strip (the menu shows the 7 sequential ones first, because the column is an amount). Hold it again, choose Size by, and drag the two range handles until the largest family is about three times the smallest (1x to 3x).
    - **What you see:** The graph recolors and resizes live while the handles move. The header now shows a swatch and a size mark; a legend appears beside the graph. The Layers sheet has two rows: "All nodes: color by PageRank, sequential, linear" and "All nodes: size by PageRank, 1x to 3x".
    - **Controls:** hold (header menu), tap (palette), drag (range handles).

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Tap the funnel on the PageRank header. The histogram opens across the width of the sheet with two handles. Drag the lower handle right, detent by detent, until its readout says 0.070 (or tap the readout and type it on the keypad). Tap Apply.
    - **What you see:** While you drag, the readout says "PageRank at least 0.070: keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages; next below: Ridolfi 0.069". Rows below the line dim and their nodes fade. After Apply, five rows remain (Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi, Tornabuoni), the status bar reads "Showing 5 of 16", the header shows a funnel, the graph frames the five, and the Filters sheet has one row: "PageRank at least 0.070, on, affects view only". (Switching its Affects cell to "view and analysis" would make later runs see only these five.)
    - **Controls:** tap (funnel, Apply), drag (histogram handle), optional keypad.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Tap Medici's cell in the Notes column (frozen at the right edge), then tap the formula bar. Dictate, or type on the in-scene keyboard under it, "Married into six families; the hub of the network." Tap Done.
    - **What you see:** The formula bar grows to several lines with a caret you move by tapping between words; holding a word offers Delete word, Delete sentence, Cut and Paste. After Done, the cell shows the start of the note, a small marker appears on the Medici node, and the Notes sheet has a row with Medici as its target.
    - **Controls:** tap (cell, formula bar, Done), typing or dictation.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You hold the PageRank header to sort it, and your tap lands on Clear filter, one line above Sort in its menu. Eleven families come back. Tap Undo on the toolbar (or press the off-hand upper face button).
    - **What you see:** Before you tap, the Undo button reads "Undo Clear filter: PageRank at least 0.070". After it, the five rows are back, "Showing 5 of 16", and Redo reads "Redo Clear filter". The Steps sheet shows every step as a row (Open, PageRank, Color by, Size by, Filter, Note) and the undone one grayed below them; the neighbor selection is not a step, because selections have their own Previous selection. Had the mistake been older, you would fix it on its own sheet (delete or edit that layer or filter row) and keep every step since, or use Undo to here on the Steps sheet after Preview.
    - **Controls:** tap (Undo) or the face button.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the Project tab (or tap its "...") and choose Save (or Ctrl+S on a paired keyboard).
    - **What you see:** The status bar reads "Saved Florentine families". The project's row on the Project sheet shows the time. (It was already being saved automatically; Save fixes a named version.)
    - **Controls:** hold (tab menu), tap.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days": set autofilters on three headers (a ticked value list on type, a histogram handle on degree, the last-30-days preset on the created column's time window). Each becomes a Filters row and rows combine with AND. For the OR, hold the degree filter row and choose "Or with...", then tap the risk_score header; its histogram opens, you set 0.8, and the row's rule cell now reads "degree > 20 OR risk_score >= 0.8". Tapping any rule cell and then the formula bar opens the rule editor: one clause per line (attribute, comparison, value as cells), an indent column for brackets, AND, OR and NOT as a cell per line, and "keeps X of Y" updating as you edit. Editing it a week later is tapping that cell again. "Select visible" turns the filtered rows into a selection, "Select where" builds the same kind of rule for a selection without filtering, and "Save as named filter" keeps the rule.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** On the Runs sheet, the PageRank row's Damping cell reads 0.85. Tap it; the formula bar shows its stepper; step to 0.75. The cell turns pending (hatched) with "Re-run as new row", "Replace" and Cancel; choose Re-run as new row and a second PageRank column appears beside the first, named "PageRank (damping 0.75)". Replace first lists what depends on the old column ("2 layers and 1 filter use it; they will follow the new one"). Logarithmic tolerances use the exponent stepper.
- **Comparing two runs.** Hold a result header and choose "Compare with...", then tap the other run's header: the two columns sit side by side with a third, "difference" (a formula column, sortable), and the status bar shows how many rows changed rank. - **Layers on subsets.** Select rows (by any means), hold the gutter, "Keep as set", accept the suggested name "Set 1" or type one: a tick column appears. On the Layers sheet add a row and tap its Target cell, then tap the set's tick header; that layer paints only the set. "Style selected" does the same in one step and keeps the target as a frozen set, so the layer does not change when the selection does. A layer's place in the stack is its row's place; drag the grip to move it. The diverging logFC layer centered on zero with missing values gray is one property row filled left to right: Color, logFC, red-blue, diverging, center 0, missing gray.
- **Recipes.** On the Steps sheet tick the steps to keep (say PageRank, Color by, Filter) and choose Save as recipe; its name defaults to the steps ("PageRank, color, filter"). On another network, Insert column, Recipes, pick it: a mapping sheet lists each input the recipe used (one row per attribute or set) with a cell to tap the matching header in the new data. Replay adds its steps to the Steps sheet one row each, and each can be undone on its own.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Add data, then "Lookup from file": choose a file from the inbox (files sent from the laptop) or a URL. A Staging sheet shows the first rows with an inferred type in each header; hold a header to change its type or skip it. Tap the key header in the new table, then the matching header in Nodes (say "symbol" against "name"). The status bar reads "4,112 of 4,350 rows matched", the unmatched rows wait on a Problems sheet, and Confirm adds the new columns to the Nodes sheet in their own column group. Importing a new network uses the same Staging sheet, where each header has a role cell (node id, source, target, attribute, skip).

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers a descriptor; it appears in the picker under its family (or a new family group), searchable by both names and on the A to Z strip, with cost and preconditions from the descriptor. Its options render as chips with the editor for each kind, folded by the advanced flag, and become cells on its Runs rows, so re-running and sweeping it work on day one. Its node and edge result fields become typed columns; its graph fields become Runs cells; a table it returns opens as a sheet. "Set the third option of an algorithm a plugin added last week" is: tap its Runs row's third option cell and use the editor in the formula bar.
- **A new layout.** It appears in the picker's Layout family; its options become chips and cells; its output is a Positions column group, so moving, pinning and scoping to a set work as for every layout.
- **A new style property.** It appears in a layer's "Add property" list from the style schema, with the editor of its kind.
- **A new object type.** It gets a tab: one row per object, its properties as columns, its verbs as the gutter menu, its notes in the Notes column. A path, a time slice or a saved query each fit this way without touching the other sheets.
- **A third-party plugin of another kind.** A reader joins the Open and Add data format list with its options as Staging cells; a writer joins the Export row; a palette joins the palette strip; a camera preset joins the graph menu; a data service ("gene list to network") is a Start sheet row or a Lookup with its arguments as chips, a gene list being a list chip; a domain metric is a function.
- **The practical limit.** Anything that is not a table: drawing a pattern template, freeform highlight shapes, placing camera keyframes for a video, and fine spatial work on dense parts of the graph. These are awkward here or go to the 2D page. Readable width is the other limit: about six data columns at headset text size, so wide comparisons depend on column groups and saved column sets.

### Why it should work

- Spreadsheets are the most widely learned data tool there is: VisiCalc (Bricklin and Frankston, 1979) set the grid, and Excel's autofilter, conditional formatting and status-bar aggregates are known to almost every analyst, so the control vocabulary needs little teaching.
- Network analysis already fits in a spreadsheet: NodeXL (Smith, Shneiderman, Milic-Frayling et al., Communities and Technologies 2009) put metrics, filters and encodings in Excel columns for non-programmers, and Bonsignore, Dunne, Rotman et al., "First steps to NetViz nirvana", SocialCom 2009, found students could do real network analysis that way. Gephi's Data Laboratory (2009 on) is the same split: a table for data work beside the graph.
- Chi, Riedl, Barry and Konstan, "Principles for information visualization spreadsheets", IEEE Computer Graphics and Applications 1998, showed a spreadsheet of visualizations can hold every operation as cells and formulas, with results as new cells.
- Histograms in column headers are shipped and studied: Trifacta and Wrangler (Kandel, Paepcke, Hellerstein and Heer, CHI 2011) and Rao and Card's Table Lens (CHI 1994) let people read a whole column's shape at a glance and brush it.
- Tables work in VR: In, Lin, North, Pfister and Yang, "This is the Table I Want! Interactive data transformation on desktop and in virtual reality", IEEE VIS 2023 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12168), had people transform tables in a headset and found VR workable for it. Gesslein, Biener, Gagel et al., "Pen-based interaction with spreadsheets in mobile virtual reality", ISMAR 2020, used a pen on a real flat surface for precise cell work in a headset, which is what Desk mode and the MX Ink stylus (2024) make buyable.
- Object-first is preserved: every object's verbs are its row's menu, which is the same "point at a thing, then choose" as the desktop app, only arranged as a list.

### What it would teach us

- Does the graph stay useful, or do people work in the sheet and stop looking up? How often do they tap nodes instead of rows?
- How many rows and columns can people read at headset text size before they need the 2D page, and does Desk mode at 60 cm read and touch better than a floating panel at 1.1 m?
- Is Follow (the graph framing selections and filters) helpful, and does anyone miss it moving while they scroll?
- Does a single surface for every list (runs, layers, filters, history) speed people up after the first session, compared with a dedicated panel per kind?
- Do people find menus by the "..." button and later switch to the hold?
- How often do people use the stylus or a keyboard when they have one, and how much faster are formulas and finds with it?
- Can people read the rule editor's indented clauses and edit a rule a week later without help?
- Does a Steps sheet with parameter columns make old mistakes easier to find than a list of step names?

### Known risks

- **Text density.** Headset text is large, so a sheet shows far fewer cells than a monitor: 10 rows and about six data columns. Mitigation: folding column groups, saved column sets, frozen name and Notes columns, Selected to top; the graph carries the overview.
- **The graph becomes decoration.** Mitigation: Follow, the leader line from the cursor row to its node, the outline of the rows in view, row menus reachable from the node itself, and the spatial verbs (neighbors, routes, region, move) that show their effect in the graph.
- **Looking down and up.** The sheet below and the graph above mean head pitch changes all session, and reading 40 degrees below eye level for long strains the neck. Mitigation: the sheet's top edge sits just under the graph; the grab bar raises it or moves it to the side at eye height; Desk mode lets the forearms rest.
- **Arm fatigue.** Touching a floating panel for long is tiring. Mitigation: rays and gaze work with the hand resting; touch is meant for Desk mode, where the desk carries the arm.
- **Motion from Follow.** Moving the graph by itself can unsettle people. Mitigation: it never moves while you scroll, only after a selection or filter change once your hands settle; it moves and scales only the graph, never you, eases over 0.3 s, cuts instead of easing with reduced motion, and Follow can be off.
- **Drag conflicts.** Scroll, range selection, handle drags and node moves all start with a pinch. Mitigation: each has its own zone (cells scroll, gutter selects, handles change values, nodes move, the bar and empty space turn the graph), nothing selects by dragging over cells, and node moves are undoable.
- **Hold is hidden.** A long press is not obvious in a headset. Mitigation: the "..." button on whatever has focus opens the same menu, the tour teaches it, and the hold time is a setting for people with tremor.
- **Text entry.** Finds by id, names and notes need typing. Mitigation: suggested names everywhere, header taps instead of typed attribute names, the A to Z strip, the keypad for numbers, an in-scene keyboard with a word caret, optional dictation and keyboards, long text and keys through the laptop.
- **Menu depth.** A column menu can grow long. Mitigation: it is generated per kind, with the verbs most used for that kind at the top and a search line at the bottom.
- **Spatial tasks are second-class.** Drawing shapes, patterns and camera paths fit a table badly. Mitigation: name them as awkward, keep the region select, node drag and in-air drawing at the graph, and hand the rest to the 2D page.
- **Rendering a text-heavy panel.** Repainting a full-sheet texture every scrolled frame is too slow on a standalone headset. Mitigation: rows are separate tiles that move while scrolling and repaint only when their content changes; a cylinder layer where the browser supports it.
- **Very large graphs.** 10,000 rows is a lot of scrolling. Mitigation: sorting, autofilters, "first N" and Find are the route, never scrolling; the sheet loads rows as they come into view.
- **Optional hardware.** The stylus, keyboards and a real desk are not standard. Mitigation: every path works with hands, controllers or eyes alone; the extras only make cell work faster.
