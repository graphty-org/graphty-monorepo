**Prototype 28: Graph Browser** -- graphty works like a web browser: every object has a page of facts and verbs, every mention of an object is a link, following a neighbor link grows the visible graph instead of jumping, and Back, Forward, tabs, bookmarks, an address bar and a basket carry navigation, comparison and finding.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. Straight ahead points at the seam between the graph and the reader panel, so neither needs a head turn: the graph's center sits about 13 degrees right of straight ahead (about 18 degrees below eye level, seated), and the panel's center about 13 degrees left, about 8 degrees below eye level. The panel stands farther back than the graph, at about 1.1 m (closer to the headset's focal distance, so reading it strains the eyes less), and is scaled so it still spans about 26 degrees wide and 20 degrees tall; its near edge abuts the graph's edge as you see it, with no gap. Looking from a page row to the graph is an eye movement of about 15 to 26 degrees, mostly within the eyes' comfortable range, instead of a 25 to 40 degree head turn. The toolbar is mirrored so Back, Forward and Home sit on the panel edge next to the graph, the point of the panel nearest the graph. Placements (Settings, or drag the panel's top edge): Beside (the default above), Seated low (the panel's center at the graph's height, about 18 degrees below eye level, for a reader who aims with the forearm on an armrest), and Right side (for left-handed readers; the toolbar mirrors again). Text is at least 1 degree tall (1.9 cm at 1.1 m) and every link or button at least 2 degrees tall, with half a button's height between neighboring buttons, so the page area holds about 12 rows; Text size trades rows for size, and scales the hover tag, the "Which one?" card, the minimap and the in-panel keyboard with it. Ergonomic cost that remains: the reader still alternates panel and graph all session; the layout keeps that an eye movement, not a head turn, but a 60-minute session of mostly reading is still a long one. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR with hands (the system's hand ray) or controllers (ray, trigger, grip, thumbstick); Apple Vision Pro with look-and-pinch (Safari's transient pointer), and PS VR2 Sense controllers where Safari exposes them. Every input is a pinch at what you aim at, so the whole scheme works with one hand, seated. Eyes are used only where the platform aims the pinch with them (Vision Pro). Voice is optional and only types into a focused text field. A paired Bluetooth keyboard, where the headset accepts one, types into the address bar and text fields.

That is the whole vocabulary: pinch, hold, drag, two-hand grab, and two pairs of toolbar buttons. Everything else is a button on a page, and buttons say what they do in words. The hold time, the 2 cm drag distance and Aim settle are Settings rows, for tremor and for slow hands (the first session uses a 0.8 s hold, so a hesitant pinch does not open the card). If hand tracking is lost mid-drag, the drag ends and records nothing: a Move and pin puts the node back where it started. On Vision Pro, hand travel is read from the transient pointer's grip pose, which Safari supplies with each pinch, so the 2 cm and 10 cm distances mean the same there; eye movement during a held pinch never counts as travel.

**Three kinds of "these nodes", and who owns every state.** The scheme keeps three subsets apart, each with its own word and home, and every dimming or window belongs to exactly one history.

A time window that should limit what algorithms see is made a filter step on purpose: the Time page's "Make this a filter step" (then Undo owns it). A run's scope field offers "this tab's time window" and records it in "How it was made".

Hide on a node, edge or basket page adds a canvas-only step to the filter chain ("Hide Medici"): the node leaves the Showing count, but algorithms still see it. A step made with "Filter what algorithms see" (the default for Filter...) changes both. The chain page marks each step with which it changes, and the Showing chip adds "(algorithms see 5)" whenever the two counts differ. "In view" is view-only and never reaches algorithms; the budget dial and the canvas-only steps need graphty-element to offer a view-only visibility apart from its filters (planned).

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put on the headset.
    - **What you see:** The graph floats ahead of you, 16 families, labeled. The reader panel stands beside it, to its left, on the graph page, "Graph: Florentine families", with an info bar at its top for the first session: "Pinch a node to open it. Drag on the graph to turn it. Tour". The toolbar reads "Showing 16 of 16"; Basket is empty; Undo is grayed.
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Read the graph page's facts. Rest the ray on a few nodes. Pinch on the graph and drag to turn it a little, as on the 2D page; pinch "View from: front" to square it up again.
    - **What you see:** Facts: "16 families, 20 marriages, undirected, 2 pieces: 15 joined, Pucci alone (link), density 0.17, average 2.5 marriages". Suggested analyses as links: "Who matters most: Influence (PageRank)", "Groups: Communities (Louvain)", "Go-betweens: Bridges (betweenness)". A name tag beside each node under the ray, and the status line, read "Strozzi -- 4 marriages". The minimap shows the whole graph with the framing outlined. The drag turned the graph even though it started on Strozzi: Strozzi did not move, and turning the graph adds nothing to Back or Undo.
    - **Controls:** read the page, hover (status line), one-hand drag on the graph, pinch a button.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the Medici node in the graph. (In a graph too big to see it, pinch the address bar, type "med" or say it, and pinch "Medici" in the Nodes group.)
    - **What you see:** The graph eases a few centimeters so Medici sits at the center of the view; the panel turns to "Node: Medici". Facts: "6 marriages; not in any run yet". Verbs, in three groups: Add to basket, Route to..., Expand..., What if removed; Pin, Hide, Style..., Note...; Merge with..., Delete.... Links: "Neighbors: +6 marriages"; "Notes: none". The breadcrumb reads "Florentine families > Medici". Back is now lit.
    - **Controls:** pinch a node (or address bar and a result link).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the stub "+6 marriages".
    - **What you see:** You stay on the Medici page. In the graph, Medici and its six neighbors become the focus: drawn full strength and labeled, while the other nine families drop to faint context. The stub opens into six links: Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni, each with its own stub ("Albizzi: +2"), and a summary line "7 families, 7 marriages among them; +5 more families at 2 hops". Three buttons sit under the list: "Add these 7 to basket", "Keep as set..." and "Fold". Fold, or Back, would fold the six away again; nothing has changed in the project, so Undo is still gray.
    - **Controls:** pinch a stub.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch "Florentine families" in the breadcrumb to go to the graph page. Pinch the suggestion "Who matters most: Influence (PageRank)". On the form, pinch Run.
    - **What you see:** The form page "Run Influence (PageRank)": Scope "Whole graph (16 families)", Damping 0.85 (stepper, detents every 0.05), Weight "None", Advanced folded (iterations 100, tolerance 1e-6). The Run button reads "Run on 16 families -- under a second". A progress bar flashes under the toolbar; the panel lands on the new page "Run: PageRank 1". Undo now reads "Undo: Run PageRank".
    - **Controls:** breadcrumb link, suggestion link, a button.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read the run page's ranking. Rest the ray on a few rows; pinch Albizzi's row, read, then press Back (or flick it on a controller).
    - **What you see:** A ranking reader (chosen because the result field is a number per node): 1 Medici 0.145, 2 Guadagni 0.098, 3 Strozzi 0.088, 4 Albizzi 0.079, 5 Tornabuoni 0.071, and on down to 16 Pucci about 0.010. A Histogram tab sits beside the ranking. The row under the ray lights its family in the graph (on Vision Pro, which has no hover, the last row you opened stays lit instead). "How it was made": PageRank, damping 0.85, whole graph, undirected, 16 nodes, under a second; caveat "Pucci has no marriages and gets only the baseline share". Pinching Albizzi opens its page; Back returns to the ranking at the same scroll position.
    - **Controls:** read, hover, pinch a row, Back.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** On the run page, pinch "Color by...". On the form, pinch the first sequential palette and Apply. Press Back once: the sent form dropped out of history, so Back goes straight to the run page. Pinch "Size by...", drag the range's upper stepper to 3x, Apply.
    - **What you see:** The color form: Paints "All nodes", Property "Color", By "PageRank" (filled in), Palette rows with swatches (sequential ones first, because PageRank is an amount), Scale "linear". The graph recolors as you pinch each palette, before you apply. Apply lands on the page "Style layer: Color by PageRank", with its legend and Edit... as its first verb; the first time, an info bar adds "Back skips the sent form; Edit... changes this layer". The size form shows "1x to 3x" and the graph resizes as you step. The graph page's layer list now reads, bottom to top, "Color by PageRank", "Size by PageRank".
    - **Controls:** buttons, swatch rows, a stepper, Back.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Press Back once to the run page. Pinch "Filter...". Drag the value's scrub strip down to 0.070 and Apply.
    - **What you see:** The form reads "PageRank [at least] [0.070]", with "Filter what algorithms see" set, and a live line "keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages" that updates at each detent, while the graph fades the families that would go, under the tag "Preview -- Apply / Discard". Apply lands on "Filter step: PageRank at least 0.070". The toolbar reads "Showing 5 of 16": Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi, Tornabuoni. Strozzi floats with no marriage left among the five, and the step page says so ("1 family kept with no ties showing").
    - **Controls:** Back, a button, scrub a number, Apply.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Medici in the graph. Pinch "Note...". Say or type "Married into six families; the hub of the network." Pinch Save.
    - **What you see:** The note form has one text field focused, with the in-panel keyboard below it (on every headset; it offers three word predictions above the keys, so the sentence takes about 25 key pinches, not 52) or a dictation light where speech works; where the system keyboard or a paired keyboard reaches the page, it types instead. Save lands on "Note: on Medici", the text shown as a sentence tile. A small marker appears on Medici. The Medici page's Links now list "Notes: 1".
    - **Controls:** pinch a node, a button, typing or voice, Save.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** Back on the Medici page (one press of Back) you mean to press Pin, but your pinch lands on Hide beside it. Medici vanishes. You notice that Back would only take you to the note page with Medici still hidden, so you pinch the curved arrow in the toolbar's far group, which reads "Undo: Hide Medici".
    - **What you see:** Before the slip, the Hide button read "Hide: 1 family, 2 marriages showing". After it, the page title reads "Node: Medici -- hidden" and the toolbar "Showing 4 of 16 (algorithms see 5)", because Hide only hides on the canvas. Undo brings Medici back with its note and marker, the toolbar returns to "Showing 5 of 16", and Redo now reads "Redo: Hide Medici". The view did not move except to frame Medici as it reappeared.
    - **Controls:** Undo (toolbar, far group).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Home. On the project page, pinch Save. Pinch the suggested name "Florentine families -- PageRank" and Save.
    - **What you see:** The project page "Project: Florentine families (not saved)". The save form offers the suggested name, so no typing. Save lands back on the project page, now "saved just now", with Recent projects and the Outbox below.
    - **Controls:** Home, a button, a suggestion, Save.

The basic journey takes about 26 pinches, 4 presses of Back, one scrub and one sentence of text.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Graph page, "Build a rule..." (or "Filter...", then "Build a rule..."). The rule form is rows: each clause row is attribute (a list), comparison (a short row of choices), value (a stepper, a value list or a date). Rows sit under group rows marked ALL OF, ANY OF or NONE OF; "Add clause" and "Add group" sit at the bottom of each group. For "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days": an ALL OF group with three rows, the middle row being an ANY OF group with two clauses. A live line reads "matches 41 of 9,812" and the matches are a link (a results page you can basket). Done lands on the rule's page, where one rule serves every use: "Add matches to basket" (select by rule), "Filter by this" (a filter step), "Keep as rule set..." (a set that follows the data), "Use as layer target" (a style form). A week later: the toolbar's "Showing" chip, then the step, then "Edit rule...", which reopens the same rows. "Save as saved filter..." files it under Bookmarks, Saved filters, with a suggested name built from the clauses.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Address bar "katz" finds the Katz page under Algorithms ("Influence at a distance (Katz)"), even though the family list shows the plain name. "Run..." opens a form with all 7 options: alpha as a stepper, tolerance as decade 1e-6 plus mantissa 1.0, weight attribute as a list of the amounts columns, normalized as a switch, the rest folded under Advanced. For a sweep, pinch Sweep beside Louvain's resolution and add 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0; Run makes four runs and lands on a sweep page with one row per run (groups, modularity, largest group), each row a link to its run. "Run again with changes..." on any run page opens the form prefilled and keeps both runs; the new run page links "Compare with the run it came from".
- **Comparing two runs.** Run page, "Compare with...": the form's second slot waits for a run; pinch the other run's link anywhere (the Runs list on the graph page, or a breadcrumb). The difference page is chosen by field kind: for two rankings, rows with old rank, new rank and change, sorted by largest change; for two community results, an overlap table (group of run 1 against group of run 2) whose cells link to their members. "Open side by side" splits the graph into two smaller views, one per run, with toggles for Same camera, Same positions and Same basket; adding a node to the basket in one lights it in both.
- **Layers on subsets.** Gather the subset in the basket (a Region page, a rule's matches, rows 1 to 10 of a ranking), "Keep as set..." with the suggested name, then the set page's "Style these...": the form's Paints field already says the set. On the graph page, the layer list shows each layer as a row with a grip; drag a row to reorder, and the graph repaints as you drag. Each layer page has "Why this look" links back from the nodes it paints.
- **A computed attribute without a formula.** Graph page, "New attribute..." (or an attribute page's "Combine with others..."): a form of rows, one per attribute taken in (an attribute list each, "Add attribute" at the bottom), a Transform per row (as is, rank, normalized 0 to 1, z-score, absolute value, log) and a Combine choice (mean, sum, difference, ratio, minimum, maximum, weighted mean with a weight stepper per row). Four rows of centralities, Transform "rank, normalized", Combine "mean", and the suggested name "mean_rank_4" or a typed "hub_score" make the hub score; the live line shows the top five nodes it would give. A free formula field sits under Advanced for anything else. The new attribute is an attribute page like any column.
- **One form for every group (Sweep over a set).** An 800-protein network after an MCL run with 15 clusters. The enrichment plugin's form, Scope field, Sweep: "each group of MCL 3 with at least [4] members -- 11 groups". The Run button reads "Run 11 enrichments -- about 40 s"; the progress bar counts them. It lands on one sweep page: a row per cluster with its top term, FDR and gene count, each row a link to that cluster's run (whose table-of-rows reader lists every term). "Label each from column...", column "top term", Apply: 11 labels in one step, each editable on its group page. On the MCL run page, "Lay out these...", Scope swept over the same groups: each cluster laid out separately in place, one step. The sweep page's "Export table..." writes one file of all terms with a cluster column. What was about 200 acts by hand is about 20.
- **Recipes.** History page: pinch "Save steps as recipe...", tick the steps (the run, the two layers, the filter), take the suggested name "PageRank, color, size, keep strong". The recipe page lists its steps and its inputs, found from the steps: every node, set, group or attribute a step read that no earlier step of the recipe made, each kept as the generated field kind its step used ("a weight attribute: none"; for a fraud recipe, "a starting account (node)", "a scope (set)", "the group to keep (choose while replaying)"). On another project, the recipe is found by the address bar or under Home, Recipes; "Replay..." opens a form with one generated field per input -- a node slot, a set or scope slot, an attribute list -- each showing what it was when recorded; a "choose while replaying" input pauses the replay on its page (the group table) with the banner "Choose the group for 'Ring 7': pinch a row". Replay stays gray, reading "2 inputs to fill", until each required input is filled; no input ever falls back to the first dataset's node ids. A step that keeps 0 nodes, or reads something missing, stops the replay there with that step's form open and says which input it came from; only a replay whose every step made something lands on the page listing what it made, each with its count and a link. A set input can also be swept, so one Replay runs the recipe once per group.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Files come through the Inbox, a page under Home. To fill it from the laptop: the project page's "Pair with laptop" shows a 6-character code; on graphty's 2D page on the laptop, "Send to headset", type the code, then drop files, paste a list or text; they appear in the Inbox within seconds and stay paired for the session. This needs a small relay service that holds a file for up to an hour, which graphty does not run today; this design specifies it and counts it as its largest build item outside the panel (see the device notes). With no laptop at hand, the Inbox's "Pick a file on this headset..." pauses the immersive session, keeping the page, the tab and the framing, opens the browser's own file chooser (headset storage everywhere; Google Drive on Galaxy XR; iCloud Drive and AirDropped files on Vision Pro), and returns to the same page when you press Enter VR again; the headset stays on. "Open as new graph..." on a CSV opens an import form: format detected, with the reader's options (separator, header row, direction); a column mapping section with one row per column, eight per page (column name, three sample values, inferred type, role: node id, source, target, attribute, skip). Roles come inferred, the source, target and id rows are pinned at the top, and "Set the rest to attribute" finishes a 40-column file in one press, so only the rows you doubt need a pinch. Submit lands on the load report page, each issue a link to the rows concerned. To join: on the graph page, "Add data...", choose the Inbox file, then a form with the key column on each side; the live line reads "matches 312 of 350 rows; 38 unmatched (link)". Submit adds the columns, each a new attribute page.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor gives a plain name, a technical name, a family, options and result fields. It appears as a link in its family section on the Algorithms page (a new family makes a new section with its own jump link), the address bar indexes both names, its page shows the description and a cost estimate, its form is generated from the option list (the third option of last week's plugin gets the same stepper, decade field or attribute list as any built-in, and every field gets Sweep), and its result fields become pages whose reader is picked by the kind the descriptor declares: per node or per edge (ranking, histogram), per group (group table), one graph value, a table of rows (typed columns -- text, number, node, node list, group -- read with sort, filter and Export table), or a series over time. Paint, Color by, Filter, Select top and Compare work on it with no new code, and its Run... appears in the address bar's Actions group.
- **A new layout.** The same, on the Layouts page; a live (iterative) layout gets Stop in the progress bar for free; a layout option that names a node gets a node slot.
- **A new object type** (a path as an object, a time slice, a group of groups, an enrichment term). It declares a page template: its facts (fields), its verbs (commands, each with an input descriptor that becomes a form), its link relations ("terms this gene is in"), whether it can go in the basket, and its marker in the graph (none, at a node, or at a region: the marker is a pick target that opens the page, as a node is). It becomes a page kind, the address bar indexes it, and every place it is mentioned becomes a link. The chrome does not change.
- **A third-party plugin.** "Biology: enrichment" with 4 algorithms becomes a family section; an option that takes a list of gene identifiers becomes a node-list slot filled from the basket (or a pasted list from the Inbox); its two numbers are steppers; its result, a table of terms (term, gene count, FDR, genes), is declared as a table of rows and read by the table reader: sort by FDR, Keep top 5 per group, and the genes cell is a link that adds them to the basket. Swept over each cluster, the four algorithms run once per group with no plugin code for that. No new page template is needed for this; one is needed only for an object people should navigate to as a thing of its own (a term with its own neighbors and links). A data-source plugin that brings typed edges ("interacts with", "regulates") turns them into stub types on every node page ("+4 regulates"), so it extends exploration as well as the catalog.
- **The practical limit.** A result shaped as none of the six kinds (a matrix, a tree, a ranked list of subgraphs) still needs a new reader, built once by graphty's team, not by the plugin author; until then it shows as a table of rows. Everything passes through a page of text in one panel: reading speed and page length are the ceiling, not the catalog. About 12 rows show at once, so a longer page scrolls or folds, a table wider than 5 columns needs a column chooser, and any input that is spatial by nature (drawing a pattern, a freeform shape, a camera path by hand) fits a form poorly. Long text is never comfortable. And the more of the work happens on pages, the less the 3D graph is looked at, which is the cost of a scheme whose strength is that nothing is ever lost.

### Why it should work

- The web browser is the most practiced interface in the world, and its navigation model is measured: in Catledge and Pitkow's study ("Characterizing browsing strategies in the World-Wide Web", 1995) Back was about 41 percent of all navigation actions, and Tauscher and Greenberg ("How people revisit web pages", International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 1997) found 58 percent of page visits were revisits. A graph analysis is mostly revisiting: back to the hub, back to the ranking. Back as a separate, harmless history fits that.
- Chrome's omnibox (2008) made one box find everything you might want to open; graphty's address bar does the same for object pages and, like the omnibox, never runs anything on its own.
- Search, show context, expand on demand: van Ham and Perer (IEEE TVCG, 2009) showed that starting from a found node and growing the graph by degree of interest makes very large graphs explorable without drawing them whole. Furnas's generalized fisheye views (CHI 1986) are the degree-of-interest function behind the budget dial. Apolo (Chau, Kittur, Hong and Faloutsos, CHI 2011) grew graphs from seeds with users steering what to expand, and users found more relevant material than with a keyword search tool.
- Typed stubs are pivots: PivotPaths (Dork, Henry Riche, Ramos and Dumais, IEEE InfoVis 2012) let people move through linked data by pivoting on a facet, and Flamenco (Yee, Swearingen, Li and Hearst, CHI 2003) showed that faceted links with counts ("+12 shared device") let people browse without getting lost, and that the counts themselves steer.
- Shneiderman's "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand" (1996): the graph page is the overview, links zoom, filters filter, and every object's page is its details on demand.
- Miller columns and the breadcrumb (NeXTSTEP, then macOS Finder) taught that showing the path you came by keeps people oriented in deep hierarchies; the breadcrumb does the same for a trail through a graph.
- Panels of pages with tabs are the shipped default of both headset platforms: visionOS Safari and Meta Quest Browser already run tabbed 2D pages people pinch at, so pinching a link at 75 cm is a learned act, not a new one.
- Generated forms scale where hand-built controls do not: Flamenco-style faceted forms, property sheets in Visual Basic (1991) and the Unity inspector render any declared field with one generator, which is the only way a plugin's option gets a control with no new code.

### What it would teach us

- Whether people still look at the 3D graph when everything can be done on pages, or whether the scheme quietly turns the headset into a large monitor; how often the graph itself is pinched compared with links.
- Whether Back and Undo stay apart in people's heads: how often someone presses Back to take back a change, or Undo to go back to a page, in the first session and the third.
- Whether "In view", "In basket" and "Showing" are three understandable ideas or one confusing one, and which pair gets mixed up.
- Whether typed stubs and the budget dial make a 10,000-node graph explorable from a searched node, and what budget people settle on.
- How many acts the six stress journeys take compared with the desktop app, and where the forms are slower than a direct gesture would be (sizing, ordering, scrubbing a threshold while watching the graph).
- How long people can read pages at 1.1 m before eye strain, and whether the 15 to 25 degree look between panel and graph still tires them over an hour.
- Whether split tabs are used for comparing, or whether difference pages do the job alone.
- Whether people miss moving nodes by dragging, now that a drag on the graph turns it and Move and pin sits behind a hold.
- How many sweeps replace repeated hand work, and whether people trust a sweep page's 11 rows enough not to open each run.

### Known risks

- **It is a desktop app in a headset.** The panel can dominate and the graph becomes decoration. Mitigation: the graph is a link surface too (pinch a node to open it, hover to name it), following any link frames its object, the stubs grow the graph in place, and the page of a result always lights its rows in the graph.
- **Back and Undo confusion.** Mitigation: different shapes (straight and curved arrows), different places (the panel's graph-side and far groups), Undo always names its step, Back's hold menu names the page it returns to, and only Back has a controller shortcut. Every state belongs to exactly one of them (the ownership table): views and bookmarks hold no project state, so Go and Back never write to Undo. A Back pressed within 10 seconds of a change shows the Undo for that change. The Help page's first card explains the difference in one line.
- **Three kinds of subset.** Mitigation: three fixed words (In view, In basket, Showing) used everywhere, three looks in the graph (full strength, a basket outline, filtered out entirely), and each in one place in the chrome (minimap, Basket, the Showing chip).
- **Slot capture surprises.** A pinch on a node fills a slot instead of opening the node. Mitigation: a slot captures only after you press its Point to choose, and only once; a banner and the status line say "Choosing To" meanwhile, the ray turns into a crosshair, Cancel on the banner disarms it, and a filled slot shows the name with an X to clear it.
- **Small node targets.** Pinching a node in a dense graph opens the wrong page. Mitigation: when the ray passes within reach of several nodes, the pinch opens a small "Which one?" card at the pinch point (not on the panel, so the eyes stay put) listing them with two values each; the address bar and stubs avoid the graph target entirely.
- **Motion when links frame objects.** On a 10,000-node graph, framing a far node moves an object that fills 40 degrees of view or more, about 15 times in a session: a classic trigger for vection. Mitigation: the graph moves, never you; small moves ease at a steady speed; any move of more than about 10 degrees of view, or a scale change past 1.5 times, fades out and in instead of easing, whatever the setting; framing never scales the graph past about 40 degrees of view; Settings offers ease, fade, or Framing off (links open the page and only light the node); grab and minimap jumps never auto-repeat.
- **Accidental node moves.** Removed as a class: a drag on the graph turns it and never moves a node. A node moves only after hold, "Move and pin", and a drag; the move is a named undo step ("Undo: Move and pin Medici"), a second hand's pinch or lost tracking puts the node back, and Unpin is on the node's page. The cost: moving many nodes by hand takes two extra acts each.
- **Turning the head between panel and graph.** The panel-to-graph look is repeated all session. Mitigation: straight ahead points at the seam, so the look is 15 to 25 degrees of eye movement rather than a head turn; Back, Forward and Home sit on the edge nearest the graph; the hover tag and the Which one? card sit at the graph; result rows light their nodes so the graph confirms what the page says. The old docked-below placement is gone: at 35 to 50 degrees below eye level it bent the neck for reading.
- **Long pages and paging.** Mitigation: the four-section anatomy, folded sections with jump links, Peek instead of opening, and counts on every collapsed section.
- **Text entry.** Mitigation: suggested names on every name field, type-ahead in the address bar, sentence tiles for notes, voice where a recognizer exists, a paired keyboard as first class, and Continue on laptop for anything long.
- **Files.** The Inbox and Outbox are only as good as the pairing relay, which this design specifies but graphty does not run yet. Until it runs: files on the headset or its cloud drive come in by the paused-session chooser; files go out by Share... (AirDrop on Vision Pro, Drive or Quick Share on Galaxy XR); on Quest, whether the browser can share files must be checked, and otherwise a file reaches the laptop only by uploading it from a Quest Browser tab or by cable. That last route is awkward and the design cannot improve it without the relay.
- **Arm fatigue from ray pointing at a panel.** On Quest and Galaxy XR the hand ray is cast from the shoulder through the hand, so aiming at the panel needs the hand at lower-chest height, elbow tucked: a sustained hover, not a rest. Mitigation: Seated low places the panel at the graph's height so the forearm can rest on an armrest; most acts are single pinches; Vision Pro aims with the eyes, so the hand can stay in the lap.
- **Stray pinches from a resting hand.** A hand at rest still points its ray somewhere, and a thumb touching a finger is a pinch. Mitigation: a pinch counts only in the pointing pose (palm away), only after the ray has settled on its target for about 120 ms, and only on release at the target it started on; Stop asks once; every other mis-press is a named Undo or one Back.
- **Frame rate with split views.** Two graph views double the draw cost. Mitigation: difference pages first; split only on request, with the second view drawing only its in-view budget (no faint context).

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: Sweep works beside any field, not only numbers. Beside a node slot, a set or scope field, an attribute list or an enumeration, it turns the field into a list of values ("each group of MCL 3 with at least 4 members", "each node in the basket", "rows 1 to 10"). One press makes one run, layout, label, export or style per value, and lands on one sweep page with a combined Export table and "Label each from column...". Recipes take inputs of the same generated kinds, and a set input can be swept, so one Replay runs a recipe once per group. No new control was added: it is the same Sweep link, on more field kinds. The 15-cluster annotation that took about 200 acts by hand now takes about 20.

Severity 3 findings:

- **A drag on the graph moved and pinned a node unnoticed** (VR interaction, onboarding and red team; journey step 2, two-hand grab). Fixed. A one-hand drag on the graph now turns it, as a drag orbits the 2D page, and never moves a node. Moving a node is "Move and pin" on a node's hold card, followed by a drag. A second pinch at any moment cancels whatever the first pinch started and puts back anything it moved. Losing tracking mid-drag records nothing. The cost is stated under Known risks: moving a node takes two extra acts.
- **The resting hand was the aiming hand** (VR interaction; ergonomics, which noted that the claim "the ray works from a hand on the knee" is wrong). Fixed. A pinch counts only in the pointing pose (palm away, read from the hand joints on Quest and Galaxy XR), after the ray settles on its target for about 120 ms (a Settings row), and on release at the target it started on. The posture claim is corrected: a Quest hand ray needs the hand at lower-chest height, and the new Seated low placement lets the forearm rest on an armrest.
- **Stop cancelled running work in one unconfirmed pinch beside often-used buttons** (VR interaction). Fixed. Stop left the toolbar. It now lives only on the progress bar, names what it stops, and asks once there ("2 finished runs are kept"). A live layout's Stop, which loses nothing, does not ask.
- **Both panel placements loaded the neck for the whole session** (ergonomics). Fixed. Straight ahead now points at the seam between panel and graph, so each sits about 13 degrees off center. Looking from page to graph is an eye movement of about 15 to 26 degrees instead of a head turn of up to 40. Back, Forward and Home sit on the panel edge nearest the graph. The docked-below placement, which put rows at 35 to 50 degrees below eye level, is gone. The panel also moved back to 1.1 m and was scaled to keep its angular size, which eases eye strain (a severity 2 finding). Not fixable inside the idea: the person still alternates between panel and graph all session.
- **Files from the laptop depended on a deferred link** (coverage and red team; areas 1, 14, 16, 19 and 21; stress journeys 3, 5 and 6). Fixed by specifying the link as part of the design. "Pair with laptop" shows a 6-character code; the laptop's 2D page sends files, lists and text drafts to the Inbox, and the Outbox's Send to laptop and Continue on laptop come back the same way. Its relay service, which graphty does not run today, is priced under the device notes. The routes that work today are also specified. "Pick a file on this headset..." pauses the immersive session and returns to the same page, with the headset kept on. Share... reaches AirDrop on Vision Pro and Drive or Quick Share on Galaxy XR. Downloads wait until the session pauses. Not fixable without the relay: on Quest, a laptop file reaches the headset (and an export reaches the laptop) only by a browser-tab upload or a cable.
- **No "for each group" repeat for the persona workflow** (expert). Fixed by the top change above.
- **An enrichment result had no reader, and a plugin could not add one** (expert and red team, extensibility). Fixed. A plugin's descriptor can declare a result as a table of rows with typed columns. The table reader sorts and filters it, offers Keep top N per group and Export table, and turns node-list cells into links. A series reader was added for values over time. The file now says what remains: a result shaped as none of the six kinds still needs a reader built once by graphty's team, and shows as a table until then.
- **Looking leaked into changing** (red team; journey 6 step 1, area 15, Fade others, form previews). Fixed with a state-ownership table: every dimming and window belongs to exactly one history. The time window, Focus on these (which replaces Fade others) and the budget belong to the tab's view, so Back takes them back, each tab has its own, and bookmarks hold them. Going to a bookmark therefore never writes to Undo, and two tabs can compare two moments. Filters, Hide, layers and pins belong to the project and to Undo. A form preview belongs to neither, and is tagged "Preview -- Apply / Discard" at the graph. "Make this a filter step" turns a time window into project state on purpose.
- **A replayed recipe silently ran on the wrong nodes** (red team, journey 6 step 6). Fixed. A recipe's inputs are found from its steps and keep their field kinds (node, set or scope, attribute, choices). Some inputs are "choose while replaying" and pause the replay on their page. Replay stays gray until every input is filled. A step that keeps 0 nodes stops the replay at that step's form and names its input. No step falls back to the first dataset's node ids.

Severity 2 findings also addressed:

- **Journey 6's key count was understated four times over** (red team). Every stress journey now counts characters, sentences included, and the key pinches they cost on the in-panel keyboard with word prediction. Journey 6 is about 170 characters and about 100 key pinches, after the report title, methods and summary are prefilled from the project, the journal and the Ring 7 note.
- **It was unclear what a style preset does to the layer stack** (red team, journey 6 step 2). A preset either swaps the palettes of the existing layers in place, keeping their order, or adds its layers on top. Its button says which, with counts, and no preset replaces the stack.
- **Text entry was written against an unverified system keyboard** (WebXR and ergonomics). Journey step 9 and every journey now assume the in-panel keyboard, with word prediction and keys scaled by Text size. The system keyboard and a paired keyboard are upgrades.
- **Feedforward at the graph was missing on Vision Pro** (red team, which lowered feedforward from 5 to 4). This cannot be fixed: the platform gives web pages no hover. The file now says so in the hover row and the Vision Pro row. The mitigations: opening a page is harmless, the last row you opened stays lit, and Peek is sticky.
- **Framing at scale moved large objects** (red team, ergonomics). A move over about 10 degrees of view, or a scale change past 1.5 times, now fades instead of easing. Framing never makes the graph wider than about 40 degrees. Framing can also be turned off.
- **Back after a change, Back after a sent form, and Back from a slot's form were unclear** (onboarding, VR interaction). Each now has a fix:
    - A Back within 10 seconds of a change shows that change's Undo.
    - The landing page leads with Edit..., and the false "as a sent web form does" claim is corrected.
    - Forward restores an unsent form.
    - An armed slot keeps its form open, with "Back to form" on its banner.
- **First-use hints were unspecified** (onboarding). Five hints are now named, along with where each appears. The tour teaches dragging to turn the graph, and the Tour link shows on whatever page the session starts on.
- **Several gestures were underspecified** (VR interaction, WebXR, onboarding):
    - The hold card has a filling ring, a 0.8 s first-session hold, and a stated way to open and close.
    - The "Which one?" card is capped at the 5 nearest nodes, and its rows light their nodes.
    - A scroll gutter keeps a short flick from opening a row.
    - The thumbstick locks to one axis.
    - The scrub strip jumps to the point you aim at.
    - Buttons have a half-height gap between them.
    - Vision Pro distances are read from the grip pose.
- **The table header's hold contradicted the fixed hold list** (coverage, expert). Each header now has a visible "..." that opens its column's page.
- **Engineering costs were unstated** (WebXR). The file now covers:
    - Region-based panel redraw with glyph-atlas text.
    - Hover picking through a spatial index.
    - Previews written to one instance buffer, falling back to preview-on-release above an 8 ms estimate.
    - Instanced labels.
    - A quad layer placed with an alpha hole.
    - A split view that draws only its in-view budget.
- **New object types had no presence in the graph** (coverage). A page template now declares a marker: none, at a node, or at a region.
- **Time had no window size, step or series reader** (coverage). The Time page now has window size and step fields, and "Measure over time..." reads in the series reader.

Left as they are, and said so in the file: long report text is still written on the laptop, freeform highlight shapes and pattern drawing remain weak, and the more of the work happens on pages, the less the 3D graph is looked at.
