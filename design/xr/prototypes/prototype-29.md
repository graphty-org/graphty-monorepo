**Prototype 29: Encoding Shelves** -- Tableau's shelves for a graph: every attribute, result, analysis, layout, set, palette and data edit is a pill in a data pane, and you put pills on fixed role shelves (Node color, Size, Label, Shape, Edge color, Width, X, Y, Layout, Group by, Select, Filter, Edits, Time, Compare, Analyze, Table, Notes) by dragging them or by tapping the pill and then the shelf, adjusting a small popover on each pill. One rule holds everywhere: nothing changes the graph's data or look except a pill on a shelf, and a pinch in the graph only opens a menu or draws a rectangle.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away, with its handle (a bar 20 cm wide with a knob at each end) just below it. Below the graph, about 50 cm from you and tilted up about 30 degrees, sits one panel curved around you, about 60 cm along its arc (about 70 degrees of view): the data pane on its left third, the shelf cards in two rows on the rest, the worksheet tabs along its top edge, and the Undo, Redo, View, Project and Help buttons at its top right. Seated, its center defaults to about 25 to 30 degrees below eye level; standing, lower chest height. From the eye, top to bottom: the graph, its handle, then the panel. Seated, the panel's top edge sits about 16 degrees below eye level, and Fit sizes and places the graph so its lower edge stays about 5 degrees above that, with the handle in the gap (the graph's center rises to about 8 degrees below eye level, and a fitted graph is about 25 cm tall at 75 cm). A popover opens directly above its own shelf, on a leaf hinged at the panel's top edge and leaning back toward you, up to about 15 degrees tall; it may cover the graph's lower part while open, and the handle hides while a popover is open so a ray cannot hit the wrong one. A popover never sits more than about 20 degrees from the shelf you just used. The keyboard opens at the panel's center, tilted toward your face, at most 30 degrees below eye level. Legends float at the graph's right side; legends, the Table window, readers and Inspect cards open beside the graph or, in Settings, dock on the panel at 50 cm. Toasts (what a graph act or an undo just did) show for 1.5 s along the graph's lower edge, where the eyes already are. The panel works by ray from a relaxed hand (the default) and by direct touch (poke and pinch) when its distance is set to touch reach. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR with hands or controllers; Apple Vision Pro with look and pinch, or hands with direct touch when hand tracking is granted. Eyes are used only as Vision Pro's system pointer. Voice is optional and only types into an open text field. Panel distance, height, tilt, handedness (data pane left or right), tap-to-drag threshold, snap distance and "Taps only" (no carrying; every placement by two taps) are settings, and the whole scheme works seated and with one hand: every drag has a two-tap equivalent, and the graph's handle turns the graph with one hand.

**The laptop channel.** On graphty's 2D page on the laptop, Project, Pair a headset shows a 6-digit code; in the headset, Project, Open, Laptop asks for it on the keyboard's digits row (or the code is entered before Enter VR on the headset's 2D page). The pair then carries files both ways (a file dropped on the laptop page appears in the headset's Project, Open, Laptop list; Send to laptop puts a headset file in the laptop page's downloads), text pasted on the laptop (API keys, gene lists, long notes), and drafts ("Finish on laptop" for a report section). It needs a small relay service that graphty does not run today, plus the pairing code exchange; that build cost is stated under Known risks. Without a pair, a file on the headset is reached by leaving the session: graphty saves every tab, the camera and any open popover before the session ends, the browser's file picker opens, and on return a large "Back to VR" button sits where Enter VR was; the session resumes as it was, and a run in progress keeps running in the page.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put on the headset, standing or seated.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families at chest height, Pucci floating alone at the edge, the graph's handle under them. Below, the panel. The data pane's Attributes section holds one pill, "name"; Results is empty; Analyses lists six families. The state of the view is already on the shelves: "Spread Out" sits on Layout and "name" on Label. One worksheet tab reads "View 1". On the first entry to VR, whatever is open, a card offers the tour (Start, Later). (Entering with no data open shows the Project, Open list as a start card, samples first.)
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch tap empty space inside the graph and choose Inspect graph. Pinch with both hands and turn the graph a quarter turn (or drag an end knob of the graph's handle with one hand); open View and choose Fit.
    - **What you see:** A card: "16 families, 20 marriages, undirected, 2 pieces (Pucci has no marriages)". The graph turns with your hands and Fit settles it back in front of you. Had you pinch-dragged with one hand inside the graph, a rectangle would have appeared and selected what it covered, with a one-time hint at its corner: "This selects. To turn, use both hands or the handle below."
    - **Controls:** pinch tap (graph click menu), two-hand pinch or the handle, View menu.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the search box at the top of the data pane and type "med" (or say it). Tap the "Medici" row in the results.
    - **What you see:** Results appear under the box while you type: one node pill, Medici, and no fields, analyses or commands match. Tapping the row frames Medici, outlines it and opens its click menu beside the row; you close the menu. Going to a node does not change the selection.
    - **Controls:** tap a text field, keyboard or voice, pinch tap a row.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch tap Medici in the graph and choose Neighbors.
    - **What you see:** A clause pill "within 1 hop of Medici" lands on the Select shelf, replacing whatever it held (here nothing). Medici and its six in-laws (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni) light up, and a toast at the graph's lower edge reads "Selected 7: Medici and its in-laws". The Select shelf reads "7 of 16 families; 7 marriages inside, 6 to the rest". Had you then asked for the Strozzi's Neighbors, the menu item would have read "replaces: within 1 hop of Medici" and the toast "Selected 5: Strozzi and its in-laws -- replaced 1 rule -- Undo"; to see only the families married to both, you would choose Narrow to these, Neighbors, and the toast would say "Narrowed to 1: Ridolfi". Tapping the pill would show a hops stepper (1 to 5), direction (both, as the graph is undirected), "include Medici", and an edge-type checklist.
    - **Controls:** pinch tap (node click menu), menu item that places a pill.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** In the data pane, open Analyses, Centrality, and drag "Influence (PageRank)" onto the Analyze shelf (or tap Analyze's "+" and tap it in the picker). In the popover that opens, leave the options as they are and tap Run.
    - **What you see:** While carried, the Analyze shelf lights up and says "Runs PageRank on the whole graph, about 1 ms"; Node color and Filter stay dim ("needs a field, this is an analysis"). The popover is the generated form: damping 0.85, iterations 100, tolerance 1e-6 (folded under Advanced), weight slot empty (none), direction as loaded, Scope slot empty ("whole graph", with "Use the 7 selected instead" under it). After Run the pill becomes "PageRank 1" with a done mark, and the Results section gains a header "PageRank 1" with one field pill, "pagerank".
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag a pill and release on a shelf (or two taps), popover, tap Run.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch tap the "pagerank" field pill in the Results section.
    - **What you see:** The field's card, its reader chosen from its kind (a number per node): a ranked list and a histogram. Medici 0.144, Guadagni 0.097, Strozzi 0.087, Albizzi 0.078, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069 ... Acciaiuoli 0.030, Pucci 0.010. Tapping a row frames that family and opens its menu. Under the reader, a "Put on" row: Node color, Size, Label, Filter, Select, Table, X, Y.
    - **Controls:** pinch tap a pill, pinch tap a row.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Drag "pagerank" onto Node color; in its popover choose a sequential palette. Then tap Size in the card's "Put on" row (the Node color popover commits as you do); set the range to 1x and 3x and tap Done.
    - **What you see:** Resting the carried pill on Node color recolors the graph as a preview; the release keeps it. The legend "Node color: PageRank 0.010 to 0.144" appears at the graph's right. Tapping Size in the "Put on" row previews too, with Keep and Cancel. Size grows Medici to three times Pucci. Each shelf shows "16 families". The Layers switch would show two stacks: Size by PageRank, and Node color by PageRank.
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag and release on a shelf; a "Put on" button; popover (palette grid, range).

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Drag "pagerank" onto the Filter shelf. In the popover, drag the range slider's left handle over the histogram to 0.070 (thumbstick or the stepper for the last detents, or tap the number and type it), and tap Done.
    - **What you see:** As the handle moves, families fade out and the popover reads "keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages". On Done the clause pill reads "Hide: PageRank at least 0.070" and the Filter shelf reads "Showing 5 of 16". Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain; the Select shelf now reads "3 of 7 visible".
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag, release on a shelf, range slider in a popover.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch tap Medici and choose Note this. Type or dictate "Married into six families; the hub of the network" (word suggestions after the first letters of each word) and tap Done.
    - **What you see:** A note pill "Married into six..." lands on the Notes shelf, bound to Medici, and a small marker appears on the node.
    - **Controls:** node click menu, text field with the keyboard or voice.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You wonder whether the PageRank rule would work better as a selection, and drag the filter pill onto the Select shelf. While it rests there, the pill reads "Move" and Select previews "Move from Filter -- AND with within 1 hop of Medici: 3 of 16", and the graph shows all 16 families again. You release anyway. Medici, Albizzi and Tornabuoni are selected and the filter is gone, which is not what you wanted. Tap Undo.
    - **What you see:** The Undo button reads "Undo: Move PageRank at least 0.070 from Filter to Select". Undo puts the pill back on Filter, a toast at the graph reads "Undid: Move PageRank at least 0.070 -- Redo", the graph returns to 5 of 16 and the selection to the Medici's in-laws. Redo reads "Redo: Move PageRank at least 0.070 from Filter to Select". (Had you let go in empty air instead, the pill would simply have flown back; had you dragged it by its grip, it would only have changed its place among the Filter steps.)
    - **Controls:** Undo button (B or Y on controllers).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Open Project and choose Save; accept the suggested name "Florentine families" with Done.
    - **What you see:** "Saved Florentine families" under the Project button. The worksheet tab "View 1" is saved with the project, so every pill on every shelf comes back with it.
    - **Controls:** Project menu, Done on a suggested name.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** After running Degree and Bridges (betweenness), the Filter shelf holds "Hide: PageRank at least 0.070" in row one. Drag "degree" onto Filter (row two, "at least 4"), then carry "betweenness" to the degree pill's joint: while hovering it reads "OR: 3 of 16" (against "AND: 2 of 16" on the shelf); the joint opens with OR chosen and the two become one bracketed pill, "degree at least 4 OR betweenness at least 0.2". Invert in a pill's popover makes NOT; Invert on the bracket negates the group, and "+ row" inside the bracket nests an AND row in the OR. The shelf header reads "Showing 3 of 16: Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi". - **Tuning algorithm options.** Tap "PageRank 1" on the Analyze shelf. Its popover is the same generated form: damping as a slider with 0.05 detents, iterations as a stepper, tolerance under Advanced as a decade stepper (1e-4 to 1e-10), weight as a slot that takes a field pill, Reset to defaults. Set damping to 0.50 and tap Re-run as new: "PageRank 2" sits beside "PageRank 1", and both runs' fields are in Results ("pagerank -- PageRank 2"). A sweep is one act: a number option's menu has Sweep, which takes a list of values (0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0 for a Louvain resolution, set with a stepper) and places one run pill per value, then Run all.
- **Comparing two runs.** Drag "PageRank 1" and "PageRank 2" onto the Compare shelf; both stay on Analyze too, because run pills are copied. Its reader says "6 families change rank; Salviati climbs from 10th to 5th, Ridolfi falls from 6th to 10th" with a rank table. ### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers a descriptor; a pill appears in its family in the Analyses section and in Analyze's "+" picker (search finds it by either name), and its name in the command search. On Analyze, its popover is generated from the options list: numbers become sliders or steppers (with Sweep), logarithmic numbers decade steppers, attribute options field slots, node and edge options slots armed and filled by a tap, set options set slots, date ranges a range slot, colors a swatch slot, choices lists, toggles switches. An option kind the generator does not know shows "Set this on the 2D page" rather than vanishing. Its result fields arrive as field pills under the run, and every shelf that accepts that kind takes them. The plugin family from the checklist ("Biology: enrichment", one algorithm taking a gene list and two numbers) shows as a new family; the gene list is a list slot that takes a set pill, a text field pill, or text pasted on the laptop.
- **A new layout.** A pill in Layouts, a generated popover on the Layout shelf, the same Scope slot and the Pinned pill. An iterative layout gets Stop and live progress from its kind.
- **A new object type.** It needs a pill kind, a pick target in the scene (tapping the drawn object opens its menu, like a node or an edge), and a list of shelves that accept it. Every shelf that takes sets reads a new kind by the elements it names (a path pill on Filter or Select means its nodes and edges), so only a shelf with a special meaning needs a rule. Example: a "path" object becomes a path pill that Select, Filter, Compare and Notes accept; its click menu's items are generated from those shelves. No new panel.
- **A new shelf.** A new role (for example "Sound" or a domain-specific "Pathway") is one more shelf on a card, with its accepted kinds and a generated popover. This is rare and deliberate; most features need only pills. Roles beyond what the panel holds go on a second page of cards, reached by a page tab at the panel's right end.
- **A third-party plugin.** Its readers, writers, palettes and data sources appear where their kinds belong. A plugin that adds a style property appears in "+ property" from the style schema. A result field of a kind no reader knows opens in the plain value table.
- **The practical limit.** The fixed shelves are the vocabulary, and about 18 shelves plus "+ property" fill the panel (folded when empty); a feature that is not "put this thing in a role" (a 2000-character report) does not fit a shelf and goes to a popover form or the laptop. Placing is the price of everything: a desktop click becomes a drag of 20 to 40 cm or two taps, and long sessions will show which people prefer.

### Why it should work

- Polaris (Stolte, Tang and Hanrahan, IEEE TVCG 2002, doi 10.1109/2945.981851) showed that a visual specification built by dragging fields onto shelves is a complete, compositional language for views; it became Tableau, which millions of analysts learned without a manual. Voyager 2 and Polestar (Wongsuphasawat et al., CHI 2017) and Power BI's field wells kept the same pattern, and Power BI and Tableau both offer a non-drag path (tick a field, or "Add to" from its menu), which is the "Put on" row here.
- ImAxes (Cordeil et al., UIST 2017) let people compose scatterplots in VR by placing axes, and first-time users built views quickly with no menus; Embodied Axes (Cordeil et al., CHI 2020) showed range handles on axes give precise selection. The X and Y shelves and the range-slider filters borrow both.
- DXR (Sicat et al., IEEE TVCG 2019) gave immersive visualizations an in-place panel that sets marks and channels from a declarative specification, much like a shelf popover generated from a schema.
- Gephi's Filters panel (Bastian, Heymann and Jacomy, ICWSM 2009) builds nested rules by dropping one filter onto another, and its users manage compound rules that way; the OR group at a joint is the same act.
- Grammar-based specifications (Vega-Lite, Satyanarayan et al., IEEE TVCG 2017) show that every encoding can be expressed as "field on channel", which is why a plugin's field reaches every role with no new UI.
- Large panel targets within reach suit hand tracking better than small targets in the scene: pills are about 6 by 2.5 cm (about 7 by 3 degrees at 50 cm), far wider than a node under 1 degree, and the redesign keeps every precise act on the panel: node verbs open from search and reader rows too.

### What it would teach us

- Is drag and drop in a headset fast and reliable enough to be the main act, with hands, with controllers and with Vision Pro's transient pointer? How many drops miss their shelf, and how often do people switch to the two-tap path or turn on Taps only?
- Do people who have never used Tableau understand roles and pills, and does the five-act tour teach the grammar in under 5 minutes?
- Do people read the shelves as the state of the view ("why is this gray? the Filter shelf says Fade") and use Why this look less because of it, now that nothing changes the view without a pill?
- Does a fixed set of about 18 shelves feel complete, or do people hunt for verbs that are not roles (merge, expand, export)?
- Can generated popovers handle the hardest forms (ForceAtlas2's 13 options, Katz's tolerance) without a keyboard?
- Do people arm node slots on purpose, or do they expect a node tap to fill a slot they can see waiting?
- Is Move in the node menu found by people who expect to grab a node, and is losing direct node dragging missed?
- Do stale marks on runs after an edit is taken out read as "re-run me", or as broken results?
- Does "replace at the graph, combine on the shelf" match what people expect from Neighbors and Select these, and do they read the toast at the graph?
- Do X and Y attribute scatters get used for comparison, and do people find their way back to the drawn layout?
- How tired does the arm and neck get after 30 minutes of placing pills at 50 cm, by ray from a resting hand versus direct touch, with popovers above their shelves?

### Known risks

- **Drag fatigue and misses.** Every act is a placement. Mitigation: the panel sits low and close and spans only about 70 degrees; popovers open above their own shelf, not at the panel's far end; ray drags work from a resting hand; nothing reflows during a carry, a pill lands only on the shelf under it (or within 1 cm), and a line shows where it will land; every drag has a two-tap equivalent ("Put on" in a pill's card, "+" on a shelf), which is the default on Vision Pro and the only path with Taps only; click menus place pills without any drag. What cannot be fixed without abandoning the idea: a placement is still a sweep from pane to shelf to graph, so sessions past about 45 minutes will tire the neck more than a design that acts at the object.
- **Accidental changes.** A tap that drifts into a drag, or a drop on the neighboring shelf. Mitigation: an adjustable drag threshold, measured in degrees at the ray's hit in the graph; a pinch in the graph can only open a menu or draw a rectangle, never move anything; a rectangle that would replace a rule says so at its corner before release; a resting-hand pose and an on-target dwell on the panel, with a dim pulse when a tap is ignored; a drop commits only after a 150 ms rest on its target and a 100 ms release; a release anywhere but a shelf or a joint changes nothing; a lost hand returns the pill; cheap roles preview before release and expensive ones describe; every placement is a named undo step.
- **Panel crowding.** About 18 shelves, a data pane and popovers on one panel. Mitigation: empty shelves fold to labels; "+ property" shelves live in the Marks card's fold; popovers open above their shelf; text size is a setting, and larger text pages the cards instead of widening the panel. Not fixed: a newcomer still sees the whole vocabulary at once; a reduced starter panel would be a second mode, and the design chooses folding over modes.
- **Not everything is a role.** Merging, expanding, exporting and pinning are verbs, not encodings. Mitigation: each verb places a pill on a shelf that can hold it (Edits, Filter, Compare, the Pinned pill on Layout), so it can be seen, switched off and taken out; export and statistics open readers and files, which change nothing in the view. The camera and the current tab are the two honest exceptions to "the state is the pills".
- **Taking out an old data edit.** Removing a merge or a removal changes the data later runs used. Mitigation: those runs are kept with a stale mark and named before you confirm; nothing is recomputed silently. The cost: a person who takes out an early edit must re-run what is stale by hand.
- **Armed node slots.** While a slot is armed, a node tap fills it. Mitigation: a slot arms only when you tap it or choose a menu item that says it waits (Route from here); the aimed node shows what a tap will do (a banner on Vision Pro); the slot disarms when filled, on Cancel, or when its popover closes; each fill or pick is an undo step.
- **Moving a node is two acts.** Node dragging is gone; Move is a menu item. People from the 2D page will pinch-drag a node expecting to move it and get a rectangle. Mitigation: the rectangle's first-use hint says "To move a node, tap it and choose Move". This is the price of a graph that never changes under a stray pinch.
- **Live previews on large graphs.** A whole-graph restyle per frame cannot hold 72 Hz at 10,000 nodes on Quest. Mitigation: previews start only after a 200 ms rest on a shelf, run only while the page's own timing of restyles on this graph stays under 8 ms a frame (graphty-element is not assumed to estimate restyle cost; the page times the restyles it applies, the first preview on a new graph is timed once and stops the moment it overruns, and the node count where previews end is to be measured, roughly a few thousand on Quest 3), and become descriptions above it; filter sliders preview on each detent's release; Time playback steps frame by frame when needed. Aim picking uses a spatial index and skips the graph while the ray is on the panel; the panel texture redraws only when its content changes, and the carried pill and shelf highlights are separate meshes over it.
- **Replace at the graph, combine on the shelf.** A person who meant to add a second family's neighbors with Neighbors gets a replacement instead. Mitigation: the menu item names what it replaces before the tap, the toast at the graph names it after, with Undo, and Add to selection and Narrow to these sit in the same menu. The opposite trap -- a menu verb that silently narrows to a wrong answer that looks right -- is gone, which is the reason for the rule.
- **Scoping by a network or a set.** A run scoped to a set of nodes sees every edge among them, which in a union of two networks includes the other network's edges. Mitigation: a network pill names its edges as well as its nodes, edge clauses can scope a run, the scope line gives node and edge counts, and Run warns when two runs would see the same scope. Not fixed: a person who scopes by a plain node set in a union still gets both networks' edges, and only the scope line's wording tells them.
- **Custom engineering this design needs from the page.** None of these is a standard WebXR event, and each must be built and tuned per device: the resting-hand pose test; the 150 ms on-target rule with its per-target dwell; the forward-motion test for pokes; the release debounce and the 150 ms drop dwell; the carry, reorder and scroll split by grip region; the rectangle's 150 ms hold for converting to a two-hand grab; per-frame picking of the aimed node while a slot is armed (on the existing spatial index); the "Which one?" proximity list; and the page's own restyle timing that decides previews. The laptop channel needs a relay service graphty does not run (a small WebSocket relay holding a pairing code for a few minutes and forwarding files and text between two browser sessions; hosting, abuse limits and file size limits included), which is new server work for a project that today ships only static pages. These are estimates of work, not risks of failure, but none has been prototyped.
- **The selection does not scope a run.** People may expect PageRank to run on the selected seven. Mitigation: the Analyze shelf always says "whole graph" or names the scope, the popover offers "Use the 7 selected instead", and the selection header offers Run on this.
- **Reading tables and lists in a headset.** Readers and the Table window need large text. Mitigation: readers show top 10 with "N more"; tables page and can dock on the panel at 50 cm; values can be sent to the laptop.
- **Text entry.** Names, finds, notes and keys need typing. Mitigation: suggested names everywhere, search by a few letters, a page-drawn keyboard with a caret, word suggestions and a digits row on every device, opened at the panel's center, dictation where the browser supports it, the paired laptop for keys and anything longer than a few sentences.
- **Two drop meanings.** Dropping on a joint combines, dropping on a shelf puts in a role. Mitigation: the joint exists only on compatible pills and only while you carry one, it is at least 2.5 cm and wins when both are under the pill, it outlines differently from a shelf, the hover preview names AND or OR with its count, and its popover confirms the combination.
- **The laptop channel and two graphs.** The pairing needs a relay service graphty does not run, and side-by-side Compare needs graphty-element to draw two graphs in one immersive session, which it cannot today. Mitigation: without a pair, the headset's own file picker with the session saved and resumed; without two graphs, Compare's overlay with a toggle between networks.
