**Prototype 30: One Question at a Time** -- A home panel asks "What do you want to do?" with tasks grouped by goal; every task is then asked as a short series of large single questions ("Which node is the start?"), skipping what can be inferred and ending on a "Check your answers" card, and every finished task stays as a card where any one answer can be changed and the task re-run -- as a new version of the card, with the old one still readable and every later card naming the version it used. Which nodes a task works on is never a question you meet by surprise: one visible working set on the bar ("Working on: the 214 shown -- last 90 days -- change") is what every task, shortcut and replay takes, and every answer that runs says so.

There is no metaphor here. It is the plain idea behind tax software, government services and onboarding forms -- one thing per page, check your answers -- turned into the only way to command graphty in a headset. Its claim on coverage is simple: every algorithm, layout, reader, writer and option in graphty already arrives as a plain-data descriptor with ordered inputs, and an ordered list of inputs is already an interview.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away. The question card (or the home panel, which takes its place) sits beside the graph, about 25 degrees to the non-dominant side, 1 m away and 15 degrees below eye level, so it never covers the graph; the task list sits on the dominant side. Both can be moved by their title strip and the side swaps with handedness. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: on Quest and Galaxy XR, the hand's target ray and the system pinch (the browser supplies both as select events), or controllers (ray and trigger); on Vision Pro, look and pinch (Safari's transient pointer). Hand joints are not needed. Eyes are used only where the browser already turns them into the pinch's target. Voice is optional, push to talk only (pinch the card's microphone, or hold the thumbstick pressed in on controllers), and does two things: say an answer's label to pick it, and dictate into a text answer. Microphone permission is asked on the 2D page before entering, because a permission prompt pauses an immersive session. The whole scheme works seated and one-handed: one hand turns the graph by dragging anywhere on it, and "Take me to..." (closer, farther, Fit, a preset, Turn left, Turn right, Turn over) replaces the two-hand scale. On Quest the system's recenter brings the graph and both cards back in front of you (the page listens for the reference space's reset event and re-places them); Vision Pro gives web pages no recenter event, so "Take me to..., Recenter" does the same on every device. Files and long text move between the headset and a laptop through a paired laptop: the "Pair a laptop" task (Settings and Help, and offered on every file or long-text card) shows a 6-character code; typing it on graphty's 2D page on the laptop connects the two pages. This needs one small signaling relay that graphty, served as static pages, does not have today; it is specified here as part of the design and priced under feasibility. Until it is built, the fallback is stated per card ("Leave VR to pick the file"): the session ends, the 2D page in the headset browser shows its picker, and re-entering VR restores the project, the view and the open interview from its draft.

### The home panel

The home panel is the first card of every task. It is a list of goals in plain words; pinching a goal shows its tasks as answers. Above the goals sit a search field (every task by name, including a plugin's), a "Recent tasks" row (the last five tasks, each with "same answers", "same answers, new target" -- which asks only the node, set or network the task works on -- or "new answers"; "same answers" takes today's working set, and when that differs from the scope the task stored it says so on the answer -- "same answers, on the 214 shown now (last time: all 1,200)" -- and goes to the Check card instead of running), and "Suggested next" (two or three tasks the last result makes likely). When opened by pinching an object, its title is the object ("What do you want to do with Medici?"), its header shows the object's key values, and only that object's verbs are listed, in pages by group: the first page is fixed for each object kind, so a node always shows Look closer, Show who it is connected to, Write a note, Take me there, Trace a route from here, Why does it look like this?, Move it and More; the "More" page holds the rest; verbs that hide, remove or merge sit in a last row set apart from the others on every page. Goals and tasks keep a fixed order so their positions can be learned; only the "Suggested next" row changes. Each task has one name everywhere; a task that fits two goals is listed under both with the same name.

### The task list

When a task finishes, its card opens where the question was, so the result is read in the same place the questions were asked; Done (or the next task) closes it to one line on the task list ("Rank by influence (PageRank) on 16 families -- Medici first"). The task list is the project's objects, not a log of every act: each task that made something lasting (a result, a style layer, a filter step, a set, a note, a saved view, a layout) is that object's card and carries the object's own verbs too (rename, reorder, hide, delete). Camera moves and selection changes are not cards; they live in "Take me back" and in Recent. A "Show:" row at the top of the list picks Everything, Results, Layers, Filter steps, Sets, Notes or Views, so 50 tasks stay readable; Layers and Filter steps show in stacking order. The current selection sits as a fixed line at the top ("Selection: 7 families"), whose card holds its statistics and the selection's verbs. Open, a task card shows:

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, choose Enter VR with nothing loaded. On the home panel, pinch Start, then Open a sample, then "Florentine families".
    - **What you see:** An empty space, the home panel beside where the graph will be, and one hint under it: "Pinch an answer. Back is always here." Only Start and Settings and Help are bright; the other goals read "needs a graph". The sample question shows 8 samples, each with a one-line description ("16 Renaissance families and their marriages"), and More answers for the rest. Opening a sample when nothing is loaded changes nothing that could be lost, so its answer reads "runs now" and there is no Check card: 16 families appear at chest height, and the load card opens where the question was.
    - **Controls:** pinch on answers (three acts).

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Read the load card. Pinch Done. Drag empty space beside the graph with one hand to turn it a little (or grab it with both hands).
    - **What you see:** Every load ends with the graph's description, so there is nothing to ask for: "16 families, 20 marriages, undirected, 2 pieces (15 connected and Pucci alone), density 0.17, 2.5 marriages per family on average", the attribute list, and the load report ("nothing dropped"). The hint line reads "Drag the graph to turn it", the first time only. The bar under the card now reads "Working on: all 16 families". Done closes it to "Opened Florentine families" on the task list; Look around, Describe this graph shows it again at any time. The graph turns with your hand; it is the graph that moves, not you. The home panel offers "Suggested next: Who is most important?, What groups are there?".
    - **Controls:** pinch on an answer, one-hand drag or two-hand grab.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Find something, then Find by name. The question "Which name?" offers letter groups and the names as answers; pinch "MNO", then "Medici" (or hold the microphone and say "Medici").
    - **What you see:** After "MNO" the answers narrow to Medici alone, with "6 marriages". Picking it frames Medici: the graph glides (or cuts, with reduced motion) until Medici sits at the center at a comfortable size, highlighted and selected. With Medici in view you could also simply have pinched it in the graph.
    - **Controls:** pinch on answers (four acts), optional voice.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Medici in the graph. The home panel opens as "What do you want to do with Medici?". Pinch Show who it is connected to.
    - **What you see:** The panel's header reads "Medici -- 6 marriages, 1 of 15 in the main piece". The task's only input, "How many steps out?", has a default of 1, so it is not asked: the task runs at once. Medici's six in-laws (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni) light up and become the selection, the camera frames the seven, the bar still reads "Working on: all 16 families" (a selection never changes it), and the task card reads "7 families, 7 marriages among them; steps out: 1 (default) -- Change". Pinching Change would show the stepper with "1 step: 6 families, 2 steps: 11 families, 3 steps: 14 families".

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Home, then Measure, then "Who is most important?", then "Tied to important families (PageRank)". On the Check card, pinch Run.

No question asks which families: the working set answers it, and the Check card's first line says so.     - **What you do:** Pinch Medici, then Write a note. The question is "What should it say?". Pinch the suggested sentence "Medici: highest PageRank, 0.145; 6 marriages", then add "the hub of the network" on the on-card keyboard (its next-word row offers "the", "hub" after a letter or two).     - **What you see:** The note's target is Medici, already answered by the pinch, so text is the only question. 10. **Take something back.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Medici, then Hide in the panel's last row, to see how the other four connect without it. Turn the graph with one hand to look. They fall apart, which is not what you wanted to show. Pinch Undo on the bar, which reads "Undo: Hide Medici".

    - **Controls:** bar Undo; task card Change an answer or Undo this task for older mistakes.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Home, then Share, then Save. The question "Save it as?" offers suggested names; pinch "Florentine families -- PageRank".
    - **What you see:** The suggestions are built from the project's tasks ("Florentine families", "Florentine families -- PageRank", "Medici and in-laws") with Type a name, Say it and Type it on my laptop below.     - **Controls:** pinch on answers (four acts).

The journey takes about 37 acts, all pinches but one stepper drag and two drags to turn the graph, plus about 8 key pinches for the note's added words where there is no recognizer. No step asks which families to work on: the working set answers it in steps 5, 7 and 8, and each Check card says so on its first line. Most are on answers at least 6 cm tall, so none needs precise aim except the two pinches on Medici in the graph, and both of those have a by-name answer as an alternative.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Filter, then Keep only some, then "Those that match a rule". The sub-interview asks: Which value? (type), Compared how? (is), Against what? (account), then "Anything else?" -- And also. Which value? (degree), Compared how? (above), Against what? (stepper, 20). "Anything else?" -- Or else; the card asks "Or else, instead of which part?" with the clauses so far as answers, and you pick "degree above 20" so the OR groups with it. Which value? (risk_score), at least, 0.8. And also: created, within the last, 30 days. That's all. The card reads back: "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created within the last 30 days", indented by group, each clause with Change. A week later, Change an answer on that filter card reopens the rule sentence; pinch the clause to change and only its three questions are asked. NOT is "Except" in the same list.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** "Ask me everything" (a toggle on the home panel and on each task's first card) asks every option in the descriptor's order. Otherwise options are reached from the Check card's Change links; advanced options sit under "Show 4 more settings". Katz's tolerance of 1e-6 is the decade row (one pinch); its attenuation is the stepper with labeled detents; the weight is the attribute list. A resolution sweep for communities: on the resolution card, pinch "Try several", pick 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0; Run makes four task cards side by side and a comparison card ("4, 4, 3, 2 groups"). ForceAtlas2's 13 options are the scheme's worst case: 13 cards with "Ask me everything", which is why layouts open on the Check card by default.
- **Comparing two runs.** Change an answer with Keep both offers "Compare the two" at once. Otherwise Compare, Two runs, "Which first?" (the task cards light up as answers; pinch one on the list or pick by name), "Which second?". The comparison card shows rows of families with both values and rank moves ("3 families change rank; Albizzi drops 2"), and offers Color by the difference.
- **Layers on subsets.** Make it readable, "Style some nodes directly" or "Color, size or label by a value"; the "Which nodes?" question takes the current selection, a saved set, a group from a result or a rule. "Change what?" lists the style properties generated from the element's style schema (8 common ones, More answers for the 40+). The order of layers is its own task, Arrange style layers: the whole stack is shown on the card above the question, top to bottom, so the order is always in view; "Move which layer?" (pinch a row) then "Put it where?" with answers like "Up one", "On top", "Just above PageRank color", each previewing the result while you hold the pinch. Filter steps are arranged the same way.
- **A run and its suggested style.** An algorithm whose descriptor suggests a style (communities color by group) lists "Color by the groups: yes" under "Settings we chose for you". Running it records two steps, the run and then its style layer, so one Undo on the bar ("Undo: Color by groups") removes the color and keeps the result; the layer is its own card on the list.
- **Recipes.** On the task list, "Save tasks as a recipe": "Which tasks?" (pinch cards to tick them, Done), then a name. Replaying is Start or Home, Use a recipe, pick it: the interview asks the inputs that depend on the data ("Which value is the weight here?", "Which node is the start?") and, again, every answer that belongs to the recipe's own dataset -- every text answer (a note, a callout, a filter's name), every named set, saved view or node id, and every hand-pointed list -- each pre-filled and marked "from the recipe's dataset: 'ACC-48213 coordinates the ring'", with Keep it, Change it and Leave this task out. Answers stored by what produced them (a rule, "the largest group", "top 10 by PageRank") replay without asking. Everything takes the working set on the bar, and Check your answers lists all of it before Run. Each replayed task lands on the list as its own card, so any one answer can be changed afterward.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor's category places it under More analyses by family and, when the category matches one, under a purpose answer in Measure. Its plain name is the answer label, its technical name the small type, so search finds both. Its options become questions in declared order, each rendered by the answer card for its kind; defaults are skipped and shown on Check; advanced options fold under "Show more settings". Its result fields choose the reader on its task card. "Set the third option of last week's plugin" is: open its card, Change on the third line.
- **A new layout.** Same path under Arrange the nodes, with its engines as a question that is skipped unless asked. An iterative layout gets Stop and Reshuffle on its card because the descriptor says it is live.
- **A new object type.** Its verbs are tasks (this needs graphty-element's descriptors to list an object kind's verbs, which they do not yet), so pinching the object (if it is drawn) opens the home panel filtered to them, and "Which (kind)?" questions about it use the generic point-at-one card with a by-name list. If it is not drawn in the graph (a time slice, a group of groups), the list is the only way to pick it.
- **A third-party plugin.** A data reader adds a format and its option questions to Start; a format writer adds a line to Export; a palette adds a swatch. The hardest case -- a "Biology: enrichment" family of 4 algorithms, one taking a list of gene identifiers and two numbers -- lands as a family under More analyses with no change to the scheme. If its descriptor declares the gene list as nodes, the list is the gather many card, whose "A list of names" answer takes a pasted, dictated or typed list and reports how many names match no node; if it declares a kind the scheme does not know, the text card asks for it with the descriptor's label, and a dedicated card can come later. The two numbers are steppers; the result fields choose the reader.
- **The practical limit.** The scheme is as good as the descriptors' labels and order: a plugin with bad labels asks bad questions. It is slow wherever many inputs interact and the user wants to see them together while tuning (ForceAtlas2's 13 options, a 40-column mapping), wherever the input is drawn (a pattern template, a free-form outline, a custom camera path) and wherever the input is long text. Those fall back to the Check card with every Change link at once, to "Try several", or to the laptop. Repeated work is slower than direct manipulation: PageRank from home is five acts against the desktop's three, which Recent tasks, the "with recommended settings" answer and Duplicate bring back to two.

### Why it should work

- One thing per page: Adam Silver's GOV.UK work (2017) and the GOV.UK Design System's question pages pattern found that one question per page helps people understand what is being asked, recover from errors and use small screens; the check answers pattern (design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/check-answers/) lets people review and change answers before submitting, and is used across UK government services with millions of users. A headset has the same constraints as a phone: large text, little room, imprecise input.
- Typeform (2012) showed one question at a time is accepted for long forms and even preferred for its focus; its conversational forms are used by millions of people.
- Microsoft's Inductive User Interface guidelines (2001, from Microsoft Money 2000) made "one task per page, with the task stated as a question or title" the basis of an interface for infrequent users. Microsoft's wizard guidelines (learn.microsoft.com/windows/win32/uxguide/win-wizards) say wizards fit infrequent, multi-step tasks and recommend skipping pages that are not needed and a summary before committing -- exactly what skipping defaults and Check your answers do.
- Slot filling in spoken dialog systems: the W3C VoiceXML 2.0 form interpretation algorithm (2004) visits only the unfilled fields of a form in order, and mixed-initiative dialogs let the user fill several at once. The AI assistant here fills slots and the interview asks only for what is left, the same algorithm.
- Text in headsets is read slower and must be larger (Dingler, Kunze and Outram, "VR Reading UIs", CHI Extended Abstracts 2018); lookups in headsets measured 1.5 to 2 times slower than on a desktop (the parity matrix in design/xr/vr-control-surfaces.md). A scheme that shows one short question and up to 8 large answers keeps reading to a minimum.
- Large targets beat precise pointing in headsets: Apple's visionOS Human Interface Guidelines ask for interactive targets of at least 60 points, and pointing at small targets in dense 3D scenes is slow (12 to 21 seconds per node in the studies cited in the checklist). Here almost every pinch is on a big answer; graph pinches always have a by-name alternative.
- Editing a past step's parameters and re-running is shipped and loved in parametric CAD (the SolidWorks feature tree, Fusion 360's timeline) and in Grasshopper; Berlage's selective undo (1994) studied undoing one old action without the later ones.
- This repository's designloom first-time onboarding workflow asks for a first analysis within 5 minutes with the tour teaching the controls; here the controls are one gesture, and the tour is the interview itself.

### What it would teach us

- How many acts do the six stress journeys take against the desktop, and where does the interview's extra act count stop being worth its error rate?
- Do people trust skipped defaults, or do they open every Check card's "Settings we chose for you"? Does "Ask me everything" get used, and by whom?
- Is "Change an answer and re-run" understood as editing a past task, and do people prefer it to undo for older mistakes?
- Do people notice the working set on the bar when it changes, or do they still run a task on the 5 kept an hour after filtering without meaning to? Count runs whose scope the person corrects on the Check card.
- Can descriptors as they ship today (labels, descriptions, order, defaults) produce questions people understand, or does every algorithm need a hand-written question? This tells us what the element's descriptor format is missing.
- Does the plain-words purpose split ("Who is most important?") help people pick the right algorithm, or do experts route around it with search every time?
- Does framing the graph on every node question help orientation, or does the moving graph disorient and cause discomfort?
- Is one place to look (the card beside the graph) less tiring than panels and handles around the graph, and is it comfortable for an hour?
- Does the task list stay readable at 50 tasks, or does it need grouping by goal or by object?

### Known risks

- **Wizard fatigue for experts.** Interviews are slow for someone who knows what they want. Mitigation: Recent tasks with "same answers", the full-size "with recommended settings" answer, Duplicate on any card, search on the home panel, the working set (which removes the scope question from almost every task), and the Check card's Change links for jumping straight to one option. Every one of these shortcuts states the working set it runs on, so speed never hides scope.
- **The working set is a mode.** One global state changes what every task works on, which is the classic recipe for a mode error: a person who filtered an hour ago and forgot runs a ranking on the 5 kept. Mitigation: it is always on the bar under the card, it changes only on a filter step or time window (never on a pinch), the bar flashes and names the step that changed it, and every "runs now" answer and Check card repeats it in words. This is the design's chosen trade: one visible state instead of a question asked on every task.
- **Hidden defaults.** A skipped question can hide a choice that matters (direction, weight, hide versus filter). Mitigation: every skipped default is listed on Check; a default that changes the result more than the descriptor's threshold, or that the data makes doubtful (a weight column exists but is not used), is asked instead of skipped.
- **Losing your place.** A long interview or a sub-interview for a rule can feel like a maze. Mitigation: progress dots, Back that never loses answers, the rule read back as a sentence after each clause, and Home keeping the interview as a draft.
- **The card competes with the graph.** Looking between the card and the graph costs head turns. Mitigation: the card sits beside the graph at a fixed angle, results open on the card rather than on the task list across the graph, answers preview on the graph while pinched, and node questions bring the candidates to the center.
- **Moving the graph for every node question.** Repeated framing can disorient or cause discomfort. Mitigation: short eased moves, a cut with reduced motion, framing only when the candidates are off view, and "Take me back".
- **Pinch on a node means two things.** It answers an open node question, or opens the object's verbs. Mitigation: while a node question is open the card says "Pinch a node to answer" and the graph shows candidates highlighted; a pinch on a non-candidate asks "Use Ridolfi anyway?"; while a question for something else is open, the pinch offers "Set it aside and work on Medici?" rather than doing nothing.
- **Not knowing when a task will run.** Some answers run the task, others lead to more questions. Mitigation: answers that run carry "runs now", anything that removes or merges data always ends on Check, and every immediate run is a named, undoable step.
- **Accidental drags and slips.** A pinch on a node could move it, and a slip could pick the wrong answer. Mitigation: a drag starts only after about 2 cm of hand travel; choosing happens on release, so sliding off cancels; verbs that hide, remove or merge sit in their own row.
- **Many interacting options.** 13 layout options asked one at a time hide how they interact. Mitigation: layouts open on the Check card, Change previews live on the graph, and "Try several" compares values; heavy tuning is a place this scheme admits is slower.
- **Compensating undo with dependents.** Undoing an old run breaks later layers and filters built on it. Mitigation: Undo this task lists its dependents first and offers to undo them too or leave them with a "missing source" warning.
- **Text entry.** Names, ids, notes and reports. Mitigation: suggested names for everything nameable, the letter strip for searches, dictation where a recognizer exists, the on-card keyboard, and Type it on my laptop for anything longer than a sentence.
- **Laptop handoff does not exist yet.** File open, long text and send-to-laptop rely on the 2D page and the headset sharing a session. The pairing needs a relay between the two pages, which graphty, served as static pages, does not have. Mitigation on day one: files in through "Leave VR to pick the file" (the session ends, the headset browser's 2D page shows its picker, re-entry restores the project and the open interview) or a URL; files out through "Save on this headset" (the headset browser's downloads, fetched afterwards some other way). This cannot be fixed inside a control design: no answer card can move a file to a laptop without a channel between the two pages, so stress journeys 3 and 6 begin or end outside the headset until the relay is built. The pairing channel is needed by every prototype and should be built once.
- **Poor descriptor labels.** A plugin with technical labels makes technical questions. Mitigation: the descriptor's plain name and description are shown; a lint in the extension docs asks plugin authors for a question-form label, with the plain name as the fallback.
