**Prototype 31: Lens Kit** -- Every operation is first tried inside a movable lens (filter, color, algorithm, layout, names, table, time, removal, camera) as a local preview over part of the graph, then spread to commit it as a step, layer or run; lenses joined rim to rim compose.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away, with a thin ring around its base about 50 cm across. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Primary input is tracked hands (pinch, fingertip, wrist pose). At 75 cm the graph is at or past full arm reach, so every rim, notch, dial and chip also works at a distance by 24's hand ray with its snap cone, from a hand resting at the waist; direct pinch is used only when a lens is within easy reach. Controllers work on Quest and Galaxy XR, with the ray and trigger standing in for pinch. Eyes, where exposed, are used only as 24 uses them (look-and-pinch). Text goes into fields through 24's in-scene keyboard (a digit row and a digit pad for numbers), a paired keyboard where the browser passes keys, dictation only where the browser has a recognizer, or the laptop. Seated or standing; everything works with one hand, because lenses park in the air and every two-hand gesture has a one-hand equivalent.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on, standing or seated.
    - **What you see:** 16 families floating at chest height, Pucci a little apart from the rest with no ties. A thin ring at the graph's base carries a plate: "Florentine families: 16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces". No lenses yet. A one-line hint above the ring: "Check your left wrist for lenses."
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Turn your off wrist as if checking a watch. Pinch the Names lens (first in Recents on a first session), pull it off and sweep it slowly through the graph. Two-hand grab empty space and turn the graph a quarter turn.
    - **What you see:** The rack shows Recents and its shelf tabs along your forearm. The Names lens is a 15 cm sphere inside a 20 cm dashed rim; every family inside it is named, and its readout says "Names: 5 inside". As you sweep it, names appear and disappear with it. The graph turns under your two hands and settles.
    - **Controls:** wrist turn (rack), pinch and pull off the rack, held lens, two-hand grab.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Sweep the Names lens toward the densest knot until "Medici" appears inside it, bring the reticle onto that node, and release: the lens parks there in the air, its reticle on Medici. Pinch the reticle. (In a graph too big to sweep, turn the off palm up and poke Find, type or say "Medici", and choose Go: a magnifier lens flies to the match and parks over it.)
    - **What you see:** Medici sits under the reticle with a ring around it. Pinching the reticle opens Medici's popover beside the lens: "Medici, 6 ties" and its verbs (Inspect, Neighbors, Route from here, Note, Color this, Hide, More).
    - **Controls:** held lens, release to park, pinch the reticle.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** In Medici's popover choose Neighbors: a Bring-neighbors lens arrives already attached to Medici. (The other order works too: pull one off the Move around drawer and release it once its reticle snaps to Medici.) Drag its dial one detent. Tap its rim. Then press More (9 o'clock) and choose Keep as set.
    - **What you see:** Inside the lens, Medici's neighbors slide in toward it on stretched edges while their real positions stay as faint ghosts: Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni. The readout: "1 hop: 6 families, 7 marriages among them"; under it, one value chip per edge type (here only "marriage"). One more detent would read "2 hops: 5 more families"; you stay at 1. The rim tap outlines the 7 families as the selection; Keep as set offers the name "Medici and 6 neighbors" and a Sets chip appears on the base ring.
    - **Controls:** popover verb (or rack and attach), dial, rim tap (select), More notch.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pull the Analyze tab's drawer, pinch PageRank in the Centrality column and pull it off (or type "page" in the rack's search). Press More > Cover graph (or pinch its rim with both hands and pull apart). Read the preview, then pinch the 12 o'clock notch and pull it outward past its detent; hold, read, release.
    - **What you see:** Inside the lens, nodes grow by PageRank and show their values; Medici is clearly largest. The readout: "PageRank, damping 0.85, computed on the whole graph, inside: 16 families". Held past the detent, the Spread label reads "Spread: keep PageRank run (16 families)". On release, a "PageRank" chip lands on the Runs arc of the base ring and the lens goes back to Recents. The nodes return to their old look: a run is a result, not a look.
    - **Controls:** rack drawer and family column, Cover graph, Spread notch.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch the PageRank chip and choose Read. A Ranking lens hangs beside the graph.
    - **What you see:** The Ranking lens lists every family in order: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069, Castellani 0.069, Bischeri 0.068, Peruzzi 0.067, Salviati 0.061, Barbadori 0.050, Pazzi 0.036, Ginori 0.032, Lamberteschi 0.031, Acciaiuoli 0.030, Pucci 0.010. Moving it over part of the graph narrows the list to the families inside. Pinching a row puts that family under the reticle of the nearest lens.
    - **Controls:** chip popover, Ranking lens, pinch a row.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** From the Look drawer, pull a Color lens and park it; pull a Size lens and drop its rim on the Color lens's rim: they click together. Both arrive set to the newest result field, PageRank. Turn the Color band's dial two detents to a sequential palette, and the Size band's dial until the largest family is about three times the smallest. Pull the composed lens's Spread notch outward, hold, release.
    - **What you see:** The composed rim shows two bands, "Color: PageRank, sequential blue" and "Size: PageRank, 1x to 3x". Inside it, the families are colored and sized; outside, nothing has changed until you hold the Spread notch past its detent, when the whole graph shows the look. The Spread label: "Spread 2 lenses: 2 style layers on 16 families". On release the look stays, a legend appears on the base ring, and two chips land on its Layers arc, recorded as one step "Spread color and size by PageRank".
    - **Controls:** rim-to-rim join (compose), dial, Spread notch.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** From the Filter drawer, pull a Range lens; it arrives on PageRank. More > Cover graph. Turn its dial down until the readout says 0.070. Spread it.
    - **What you see:** Inside the lens, families under the threshold fade as the dial turns. The readout: "PageRank at least 0.070 -- inside: fades 11 of 16; spread: keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages". On release, only Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain; a filter chip "PageRank at least 0.070" lands on the Filters arc, and the plate reads "Showing 5 of 16".
    - **Controls:** rack, Cover graph, dial, Spread notch.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch Medici's reticle and choose Note (or pull a Note lens from the Read drawer and release it once its reticle snaps to Medici). A note card unfolds from its rim with a text field and 24's in-scene keyboard under it. Type "Married into six families; the hub of the network", or say it where the browser has a recognizer. Pull the Spread notch, which here reads "Pin note to Medici".
    - **What you see:** The note card shows your text. After the pin, Medici carries a small note marker, and the note is listed in 24's Notes tab.
    - **Controls:** popover verb (or rack and attach), keyboard or voice, Spread notch.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You mean to put away the Bring-neighbors lens still parked on Medici from step 4, but your pinch lands on its 12 o'clock Spread notch instead of 6 o'clock, and you pull and release in one quick motion, before reading the label "Spread: filter to within 1 hop of Medici" or the shrinking graph. Turn your off palm up and poke Undo, which is labeled "Undo: filter within 1 hop of Medici". (The same Undo chip sits on the base ring.)
    - **What you see:** After the mistake the graph showed only 3 of 16 (Medici, Albizzi, Tornabuoni). The undo puts the 5 strong families back; the plate reads "Showing 5 of 16" again. A Redo chip shows the undone step until your next change. Had the mistake been an older step, you could instead remove it from its chip's popover, or pull its chip off the Filters arc and put the lens away, without touching later steps.
    - **Controls:** hand menu Undo (or base ring Undo chip), with the step's name.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Turn the off palm up and poke Save.
    - **What you see:** 24's Save sheet offers the name "Florentine families"; confirm it. If any lens is still unspread, the sheet says so ("2 lenses not spread: Names, Bring-neighbors") and saves them as parked lenses with the project. The plate reads "Saved".
    - **Controls:** hand menu Save, confirm.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days." Pull three Rule lenses (type, degree, risk_score) and a Time lens. Join the degree and risk_score rims, press More and set the combine mode to OR: the composed rim shows "degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8" and the readout counts the union inside. Join that pair to the type lens and the time lens: joining combines as AND by default. Flip the composed lens: its back shows the rule as nested rows, generated from the attribute list, where any value can be typed on the digit pad or dialed. Spread it as one filter step. A week later, pull its chip off the Filters arc: it comes back as the same composed lens, suspended in the graph, so editing the 0.8 shows the change inside before spreading again.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Flip the PageRank lens: the back shows the generated form (damping 0.85 with a slider and a digit pad, maximum iterations, tolerance as an exponent stepper then a mantissa, weight attribute from the attribute list, direction, seed, compute on whole graph or only inside, Advanced fold, Reset). For a resolution sweep on Louvain, press More > Twin three times: four twin lenses show the same region side by side, and you dial 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 on them; each rim reads its group count and modularity. Spread one to keep one run, or More > Spread all twins to keep four runs.
- **Re-running and comparing runs (stress journey 1, steps 6 to 8).** Run Louvain from Suggested and spread it; pinch its chip and choose Read: a Groups lens lists 6 groups by size. Pinch the chip again and choose Re-run in a lens: a new Louvain lens arrives with the same options, the original chip stays solid. Dial resolution to 1.5 and spread: a "Louvain 2" chip joins it. To compare, open a Groups lens for each, press More > Compare with on one and pinch the other: rows join by node, nodes whose group changed are outlined inside and the rim reads "14 characters changed group". Spreading a compare lens opens the full difference table in 24's window. Because a run never changes the look, the "color change" to undo is the Paint step you spread from Louvain 2's chip; Undo names it.
- **Fraud ring from a flagged account (stress journey 2).** Find `ACC-48213` with 24's Find (digit row of the in-scene keyboard) and choose Neighbors in its popover: a Bring-neighbors lens arrives attached. Dial 2 hops; tap the value chips so only "shared device" and "shared phone" stay lit; the readout says "adds 37 accounts". Spread it to a filter step. Pull a Time lens, More > Cover graph, dial back to "last 30 days" and spread; the Time chip on the base ring carries step and play arrows, so "back a week" is one poke on its left arrow, with no lens. For the route, choose Route from here in the account's popover, then in Find type `ACC-10077` and choose Use as route end on the result: the tube forms. Dial k to 3; the Previous and Next value chips step through the three tubes and the readout lists each one's intermediate accounts. Run communities only on the expanded set: pull a Louvain lens and press More > Attach to > the Bring-neighbors lens's set. Keep the suspicious group from its Groups lens row (Keep as set, name "Ring 7" on the keyboard), drop a Note lens on the Ring 7 chip for the two-sentence note, Save filter from the filter chip's popover, and frame the picture with a Camera lens with legend on.
- **Layers on subsets.** Pull a Color lens and press More > Attach to > "Medici and 6 neighbors": the lens's scope becomes those 7 families wherever they sit, its rim floats at their center with leader lines, and its Spread label says "Spread: layer on 7 families" instead of the whole graph. Layer chips on the base ring reorder by dragging along the ring; a Layer lens held over a node peels layers from the top one at a time and names which layer painted it.
- **Recipes.** In 24's History tab, check the spread steps you want and choose Save as recipe. The recipe appears on the rack's Recipes drawer as one stacked lens. On new data, drop it over part of the graph to preview every step inside; flip it to map inputs (the recipe's "weight" to this graph's "trade_volume"); spread it to replay. Editing a recipe step by step happens in 24's History tab.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Import is window work: 24's Project window opens the file from the laptop inbox or a URL, shows the import sheet with a role menu on each column header, the first rows and the load report. To join a table, choose Add data in the Project window, pick the file and the key column; graphty then hands you a Join lens. Held over part of the graph it shows the new columns on matched nodes and hatches unmatched ones; the readout reads "inside: 38 of 41 match; spread: 412 of 450 nodes match by gene symbol". Spread commits the join; pulled back off its chip, you can change the key and see the match count change before committing again.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor places it in the Analyze drawer under its category (a new category becomes a new column) and in rack search by plain and technical name. The dial takes its first option marked main, else its first numeric option, else it chooses which result field to show. The back renders every option from the descriptor (ranges, logarithmic numbers, attributes, enumerations, toggles, node choices, Advanced). Inside the lens, each result field is painted by its kind: numbers as size and value labels, groups as colors with a group list on the readout, orders as rank numbers, edge fields as edge width. An algorithm with two node inputs gets a Route-style lens with two ends; one with a list input (gene identifiers) gets a field on its back that the keyboard, 24's Find and the laptop inbox can fill. The worked case: a plugin family "Biology: enrichment" with four algorithms becomes a new column in the Analyze drawer; the one that takes a gene list and two numbers shows the list field and two dials-or-pads on its back, and its group result reads in a Groups lens.
- **A new layout.** It becomes a Layout lens kind. The lens computes it on the nodes inside, with the boundary nodes pinned, so every layout gets "lay out only this part" for free; spread runs it on the lens's reach. Live layouts get Stop on More.
- **A new object type** (a pattern match, a time slice, a group of groups). It declares its verbs and gets a chip arc on the base ring with a popover; it can be a lens scope (Attach to it) and, if it carries a preview and a commit, it can register a new lens kind: a scope shape it accepts, what to draw inside, its toggleable values for the chips, and what spread creates. Its popover verbs that have a preview hand out their lens attached, like a node's. Notes attach to it like any target.
- **A third-party plugin** of readers, writers or palettes. A reader joins 24's open and format lists; a writer joins the Camera lens's and the export sheet's format lists with its options form; a palette joins the Color lens dial in its kind (sequential, categorical, diverging, highlight).
- **The practical limit.** Operations that have no local meaning: loading, project management, history, long text. Those stay in 24's windows, so this scheme is a lens layer over a window app, not a full replacement for it. Second, previews of costly algorithms on large graphs: the preview either waits with a cost estimate or computes on the inside only and says so ("rough: computed on 380 nodes inside"). Third, clutter: past about five parked lenses the graph becomes hard to read, so parked lenses beyond that shrink to tokens on a Parked arc of the base ring.

### Why it should work

- Bier, Stone, Pier, Buxton and DeRose, "Toolglass and magic lenses: the see-through interface" (SIGGRAPH 1993): a movable lens that changes the view only inside it, and lenses stacked to compose their effects, both shown working on the desktop.
- Viega, Conway, Williams and Pausch, "3D magic lenses" (UIST 1996): volumetric lenses in 3D and VR that apply a filter only inside a box or sphere, the same shape this scheme uses.
- Tominski, Gladisch, Kister, Dachselt and Schumann, "Interactive lenses for visualization: an extended survey" (Computer Graphics Forum 2017, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.12871): more than 50 lens techniques fit one model of selection, lens function and join, which is why lens kinds can be generated from descriptors rather than hand-made.
- Tominski, Abello and Schumann, fisheye tree views and lenses for graph visualization, with the Bring Neighbors and Local Edge lenses (IV 2006): graph-specific lenses that bring neighbors near and clear edges out of the way.
- Wong, Carpendale and Greenberg, "EdgeLens" (InfoVis 2003): bending edges inside a lens made it easier to tell which edges touch which node in dense graphs.
- Moscovich, Chevalier, Henry, Pietriga and Fekete, "Topology-aware navigation in large networks" (CHI 2009): Bring and Go and Link Sliding were faster than pan and zoom for following links.
- Kister, Reipschlager, Matulic and Dachselt, "BodyLenses" (ITS 2015): lenses moved by the body in front of a large display were understood quickly; here the off hand carries them.
- Stoev and Schmalstieg, through-the-lens techniques for VR (2002): looking at and manipulating a remote part of a scene through a window without moving yourself.
- Furnas, generalized fisheye views (CHI 1986), and Sarkar and Brown, graphical fisheye views (CHI 1992): detail in context keeps the overview while reading a part.
- Mota and colleagues on 3D lenses in VR (2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.11410): recent work bringing lens techniques into immersive analytics.
- Preview before commit is shipped and familiar: Microsoft Office's live preview (2007) shows a style on the document while the pointer rests on it; Photoshop's filter preview window and adjustment layers apply an effect locally and reversibly; Lightroom's before and after view.

### What it would teach us

- Do people keep a clear sense of what is a preview and what is committed, or do they believe a lens's look is the graph's? How often do they leave a lens unspread and later wonder why the saved project looks different?
- Is joining two rims an understandable way to compose, is the separate Compare with verb found when needed, and do people read the apply order from the rim's bands?
- Does the held Spread preview (the whole graph changing while the notch is held) stop wrong commits, or do people release too fast to see it?
- Can a sphere's interior be read inside a 3D graph, with nodes in front of it and behind it, or does the lens need to cut away what lies between it and you?
- Is the local preview of a global algorithm (computed on the whole graph, shown inside) trusted, and is "computed on only what is inside" confused with it?
- How many lenses can be parked before the graph becomes unreadable?
- Do people start from objects (popover verbs that hand out attached lenses) or from the rack, and does having both orders confuse them?
- Does the fixed four-notch rim become muscle memory fast enough that spreading feels like one act, near and by ray?
- How many acts does the basic journey take compared with the desktop app, given that most steps are a pull, a dial and a spread?

### Known risks

- **Preview mistaken for applied.** Mitigation: uncommitted lenses have a dashed rim and the word "preview" on the readout; the base ring plate counts unspread lenses; Save and Export warn by name and save them as parked lenses.
- **Wrong notch.** Spread and Put away are both on the rim. Mitigation: Spread needs a pull outward past a detent and shows its label and the whole-reach preview while held; Put away is a pinch on the opposite side; Spread of anything that hides more than half of what is shown or removes elements asks for a second pull; named Undo is one poke.
- **Accidental composition.** Parking one lens inside another (easy once one covers the graph) could merge them. Mitigation: only a rim dropped on a rim joins; overlapping interiors never compose, and each readout lists what else is previewing inside it.
- **3D occlusion.** Nodes in front of a lens hide its interior. Mitigation: the lens dims and thins whatever lies between it and your eyes; a magnifier draws its interior enlarged on its front face.
- **Clutter.** Many parked lenses cover the graph. Mitigation: more than five shrink to tokens on the Parked arc of the base ring; Recents keeps put-away lenses with their settings, so putting one away loses nothing.
- **Misleading local results.** A centrality shown on part of the graph can look different from the whole. Mitigation: the readout always says where it was computed; "computed on only what is inside" carries a caveat into the run's "how it was produced".
- **Costly previews.** Mitigation: the readout shows the cost before computing; large previews compute once and repaint as the lens moves; a "rough" preview on the interior when the full one would take more than a few seconds; previews are overlays, not restyles of the scene.
- **Arm fatigue and reach.** The graph at 75 cm is at full arm reach, and holding a lens up is tiring. Mitigation: every rim and chip works by hand ray from a resting hand; lenses park in the air and attach to nodes and sets; Cover graph replaces wide two-hand pulls; the graph can be pulled closer and lower when seated.
- **Two-hand conflicts.** Two-hand pinch on a rim resizes the lens; two-hand grab on empty space moves the graph; an off-hand pinch can also be the add modifier. Mitigation: a rim has a 2 cm catch zone that lights before the pinch; the modifier needs the off hand pinched and still in empty space for 0.3 s before the tap, while a grab needs both hands moving.
- **One-handed and seated use.** Mitigation: Cover graph and the Size slider replace two-hand resize; Add mode replaces the modifier; the rack can move to the base ring in Settings so the off arm never lifts.
- **Gestures near the system's.** The watch-check wrist pose and the palm-up hand menu are close to Quest's and Vision Pro's own palm gestures. Mitigation: the rack faces the back of the hand, never the palm; hand menu buttons are poked by the other hand, never pinched by the palm hand; both can move to the base ring.
- **Text entry.** Names, notes, formulas and search need text. Mitigation: suggested names for sets, filters and views; 24's in-scene keyboard with a digit row for ids; dictation where the browser has a recognizer; long text through the laptop in 24's windows.
- **Not every task is local.** Loading, history and project work cannot be a lens. Mitigation: be explicit that 24's windows are the base surface; the lens layer adds preview-then-commit on top, it does not try to replace them.
