**Prototype 32: Patch Bay** -- The analysis is a small patch of operator boxes wired on a tilted board below the graph -- sources, keeps, algorithms, layouts, style layers, comparisons, exports -- each drawn from its descriptor, so two-input operations, joins, comparisons, batches, sweeps and recipes are just wiring, and ordinary graph verbs build the patch for you. A work strip just under the graph is the one place that states what is in force -- the selection and what each verb will act on, what the graph shows, an armed port, the last result, named views, stale and changed boxes -- and it is where box faces, readers and the keyboard open; the board stays below as the record and the wiring bench. A person's own call -- "this group is the ring", "these two records are one person" -- is a box too: a Review box takes candidates, shows them one at a time with Yes, No and Skip, and its decisions are part of the record and replay as a review step on new data.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away, about 15 to 20 degrees below the eye line. Under it, about 10 cm below the graph's base ring and at the same distance, the **work strip**: a gently curved band about 60 cm wide (under 25 degrees to each side), 7 cm tall. For the Florentine graph (about 25 cm tall, centered about 15 degrees down) the strip sits about 30 degrees below the eye line: it is read in a glance, in short forms, not studied. The **face slot**, where box faces, readers and the keyboard open, is anchored by angle, not to the strip: beside the graph on the side of the dominant hand, about 20 degrees off center and 5 to 15 degrees below the eye line, at the graph's distance, so reading a result is a turn of the eyes, not a nod. Below and closer, the **board**, about 50 cm in front of the eyes and about 45 degrees below the eye line, tilted 35 degrees toward you, its near edge at about elbow height: 60 cm wide by default when seated (edges under 30 degrees to the side), 90 cm when standing, 35 cm deep. Its nearest 6 cm is a bare rail with no controls, so a resting or passing hand presses nothing. On entry a posture card starts on **Seated** and offers four postures, each with a one-line description and each a named preset of board height, distance, tilt and input (changeable in Settings): **Standing**; **Seated** (the default above); **Lap** ("arms resting, no desk": board raised to just under the work strip and worked by ray from a resting forearm -- for an armchair, a sofa or a wheelchair without a desk; the wrist rests on the armrest or thigh with the hand tilted up, not lying flat, so the pinch stays inside the headset's hand-tracking view -- a device check below); **Table** ("forearms on a desk": touch the desk with an index fingertip at three points and the board lies on it). The same card has two switches, **Steadier input** (hold tolerance and the steady knob, below) and **Ray only** (Ray mode). On Vision Pro without the hand-joints permission, Table is grayed with "needs hand access, granted at your next entry". Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands (pinch, fingertip press) and controllers where the device has them (ray and trigger, grip, thumbstick, haptic detents). A hand within about 12 cm of the board touches it with the index fingertip (palms, knuckles and forearms are ignored) and stays in touch until it is 18 cm away, so it does not flicker between modes; a fingertip dot shows in touch mode and a ray shows only when it is live. **One owner per hand:** a hand in touch mode has no ray, and the board consumes every select event that hand fires (on Quest and Galaxy XR a pinch fires select along the hand's ray wherever it points; here that select never reaches the graph or the strip). On Vision Pro, a transient pointer whose pinch begins with that hand within 18 cm of the board belongs to the board too. To pick in the graph from over the board, lift the hand past 18 cm; the ray then appears. In **Table** mode a resting fingertip does nothing: a hold is a lift and a touch kept still (tap, then hold), and a press is desk contact kept for 0.3 s while a ring fills, since a fingertip on a desk cannot press through. Farther away, a hand points with a ray (Quest, Galaxy XR) or with look and pinch (Vision Pro). Ray mode (a setting, and the input in Lap) works the board by ray from any distance. Eyes are used only where the platform already routes look-and-pinch. Voice is optional and only types into an open text field. Every input works with either hand; the library drawer opens at the hand that opened it (by a dropped wire, a pad or a verb).

### The control vocabulary

Every target answers three acts the same way: **tap** (pinch and release, or press through with a fingertip) does its main thing; **hold** (pinch, or fingertip contact, kept still for 0.5 s) opens its verb list, and a ring fills on the target while you hold; **drag** (pinch and move before 0.5 s) moves it or pulls something out of it. Drag is measured from the ray frozen at the moment the pinch closes, so the wrist turn of the pinch itself does not count, and it starts at 4 degrees by ray or 3 cm by hand. A **hold tolerance** setting widens this to 8 degrees and the hold time to 0.3 to 1.0 s, for tremor or an unsupported hand. Verb-list entries fire on release; a list closes when you pick, or pinch anywhere else, and that closing pinch does nothing else. A pinch on empty space or the base ring that pauses and then moves still turns the graph (empty space has no verb list); the Graph label sits on the front of the ring, off its grab area.

### The work strip

The strip is the one place that says what is in force. It sits just under the graph so the person reads it with a glance, and every chip on it answers tap (its main thing) and hold (its verb list) like everything else.

**Text size and short forms.** Strip text is never smaller than 1 degree of cap height (about 1.4 cm at 80 cm, about 0.8 cm per character), so the 60 cm strip holds about 70 characters including icons and gaps. Every chip therefore has a **short form** (an icon plus a few characters) that is always shown, and a **long form** that expands in place, above the strip, when the ray rests on the chip for 0.3 s (or on a tap, on Vision Pro, which has no hover). **Adjust the existing keep** opens the Keep box already fed by that output, so a second "Keep by this" never silently caps the first; picking Keep by this anyway adds a second keep and the Showing chip names both. **Inspect** on a node lists the layers that painted it and, under "Changed by", every box that changed or removed it (a Merge, a Keep, a Drop, a Review decision), each with Open box and Bypass -- so "the merge that touched Smith" is found from Smith, not by searching 200 boxes.

### The Review box (a person's call, recorded)

Some steps in an analysis are not computed: a person looks at Louvain's groups and decides one is a fraud ring, or looks at two records and decides they are one person. A **Review box** makes that call a stage of the patch, with the same grammar as every other box.

| Part                    | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Input                   | Any set of candidates: a groups output (one candidate per group), a ranking or values wire (one per node), a table's rows, or a table of candidate pairs (from link prediction, a key match, or a Merge box's candidates port). Reached by Review these... on a hull or groups row, Review... on any output port, or by wiring.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Face (in the face slot) | One candidate at a time, with the graph framed on it: for a node or group, its card (size, top members, the values that put it in the queue) and its hull lit; for a pair, both cards side by side with the merged preview between them, using the conflict rules of the Merge box it feeds. Three large pads: **Yes**, **No**, **Skip**, each recording the decision and moving to the next candidate in one act; **Back** returns to the previous one. A sort knob orders the queue by any values wire ("by similarity, highest first"). A progress line: "37 of 200 -- 21 yes, 14 no, 2 skipped". "Decide the rest..." sets every remaining candidate to No or Skip in one act. The face never closes by itself while a review is in progress. |
| Outputs                 | A decision table keyed by ids (yes, no or skip, and when), and a set wire of the Yes candidates' members. Pairs: the Yes pairs feed a Merge box's decisions port, so the conflict rules are set once, on that Merge, for every pair.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| Undo                    | Each decision is an undo step; consecutive decisions coalesce ("Undo: Review, 3 decisions"). Bypass on the box turns every decision off at once; Inspect on a node lists the decision that touched it ("Review: Smith and J. Smith -- Yes, Oct 3") with Open box and Bypass, so one bad decision is undone without finding its row.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| Picking from a result   | Keep as set on a hull or a groups row of a result inserts a one-decision Review box over that result (Yes on that group) before the kept set, with no extra act: "Ring 7" is then a decision over Louvain's groups, not a frozen list of last week's ids. A kept set made from the Selection or a Rule has no review and stays a plain set.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| On new data             | A review is a review step, never a frozen port. Its face opens on the new candidates. Where ids match, last time's decision is shown on the card ("last time: No") and "Keep last decisions where ids match" applies them all in one act; "No" decisions stay no, so a rejected pair does not come back after a refresh. A group from an unseeded run is matched by its members, not its number ("was Ring 7 last time: 82% of the same members").                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |

A Review box adds no input and no surface: it is a box whose face holds three pads, and its outputs are ordinary set and table wires.

### Recipes and folding

Two separate acts that the first version of this design merged into one:

- **Save as recipe...** (on any box's list) arms box ticking: tap each box to include (the boxes you tap need not form a chain; "from here to..." ticks a whole chain in one tap; "with everything it needs" ticks every box upstream of the ticked ones, so a branching patch packs in one tap), then Done. The ticked boxes are **copied** to My recipes with their knobs; the patch is unchanged. Before it saves, a **recipe sheet** in the face slot lists everything bound to this data and what it will become on new data, each with a switch:
    - **Input** (the default for anything upstream of every analysis: a start account, a target, a Find box's typed text, every relative date): becomes a port that asks for its counterpart ("a start account", "as of").
    - **Finding** (the default for anything downstream of an analysis: a kept set that came from a Review, a node or hull token picked from a result, the Callout and Note on them): becomes a review step over the output it was picked from. A Callout or Note follows its subject's review ("which node is the lead of this ring?").
    - **Leave out.**

    The sheet ends with one line per kind: "Ring triage asks for: a start account, a target account, a date anchor. You will review: Louvain's groups (pick rings), each ring's lead node." So a recipe of an investigation replays as compute, then review, then render -- it never asks for the answer it was built to find.

- **Fold...** ticks boxes the same way and shows them as one tile on the board, to keep it readable. Folding is a view: Open inside shows the boxes in place with every knob reachable, Unfold puts them back, and neither touches results.

A recipe pulled from My recipes offers **Run on new data in a new project** first (it asks for the data from the Inbox, a sample, a file or Recent, and leaves this project untouched) and **Run here** second. Either way it arrives as a folded tile wired to its Source; Open inside and Unfold work on it the same way. An entry from **My filters** inserts on the Viewer's graph chain as a Keep tile, so applying a saved filter always changes what is shown, and the Showing chip names it; a filter's name step has a description field.

### Saves, dates and replay

- **What a save holds**, everywhere this file says "save": the data, the patch (every box with its knobs and wires), every box's output (cheap boxes too), the **run log** (every run, replaced or not, with its options, input size, seed and time), every Review decision, the notes, the views, and the date of the save.
- **The Methods page** (the log reader on the pad strip, and Open box on any result) lists every run in order; runs that a Re-run replaced are listed dimmed ("Louvain resolution 0.8, 1.0 -- replaced; 1.2 -- in use"), and Review steps are listed with their counts ("Review of Louvain groups: 1 yes, 11 no"). Add to report has an "include tried runs" switch, on by default for a report.
- **Reopening** shows exactly what was saved; nothing recomputes until you change an input. Each box's meter reads "as saved".
- **Relative dates.** A box with a relative date (a Window set to "last 30 days", a Rule "created in the last 30 days") always shows its resolved dates ("last 30 days = Sep 3 to Oct 2") and has an "as of" switch: the save, today, the data's latest date, or a date. A new box starts "as of today"; a reopened version starts "as of the save". Flipping it is an undo step, and the Status chip then reads "differs from saved: Window (dates moved)".
- **Kept sets** are frozen by default (a named list of members); a "rule" switch makes one recompute. A set kept from a result comes out of a Review box, so in a recipe it becomes a review step, not a frozen list.
- **Seeds.** graphty-element's default stays unseeded; a box with a seed option records the seed each run used, and a re-run from a saved version offers "same seed as saved" first.
- **Replay on new data.** A recipe with a relative date asks for its anchor, offering "the data's latest date" first. Before Run, each folded stage shows its count; a stage that keeps 0 turns amber and names its box and the cause it can see ("Window keeps 0 of 9,400: no accounts in Sep 3 to Oct 2"; "Keep (Ring 7) keeps 0: this set was a fixed list from another dataset -- turn it into a review"). Review steps wait for the person and show "review: 12 groups waiting" on the Status chip.
- **Two networks in one project.** A second Source (from Add another network) has Remove on its list, and every writer's scope names its Source ("whole data: Case 2026-114 accounts, 1,812" / "what is shown: 43"), so an export or a save never mixes datasets without saying so.

### Frame budget

At 90 Hz a frame has 11 ms. A cost estimate for a style or filter change decides how a knob behaves: under 4 ms, the graph restyles at every detent; above it, the meter and the Showing chip count live as the knob turns (counting is cheap) and the graph restyles after the turn settles. graphty-element today costs only algorithm runs, so until it estimates style and filter changes (graphty-element work that every consumer needs), the rule is by size: live restyle under 2,000 visible nodes plus edges, settle above. A restyle above the threshold is never one frame's work: it is applied through per-instance color and scale buffers where the element has them, and otherwise time-sliced at about 2,000 nodes a frame, nearest the head direction first, so the world never hitches; the meter shows "repainting" until it is done. The probe crossfades only under the same threshold and otherwise swaps by the same time-sliced apply. Ray hover picks against a spatial index, at most once per hand per frame. Autosave writes only changed outputs, from a worker. The overview is a cached low-detail image redrawn on change, shown only on request. A second full Viewer is drawn at reduced detail and can pause; it needs graphty-element to render a second view of a graph in the same immersive scene, which it cannot do today (see Known risks). Box faces are textures redrawn at most once a frame.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days". Hold the Graph label, Keep..., Rule. A Rule box has an attribute port (pick from the port's list; picking "degree" adds the cheap Degree box it needs); for a text or category attribute its knob becomes a tick list of the values ("account", "merchant"); for numbers, a comparison switch and a value knob; for a date, day detents and a "last N days" switch that shows its resolved dates and its "as of" switch. "Add clause AND" and "Add clause OR" on a Rule's list insert the next Rule box already wired into an AND or OR box with it, so no clause needs a hand-drawn wire: about 6 acts per clause, 25 for this rule. The AND box's output set can feed the Selection box, a Keep box and a layer's "paints" port at once. A week later, turn the risk_score Rule's knob: all three recompute, and the meters and the Showing chip say how many each one now passes. The nesting is the wiring, so it is visible without reading a formula. Building it is still slower than typing it on the desktop; editing it later is faster.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Katz centrality's 7 options: the face holds attenuation, tolerance (a log knob, one click per decade; Type a value for 1.5e-6), normalized and direction; the weight attribute is a port; More shows bank 1 with the advanced options. ForceAtlas2's 13 options are 4 on the face plus two banks (8 and 1). Change damping on a PageRank box that has run and tap Run as copy: both boxes now exist, both with their own outputs. Reset to defaults is on each bank and on the box's verb list.
- **Weighted score.** Wire four score ports into a Formula box set to its "weighted sum" form: each input gets a weight knob on the face, so the ranking reshuffles live as a weight turns, and any weight can take a Sweep. The input mark warns if one score was computed on a kept graph.
- **Comparing two runs.** Wire both PageRank "score" ports into a Compare box: its table lists each family's rank in both runs and marks movers ("6 families change rank: Salviati 10th to 5th, Ridolfi 6th to 10th, Albizzi passes Strozzi; Medici stays first, 0.145 to 0.116"). Its crossfade knob, wired into the Color box's values port, blends the colors between the two runs on the graph. For Louvain at four resolutions, a Sweep box (from 0.5 to 2.0, step 0.5) wired into the resolution knob fans out four runs; a Compare box on their groups ports reads group counts and modularity side by side.
- **Two conditions.** Hold the Graph label, Add another network: a second Source box. Two Source boxes (control and disease) feed a Merge Networks box (union, gene id key ports, a suffix switch that keeps logFC_control and logFC_disease side by side). A Degree box on each Source feeds a Formula box ("difference"), whose values, keyed by gene id, feed a Top N box (20, by absolute value) into a Keep. The Merge box's membership output feeds an edge Color box for "only in disease, only in control, both". A second Viewer floats beside the first, close enough to compare with a short glance; both take their positions from one Layout box on the merged graph, and each takes its own Source's graph. Both degree outputs wired into each Viewer's card fields port mean tapping a rewired gene in either view shows its degree in both; a "link selection" switch lights it in the other view. Swap moves the second view into the first's place. (Two Viewers need a second graph render in one scene; see Known risks. Today's path is a **Split** switch on the one Viewer: it draws the merged graph twice, side by side, as one graph -- each copy keeps only one condition's membership, both take positions from the same Layout box, and a tap on a gene lights it in both copies with both degrees on its card. It needs no second render; it costs twice the drawn nodes, so above the frame budget it asks to keep the Top N first.)
- **Layers on subsets.** Wire the Neighbors output (the Medici in-laws) into a new Color box's "paints" port and pick a fixed color from its swatch grid: it paints only those seven. Layer order is the order along the style chain; drag a box along the chain to move it above or below another, and the graph repaints at each slot. Keep boxes reorder along the graph chain the same way. "Why this look" on a node (Inspect) lists the layers that painted it and lights those boxes on the board.
- **Recipes.** Hold the PageRank box, Save as recipe..., "from here to..." and tap the Keep box, Done. The recipe sheet reads "asks for: nothing beyond the graph"; name it "Who matters" (default offered). The patch stays as it was. On another dataset, pull it from My recipes, Run on new data in a new project (or Run here): it arrives as a folded tile wired to the new Source; fill the ports it asks for, check each stage's count, and Run. Open inside shows every knob. The transport plays it stage by stage, so a colleague can watch how a result was built.
- **Joining tables / importing data.** On the laptop, "Send to headset" puts a CSV in the Inbox source box. Its file wire goes into a Table box. Wire the Table into a Join box with the graph; tapping the table-key port lists the 25 columns, each with its match count against node names ("gene_symbol: 1,412 matches"), best first, so the key is one tap. The Join box's face reads "1,412 of 1,530 rows matched; 118 unmatched", and the unmatched rows come out of their own port for a Table reader. For an edge-list CSV, a Build Graph box has id, source and target ports and arrives pre-wired with its best guess. Build Graph and every Table box fed by a file have a Columns bank with one switch per column (type: text, number, date, category; or skip), so a logFC column read as text is fixed before it is joined.
- **Resolve duplicates (knowledge graph, 40,000 entities from two systems).** Two edge-list CSVs come in through the Inbox into Build Graph boxes (Columns banks fix three types). ### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor gives the box: plain name as title, family as the library section, inputs as ports (graph, scope, plus any node or attribute options), the first 4 non-advanced options as face knobs (numbers to knobs with the range and step, logarithmic numbers to log knobs, integers to stepped knobs, enumerations to rotary switches or, past 8 entries, pick lists, booleans to switches, colors to swatch grids, seeds to a knob with a dice pad), the rest in banks of 8, and each result field as a typed output port whose reader is chosen by kind (past 4 fields, a More outputs bank). The cost estimate and preconditions fill the Run pad. "Set the third option of an algorithm a plugin added last week" is: pull the box, turn the third knob (or the third slot of bank 1).
- **A new layout.** A Layout box with the same generation; a static layout gets a Run pad, an iterative one gets Run and Stop and plays live; "only on" and focus ports come from its descriptor.
- **A new object type.** If it flows (a time slice, a path set, a motif match), it becomes either an existing wire type or a new wire type that brings a reader and a short verb list; it gets a generic plug with a text label unless graphty's team draws it a shape; boxes that accept it declare it on their ports, and the library filter starts working for it at once. If it is something you point at in the graph, it gets a verb list, whose entries are boxes.
- **A third-party plugin.** "Biology: enrichment" with 4 algorithms becomes a library section. The algorithm that takes a list of gene identifiers gets a set port (wire the Selection box, a cluster's set, or a Find box; labels are sent as identifiers) and two number knobs on its face; its table result opens in a Table reader. Nothing on the board is hand-built for it.
- **The practical limit.** Three things do not fit the box model and are handled outside it: direct manipulation in the graph (moving and pinning nodes, drawing a region or a callout's outline), project housekeeping (open, close, settings), and long text. The stress journeys build patches of 10 to 16 boxes (two conditions is the largest); a board past about 30 boxes gets hard to read, and folding keeps it near 12 visible tiles without touching any result. Repeated judgments never become one box each: 200 pair decisions are one Review box and one Merge box. Options of a kind the box cannot render (free text, a file) become ports fed from the Inbox or a text field.

### Why it should work

- Patching is a 35-year-old shipped practice for non-programmers: Max (Puckette, "The Patcher", ICMC 1988) and Pure Data (Puckette, 1996) are used by musicians and artists to build live systems by wiring boxes, with live recompute and per-box parameters.
- Visual dataflow is an established way to do data analysis without code: KNIME (released 2006; Berthold et al., 2008) and Orange (Demsar et al., JMLR 2013), whose typed signals between widgets match the wire types here, are used heavily in biology and teaching.
- Graph analysis already has this shape: Gephi's filter queries (Bastian, Heymann and Jacomy, ICWSM 2009) compose filters and sub-filters as a tree you drag and drop, and its users build them without training.
- Provenance and parameter exploration: VisTrails (Callahan et al., SIGMOD 2006) showed that keeping the pipeline as the record makes results reproducible, versionable and sweepable, which is the patch-as-record, versions and Sweep box here.
- Typed search from a dragged wire is shipped: Blender added link-drag search to its node editors in 3.1 (2022), and Houdini's tab menu filters by context, so a long catalog is cut to what can connect before you read it.
- Dataflow authoring in VR has been built and studied: FlowMatic (Zhang and Oney, UIST 2020) let people author reactive programs by wiring in a headset; PatchWorld (patchxr.com) ships hands-only patching of synth modules on Quest; Rec Room Circuits V2 (2020) has large numbers of non-programmers wiring chips in VR.
- Shapes that only fit where they belong stop errors before they happen: Scratch (Resnick et al., CACM 2009) lets only fitting blocks snap; here only fitting ports light, and each wire type has its own plug shape.
- A generic bank of 8 knobs covers any plugin's options: Ableton Push maps every device's parameters to 8 encoders in banks, for third-party plugins it has never seen.
- Lowered arms tire less: Consumed Endurance (Hincapie-Ramos et al., CHI 2014) measured how fatigue rises with raised arms; the graph's verbs are pinches with the hand at rest, the work strip is read without reaching, Lap mode works the board by ray from a resting forearm, and Table mode lets forearms rest on a real desk.
- Our own control-surface research (design/xr/vr-control-surfaces.md, the pipeline rail in section 6) proposed a waist rail of stages with a live count on each; this is that rail made general, with the counts that matter repeated at eye level.

### What it would teach us

- Can people who have never seen a node editor use graphty through the work strip and the fast path alone, and does the board, once unfolded, read as "how this was made" or as a programming tool they did not ask for?
- Do people read the work strip's scope and status before acting ("Neighbors of 3 selected", "showing 43 of 1,812"), or only after a surprise?
- Does the probe help people understand what a filter or a layout did, compared with undo-and-redo to see before and after?
- How often do people wire by hand once they know the fast path, and for what (two-input operations, compares, sweeps, recipes)?
- Is the glance between the board and the graph comfortable over 30 minutes in each posture, now that reading and turning happen at graph height and only wiring happens below?
- Does the tap, hold, drag grammar hold everywhere with the frozen-ray threshold, and what hold tolerance do people with tremor choose?
- Do generated faces (4 knobs plus banks of 8) work for ForceAtlas2's 13 options and a plugin's options with no hand tuning?
- How big do real patches grow in the six stress journeys, and how often do people fold?
- Do people understand the difference between undo, bypass and unplug, and choose the right one for an old mistake?
- Do people trust a reopened version, and do they understand "as of the save" on a relative date?
- Does a Review box feel like making a call or like data entry, and do people accept "last time's decisions" on replay without checking them?
- Can the work strip be read at a glance in its short forms, at the stated minimum text size, about 30 degrees down?

### Known risks

- **It looks like programming.** Newcomers may freeze at a board of boxes. Mitigation: the board starts folded and nothing in a first session needs it; every first-session act is a verb on the graph or a chip on the work strip that builds boxes silently; box titles are plain names.
- **The board is below the eyes, and that is the idea.** Reading results, turning knobs, typing and checking what is in force moved to graph height, but wiring by hand -- two-input operations, joins, compares, sweeps, recipes -- still happens on the board, about 45 degrees down. Moving the wiring bench to eye level would put a 60 cm board of boxes in front of the graph, which is a different design. Mitigation inside the idea: Lap mode raises the board to just under the strip and works it by ray from a resting arm; Connect to... replaces long drags.
- **Board clutter in long analyses.** Mitigation: boxes land in their stage column automatically; Fold turns ticked boxes into one tile, reversibly; the board scrolls sideways; Follow on any wire finds the other end.
- **No surface under a virtual board.** Pressing in mid-air has no stop and arms cannot rest on it. Mitigation: pads, switches and port arming need a press-through; knobs need a pinch; a bare rail at the near edge; Lap and Table modes.
- **Accidental touches.** A hand reaching across the board brushes wires and knobs. Mitigation: a bare fingertip never turns a knob, never scrolls the board and arms a port only with a press-through; probes are beads; every board change flashes on the strip at graph height, so a slip is seen where you look.
- **Recompute cost and frame rate.** A cheap change upstream recomputes a long chain, and at 10,000 nodes a restyle per detent would stutter. Mitigation: the frame budget above; boxes above a cost threshold wait for Run and are named on the Status chip; every box's output is cached and saved; Run all stale is one pad; a recompute shows its ring on each box and can be stopped.
- **Wiring precision with hands.** Mitigation: ports are 2.5 cm (3.5 cm on Vision Pro), 4 cm apart, with a snap to the nearest lit port and the target's label at the fingertip; only fitting ports light; Connect to...; Ray mode, controllers and Vision Pro use the ray or look and pinch.
- **Cycles and dead ends.** Mitigation: a port that would close a loop never lights; a box with a required port unfilled is hollow and blinks the port, and its Run pad says what is missing; Delete leaves dependents hollow rather than deleting them.
- **Three kinds of take-back confuse people.** Mitigation: all three (undo, bypass, unplug) are named steps in the same history; the Undo chip always says what it will take back.
- **Hidden effects.** A keep placed before an algorithm changes its results. Mitigation: graph verbs tap what you see; every Run pad, the Showing chip and every meter name the input; the input mark on Formula, Compare, Table and Overlay warns when inputs came from different graphs; exports ask for their scope.
- **Engineering cost.** A typed dataflow runtime (caching, staleness, saved outputs, a run log, recipes with review steps, ghost patches) and a 3D node editor with knobs, cables and a keyboard are bespoke work beyond graphty-element's panels; no revision removes this.
- **A second graph view.** Two Viewers and the overview need graphty-element to draw a second view of a graph in one immersive scene, which it cannot do today. Until it can, the overview is a cached image and two conditions use the Viewer's Split switch (the merged graph drawn twice as one graph), which doubles the drawn nodes.
- **Style cost estimate.** The frame budget wants graphty-element to estimate the cost of a style or filter change, which it does not today; until it does, the rule is by size.
- **Replaying an investigation still takes the person.** A recipe of a triage replays its computed steps and asks for its calls as review steps, one act per candidate. It does not and should not decide which group is a ring.
- **Construction work stays faster at a desk.** Mapping columns, setting conflict rules and checking constraints are fewer acts with a keyboard and a query language; the Review box makes pair decisions one act each, but a knowledge engineer will still do the setup at the desk and come to VR to look and to review.
- **The strip is about 30 degrees down.** The graph is fixed at chest height, so the strip under it cannot be at eye level; it carries short forms only, and everything read at length opens in the face slot beside the graph.
- **Text entry.** Names are offered automatically; Find, Formula and Note take short text on the keyboard (by ray or swipe in the face slot, or by fingertip when it comes forward) or by voice where a recognizer runs; reports, long notes and API keys go through the laptop or a paired keyboard, mirrored live in the face slot. Without a pairing, long text stays slow.
