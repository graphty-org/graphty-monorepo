**Prototype 34: Point and Ask** -- Pinch what you mean, then say or type what you want in your own words; it is compiled only into graphty's own undoable commands (by a local compiler on the headset for plain requests, by a language model for the rest, never into free text), shown as editable slot tiles over a preview, and each request leaves a live control on a Pin Board rail; anything else can be pinned there too, and a saved rail is a template for the next dataset. Every request has a hand path through Hand Menu and Windows (24), so nothing depends on voice or on the model.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away in 24's volume window, with 24's toolbar ornament and status line under it. The target tray sits at the left end of the status line and the Talk key at its right end, both at the height of the status line. The Pin Board rail is a curved strip about 60 cm wide, about 55 cm away and 25 to 30 degrees below eye level, just under the status line and clear of the graph's lower edge, tilted toward the face; the same placement seated and standing, and a setting lowers it. Its tiles are worked by ray and pinch only, never by touch, so a resting forearm cannot move a control. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands or controllers for pointing and pinching; Vision Pro's look-and-pinch; voice for requests where a recognizer exists; 24's in-scene keyboard for typed requests and text. Speech is turned into text by one of: a speech service the person chooses, called directly from the page with their own key; the browser's own recognizer where it runs inside an immersive session; or a recognizer the page runs itself. Plain requests are compiled on the headset; the rest go to the person's language-model provider, called directly from the page with their own key. Keys are typed or pasted on graphty's 2D page (in the headset's browser before Enter VR, or on a laptop driving a tethered headset), never inside the immersive session, and stay in that browser. graphty runs no server for any of this. Without a provider or without speech, the scheme is Hand Menu and Windows (24) plus the tray, the rail and its Pin verb, and a typed request field that uses the local compiler: nothing is reachable only by voice or only by the model.

**How an utterance is handled.** Each utterance goes at the same time to a small keyword recognizer that runs in the page (a WebAssembly model) and to the speech recognizer. If the whole utterance is one of eight phrases -- do it, go, cancel, stop, undo, redo, pin that, no this -- the keyword recognizer handles it on the headset and the other result is dropped. Otherwise the transcript (or the typed request) goes to the local compiler on the headset: the command palette's matcher over every command's plain name, technical name and example phrasings, plus slot parsing for attribute names, the data's own node names and ids, numbers said as words, comparisons ("at least", "fewer than"), find modes ("starting with", "ending with", "containing", "exactly"), tray references and lists joined by "and". If it compiles the request into one command, or a short list of commands, with every slot accounted for, the echo shows it at once with no network. Otherwise, if the project's assistant setting allows, the request goes to the model. A request neither can compile comes back as a "Not available here" line with the nearest commands as tiles to pick.

**Free text is always your words.** Every command declares its free-text slots: note text, label, callout, find text, and every name and title (project, view, set, saved filter, recipe, report title, report summary). Whichever compiler chose the command, it returns only where that slot's words start and end in the transcript; the page copies those words into the tile verbatim and marks it "your words". The model never writes text into a slot. If it cannot point at a span of your words, the tile stays empty and glowing. The word arrows on the tile move the span's edges a word at a time ("recipe Ring triage" to "Ring triage"), and 24's keyboard fixes a misheard word.

**What the model may fill.** It may choose a command, an attribute, a run or a reading of an ambiguous word, always with the alternatives shown. Numbers come from your words, from the option's default (gray), or from a short fixed list of idioms, always shown amber: "on their own" = 0 ties; "neighbors", "married into", "next to" = 1 hop; "finer" = the current resolution x 1.5 and "coarser" = divided by 1.5; "top" or "most" without a number = 10. Any other number stays empty and glowing. Names and ids come only from your words (as spans, matched to the data's own list, with the closest matches offered and none chosen for you) or from a tray chip. A request about how something looks rather than what it is ("the big red ones") is not compiled into a guess: it comes back as "Not available here: name the attribute", with the attributes that drive the current size and color as tiles. The local compiler follows the same rules.

Requests that only change what you see (find, select, frame, inspect, go to a view) apply after the echo has shown for half a second, and Back (or "undo" for a selection) returns. Requests that change the project (runs, styles, filters, notes, edits, removals, saves) wait at the Go gate unless the autonomy dial says otherwise.

**The preview needs graphty-element.** "Changes N of M" and the faint preview need a preview-and-commit API in graphty-element, which does not exist today: a count for every command before it runs, a drawn preview only for styles and filters, and layouts previewed by their cost and a progress bar, then committed as one undoable step. That is a change to graphty-element, made there for every consumer, not in the app.

A tile is about 12 by 9 cm, large enough for six rows of text at that distance, so about five tiles fit a page. Pages are tabs above the rail ("Starter", "Pinned", and any you name). The rail's left end is fixed on every page: Talk, Undo and Redo with the step names, the autonomy dial (labeled with what it does: "Preview -- you approve each change"), and "What can I ask?". Every tile's header menu holds the verbs of the object it shows, so a tile is the object, not a shortcut to it. Unpin leaves a stub on the page for the rest of the session with Restore.

Tiles bound to a run name that run ("PageRank, run 1"), not "the latest run", so turning a knob never silently changes a filter or a color that depends on the earlier run; a "follow latest" switch on the tile does that on purpose. When a newer run of the same algorithm exists, older-bound tiles say "run 1 of 3", and their menu offers "Move everything to run 3" as one step. A knob rerun never repaints: the new run's tile offers Paint, which is its own step.

The rail is saved with the project. A saved rail and a recipe are one kind of object, a recipe, in one list of names: "save this rail as Ring triage" saves a recipe with no steps, and "save the last six steps as Ring triage" saves the steps with the rail page beside them. A name already in use is refused on its name tile.

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** Open graphty's 2D page in the headset's browser (or on the laptop, with a tethered headset) and open the Florentine families sample. The first time, the page shows the assistant setting with Local only chosen, what each setting sends, two optional fields for a speech service key and a model provider key (typed or pasted with the platform's keyboard on this 2D page), and Skip. Choose Enter VR; allow the microphone when asked, before the session starts.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families at chest height in their window. Under it, the status line with the empty tray on its left and the Talk key on its right. ### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor (plain name, technical name, category, options, result fields) becomes one more command for both compilers, generated by the same code that builds 24's catalog window, so both name forms resolve and its options become slot tiles. The first run leaves its main option on the rail: the option the request named, otherwise the option the descriptor marks primary (an optional flag), otherwise the first number option not marked advanced; an algorithm with no number option leaves only its reader. Its result fields get readers by kind. Nothing is written by hand.
- **A new layout.** The same: one more command, its options as tiles and as a pinnable form, and "lay out these with X" scoped to a set.
- **A new object type** (a time slice, a collapsed group, a comparison). It registers its verbs as commands and declares a reader for its card. Its card and its chip in 24's ornaments are pinchable, so it enters the tray like any object and "this slice" binds to it; 24's popover lists its verbs, and the Pin verb puts its card on the rail. An object people manipulate directly (a time window with handles) still needs a small tile designed once by graphty's team.
- **A new option kind.** It needs a generated control, once, for 24's forms; the slot tile and the rail tile reuse that control.
- **A third-party plugin.** A "Biology: enrichment" family with four algorithms, one taking a list of gene identifiers and two numbers: pinch a set chip, "run enrichment on these" binds the list slot (a node list option) to the set or the selection, the numbers appear as tiles with their defaults in gray, and the result's group field reads as a group table. The family appears in 24's catalog window and in "what can I ask?". A plugin may also give example phrasings in its descriptor, which both compilers use; this is an optional addition to the descriptor.
- **The practical limit.** The compile target and the quality of descriptors. The model is given every command's full schema while the catalog is small; the catalog part of the prompt is the same for every request, so prompt caching makes its size cheap after the first request. Past about 300 commands, the model is first given a one-line index of every command (plain name, technical name, a one-line description, result field names), picks up to 20 by meaning, and then compiles against their full schemas: one extra round trip of about a second, only for catalogs that large, and never a word-overlap cut that drops "who matters most" before the model sees it. The local compiler needs shared words, which is why paraphrases need the model. The rail's limit is space: about five tiles a page, and more than five pages are hard to keep track of.

### Why it should work

- Speech plus pointing is the oldest multimodal interface: Bolt's "Put-That-There" (SIGGRAPH 1980) bound "that" and "there" to a pointed position. Kaiser and colleagues, "Mutual disambiguation of 3D multimodal interaction in augmented and virtual reality" (ICMI 2003), showed speech and pointing correcting each other's errors in a headset.
- People point before they speak the word: Oviatt, DeAngeli and Kuhn, "Integration and synchronization of input modes during multimodal human-computer interaction" (CHI 1997), found the gesture usually precedes or overlaps the spoken reference. Pinching first and talking second follows that order, and the tray makes the pointing a committed pick rather than a guess at where a moving ray was.
- Natural language for visual analysis works when ambiguity is shown and fixable: DataTone's ambiguity widgets (Gao and colleagues, UIST 2015) are the slot tiles' ancestor; Eviza (Setlur and colleagues, UIST 2016) and Orko (Srinivasan and Stasko, IEEE TVCG 2018, a network-visualization tool combining speech and touch) showed it for graphs; NL4DV (Narechania, Srinivasan and Stasko, IEEE TVCG 2021) compiles questions into a structured specification rather than free output.
- Compiling into a closed command list is now a shipped practice: tool calling with strict JSON schemas (OpenAI structured outputs, 2024; Anthropic tool use) constrains a model's output to declared commands, which is what keeps the assistant inside graphty's undoable session commands. Both providers accept calls made directly from a browser with the user's own key, so no relay is needed.
- Human-AI guidance supports preview, scoping and cheap correction: Amershi and colleagues, "Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction" (CHI 2019): show what the system can do, support efficient correction and dismissal, scope services when in doubt. Horvitz, "Principles of mixed-initiative user interfaces" (CHI 1999), and Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens' levels of automation (IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics, 2000) are the basis for the autonomy dial.
- Pinning a result as a live tile is shipped and understood: Power BI's "pin to dashboard" and the dashboards Sarikaya and colleagues surveyed ("What do we talk about when we talk about dashboards?", IEEE TVCG 2019) show people assemble a working surface from the pieces they used.
- Our own model scoring (Point and Ask in `design/xr/vr-control-surfaces.md`) gave it the highest core figure of the six control models (39 of 45 core functions natural), with the risks of network, provider, room and privacy noted.

### What it would teach us

- Do people pinch first without being reminded, do they read the tray badge before saying "do it", and how often does "this" take a chip they had pinched only to read?
- Do slot tiles catch wrong compilations ("fewer than three" read as "more than three") before commit, or do people approve on the preview alone?
- What share of real requests the local compiler handles with no network, and which requests are faster spoken than by 24's hand path. Our guess: compound rules, multi-layer styles, far path targets and named finds win; single toggles lose.
- Does the rail grow into a useful personal console, or into clutter people stop looking at? How many tiles survive a session?
- Does a recipe rebind correctly to a second dataset, and what do people do with the empty inputs?
- Is undo by meaning (a compensating step) understood, and trusted more than jumping back in History?
- Will people speak to a headset in the rooms they actually work in, and how much does latency (1.5 to 4 seconds per model request) cost them?

### Known risks

- **Wrong target.** A pick the person forgot is still in the tray. Mitigation: the tray is always visible at the status line, the number badge is drawn on the object in the graph, used chips leave, the target chip in the echo names the target, and "no, this" rebinds without repeating the request.
- **Misheard ids and names.** Speech-to-text mangles codes like ACC-48213. Mitigation: project names sent as recognition hints where the service takes them, the heard code matched against the data's ids with the closest under the tile, and 24's keyboard to fix it.
- **Approving without reading.** People say "do it" on autopilot. Mitigation: the count on the Go gate, the badge on the bound object, an empty glowing tile blocks Go, a "changes 0" card blocks Go, a destructive step always stops at the gate in every autonomy setting, and undo is local and instant.
- **Invented values or commands.** Mitigation: the compile target is closed; numbers come from your words, defaults or a fixed idiom list shown amber; text is always a span of your words copied by the page; a request it cannot compile comes back as "Not available here" with the nearest commands as tiles.
- **Latency.** 1.5 to 4 seconds per model request. Mitigation: the eight local phrases; the local compiler handles plain requests (find, select by attribute, run by name, color or size by, filter at a value, note, label, save as) with no network; view-only requests apply at once; the rail lets the next change be by hand with no round trip.
- **Voice on Quest needs a key.** There is no Web Speech in Quest Browser and no confirmed in-page recognizer. Until one is confirmed, voice on Quest needs the person's own speech service key, and without it Quest has no voice: the typed request field with the local compiler, the tray and the rail remain. This is stated on the Starter page, not hidden.
- **Provider, network, cost and privacy.** Mitigation: the scheme is complete without the model; the assistant setting per project, with Local only as the first-class mode for confidential work; both services called directly from the page with the person's own key, so graphty runs no relay. The limit, stated: in Local only on a headset with no on-device recognizer there is no voice, and paraphrases, OR rules and computed formulas fall to 24's hand path.
- **The preview needs an element change.** graphty-element has no preview-and-commit API today. Until it does, counts exist only where graphty-element can estimate them, and drawn previews do not exist; the Go gate still holds every project change.
- **Files from the laptop.** Not solved by this design: it shares 24's paths (the headset browser's file picker, or the 2D page before entering VR), and the laptop inbox and outbox need a pairing that needs a relay graphty does not run.
- **Speaking in shared rooms, or not being able to speak.** Mitigation: the typed request field on the hand menu goes through the same compilers, with command palette matches first; the tray, the rail and the hand path need no voice.
- **Arm and neck fatigue.** Mitigation: no held pose to talk (a tap, ended by a pause); picks are 24's ray from a low hand; the rail at 25 to 30 degrees down, at the status line on Vision Pro for Talk.
- **Rail clutter.** Every request leaves a tile. Mitigation: tiles replace their own earlier version when the same request is repeated, unused tiles fade to a stub after a session, and "clear this page" is one request; arranging the rail is never an undo step, so tidying is free, and an unpinned tile can be restored from its stub.
- **Knob reruns of slow algorithms.** A detent on betweenness over 10,000 nodes is not cheap. Mitigation: the rerun happens on release, not per detent; the knob shows the cost, and over 5 seconds it asks for Go.
- **Recipes rebinding to the wrong input.** "Score" in one dataset is not "score" in another. Mitigation: every input is shown as a tile with its kind and range before replay, nodes rebind only by exact id, and replay waits until each input is filled or skipped.
- **Compensating steps that do not exist.** Some steps (a merge, a removal) have no inverse command. Mitigation: "undo just that" then falls back to History's Go back to here, listing the later steps it would drop, at the Go gate.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: "this", "that" and "there" bind only to objects you explicitly picked into a visible target tray, never to where the pointer rested while you spoke.

- A pinch (or a Find result, a "which one?" pick, a pinched table row, chip or tile title) drops the object into a numbered tray at the left of the status line, with the same number as a badge on the object in the graph.
- Words take chips by position: the last word takes the newest chip. "These" takes a set chip or the selection.
- A word with no chip leaves an empty, glowing target that blocks Go. Used chips leave the tray; Clear empties it.
- What this removed: pointer-rest binding, the hidden 10-second pinch queue, the need for word times, the per-frame pick against the whole graph while talking, and the Vision Pro and Galaxy XR special cases.
- What it cost: the "point while you speak" moment. A person now pinches first, then talks.

Severity 3 findings:

- **Pointing while speaking had no disambiguation in a dense graph** (VR interaction; journey steps 4, 6, 10). Fixed by the tray: every target is picked through 24's snap cone and "which one?" list. Steps 4, 6, 7, 9 and 10 are rewritten for pinch-then-ask.
- **Act mode labeled the wrong module with only a toast** (expert, persona step 5). Fixed:
    - Targets now come only from the tray.
    - A group table row binds as its group.
    - Act stops at the Go gate for any text slot, any amber number, and any target reached through another object ("community 3 via Pucci").
    - Act draws a ring on the echo and the pointer, and returns to Preview when the session ends.
- **The talk pinch was one finger from 24's add modifier and close to a rest pose; "do it" could fire from conversation** (VR interaction, ergonomics, webxr). Fixed:
    - The off-hand talk pose is gone. Talk is now a tap on the Talk key (at the status line and on the rail) or a held controller button. Listening ends on a 1 second pause.
    - No hand pose can open the microphone.
    - "Do it" counts only as a whole utterance while an echo is open.
- **The rail sat where the forearm rests, could be pressed by touch, and committed on release, including on tracking loss** (VR interaction, ergonomics). Fixed:
    - The rail moved to 25 to 30 degrees down, just under the status line.
    - Rail tiles are ray and pinch only.
    - Knobs and sliders move only after a 150 ms pinch-grab, drag sideways one detent per 1.5 cm, and hold still for the last 100 ms before release.
    - Tracking loss springs the control back with no commit.
    - Each tile has one drag strip (the header) and one control (the face), so a drag no longer has three meanings.
- **Keys and the voice and model path could not reach a standalone headset; journey step 1 worked only tethered** (coverage, webxr). Fixed for keys:
    - Keys are typed or pasted on graphty's 2D page in the headset's own browser before Enter VR, and stay there.
    - Both services are called directly from the page with the person's key, so no relay is needed.
    - Step 1 is rewritten.
- **Files from the laptop.** Not fixed, and the file says so (area 1, Known risks). Files ride 24's paths, and the laptop inbox needs a pairing that needs a relay graphty does not run. Fixing it is not within this design's idea.
- **A first session without providers taught a dead control** (onboarding). Fixed:
    - A local compiler on the headset compiles plain requests with no key and no network.
    - New projects start in Local only.
    - The Starter hint shows typing on the hand menu when no recognizer exists.
    - "What can I ask?" is a key on the rail's fixed end.
    - The stress journey 1 walk now says which requests compile locally.
- **The privacy advice switched the assistant off for the analysts it serves** (red team). Fixed with a per-project assistant setting: Full, Names withheld, Local only and Off. Local only is first-class, and a confidential project is locked to it.
    - Names withheld replaces node names and ids with placeholders before the model sees them, and says the speech service still hears the audio.
    - Stated limit: in Local only, on a headset with no on-device recognizer, there is no voice, and paraphrases, OR rules and formulas fall to 24's hand path.
- **The verbatim guarantee broke in stress journey 6** (red team). Fixed:
    - Every command declares its free-text slots: notes, labels, callouts, find text, and every name and title.
    - Whichever compiler chose the command, it returns only the span of your words. The page copies the span verbatim, marked "your words", and the model never writes text.
    - Word arrows trim the span ("recipe Ring triage" to "Ring triage"), and text slots open 24's keyboard rather than a digit pad.
    - Journey 6 is rewalked, including:
        - "open last week's case" now lists projects and chooses none for you
        - the fade that affects nothing now shows a red "changes 0" card that blocks Go
        - recipes now list their inputs, nodes rebind by id, and replay waits until every input is filled or skipped
        - one namespace now covers recipes and rail templates
