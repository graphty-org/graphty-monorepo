**Prototype 35: Hint Keys** -- Pointing becomes saying or typing: one input puts two-letter tags on every visible node, list row and chart bar, and you run anything by saying or typing a tag or a visible label -- spoken (see it, say it), typed on a 12-key keypad on your off-hand finger segments, pinched with a ray or a look, or struck on a controller stick. Every key and button means one thing, however long you press it. Naming something makes it the thing you are working on; it never throws away what you selected. One Talk hold carries one whole command ("mike alpha neighbors two"), read back on the bar before it runs.

**Taps only.** Every key, button and stick direction means the same thing however long it is pressed; no key has a held second meaning. A finger key counts only as a tap: the thumb tip comes down onto the segment from at least 1 cm above the finger's surface, moving toward it, stays in contact for at least one frame, and leaves within 400 ms. So the thumb's rest against the side of the index finger never types, lifting the thumb off that rest never types, and a thumb sliding across segments on its way to another key never types; a contact whose release is lost to tracking types nothing, and the segment flashes. The practice card counts stray keys as well as misses, and the page keeps a running miss rate per segment during the session: when a segment degrades (the hand moved to a worse rest), its keys switch off on the hand with a line on the bar ("Enter and Back moved to the bar: little finger not tracked"). A stick direction counts once per flick past 70% and back through the center. Characters that have no key of their own come from the bar's second row: the decimal point, minus and "e" in a number field; Shift, Caps, the digits and symbol switches in a text field.

The tag alphabet is eight letters, **A D G J M P T W**, the first letter on each keypad key 2 to 9. A tag is two of them, so there are 64 tags at once, each exactly two key taps with no ambiguity, and each spoken as two words of the phonetic alphabet (alpha, delta, golf, juliet, mike, papa, tango, whiskey), which sound nothing alike. 0 and 1 are never part of a tag, so a pick run and Through cannot be mistaken for one. An element whose name starts with one of the eight letters gets a tag starting with it where one is free (Medici MA, Guadagni GA); others (Strozzi, Valjean) get any free tag. An element keeps its tag for as long as it stays visible, unless More tags or Grid renumbers it; the current object and every pick in an open run keep their tags through More tags, Grid and Go, and a command that names any tag reassigned in the last 2 seconds waits for OK. For the first three sessions each tag shows its spoken form under it ("mike alpha").

Voice phrases put these in order: tag, label, value -- "mike alpha neighbors two", "golf alpha route to mike alpha", "graph filter at least zero point zero seven OK". A pick run is spoken "plus", not "add", because "add" and "and" are easily confused and "and" is a word in the rule form.

**What the recognizer can hear.** Its vocabulary is what is printed now plus one level down from it: the bar's always words, the Home sections and each section's first-level labels, the phonetic alphabet, numbers, the labels of open panels, and, for each of those, the labels it would open next -- the verbs of any element's popover (every popover of one kind has the same verbs, so tags add only the phonetic words), each catalog family's entries, each form's field labels and the value words their kinds take ("hops", "to", "point", "minus", "days"). That is a few hundred words, not the few dozen printed at once; it is still a closed list far smaller than open dictation, and the page parses what was heard against the chain, so a word that is valid in the vocabulary but not at that point in the chain is shown struck through and ignored, never run. The vocabulary is rebuilt in a worker whenever panels change, off the main thread. Each label has one sayable name, printed on the shortcut sheet and under the label in the first sessions: its plain name, or a spoken alias from its descriptor. The label generator checks every sayable name against the always words and the Home sections as well as the other labels on screen and one level down, so no word means two things (the communities family is labeled "Groups" and Louvain is "communities"; the Time panel steps "earlier" and "later" because "back" is an always word). A label whose name the recognizer cannot hear (a coined plugin name, "Essentiality") gets a printed spoken name built from its family and its position ("centrality four"), so every printed label can be said with no work by its author; an author may supply a better alias. Node names are always reached by tag, keys or Search, never said; anything else is ignored.

**The typing pose.** The keypad is armed only while the off hand is held palm sideways (as if holding a phone edge-on) with the fingers extended and slightly spread, and the thumb free. ### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. The 2D page asks for the microphone before you enter, if you want voice. In the headset, allow hand tracking.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families at chest height. At the graph's near edge, at eye line, the bar: "Graph" and "Selection: none" on the left, the buttons Tags, Talk, Back and a dimmed Enter, and Undo and Redo at the right end, and the line "You can say: Statistics, Analyze, Layout, Style, Filter | Tags, Talk, Go, Grid, Search, Home, Graph, Selection, Save, Results, Undo, Redo, Back, OK, Shortcuts", the always words smaller after the divider. To the left, the Home panel's sections. A hint over the bar: "Pinch any word, or hold Talk and say it. Pinch empty space and drag to turn the graph." The finger keypad is never pushed on you: from the first time you type anything on the bar's keys, a small chip on the bar reads "Type on your hand? Try it", and the practice card opens only if you pinch it. No two first-use hints ever appear in the same step.
    - **Controls:** none yet.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Hold Talk and say "statistics" (or pinch "Statistics" on the bar; or, with the off hand in its typing pose, type 7 8 2). Then close both fists on the graph and turn it to see its depth (or pinch empty space and drag).
    - **What you see:** As you type 7, the bar dims every label not starting with P, Q, R or S; at 7 8 2 only "Statistics" is left and the card opens at once, because opening a card changes nothing. It lists "16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci has no marriages), density 0.167, average 2.5 marriages, undirected". Labels are shown on the largest families.
    - **Controls:** Talk and a label, or keys, or ray pinch; two fists.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Show tags: tap 0, pinch Tags on the bar, or say "tags". Type 6 2 (or hold Talk and say "mike alpha", or pinch the MA tag, or pinch the Medici node itself). In a graph too large to see every node, you would say "search" and type 6 3 3 (M E D), and the prediction offers "Medici" from the graph's own names.
    - **What you see:** Sixteen tags on leader lines, each with its spoken form under it in the first sessions; the bar's chip reads TAGS. Typing 6 dims every tag but MA. Medici is outlined as the current object, the tags hide, its popover opens, and the bar slides under the popover: "Graph > Medici", "Medici, 6 marriages". The selection chip still reads "Selection: none": naming a node never selects it.
    - **Controls:** Tags, a tag (two keys, two words or a pinch).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Say "neighbors" (or type 6 3, or pinch it). The Hops field shows 1. Pinch the bar's Enter, which reads "Select 7 families" (or say "OK", or tap #). Steps 3 and 4 are also one Talk hold: "mike alpha neighbors OK".
    - **What you see:** As the form opens, the bar reads "Medici > Neighbors > 1 hop: 7 families, 7 marriages" and the six in-laws glow: Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni. Saying "more" first would have read "2 hops: 12 families". After Enter the seven are the selection, the chip reads "Selection: 7", the selection becomes current, and its popover replaces Medici's: "Graph > 7 families".
    - **Controls:** a label, Enter; "more" and "less" for the hops; or one Talk hold.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch "Graph" in the bar's breadcrumb (or say "graph", or type 4 7 2) to make the whole graph current. Say "analyze", "centrality", "influence" -- PageRank's sayable name, since the recognizer does not know the word PageRank -- one word at a time, or all in one Talk hold ("graph analyze centrality influence"); or type 2 6 2, 2 3, 7 2 4, each opening as soon as it is unique, where typed keys in a catalog list match the technical name too. The options form opens; the bar's Enter reads "Run". Pinch it, say "OK", or tap #.
    - **What you see:** The catalog lists its families as labels (Suggested, Recent, Centrality, Groups, Paths, Structure, Flow, Prediction). Each algorithm shows its plain and its technical name: "Influence (PageRank)", "Influence at a distance (Katz)". In the Centrality list, PageRank's prefix is 7 2 4, because "Reach (closeness)" also starts on key 7 and the always word "Save" shares 7 2. The form shows Damping 0.85, Max iterations 100, Tolerance 1e-6, Weighted off, Run on: whole graph, 16 families, instant. On Run, a ranking opens beside the graph and the bar follows it.
    - **Controls:** the breadcrumb, three labels, Enter.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read down the ranking. Tap 0 (Tags), and type 9 3 (Strozzi's tag WD) to look at the third row more closely; or move the ranking's cursor with Next.
    - **What you see:** Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.0696, and so on down, Pucci last. With tags shown, the ranking panel takes its tags first, and each row carries the same tag as its node in the graph, so MA sits on both the Medici row and the Medici node; the panel's title carries a tag too. WD makes Strozzi current; its row opens to "rank 3 of 16, 0.088, 4 marriages", the node is outlined, the breadcrumb reads "Graph > PageRank > Strozzi", and the selection chip still reads 7.
    - **Controls:** reading; Tags and a tag, or Next.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Make the run current: pinch "PageRank" in the breadcrumb (or the ranking's title, which carries a button outline), or say "influence", the run's sayable name, which the breadcrumb prints under it. Say "size by one to three OK" in one hold, or word by word with Enter (keys: 7 4, then 1, Enter, 3, Enter). Say "color by"; the palettes list opens with tags shown, the sequential ones first; say the first one's name, or type its tag, then Enter (keys: 2 6 5, a tag, #).
    - **What you see:** "Size by" is already filled with PageRank, because you came from that run. The bar reads "Size by PageRank, 1x to 3x, 16 families" and its Enter reads "Apply"; the sizes preview once you pause. The palette list recolors the graph a moment after each tag is typed. Two layers appear in the Style section ("Size: PageRank", "Color: PageRank, sequential") and a legend opens.
    - **Controls:** labels, a range, a tagged list, Enter.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** With the run still current, say "filter at least zero point zero seven" in one hold and pinch Enter after reading the count (or word by word; keys: 3 4, 2 8, then 0 7 -- the field already shows "0." because PageRank lies between 0 and 1 -- and #).
    - **What you see:** The bar previews "PageRank at least 0.07: keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages", its Enter reads "Keep 5", and the dropped families fade once you pause. Ridolfi, at 0.0696, just misses; saying "less" once would step to 0.065 and keep it. After Enter: Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi, Tornabuoni, with Strozzi standing alone, and the bar reads "Showing 5 of 16 -- 1 filter step".
    - **Controls:** labels, a typed or spoken value, Enter.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Tags, 6 2 (Medici), then "note" (6 6, and Enter takes Note, the first candidate, over the Home section Notes); or one hold, "mike alpha note". The note field is focused. Hold Talk and dictate "Married into six families; the hub of the network" -- in a text field Talk is open dictation by default. Silently, type it: on the finger keypad with whole-word prediction (about 25 taps), on the bar's keys by pinch (or "Keypad" for a large panel), on a paired keyboard, with "Type in 2D" and the system keyboard, or as a laptop draft. Enter saves it. The form's Callout toggle would show it as a callout instead of a marker.
    - **What you see:** A note card beside Medici with your text and a Keep or Fix choice under dictated text; Fix puts a tag on each word, so "golf alpha" plus a new word replaces one. Saved, it becomes a small marker on the node. The selection is still the seven in-laws.
    - **Controls:** a tag, a label, Talk or a keypad, Enter.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You mean to hide Albizzi (AD) but say "mike alpha hide OK" in one hold. The bar showed "Medici > Hide" and the caption "Hide Medici" at Medici while you spoke, but the "OK" you said last commits it on release (without "OK" it would have waited for Enter), and Medici disappears. Pinch Undo on the bar (or say "undo"; on controllers, aim at Undo and pull the trigger).
    - **What you see:** The bar reads "Undo: Hide Medici". Medici comes back with its note. Redo stays on the bar until your next step. For an older mistake you would say "history" (a Home section) and show tags; each step shows its name and target ("Hide Medici", "Filter: PageRank at least 0.07", "Style: Color by PageRank"), so similar steps are told apart by their targets. "Undo to" plus a tag goes back to that point; "revert" plus a tag takes back that one step and keeps the later ones.
    - **Controls:** Undo on any channel; history tags for older steps.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Say "save" -- one of the bar's always words -- or pinch it, or type 7 2 8 (whenever a run is current, 7 2 alone also matches its Paint, so 7 2 opens neither). On a first save the name field opens holding "Florentine families"; press Enter, which reads "Save".
    - **What you see:** The bar reads "Saved Florentine families". The project keeps its history and its runs. The project's other verbs (Save as, Rename, Recent, Send to laptop) are in the Project section.
    - **Controls:** an always word, Enter.

### Advanced journeys

Every word below is on screen when it is said, or one level under a word that is: a Home section, an always word, a label of the panel that the previous word opened, or a label that panel would open next. A run of them can be said word by word, or as one Talk hold read back on the bar before Enter; both are given where the difference matters.

- **Compound rule.** Say "filter", "add rule". The rule form is a list of clause rows, each tagged, with labels at the bottom: Attribute, Is, Above, At least, Between, In the last, And, Or, Not, Group. "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" is built one clause at a time: "attribute", the tagged column list, "type"; "is", "account" (the values of a category column are a tagged list); "and"; "group" opens a nested row with its own "or"; "in the last", 30, "days". The bar reads the whole rule back in words with the count it keeps. Each clause is one Talk hold ("attribute type is account", "and group degree above twenty", "or risk score at least zero point eight", "and created in the last thirty days"), so the whole rule is four utterances and an Enter. A week later: "filter", the step's tag, "edit", the clause's tag, "value", "25", Enter -- or one hold, the step's tag then "edit", the clause's tag, "value twenty five". Nothing about the rule is drawn or dragged, so editing is as exact as creating.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Name a run, say "options": every field from the algorithm's descriptor is a labeled row, and "Advanced" opens the rest. "damping zero point nine", "again" re-runs and keeps both runs. For a resolution sweep on Louvain: "resolution", "sweep", then 0.5 Enter, 1 Enter, 1.5 Enter, 2 Enter, Enter; four runs land in the results list, each tagged. "Reset" puts back the defaults. ForceAtlas2's 13 options are 13 labeled rows, the rarely changed ones under Advanced; its tolerance field shows "1e-", so 1e-6 is the key 6 and Enter.
- **Comparing two runs.** Say "results" (an always word), then "plus", one run's tag, the other's tag, "OK", "compare" (keys: 0, a tag, a tag, #, then Compare's prefix). The reader is chosen by the runs' result kind. Two number results open a difference table with both values per node and the rank change. Two community results (Louvain at resolution 1.0 and 1.5) open a partition table instead: each group of the first run matched to the group of the second it overlaps most, with the shared count, which groups split and which merged, and an agreement score for the two runs; a group's tag selects its members in the graph. The matching and the agreement score are computed by graphty-element, which the reader only displays. Rows carry tags, so a row tag frames its node or group. In the reader's popover, "Split" puts two views side by side, each showing one run's colors (or, for two networks, one network); an element has the same tag in both, so naming it makes it current in both. Above a node count set by graphty-element's cost estimate (about 20,000 on Quest 3S), Split is replaced by one graph colored by the difference, because two copies of the scene would halve the frame rate.
- **Removal what-ifs.** On a node, an edge or a selection, "simulate removing" opens the before-and-after table (pieces, largest piece, average distance). To weigh several candidates, open it from a ranking row: Next then removes the next row's node instead, keeping each result as a tagged row of the same table, so the top five brokers are "simulate removing" and four Nexts.
- **Triage a list of matches (Sort into).** On any list -- query matches, search results, a ranking -- say "sort into". A short form asks for up to eight sets: existing sets by tag, or new names typed or said ("Threat", "False positive", "Benign", "Later"). The bar then shows each set on a key, 2 to 9, the chip reads SORTING, and the cursor is on the first row, framed and inspected. One key (or the set's name, or a pinch on it) puts that row's element in the set; the row shows the set's name for 400 ms, in which Back cancels it, and then the cursor moves on; Next (or 1) skips; 0 shows tags to name a node without leaving sorting; # ends it. Forty matches are forty sorting acts plus reading.
- **Layers on subsets.** Select the subset by a pick run (with tags hidden: 0 shows them, 0 again starts the run, then MA GA TA, then #), by a rule, by grid cells, or from a result ("select top", "five"; "select top" is also on any numeric column's tag in a table). Say "keep as set" (the name field offers "Set 1"; type or say a name, or accept it), then "style": a new layer painting only that set. In the Style section every layer carries a tag; a layer's tag makes the layer current (the node selection is untouched), and "up" or "down" reorders it, and its form's rows come from the style schema, so "border width three" is three words. "Presets" in the Style section lists the style presets, colorblind-safe ones marked; a preset adds its layers on top of the stack and never replaces your layers.
- **A combined score.** Say "analyze", "formula": a field with tagged lists beside it -- columns (degree, betweenness, closeness, eigenvector), operators and functions. "rank", the degree tag, "plus", ... ends as "(rank(degree) + rank(betweenness) + rank(closeness) + rank(eigenvector)) / 4", read back on the bar with the value it gives the current node. A z-score is "standardize" and a column tag. "name", typed "hub" with prediction, Symbols, "_", "score", Enter: a new column every reader and layer can use.
- **A pattern query.** Say "analyze", "pattern". The pattern form is generated the way the rule form is: node rows and edge rows, each tagged, with clause labels at the bottom (Type, Is, At least N distinct, Within, First seen, Connect, Add node). For "an account authenticates to 5 or more hosts it has never touched within one hour": "add node", type "user"; "add node", type "host"; "connect", the user row's tag, the host row's tag, edge type "auth"; on the host row "at least", 5, "distinct per", the user row's tag; on the edge row "within", 1, "hour", and "first seen". Spoken as chains, that is five holds: "analyze pattern add node type user", "add node type host", "connect" plus the two row tags and "type auth", the host row's tag plus "at least five distinct per" and the user row's tag, the edge row's tag plus "within one hour first seen". The bar reads "user -auth-> host: at least 5 distinct hosts per user, within 1 hour, first seen: 40 matches" before Enter. Matches open as a tagged list, ready for Sort into. "Save query" keeps it as a named, tagged query; a saved query's "duplicate and edit" starts the day's second hypothesis from the first. The template offers exactly the constraint kinds that graphty-element's pattern search declares; it has no matcher of its own.
- **Recipes.** Say "history", show tags, then 0, the first step's tag, 1, the last step's tag, Done, "save as recipe", and a name (a range longer than 64 steps takes More tags between the two ends; the Through survives it). The save form lists the range's steps, each tagged; steps that were reverted, and their reverts, are left out by default and can be put back by tag. It also lists the recipe's inputs: every target any step named -- a set, a node, a selection, a view, a column, a time window -- each shown with what it resolved to ("Set: Ring 7, 12 members", "Window: 2026-08-10 to 2026-09-08"), and each can be renamed ("suspect ring"). On another graph, "Analyze" lists Recipes as a family; picking one opens a form mapping each input to a tagged list on this graph (a set, a node, a view, a column) or to "skip"; a step whose input is skipped is left out and named on the bar before Enter. Recipes belong to the person, not the project, so they survive opening another project.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Say "data", "add table": the Inbox lists the files in the laptop's shared folder, tagged, and naming one pulls it, with nobody at the laptop. Its tag. The preview's 25 column headers each carry a tag: type a header's tag, then "key", and the bar reads "gene_symbol as key: 3,112 of 3,400 rows match" before Enter. On a fresh import, column roles (id, source, target, skip) and types are set the same way, one tag and one role or type label per column, so 40 columns never need a precise pinch.
- **Twelve scattered nodes in a 400-node graph.** Tags reach 64 at a time, nearest the ray first, and a 20-row ranking panel takes 21 of them, so close the panel first (Back). "Grid", 5 (the dense center), and the tags renumber inside the cell; 0 0 (tags, start the run), four tags; "grid" again (the GRID state wins over TAGS, so 3 picks the cell even mid-run), three tags; "last view", More tags, five tags; Done. About 30 acts for twelve nodes, none needing a pinch on anything under 2 degrees. In a core where even a ninth of the view is too dense, a cell can be gridded again.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor gives a plain name, a technical name, a family, options and result fields. It appears as a label in its family's list (or a new family label), both names become typable at once, and it is sayable at once: by its plain name if the recognizer knows the words, otherwise by a printed spoken name made of its family and position ("centrality four"). If its sayable name equals any label or always word, the generator adds the family name. A spoken alias is an optional descriptor field graphty-element does not have today; adding it is an additive change, and nothing breaks without it. Its options form is generated row by row from the option kinds in the parameter table above, so "set the third option of an algorithm a plugin added last week" is: its label, "options", the third row's label (or tags and the row's tag), the value.
- **A new layout.** A label in the layout list; its engine and options as rows; a focus-node option takes a tag.
- **A new object type** (a time slice, a pattern, a group of groups). It declares its verbs and a Home section; its verbs become its popover's labels, its instances get tags when visible, its list (under that section) gets tagged rows, and Search finds its instances by name. Its scene marker is the generic one (a small labeled disc) unless it declares another. No new gesture, key or word is needed.
- **A third-party plugin.** "Biology: enrichment" with four algorithms becomes a family label. An option taking a list of gene identifiers is a node-list field: a pick run of tags, a saved set's tag, a column of an Inbox file, typed ids with prediction from the graph's names, or the laptop clipboard. An option taking several feature columns is a several-attributes field. Its results read through the generic readers, with rows tagged.
- **Stable addresses, honestly.** Typed prefixes are the label's own letters, so the dimming teaches them and the shortcut sheet prints them; the price is that they are not frozen. When a plugin adds a label that shares its first letters with one you know, both need one more key (a plugin's "Rahman centrality" makes PageRank 7 2 4 3 and the newcomer 7 2 4 6), the shortcut sheet marks the change, and a typed prefix that now matches two labels opens neither, so the change costs a key, never a wrong action. A person who wants frozen codes would need assigned codes printed on every label, which would lose the letter dimming that teaches them; this design keeps the letters. Search always finds anything by name.
- **The practical limit.** 64 tags at once: a 77-node graph needs More tags or a Grid cell for the last 13. About 30 labels in one panel before keypad prefixes reach three or four keys. Only names starting with A, D, G, J, M, P, T or W can get a tag with their own initial. Node names the recognizer does not know (gene symbols such as TP53 and TP63, family names) are reached by tag, keys or Search, not said; coined plugin names are said by their printed family-and-position names. Long prose has no good channel here.

**Performance rules.** A preview (a recolor, a fade, a resize) starts only after typing has paused for 200 ms, never per key. It runs only when graphty-element estimates the repaint at under 8 ms a frame; that estimate is a new capability graphty-element would provide (its cost estimates today cover algorithm runs, not style repaints), and until it exists the preview runs only below 2,000 affected elements. Above the limit, the preview is the count on the bar alone, with a highlight only when fewer than 2,000 elements are affected, and the graph changes on Enter. WebXR delivers hand joints and gamepad state only once per rendered frame, so a key tapped during a stalled frame cannot be seen; the design does not pretend otherwise. Instead, a commit that repaints is spread across frames by graphty-element (so every consumer gets it), and while it runs the bar reads "Applying..." and the keypad is paused, with its segments dimmed; a key typed then is refused visibly, never half-taken and paired with the next key. At 100,000 nodes, "keeps 37 of 98,412" is a count, not a repaint, and it is current for the value Enter will apply.

### Why it should work

- Hint labels replace precise pointing with typing in shipped tools used daily: Vimium link hints in the browser (github.com/philc/vimium, 2010), Emacs ace-jump-mode (2012) and avy (2015), and Rango, which puts hint labels on web pages for voice users of Talon (github.com/david-tejada/rango). Cursorless (github.com/cursorless-dev/cursorless, 2021) places small letter "hats" on code tokens so a spoken letter targets any token on screen -- the same idea as a tag on a node.
- Voice control with numbers and grids is shipped for people who cannot point: Dragon NaturallySpeaking's MouseGrid (late 1990s), Apple Voice Control "show numbers" and "show grid" (iOS 13 and macOS Catalina, 2019), and Windows Voice Access (2022). Mixed reality uses the same rule: Microsoft's HoloLens guidance "see it, say it" makes every visible button label a voice command, with MRTK's See-it Say-it label (learn.microsoft.com/windows/mixed-reality/design/voice-input). Voice Control's own fix for commands that are not on screen is the same as this design's: a visible list of what can be said.
- Selecting small, dense targets in VR by ray is slow and error-prone; refining in steps works better (Kopper, Bacim and Bowman, "Rapid and accurate 3D selection by progressive refinement", 3DUI 2011). The grid and group tags are progressive refinement with names on each step.
- A panel that shows the next keys teaches the expert path: Spacemacs (2014) and which-key (2015) list what each next key does; Kurtenbach and Buxton showed with marking menus (CHI 1994) that when the novice path rehearses the expert's motions, people move to the expert path without training. The shortcut sheet prints the exact keys an expert types blind.
- Thumb-to-finger keypads have been built and measured: DigiTouch (Whitmire and colleagues, IMWUT 2017), FingerT9 (Wong, Zhu and Fu, CHI 2018) and TipText (Xu and colleagues, UIST 2019). Meta's microgestures (2025) ship thumb taps along the index finger on Quest, though only to native apps; this design does not depend on them.
- A recognizer limited to a short phrase list makes far fewer errors than open dictation, and the phonetic alphabet was designed for intelligibility (ICAO, 1956). Vosk accepts such a phrase list and runs in the page (github.com/ccoreilly/vosk-browser), so audio never leaves the headset. Keeping every sayable word on screen also keeps the list short.
- Making every function operable without precise pointing is an accessibility requirement on the web (WCAG 2.1.1, Keyboard); this scheme meets the spirit of it in a headset, which also helps users with tremor or limited reach. Removing timing from inputs follows WCAG 2.5.1 and 2.2.1's intent: no meaning depends on how long something is held.

### What it would teach us

- Which channel people choose per task when all are available: voice for verbs, keys for tags and numbers, pinch for one-offs? Does mixing them in one command feel natural or confusing?
- How accurate is a phrase-list recognizer in the page on Quest (latency, errors, CPU cost while rendering), and how often does a person need to say a tag twice? How much accuracy does the whole-command vocabulary (one level under what is printed) cost against printed words only, and do people speak chains or single words once both work?
- Do people notice the difference between the current object and the selection, and does the selection chip keep them from losing a hand-built selection?
- Can thumb taps on finger segments be detected reliably from WebXR hand joints, at what segment size and in which resting poses, and how fast do people type tags and numbers on them after ten minutes? Is the typing pose distinct enough from a fist and a relaxed hand?
- Do tags beat ray pointing for nodes under 1 degree wide, and is reading a tag then typing it faster than reaching?
- Do people learn keypad prefixes of common labels from the shortcut sheet without trying, as which-key users do?
- Is the "where keys go" rule (tags shown, sorting, field focused, otherwise labels) clear, with the state on the hand and the bar, or do people type into the wrong place?
- Does a vocabulary of on-screen labels scale to a 60+ algorithm catalog and plugin families without collisions that confuse the recognizer, and do people accept saying plain names when the technical name is the one they know?
- Does a bar that follows the current object stay readable without covering the part of the graph being worked on?

### Known risks

- **Finger keypad unreliable in the browser.** Thumb-to-segment taps are unmeasured from WebXR joints, and the little finger's keys are the most often hidden from the cameras. Mitigation: the keypad is an accelerator, never required; a pinch on the bar or the controllers do everything; a key needs an approach from above, a short contact and a release, so resting and sliding never type; the practice card measures misses and stray keys per segment, a running miss rate keeps measuring in session, and failing keys move to the bar with a note; segment size and tap sensitivity are settings.
- **The keypad hand alone is no longer a complete channel.** Undo, Redo, Talk, More tags, the decimal point, minus and symbols are on the bar, not on the fingers, because the only way to fit them on twelve keys was holds. A silent person typing on the finger keypad pinches the bar with the other hand for those. This is the price of giving every key one meaning, and it is accepted.
- **No recognizer on the device.** Quest Browser has none. Mitigation: the page ships Vosk in a worker with a word list, downloaded once and cached; voice is optional everywhere.
- **Recognizer lexicon.** The small offline model ignores words it does not know, which includes many algorithm names and most node names. Mitigation: sayable names are spoken instead, a label it cannot hear gets a printed family-and-position name, and nodes are named by tags, which are always in its lexicon.
- **A larger spoken vocabulary.** Whole-command chains need the recognizer to know the words one level under what is printed: a few hundred words instead of a few dozen. Errors will be higher than with the printed words alone. Mitigation: the chain is parsed, so a heard word that does not fit the chain at that point is struck through and ignored rather than run; nothing changes until Enter or a final "OK"; the novice path (one printed word at a time) still works. This is the one place the design trades some of its "only what is printed" accuracy for speed, and it should be measured.
- **Voice false triggers and mishearing.** Mitigation: Talk only from a visible button held (or toggled) on purpose, with a light and a tone; the chain read back on the bar and a caption at the target, and nothing changes until Enter or a final spoken "OK" (there is no timer); destructive verbs (remove, delete, merge, reset, clear, close, replace) wait for Enter even when spoken; a setting makes every spoken change wait for OK; Undo on every channel; "plus" for a pick run so it cannot be heard as "and".
- **Tag clutter and split attention.** Tags cover the graph and draw the eye from the structure; in a dense core they overlap and leader lines cross. Mitigation: tags only on demand and hidden after a pick; at most 64, the focused panel first, then nearest the ray or gaze; tags placed by label placement with a margin, not on the node; tag size and opacity are settings; Grid when a core is too dense. Overlap in a very dense core is not fully solved; Grid costs one or two more acts.
- **The bar covers the graph.** It follows the current object. Mitigation: it sits under the popover, on the side away from the graph's center, and collapses to its first line while you turn the graph.
- **Mode confusion over where keys go.** Mitigation: the state chip on the bar and the changed segment letters on the hand (set names while sorting); the states win in a fixed order (grid, tags, sorting, field, labels); typed label prefixes match one pool, so a panel label never shadows an always word; Back empties the input, then hides the tags.
- **Losing a selection by naming something.** Fixed by the model: naming makes an element current and never touches the selection, which stays on the bar's chip; replacing a selection of two or more is an Undo step, and "last selection" brings back the one before.
- **System gesture collisions.** The off-hand thumb-to-index tap resembles Quest's system menu pinch and Vision Pro's palm-look Home, and every pinch also arrives as a system select. Mitigation: the typing pose is sideways; the page ignores keypad-hand selects while the keypad is armed; the dominant hand uses only the standard pinch; controllers as an alternative.
- **Arm fatigue.** Mitigation: the keypad works with the off hand resting on a thigh or armrest when the practice card measures it in view; voice and controllers need no raised arm; nothing requires reaching into the graph.
- **Learning the phonetic alphabet and the keypad.** Mitigation: only 8 phonetic words are used for tags and their spoken forms are printed under the tags at first; the hand shows letters; voice and pinch need no keypad skill.
- **Prefix churn when plugins arrive.** Prefixes are the labels' own letters, so a new label sharing a known label's first letters lengthens both by a key. Not hidden: the shortcut sheet marks the change, an ambiguous prefix opens nothing (so the cost is a key, never a wrong action), and Search finds anything. Frozen codes would need assigned codes instead of letters and would lose the dimming that teaches the keys; the design keeps the letters.
- **Sound-alike names.** Gene symbols, codes and look-alike names cannot be told apart by voice. Mitigation: tags, spelling, and Search results with tags.
- **The laptop pairing needs a relay.** The pairing is part of this design, but it needs a service both pages can reach (a small relay or WebRTC signaling), which graphty does not run today; that is build cost. It can run on a closed network, the code only authenticates a key exchange, and data is end-to-end encrypted. Pulling files needs the folder grant, which only Chromium desktop browsers offer; from Safari or Firefox the laptop must send each file, so a mid-session file means lifting the headset. While the relay is unreachable, the bar says so, sends wait in the Outbox and go when it returns, and the headset's downloads are the last resort.
- **Prose.** Notes and reports are the weak spot. Mitigation: dictation where available, keypad prediction for one sentence, the bar's keys, a paired keyboard, and laptop drafts for anything long. With whole-word prediction a one-sentence note on the finger keypad is about 25 taps, and a two-sentence summary about 70; "Type in 2D" leaves the immersive session for the system keyboard. Fast silent prose is not fixable inside a keypad design.
- **Speaking aloud in shared spaces.** Mitigation: the keypad, the bar and the controllers cover everything silently.
- **Engine features this design only displays.** Partition matching, pattern constraints (counts, time windows, first seen), the methods table, the style-repaint cost estimate, commits spread across frames and the optional spoken-alias descriptor field are graphty-element's to provide; the readers and forms here are generated from what it declares.
- **In-scene panels are the largest build item.** Popovers, Home, forms, tables, the bar and the readers are all in-scene panels, and graphty-element's XR code has no panel system today. One toolkit generates them all: Babylon GUI on mesh textures, with WebXR quad layers for text-heavy tables where the browser supports layers (Quest does; Vision Pro Safari does not).

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: every command has one meaning and one visible target.

- Every hold and every dominant-thumb gesture is gone. No key, button or stick direction changes meaning with press length. Talk is the only input that is held, and it has a single meaning.
- The echo line became the bar, placed at the current object. It reads the input back, shows the state and a breadcrumb, and carries pinchable Tags (renamed from Hint, never paging), Talk, Back, Enter (named for the verb it runs), Undo and Redo, plus the keys and extra characters the current field needs.
- The bar's "you can say" line lists every word voice accepts at that moment: the focused panel's verbs and a fixed set of always words. The Home sections are always on screen too. The journeys and advanced journeys were rewritten so that every word said is on screen when it is said.
- The finger keypad stays, for tags, label prefixes, digits and letters only. It is armed only in the typing pose. The bar's second row replaces the holds.

Severity 3 findings:

- **Hidden global commands: "save" and a dozen others could be neither said nor typed** (red team; Florentine step 11, advanced journeys, areas 9, 16, 18, 19). Fixed.

**Round 2.**

The red team's top change, adopted: one Talk hold carries one whole command.

- The recognizer's vocabulary is now what is printed plus one level down from it: the verbs of any popover, each catalog family's entries, each form's field labels and value words. It runs in a worker, and the page parses what was heard against the command chain.

- On release nothing changes until Enter, or "OK" said as the last word; destructive verbs still wait for Enter. The 1.5-second spoken timer is gone.

A word that does not fit the chain is struck through and ignored, never run, and the one-word-at-a-time path still works. Listed as a risk and as something the prototype must measure.
