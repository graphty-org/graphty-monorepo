**Prototype 36: Focus Remote** -- A TV remote for the graph, with no pointing at all: exactly one focus ring moves in discrete steps (along edges in the graph, down shelves of verbs, across rows of a form), driven by a thumbstick, by thumb swipes read from hand joints, by pinch-drags on Vision Pro, or by one or two accessibility switches; one input acts, one opens the verbs, one backs out, one marks, one undoes.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away, a panel column to its right at reading distance, and a status strip under it. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. The reference input is two controllers. The same nine inputs come from:

- one controller alone;
- thumb swipes and pinches read from hand-joint poses (Quest, Galaxy XR);
- pinches, pinch-drags and the gaze at pinch start on Vision Pro;
- a paired keyboard;
- one or two accessibility switches, by scanning.

Eyes are used only to move focus coarsely where the browser exposes them. Voice is optional and only fills a text field. Every input works with either hand (handedness is a setting) and seated. Hands rest on the thighs or an armrest, palms down or sideways, never palm up: a palm-up pinch is the system menu gesture on Quest and on Android XR.

Nine inputs: four directions, Act, Verbs, Back, Mark, Undo. View, Look and Redo are shortcuts for things that also have a tile (the View row turns the graph, Find moves the focus, Redo sits beside Undo), so a switch user or a one-handed user loses nothing.

**Keyboard.** Arrows move, Enter acts, Escape is Back, Space marks, the menu key or Shift+F10 opens Verbs (the desktop context-menu convention), Ctrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes. Typing letters while a shelf or list is open goes straight to its Search row.

**Switch scanning.**

- With one switch, a highlight scans through the current row: its tiles, the focused node's edges in order around it, or a form's rows. After the row it scans a fixed strip: "Back, Verbs, Mark, Undo". On a number row or in a wheel, the strip adds "Less, More". At the group level, the groups scan in order. A press acts on the highlighted item.
- With two switches, one steps the highlight and the other acts.
- Scan speed, the dwell before scanning resumes, and "skip empty" are settings.
- The edge ring and every shelf are already linear lists, so scanning needs no separate layout.

**Groups.** The group level shows the latest grouping result (communities, clusters) if there is one. Otherwise it shows the connected pieces, and any piece over about 200 nodes is split into regions of the current layout. A region over 200 nodes splits again. Regions are a navigation index, recomputed silently; they are never an analysis step and never appear in history. So a 10,000-node graph is always a few levels of small choices, and no grouping is run on the user's behalf. "Group by" on the Graph shelf switches to any category attribute.

**Directions in the graph** are read in the view as it stood when the focus arrived, so small head movements do not change which neighbor a press picks. Before a press is taken, the edge it would take lights up: a half push on the stick, or a pause in the swipe or drag, previews it. The edge back to where you came from is labeled "back".

**Signposts.** A signpost names the neighbor and how many nodes are reached most quickly through that edge ("Albizzi -- 4 beyond"):

- "dead end" when there are none;
- "only route" when the edge is the only way to what lies past it;
- when a route target is set, the steps to the target through that edge;
- when a grouping result exists, how many groups lie beyond.

On a node with more than 8 edges, signposts show only on the edge nearest each direction. The edge ring then becomes a list grouped by group or edge type ("500 ties -- 12 groups"), with a letter strip and a sort row (by group, weight, name).

### The shelves

Every shelf has the same structure:

- **Quick** (row 1): Undo, then the object's top verbs. The shelf opens on the first verb, so Undo is always exactly one Left, and an Act straight after opening never undoes. Redo sits left of Undo when there is one.
- **Always** (row 2): Note, Find, Ask, the same on every shelf. Down from Quick lands on Note.
- Then one row per verb group. Up and Down land on a row's first tile (or its letter strip).
- **Search** (last row): letters typed there search every verb, catalog entry and setting by plain or technical name. Up from Quick wraps to it.
- Each row shows up to 8 tiles plus More. A longer row starts with a letter strip: only letters that begin an entry appear, Left and Right walk the strip, and Act jumps to the first entry with that letter.

**What a tile does.** Act does what the tile says, with the values it shows ("Mark neighbors -- 1 hop: 6 families"). A tile that names a thing still to be set up (an algorithm, a layout, an export format, Color by) opens its form or chooser on Act, focused on Run or on the first choice, so Act, Act runs with defaults. Verbs on any tile opens its form focused on the first option, for changing values first. Tiles that destroy something (Delete, Remove, Merge, Remove selected, Close project, Leave VR) show a confirm row naming the counts ("Remove 7 families and 9 marriages?"), which takes a second Act. Everything else relies on Undo.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on, with hands or controllers resting.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families floating ahead, the focus ring around the whole graph, and the status strip: "Florentine families -- 16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces. Act: go in. Verbs: what you can do." The first time, a card beside the strip shows the nine inputs drawn for the device in use (sticks and buttons, thumb on finger, or pinch-drags). It stays until dismissed from Help.
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Press Verbs. The Graph shelf opens on Statistics; press Act. Read, then press Back. Push the off-hand stick right twice. Press Act to go in.
    - **What you see:**
        - The shelf opens under the graph. One Left of the focus, "Undo" is dimmed: there is nothing to undo.
        - The Statistics panel lists "16 nodes, 20 edges, density 0.167, average 2.5 ties, 2 pieces, largest piece 15 (94%), longest shortest route 5 steps, undirected, unweighted". Back returns focus to the graph; the panel stays to the right.
        - The graph turns 30 degrees in two eased steps; focus stays on the graph.
        - Going in, the ring drops to the groups level: "Piece 1 of 2: 15 families, 20 marriages". Pressing Right would reach "Piece 2 of 2: Pucci, no marriages".
    - **Controls:** Verbs, Act, Back, View (x2), Act.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** With Piece 1 focused, press Act.
    - **What you see:** Focus enters the piece at its most central node. With no result yet, that is the node with the most ties: Medici. The graph eases until Medici sits at the reading spot, and six signposts appear at the far ends of its edges. The status strip reads "Medici -- node 1 of 15 in piece 1, 6 ties".
        - If the entry node is not the one you want, use Find. Press Verbs, Down to the Always row, Right to Find, and Act, then M on the letter grid. Only one family starts with M, so the prediction row shows "Medici" alone. Up and Act land on it: about nine presses.
    - **Controls:** Act (Find: Verbs, Down, Right, Act, letters, Up, Act).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Read the six signposts. Press Verbs: the Node shelf opens on "Mark neighbors -- 1 hop: 6 families, 7 marriages". To check 2 hops first, press Verbs on that tile: its form opens on Hops; Right shows 2, Left goes back to 1, Back closes the form. Press Act on the tile.
    - **What you see:**
        - The signposts: "Albizzi -- 4 beyond", "Ridolfi -- 3 beyond", "Tornabuoni -- 3 beyond", "Barbadori -- 2 beyond", "Salviati -- 1 beyond, only route", "Acciaiuoli -- dead end".
        - At 2 hops the form reads "2 hops: 11 families".
        - Act marks Medici and the six (Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni). The status strip reads "7 marked".
        - To look around instead, press Left: focus crosses the tie lying left to Albizzi, whose own signposts now show ("Guadagni -- 3 beyond", "Ginori -- dead end", and "Medici -- back" on the edge you came by). Right goes back along it.
    - **Controls:** Verbs, (Verbs, Right, Left, Back), Act; directions to walk.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Press Back twice (node to piece, piece to graph). Press Verbs, Down twice to the Analyze row, Right to Centrality, and Act: the family opens as its own row. Press Right four times to "Influence -- PageRank" and Act. The form opens with the Run tile focused; press Act.
    - **What you see:**
        - The Centrality row: Connections (degree), Bridges (betweenness), Bridge edges, Reach (closeness), Influence (PageRank), Influence by association (eigenvector), Influence at a distance (Katz), Hubs and authorities (HITS). At 8 tiles it needs no letter strip; a family of 9 or more gets one.
        - The form's rows: "Run on: whole graph" (cycles to "the 7 marked" or a set), "Damping 0.85", "Iterations 100", "Tolerance 1e-6", "Weight: none", "Advanced (2)".
        - The Run tile reads "Run PageRank on 16 families, under a second".
        - On Act, the run appears in the Results panel to the right, and focus moves to its first row.
    - **Controls:** Back, Back, Verbs, Down, Down, Right, Act, Right (x4), Act, Act.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Press Down through the rows, then Up back to row 1.
    - **What you see:** "1 Medici 0.145", "2 Guadagni 0.098", "3 Strozzi 0.088", "4 Albizzi 0.079", "5 Tornabuoni 0.071", "6 Ridolfi 0.070", "7 Castellani 0.069", "8 Bischeri 0.068", "9 Peruzzi 0.067", and on to "16 Pucci 0.010". Each focused row rings its family in the graph; the graph holds still unless the family is out of view, so reading is not a ride. A histogram row at the bottom has five focusable bars ("0.06 to 0.08: 7 families").
    - **Controls:** Down, Up.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Press Verbs on row 1: the Result row shelf opens on Paint. Press Right to "Size by PageRank, 1x to 2x" and press Verbs to change it first: the form opens on Smallest. Press Down to Largest, Right four times until it reads 3x, Up twice to Apply, and Act. Then press Verbs, Right twice to Color by, and Act: the palette chooser opens on the first sequential palette. Press Act.
    - **What you see:**
        - Each Right adds 0.25x, and the graph resizes live as a preview. Apply commits a style layer; Back would have discarded the preview.
        - The palettes show as tiles with their swatches, and the focused tile previews on the graph.
        - A key appears under the status strip ("Size: PageRank", "Color: PageRank"), and the Layers list now holds two layers.
    - **Controls:** Verbs, Right, Verbs, Down, Right (x4), Up (x2), Act; Verbs, Right (x2), Act, Act.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Press Down to row 9, Peruzzi. Press Verbs and Right three times to "Keep at least 0.067". Press Act.
    - **What you see:** The threshold comes from the focused row, so it is exact without typing. While the tile is focused it reads "keeps 9 of 16 families, 13 marriages" and previews the fade. After Act, Salviati, Barbadori, Pazzi, Ginori, Lamberteschi, Acciaiuoli and Pucci fade out, and the status strip says "Showing 9 of 16 -- 1 filter step". To fine-tune, Verbs on the tile opens its form, where Left and Right move the threshold by 0.005.
    - **Controls:** Down (x8), Verbs, Right (x3), Act.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Press Up to row 1, Medici, and Act: focus leaves the panel and lands on Medici in the graph. Press Verbs, Down to Note, and Act. The note card opens on its phrase row. Act on "Married into 6 families", Right, Act on "Highest PageRank (0.145)", then Up twice to Save and Act. (Or dictate, or type on a paired keyboard.)
    - **What you see:** The phrase row is built from what is known about Medici: its ties, its names, the run just made. Each chosen phrase is added as its own sentence: "Married into 6 families. Highest PageRank (0.145)." Any word can be replaced or deleted from the text row. Once saved, the note becomes a marker on Medici and a row in the Notes list.
    - **Controls:** Up (x8), Act, Verbs, Down, Act, Act, Right, Act, Up (x2), Act.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You mean to pin Medici. You press Verbs and hold Right toward Pin, but the repeat carries you one tile past it onto Hide, and you press Act before reading it. Medici vanishes. Press Undo.
    - **What you see:**
        - The Hide tile had read "Hide Medici: 1 family, 3 marriages hidden". It is 3 because the filter already hides Acciaiuoli, Barbadori and Salviati.
        - A hidden node keeps a ghost ring, so focus stays on it.
        - Undo brings Medici back with its note, and the status strip echoes "Undid: Hide Medici".
        - On every shelf, one Left from the opening tile now reads "Undo Note on Medici", with "Redo Hide Medici" beyond it.
        - Back would only have closed the shelf; it never undoes.
    - **Controls:** Undo (off-hand Y; with one controller, Verbs, Left, Act).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Press Back until focus reaches the graph, then Back once more to open Home. It opens on Save; press Act.
    - **What you see:** Home's Quick row: Undo, Save, Save as, Recent. Save reads "Save as 'Florentine families'". On Act, the status strip says "Saved Florentine families". Save as would open the name field with the dataset name in the prediction row.
    - **Controls:** Back (x3), Act.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Open the Graph shelf, Filter row, "By rule".
    - The rule form has one row per clause: attribute (a pick row with letter strip), comparison (cycles), and value (a stepper or wheels, relative date tiles, or a category list where Mark keeps values).
    - "Add clause" adds a row joined by AND. Act on the joiner cycles AND, OR, NOT.
    - To nest, Mark two clause rows and choose "Group these" from their shelf; they indent under one joiner.
    - "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" is four clause rows and one group: about 40 presses, every one exact. The tile shows "keeps 212 of 9,840 accounts" before Apply.
    - To edit it a week later: the filter step's shelf, Edit rule, the same form.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** On the Result row or Run shelf, "Re-run with options" opens the form filled with this run's values, focused on the first option.
    - A number row steps. Act opens digit wheels: Left and Right pick the digit, Up and Down spin it, Act accepts.
    - A tolerance opens a decade wheel (1e-4 to 1e-10), then a mantissa wheel.
    - Apply makes a new run beside the old one.
    - For a resolution sweep, Verbs on the stepper row offers "Sweep". Set from, to and step (0.5 to 2.0 by 0.5), and four runs queue, each a row in Runs.
- **Comparing two runs.** On a run's shelf, choose "Compare with" and pick the other run from the Runs list.
    - Two Results panels sit side by side with linked focus: the row focused in one is focused in the other, so Down walks both rankings at once.
    - A Changes column says "up 2" or "down 1".
    - Two synchronized graph views follow the same rule: focus in one rings the same node in the other.
- **Combining scores.** In the Runs list, Mark several runs and choose "Combine with marked runs". A generated form asks the method (mean of normalized ranks, mean of z-scores, sum, difference of two), the name (from the prediction row), and shows the new column's first five values. Common formulas become a form, so free text is needed only for a truly new formula.
- **Layers on subsets.** Mark a set, by walking with Mark held or with Select by rule. Focus the status strip's "7 marked" and press Verbs to get the selection's shelf, then choose "Style this": the layer paints only that set. In the Layers list, Move up and Move down reorder layers; the list reads bottom to top, in painting order.
- **Regions.** Select row, Region: a box starts at the focus. Directions move its opposite corner one step at a time, through the full depth of the graph; the tile reads the count ("38 nodes"). Act accepts, Back cancels.
- **Moving a node.** Node shelf, Move: directions nudge it in the view plane, accelerating while held; a Depth row on the move strip moves it nearer or farther; Act drops it, Back puts it back.
- **Recipes.** In the History panel, Mark the steps to keep, press Verbs and choose "Save as recipe". Name it from the prediction row or the letter grid. On another graph, Recipes is a tile in the Analyze row. Act replays a recipe, and a form asks for each input it needs (an attribute per input, picked from a row).
- **Joining tables and importing data.** The file comes from the laptop.
    - On the 2D page, "Send to headset" puts the file in the "From laptop" list on Home (or in Data, Add data).
    - Act opens the import form: the format (detected; cycles if ambiguous), the format options as rows, then one role row per column (id, source, target, skip, or attribute with its type), already filled by detection, then the load report as a list.
    - To join a table, the key pick rows are sorted by match count, so the likely key is first and its tile reads "gene_symbol: 412 of 430 rows match" before Act.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.**
    - Its descriptor places a tile in its family's row and in the Search row. A new family adds a family tile to the Analyze row; past 8 tiles, its row gets a letter strip.
    - Its options become form rows by kind; a node option uses pick mode.
    - Its result fields choose their reader: ranking rows, group rows or histogram bars.
    - It inherits Run on, cost, Re-run, Compare, Combine, Paint and Filter from the Run and Result row shelves.
    - For the planned biology family, the gene-list option is a node-list row: add the focus, add the marked, use a set, or paste a list sent from the laptop.
- **A new layout.** A tile in the Layout row, its engine choices as a cycling row, its options as a form. Live layouts get the same Stop tile.
- **A new object type.** It declares its verb groups and its top verbs. Its shelf is built as Quick, Always, then one row per group, and its panel as a list of rows. If it lives in the graph (a callout, a time slice), it joins the focus levels by declaring which level it belongs to and what Act does on it.
- **A third-party plugin.** It never adds an input. A reader, writer or palette becomes a tile in Add data, Export or the palette chooser, and a whole plugin is findable by name in the Search row.
- **The practical limit.** The number of presses grows with the length of what the user must walk, not with the number of features.
    - Thanks to the letter strip, a list costs about one more press each time it doubles in length.
    - Free text, a scattered hand-picked set and a freeform highlight shape cost presses in proportion to their size.
    - Those are the places where it hands off to rules, predictions, a paired keyboard or the 2D page.

### Why it should work

- **TV and console interfaces.** They have taught hundreds of millions of people to run large catalogs with a direction pad, select and back. Shipped proof that rows of tiles plus letter jumps scale to thousands of entries:
    - Apple's tvOS focus engine and its focus and selection guidelines (developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection);
    - Android TV's D-pad navigation;
    - the Netflix and YouTube TV row layouts.
- **Pointing is the expensive part of VR graph work.** The functionality checklist cites 12 to 21 seconds per node for ray selection in dense 400-node graphs. Discrete focus takes target size out of the cost entirely.
- **Moving along links instead of through space.** Moscovich, Chevalier, Henry, Pietriga and Fekete, "Topology-aware navigation in large networks" (Bring and Go, Link Sliding), CHI 2009, found link-following navigation faster than pan and zoom for reaching neighbors in large graphs.
- **Directional keys over nodes and edges.** Data Navigator (Elavsky, Nadolskis and Moritz, IEEE VIS 2023) showed that any visualization, network diagrams included, can be navigated with directional keys over a structure of nodes and edges, for screen reader and keyboard users. This scheme makes that structure the main control, not an accessibility layer.
- **Switch access.** It is a shipped, proven way to run a whole device: Apple's Switch Control, including on Vision Pro (support.apple.com/guide/apple-vision-pro/set-up-switch-control-tanfddd1bb7c/visionos), and the Xbox Adaptive Controller (2018).
- **Microgestures.** Meta's microgestures (developers.meta.com/horizon/design/design-microgestures/) offer thumb swipes and taps on the index finger as a low-effort, hands-resting input for exactly this kind of directional control. The WebXR Hand Input joint poses (w3.org/TR/webxr-hand-input-1) may be enough to approximate them.
- **Our own research.** It rated Walk and Flick, the edge-walking model with signposts, among the top schemes (`design/xr/vr-interaction-options.md`), for being silent, cheap and hands-resting.

### What it would teach us

- How many presses each of the six stress journeys takes compared with the desktop's clicks, and whether exactness (no misses, no retries) makes up for the count.
- Whether people build a mental map from walking edges with the graph moving to them, or get lost without seeing the whole graph. Does the group level work as an overview, and do layout regions make sense as groups?
- Which edge a direction press picks when six neighbors share four directions, and whether people can predict it with the lit preview. Do they use the edge ring, or just press until the right signpost lights?
- Whether people read signposts ("4 beyond", "only route") and use them to choose a direction.
- Whether thumb swipes and two off-hand pinches can be told apart from joint poses in the browser reliably enough (false inputs per minute) to be a daily input on Quest and Galaxy XR, and whether pinch-drags read as four directions on Vision Pro.
- Whether a switch user completes the basic journey, and in what time.
- Whether the separate Verbs input, and the rule "Act runs the tile, Verbs opens its options", confuse people used to "select opens the menu".
- How fast the word-level text field with predictions is for set names, labels and one-sentence notes.

### Known risks

- **Press count.** Long lists and forms cost many presses; the stress journeys take 110 to 250.
    - Mitigation: letter strips, Suggested and Recent, the Search row one Up from every shelf, counts and defaults filled from the focus (as in step 8's threshold), detected import roles, the key sorted by match count, held directions that accelerate, Sweep, and Combine instead of formulas.
- **Directional ambiguity in the graph.** Several neighbors lie the same way, or the one you want lies diagonally.
    - Mitigation: the lit preview of the edge a press would take, directions fixed to the view as it stood when the focus arrived, the "back" label, and the edge ring (Act on a node) listing every edge in order, grouped on hubs.
- **Getting lost.** Walking a 10,000-node graph hop by hop loses the big picture.
    - Mitigation: the group level above nodes (results, pieces or layout regions), Back up to it at any time, the trail list as history, Fit and presets on the View row, and the status strip naming the group and position.
- **Unreachable or isolated nodes.** Pucci has no edges to walk to.
    - Mitigation: pieces are groups, so Pucci is its own group at the group level. Find reaches anything, and Tables and Results reach any node by its row.
- **Hand gesture reliability.** Thumb swipes and pinches read from joint poses are unmeasured in browsers and may misfire when hands move; the off hand's index and middle pinches (Back and Mark) may be confused.
    - Mitigation: a swipe must start from a resting pose and finish within a set time; a sensitivity setting; both confusable inputs are harmless (Back closes, Mark toggles back); controllers or a paired keyboard as the reference input. On Vision Pro, joints are not used at all.
- **System gestures.** A palm-up pinch opens the system menu on Quest and Android XR.
    - Mitigation: the resting pose is palms down or sideways; the input card says so; a hand turned palm up pauses gesture reading.
- **Text.** The letter grid is slow (TV keyboards run at a few words per minute).
    - Mitigation: phrase rows and word prediction from the graph's own words and the context, word-level editing, dictation where the browser supports it, a paired keyboard, and long text handed to the 2D page.
- **Gathering scattered nodes and drawing shapes.** No pointer means no lasso.
    - Mitigation: rules, regions grown by steps, Mark held while walking, Find then Mark, and top N from results. Freeform highlight shapes stay on the 2D page.
- **Motion.** A graph that slides to every focus can cause discomfort, and at node level a large graph scaled up could surround the user.
    - Mitigation: the viewer never moves and the graph never rotates on its own; against a solid background there is no scenery to stream past. Eases are short. In panels the ring moves, not the graph, unless the element is out of view. The graph is scaled only within a working volume in front of the user, never closer than about 40 cm; nodes beyond it fade. Reduce motion turns eases into cuts.
- **Mixing up Back and Undo.** People used to Escape-as-cancel may expect Back to revert a change.
    - Mitigation: nothing is committed until Act on Apply or on a tile, so Back from an open form discards only what was never applied. The status strip always names what Undo would undo, and the dedicated Undo is on the other hand from Back.
- **One controller.** Without the off hand, Undo is three presses.
    - Mitigation: Undo is always exactly Verbs, Left, Act, and every destructive tile previews its count first.
