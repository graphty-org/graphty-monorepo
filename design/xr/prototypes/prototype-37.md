**Prototype 37: Zoom Stream** -- One chooser for everything: a column of boxes beside the graph, each sized by how likely you are to want it; steer into a box and it grows and opens into its own choices until the whole command -- or an id, a set name or a sentence of a note -- is spelled out, with a language model built from the project's own words and the registry. You steer with a small "air stick": pinch anywhere and move the hand a few centimeters, so the hand can rest in your lap.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. The stream stands to the right of the graph (left for left-handed users), at the same distance and close to the graph's edge, so the graph and the stream are both within about 30 degrees of straight ahead. It is about 35 cm tall and 25 cm wide, angled to face you. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: either hand (pinch), a controller (trigger as the pinch, thumbstick as a stick), eyes only where the platform uses them to land a pinch (Vision Pro), voice optional, only to fill a letter stream. Steering is relative: once a pinch has landed on the stream, only the hand's movement counts, so the hand can rest in the lap or on an armrest. Everything works one-handed and seated. Settings: steering speed, steering gain (how far the cursor moves per centimeter of hand), smallest box size, "steady sizes", and "latch" (a quick pinch on the stream starts steering and another stops it, for people who cannot hold a pinch).

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families float at chest height, with Pucci apart from the rest. To the right stands the stream. The first time, its one huge box reads "Learn to steer (about 2 minutes)"; below it a small "Skip". Steering into it runs a short drill: pinch on the stream, let the hand drop to your lap, move it up to the green box, back up, steer through Go slowly.
    - **Controls:** pinch and hold on the stream, move the hand, release.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Quick-pinch empty space beside the graph to make the graph the subject. Pinch on the stream and move the hand a little right, toward "About". Pinch with both hands and drag to turn the graph a little.
    - **What you see:** The stream shows the graph's verbs with Undo greyed at the top (nothing to undo yet). As you steer over About, the readout card fills: "16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci has no marriages), undirected", and the attributes the sample carries. You let go before Go; About is a reading, so nothing was needed.
    - **Controls:** quick pinch (graph), steer, release, two-hand pinch and drag.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** In the graph's stream, steer into Find, then into M.
    - **What you see:** Find opens a letter stream. Only one family starts with M, so the M box already holds the whole word "Medici". Steering through it reaches the Go box, which reads "Go to Medici". Through Go, the view frames Medici, it lights, and the stream changes subject: "Medici" heads the breadcrumb and its verbs fill the column. (With 16 visible labels you could just as well quick-pinch Medici in the graph; Find is how the same thing works on 20,000 accounts.)
    - **Controls:** steer through letters, steer through Go.

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Steer into Neighbors (a big box: it is the most used node verb). The hop ruler opens; steer into 1, then through "Select" and Go.
    - **What you see:** Over "1 hop" the six in-laws light in the graph and the box reads "1 hop: 6 families, 7 marriages among them"; drifting down over "2 hops" lights 11 more-distant relations and you drift back. The breadcrumb reads "Medici: select neighbors, 1 hop, Go". Through Go, the tray shows "7 selected" with chips for Medici, Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni.
    - **Controls:** steer, number ruler, Go.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Quick-pinch empty space to make the graph the subject. Steer into Analyze. At the top of the level, as a suggested analysis, "Influence (PageRank)" is a big word box; steer into it. The next level's biggest box is "Go with defaults"; steer into it and through Go.
    - **What you see:** The Analyze level shows Suggested on top, then the families alphabetically (Centrality, Communities, Flow, Paths, Prediction, Structure), then Recipes and Spell. While the cursor is on an entry, the readout card gives its one-line description. The breadcrumb reads "Florentine families: run Influence (PageRank), damping 0.85, on the whole graph, Go". The Go box says "instant". Through Go, the run is made and the stream changes subject to the run.
    - **Controls:** quick pinch (graph), steer through three levels, Go.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** In the run's stream, steer slowly into Ranking.
    - **What you see:** Ranking is a node stream ordered by value, and each family's box is as tall as its PageRank: Medici (about 0.145) dominates the top, then Guadagni (about 0.098) and Strozzi (about 0.088), Albizzi (about 0.079) and Tornabuoni (about 0.071), a band of near-equals around 0.06 to 0.07 (Ridolfi, Castellani, Bischeri, Peruzzi, Salviati), then Barbadori, Pazzi, Ginori, Lamberteschi and Acciaiuoli, and Pucci a sliver at the bottom. The family under the cursor lights in the graph and the readout gives its value to three decimals. The shape of the column is the distribution: one standout and a long flat middle. For the full table, steer through "Open as table" to get the ranking in a window.
    - **Controls:** steer (reading by cursor), release to pause on a value.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Back up one level to the run's verbs, steer into Paint. Its biggest box is the suggested style, "Color and size"; steer into it. The palette level shows the 7 sequential palettes biggest (PageRank is an amount); steer into the default. The size range ruler opens with "1x to 3x" biggest; steer through it and through Go.
    - **What you see:** Over each palette the graph recolors as a ghost; over the size range, the nodes grow as a ghost. Breadcrumb: "PageRank: paint color (default sequential) and size 1x to 3x, Go". Through Go, the colors and sizes become solid, a legend appears under the graph, one style layer is added, and the last-step chip reads "Paint PageRank".
    - **Controls:** steer back, steer through a list, a palette level and a range ruler, Go.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** In the run's stream, steer into Filter, then "at least". The value ruler spans the result's range. Steer into 0.0 to 0.1, then into 0.07, then through "0.070" and through Go.
    - **What you see:** While you steer down the ruler, families below the value under the cursor fade in the graph as a ghost, and the box under the cursor reads its effect: "0.080: keeps 3 of 16", "0.070: keeps 5 of 16 families, 4 marriages". The finer intervals open around the gaps in the data, so the marks between Tornabuoni (0.071) and Ridolfi (0.069) are big and easy to land between. Before Go, a short level asks "Filter (analyses see only these)" or "Only hide on screen", with Filter biggest. Through Go, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain, and the readout says "Showing 5 of 16 -- 1 filter step".
    - **Controls:** steer, zooming ruler, short list, Go.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Quick-pinch Medici. Steer into Note. Spell "Married into six families; the hub of the network" in the letter stream, then steer through Done and Go.
    - **What you see:** The letter stream sizes its boxes from the project and English: after "Ma", "Married" is a whole word box; after "six f", "families" is whole (it is a word of this dataset). "Medici", "marriages" and every family name are always one box away. The ten words take about a minute for a practiced user; in a first session, expect three to four minutes, since Dasher novices start near 2.5 words per minute. If the browser offers speech, "Say it" fills the sentence instead and you steer through Go. Through Go, a small note marker sits on Medici.
    - **Controls:** quick pinch, letter stream, Done, Go; optional "Say it".

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You want bigger labels: graph stream, Style, Labels, Size. Zoomed in on Medici, 4x looks right in the ghost, so you take it through Go. Then you steer through View, Fit and see the labels piled over each other. Quick-pinch the last-step chip, "Label size 4x", and steer through "Undo this".
    - **What you see:** The labels return to their earlier size, the chip now reads "Note on Medici", and the top box of every verb level reads "Redo: Label size 4x" above "Undo: Note on Medici". Had the mistake been older, the History stream shows the steps newest first, each named with its values ("Filter: PageRank at least 0.070", "Paint: PageRank color and size"); steering over one shows that state as a ghost, and steering through "Return here" goes back to it.
    - **Controls:** quick pinch (chip), steer through Undo this.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** In the graph's stream, steer into Project, then "Save as". The name level offers "Florentine families -- PageRank" as a whole word box (made from the dataset and the latest run); steer through it and Go.
    - **What you see:** The readout says "Saved Florentine families -- PageRank". A plain Save (one box) would have saved under the current name.
    - **Controls:** steer, a suggested name, Go.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Graph stream, Filter, then the rule as a sentence: attribute stream (type), "is", value stream (account), then "and", then "any of (", degree, "above", ruler 20, "or", risk_score, "at least", ruler 0.8, ")", "and", created, "within the last", 30 days, Go. The breadcrumb shows the rule with each clause as a separate phrase. A week later, steer into Filters, the step, Edit: the whole sentence returns already spelled; steer back to the clause "above 20", forward again with 15, and through Go. Nested groups go one deep in comfort ("any of (" and "all of ("); deeper rules are spelled the same way but get long, and "Open as form" shows the rule as a tree in a window.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Steer into "More options" instead of "Go with defaults". The options appear as one box each, in the descriptor's order, each showing its current value: damping 0.85, iterations 100, tolerance 1e-6, weight attribute (none). Steer into tolerance: exponents first (1e-6 is biggest because it is the default), then mantissa; steer back out to the options list and through Go. "Reset to defaults" and "Open as form" are boxes in the same level. A resolution sweep for Louvain is "Sweep" on the resolution ruler: pass through 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 as stops, then Done and Go, and four runs are made side by side.
- **Comparing two runs.** In a run's stream, "Re-run with" brings back its sentence fully spelled; steer back to damping, forward to 0.5, Go: a second run, the first kept. Then "Compare with", and the stream of other runs (the new one biggest), Go: a comparison card shows how many families changed rank and by how much, and the ranking level shows both columns side by side with the movers' boxes outlined.
- **Picking scattered nodes.** Tray stream, "Pinch to add or remove": the tray glows and every quick pinch in the graph toggles one node (pick zoom works too, for small ones); steer through Done. One hand is enough.
- **Layers on subsets.** Select by the tray (or keep a set), then the selection's Style: the sentence starts "Layer on 7 selected" and continues exactly as in step 7. Graph stream, Layers lists layer boxes bottom to top; a layer's Move opens a ruler of stack positions labeled "above Community color" and so on; Edit returns its sentence for change.
- **Recipes.** History stream, or a history step's "Save as recipe from here": steer into the first step of the range, then the last, then a name (suggested from the steps) and Go. On another graph, Analyze shows Recipes as one more family; steering into one asks each input as a level ("weight attribute for PageRank:" as an attribute stream, with the closest name biggest), then Go replays it.
- **Joining tables / importing data.** On the 2D page (or the laptop beside you), choose the file; the headset's stream then opens "Add data: expression.csv". "Join onto nodes" asks the key column as an attribute stream; the column whose values best match node ids is biggest, and its box reads "18,402 of 20,000 rows match". The preview opens in a window beside the stand. Column roles are a short level per column ("this column is: amount, group, id, skip, text, time"), with the guessed role biggest and "Accept all guessed roles" as the top box of the first column.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor puts it in its family level (or a new family box, in alphabetical place), its description fills the readout card, and its plain and technical names join the language model, so Spell finds it after two or three letters. Its options become levels by kind: number to ruler (range, step, default biggest), logarithmic to exponent and mantissa, choice to list, toggle to two boxes, attribute to an attribute stream filtered by kind, node to a node slot, list of ids to a gather level with "From selection", "From set" and "Spell ids" on top, text to a letter stream with the descriptor's examples as word boxes, color to the color level, date to the date ruler, file to the 2D page. An option kind none of these covers is shown in the form window, so a new kind never blocks a plugin. Its result fields get readers by kind: a node number becomes a ranking level and a histogram, a group becomes a groups level, a graph number goes to the readout card, an edge number gets an edge ranking. The checklist's hardest case -- a "Biology: enrichment" family with an option that takes a gene list and two numbers -- is a new family box, a gather level whose biggest box is "From selection", and two rulers, with no change to the stream.
- **A new layout.** The same: an entry in the Layout level, its engine and options as levels, live layouts get Stop on top of their stream.
- **A new reader, writer or palette.** A reader joins the format level and format detection, with its options as levels; a writer joins Export, Data; a palette joins its kind's group in the palette level.
- **A new object type.** It declares its verbs and its label; picking one opens a stream of those verbs, its labels join the model, and if it has values it gets a value-ordered level like a run.
- **A third-party plugin.** Needs no headset work. It may optionally register extra words (synonyms, abbreviations) to make its entries bigger sooner.
- **The practical limit.** Depth and length. Sentences beyond about six pieces become slow to read back and to edit; forms with more than about eight changed options are faster in the form window; free text beyond a few sentences is slower than a keyboard; and text the model cannot predict (keys, hashes, novel names) costs one steer per character.

### Why it should work

- Dasher (Ward, Blackwell and MacKay, "Dasher: a data entry interface using continuous gestures and language models", UIST 2000) showed that steering into boxes sized by a language model lets people write with only a pointing device, with expert rates around 30 words per minute and fewer errors than an on-screen keyboard. Its later gaze version reached about 25 words per minute (Ward and MacKay, "Fast hands-free writing by gaze direction", Nature 2002).
- Tuisku, Majaranta, Isokoski and Raiha, "Now Dasher! Dash away! Longitudinal study of fast text entry by eye gaze" (ETRA 2008) followed novices over ten sessions: from about 2.5 to about 17 words per minute, which is both the evidence for the scheme and the measure of its learning cost.
- Dasher has shipped for twenty years as an accessibility tool on Linux, Windows and Android and is used by people who cannot use a keyboard (inference.org.uk/dasher). A headset without a keyboard is in a similar position.
- Progressive refinement for dense 3D selection works better than precise pointing: Kopper, Bacim and Bowman, "Rapid and accurate 3D selection by progressive refinement" (3DUI 2011). Shift (Vogel and Baudisch, CHI 2007) showed that showing the occluded spot enlarged, then springing back, beats precise aiming at tiny targets. Pick zoom is the continuous version of both.
- Zooming cannot separate nodes that lie behind one another along the pointer; stepping a depth cursor through them is the depth ray of Grossman and Balakrishnan, "The design and evaluation of selection techniques for 3D volumetric displays" (UIST 2006).
- Stellmach and Dachselt, "Look and touch: gaze-supported target acquisition" (CHI 2012) and their gaze-supported pan and zoom work (ETRA 2012) found that zooming toward a point of interest makes small targets selectable with imprecise pointing.
- Rate-controlled zooming keeps pointing time logarithmic in the scale of the world (Guiard and Beaudouin-Lafon, "Target acquisition in multiscale electronic worlds", International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 2004).
- Fixed order with variable size keeps spatial memory: Dasher keeps the alphabet in a fixed order and changes only box sizes, and adaptive menus that change size or emphasis rather than position (Findlater and McGrenere, CHI 2004 and later) avoid the slowdowns that reordering causes.

### What it would teach us

- How long does steering take to learn for commands, as opposed to text? Is a first session's basic journey under fifteen minutes, and how fast is it by the third session?
- Does a single chooser for verbs, options, numbers and words feel simpler than separate surfaces, or does it blur what kind of thing is being chosen?
- Does relative steering with the hand resting in the lap match aimed steering for speed and errors, and how much less tiring is it over half an hour?
- Do people read results by steering over a value-sized ranking, or do they always open the table window?
- Does pick zoom with its depth step keep people oriented in a 400-node graph, and is it faster than a candidate list at 300 and 3,000 nodes?
- How accurate is a language model built from one project's ids and labels, and how many steers does a typical id, set name or attribute value take?
- Does the Go box's speed limit prevent accidental commits without making deliberate commits feel sticky?

### Known risks

- **Learning curve.** Dasher took hours to become fluent in its studies. Mitigation: the two-minute drill at first entry; big suggested boxes so a beginner's first commands are two or three steers; the thumbstick on controllers; slower default speed. A first session's text entry is slow (about 2.5 words per minute), so suggested names are offered wherever a name is asked.
- **Box sizes changing under you.** Adaptive sizing can undo what spatial memory learned. Mitigation: order never changes, only size, within limits; a "steady sizes" setting freezes command levels and keeps prediction for text only.
- **Overshooting and accidental commits.** Fast steering can carry the cursor into the wrong box. Mitigation: nothing changes before Go; the Go box accepts only a slow crossing; steering back before Go is free; the last-step chip and the named Undo box after Go.
- **Rare choices are small.** An option nobody uses is hard to hit. Mitigation: a minimum box height of about 1.5 degrees with the label on the readout card; Spell finds anything by name; "steady sizes".
- **Arm fatigue and holding a pinch.** Mitigation: steering is relative, so the hand rests in the lap and moves a few centimeters; releasing pauses the stream mid-sentence and it waits; controllers use the thumbstick; "latch" removes the held pinch altogether.
- **Motion from zooming.** Pick zoom swells the graph toward you and springs it back. Mitigation: a speed cap; nodes within about 35 cm of the eyes fade out instead of passing through your head; the background never moves; reduce motion turns pick zoom into a quick fade to the magnified view and back, and view previews into fades.
- **Stacked nodes.** Zooming alone cannot separate nodes behind one another. Mitigation: the depth step by pushing or pulling the hand; Find and node streams for the rest.
- **Long sentences.** Compound rules and long option lists make long breadcrumbs. Mitigation: each clause is shown as a separate phrase, steering back stops at clause boundaries first, and "Open as form" takes long forms.
- **Reading tables.** The stream is a poor table. Mitigation: windows from Hand Menu and Windows (24) for tables, histograms and import previews, scrolled by the same air stick; the stream only drives them.
- **Text the model cannot predict.** Keys, hashes and new names cost one steer per character. Mitigation: hand those to the 2D page; suggested names for sets, views and projects so naming rarely needs spelling.
- **Preview cost.** Live ghosts on a 20,000-node graph can stall the frame. Mitigation: ghosts only for cheap verbs, at most ten refreshes a second; everything else shows a count and a cost.
- **Privacy of the model.** The model is built from the user's data. Mitigation: it is built and kept in the browser, never sent anywhere, and dropped when the project closes.
- **Vision Pro without continuous gaze.** The gaze Dasher evidence does not transfer directly. Mitigation: steering never needs gaze after the pinch lands; test on the device early.
