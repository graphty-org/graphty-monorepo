**Prototype 38: Prop and Plane** -- The off hand holds a stand-in for the graph and turns it; the dominant hand pushes a flat plate through the graph, and the nodes in the plate's slab appear flattened on its face, uncluttered, where you pick precisely; flip the plate face up (or press B) and it becomes a clipboard of verbs and forms. Time works the same way: moments are stacked in depth and the plate slices one.

This is the surgical-planning interface of Hinckley and colleagues (CHI 1994) -- a doll's head in one hand, a cutting plane in the other -- applied to a node-link graph, with one honest admission: the clipboard is an ordinary hand-held panel, and most of graphty's 22 areas run through it. The scheme's new ideas are in navigation (the graph's orientation is in your off hand), selection (a slab that removes occlusion), thresholds (cutting a graph laid out along an attribute) and stacks (time, runs and networks laid in depth and sliced).

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away. ### The control vocabulary

Controller mapping first, hands in parentheses. "Off" is the non-dominant hand. With hands there are only two poses: a pinch (thumb tip to index tip) and a flat hand. The off hand only pinches, to hold the graph; the dominant hand is flat (the plate) or pinches (pick, sweep, press). Everything else a controller does with a button, hands do with a labeled key on the off-wrist strip. Every input keeps one meaning; where a stance changes its object (the plate or the clipboard), the verb stays the same: trigger commits, A toggles in the selection, grip grabs, thumbstick moves within the surface, B goes to or back on the clipboard.

Gesture safety: with hands, nothing is read from finger sides, middle fingers, double pinches or fists -- only a pinch and a flat hand. No gesture asks for a palm toward the face, which Quest, Android XR and Vision Pro reserve for their system menus: the plate's face is drawn on the back-of-hand side, so reading the slice means looking at the back of your hand, and the plate never rolls face up; a hold that starts palm-to-face is ignored and a hold's roll stops before the palm reaches the face; the wrist strip faces up from a palm-down forearm and is poked with the dominant fingertip, because a fingertip near a wrist tracks far better than one hand laid over the other. On Vision Pro every pinch also arrives as a transient pointer select; the page matches it to its hand by handedness and drops every select from the off hand, and the dominant hand's while that hand is the plate, so the clipboard is pressed only by the dominant fingertip or a dominant gaze-pinch.

Destructive verbs (remove elements, merge, delete a set or layer, clear filters, reset styles, remove a run) never act on the first press: the preview ghosts what changes, and the press turns the verb into a confirm row ("Remove 11 nodes and 14 edges -- Confirm / Cancel"). Every one of them is also an undo step.

**The laptop link.** Data and Project both show a 6-digit pairing code; typed once on the laptop's 2D page (Settings, Pair a headset), it links the two pages for the session through a relay on graphty's own origin. The Inbox (on Data, and in every text field as "Paste from laptop") receives files dropped on the laptop page and text pasted there: an alert id, a gene list, the assistant's key, a long report section. The Outbox (on Project, and the default destination on every export form) sends files, notes and the report draft to the laptop page, which offers each as a download. Opening a file through the headset's own picker instead ends the immersive session on Quest and Vision Pro: the page saves the session (graph, view, selection, open clipboard page) before the picker opens, and one press on Enter VR restores it. The relay is a service graphty does not run today; its cost is under Known risks.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** In the headset's browser, open graphty, open the Florentine families sample on the flat page, and choose Enter VR. Pick up both controllers.
    - **What you see:** 16 families floating at chest height, Pucci alone at the edge with no marriages. On the off controller, a palm-size axis cross mirrors the graph's orientation; on the dominant controller, the plate, translucent and empty. A first-run line on the plate's edge, worded for what the headset is tracking, reads "Hold left grip to turn the graph. Push the plate in to slice. Press B for the menu." With hands it reads "Pinch with your left hand to turn the graph. Lay your right hand flat and push it in. Poke Menu on your left wrist.", a ghost plate sits on the open right hand, and Menu pulses once. A pinch aimed at the graph from outside it shows "Lay your hand flat inside the graph to pick". The three-lesson tour runs on the first graph loaded, on the plate's edge, with Skip.
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the off grip and turn the controller in your lap; the graph turns with it. Push the off thumbstick up to make it half again as large (hands: poke Scale + twice). Release. Then roll the plate face up beside you (or press B; hands: poke Menu).
    - **What you see:** The graph turns exactly as your hand does, so its depth reads at once. The plate flies to your off hand as the clipboard, opened on This for the graph: "16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci alone), undirected, density 0.17", and the node attributes the sample carries.
    - **Controls:** off grip (hold the graph), off thumbstick (scale), flip. Hands: off pinch held, Scale +, Menu.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Press the dominant grip to take the plate back (with hands the plate is your hand: lay it flat). Push it into the middle of the graph where the biggest node sits. The crosshair snaps to the nearest label; nudge the thumbstick once to the right onto Medici; pull the trigger. (In a large graph: B, type "Med" in the command field, press Medici; the graph turns to face it and the plate's next slice holds it.)
    - **What you see:** The plate shows the five families inside its 4 cm slab, flat and unoccluded with labels; in the graph, the slab's outline glows and those five are ringed, and nothing else changes. The crosshair hops from Ridolfi to Medici with a tick. On the trigger, Medici is outlined in the graph and on the plate, and the plate's edge reads "Medici, 6 ties -- 1 selected".
    - **Controls:** dominant grip (plate to hand), plate in the graph (slice), dominant thumbstick (nudge), trigger (select).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Press B. On This (Medici), rest the ray on Neighbors, leave the hops stepper at 1, and press Select.
    - **What you see:** While the ray rests on Neighbors, the six in-law families glow in the graph and the verb reads "1 hop: +6 families, 7 marriages among the 7". On press, Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni and Medici are selected; the subject chip becomes "Selection (7)", and its This page offers Keep as set with the suggested name "Medici in-laws".
    - **Controls:** B, ray press.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** On the clipboard, open Catalog, press Centrality, then "Influence (PageRank)". On its card, the scope chips read "Whole graph (16)" and "Selection (7)"; leave Whole graph. Press Run.
    - **What you see:** The card says "Instant. Options: damping 0.85, more ..." before you run. On Run, the clipboard switches to the run's page on Results, and the plate (when you next slice) shows each family's PageRank beside its label.
    - **Controls:** ray presses (4).

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days": Filters, Add step, Rule. The rule tree is indented rows (attribute picker, comparison, value) with And / Or / Not groups and a live count. Each number's value field has "Set with the plate": the graph lays out along that attribute, you cut, and the cut's value fills the field. The OR group is built on the clipboard; the plate only supplies values. Edited a week later from the step's page on Filters. Each plain cut kept as a filter is an AND step; cuts never make an OR on their own.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** On a run's page, Re-run with changes opens the form filled in: damping slider 0.85 (step 0.05), maximum iterations on a keypad, tolerance as a logarithmic stepper (exponent first, 1e-6, then mantissa), weight attribute picker, "More options" folded, Reset. "Keep both runs" is on by default. A number field accepts a list ("0.75, 0.85, 0.95") and makes one run per value; Stack with ... then lays the sweep in depth, and sliding the plate through the layers shows the ranking change from run to run on the same node positions.
- **Comparing two runs.** On Results, tick two runs (Louvain at resolution 1.0 and 1.5), press Compare: a difference table on the clipboard (groups that split or merged, nodes that moved) and Stack lays the two runs one behind the other with identical positions. A thin slab shows one run; sliding the plate across the boundary flips between them in place, so a node that changes group changes color under your eyes; a thick slab that holds both draws each node as a split disc on the plate.
- **Two conditions side by side.** Data, Stack networks, then "Side by side" on the stack's page: the two networks sit left and right with identical positions, and the prop turns both at once. Slicing one copy outlines the same slab in the other; picking a gene in either selects it in both, and its This page shows a two-column record (control, disease).
- **Layers on subsets.** Sweep the plate through a region with the trigger held; B; This (Selection, 23) offers Keep as set ("Set 1", rename on the keyboard) and Style this set, which adds a layer painting that set, with the generated property form (color, size, shape, outline, label ...). Layers rows drag to reorder; This on any node shows "why this look" as the layers that painted it.
- **Recipes.** On History, tick the steps (Run PageRank, Add layer, Add filter), Save as recipe with the suggested name "PageRank and strong families". The recipe lives in the app's library, not the project, so it survives closing the project and opening another dataset. Every node, set and attribute a ticked step used becomes an input slot: a node slot is filled by a plate pick, the command field or an id pasted from the laptop; a set slot by a kept set or the current selection; an attribute slot by a picker. On another graph, Catalog, Recipes family, the recipe's card lists its inputs and the count it will change; Run stays disabled until each required input is filled or skipped, and a replayed step whose count falls to 0 turns amber and names the input it came from. On any node's or set's This, "Run recipe from here" fills the first slot of its kind, so an investigation saved once replays from a new flagged account in about 4 acts. Run replays as named steps you can undo one by one.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Data, Add a table. The file comes from the Inbox (dropped on the paired laptop page), or from the headset browser's file picker, which reaches the headset's own storage and any cloud drive signed in there, with the session saved and restored around it. The clipboard shows the file's columns transposed: one row per column of the file, with its inferred type, three sample values and a role picker (node id, source, target, attribute, skip), so a 40-column file is a 40-row list scrolled by the thumbstick, not a wide table. Pick the key column; the match count reads "14 of 16 nodes matched, 2 rows unmatched" before Join. Import mapping and the load report use the same transposed list.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers a descriptor: plain name, category, options, result fields. It appears in its family on Catalog (or a new family row), in the command field and in the assistant's list. Its options render as a generated form on its card; its result fields pick their readers on its run page. Each numeric field also becomes an axis the plate can cut along, and each group field a stack the plate can slice, with no new controls. The hard case from the checklist -- a "Biology: enrichment" family whose algorithm takes a gene list and two numbers -- gets a new family row; the gene list is a "nodes" input filled from the current selection (a plate sweep), a kept set, or a list typed or pasted on the 2D page; the numbers are sliders.
- **A new layout.** It joins the Layouts section with its engine choice and generated form; an iterative layout runs live, and the plate keeps slicing while it moves (the slab's content updates each frame).
- **A new object type.** It declares its verbs and fields; This renders its record and verbs for it. If it has a place in the graph (an annotation, a path), the plate can pick it; if not (a saved query, a report), it declares which existing tab lists it (Results by default) and is picked there; it never adds a tab. It can be noted, because a note's target is any object.
- **A third-party plugin of another kind.** A reader joins Data's format list with its options form; a writer joins Project's export list; a palette joins the palette strips; a camera preset joins Views. Nothing in the plate or the prop changes.
- **What graphty-element must add for the plate to work.** Laying out along an attribute (as a layout any consumer can use), the slab outline and optional dim (one plane and a thickness as a shader uniform in the node, edge and label materials, not per-node style changes, so it is cheap and never enters the layer list or undo), a highlight mask for previews of thousands of elements, stacked and side-by-side copies of the graph with shared positions, and two networks in one project (already planned). The laptop link's relay is a service the app needs; nothing else here needs one.
- **The practical limit.** The clipboard is an ordinary 30 x 22 cm panel with about 12 rows of large text: a 13-option form is two screens, a table shows 6 columns at a time, and every command that is not a pick or a cut is a press on a list, at hand-held-panel speed. The plate earns its place only when the graph has spatial structure: at 10,000 nodes a 2 cm slab still holds hundreds, so you scale the graph up first (which dims nothing, so it costs no overview), and a graph whose layout means nothing gains little from slicing except declutter. The rail holds 11 tabs; new features join tabs, never add them.

### Why it should work

- The exact pairing has been built and tested: Hinckley, Pausch, Goble and Kassell, "Passive real-world interface props for neurosurgical visualization" (CHI 1994), gave surgeons a doll's head to hold the brain's orientation and a plate to set a cutting plane through it; surgeons used it with about a minute of instruction, because the relation of two hands to two objects was obvious.
- Two hands with asymmetric jobs match how people already work: Guiard's kinematic chain ("Asymmetric division of labor in human skilled bimanual action", Journal of Motor Behavior, 1987) -- the non-dominant hand sets the frame (the prop holds the graph), the dominant hand works finely inside it (the plate picks).
- Slicing is an established answer to 3D occlusion: Elmqvist and Tsigas's taxonomy of occlusion management (IEEE TVCG 2008) lists cut planes and volumetric probes among its families; Coffey et al.'s Slice WIM (IEEE TVCG 2012) showed a slice of a 3D dataset on a 2D surface for picking and reading. Medical VR ships this today: SlicerVR (Pinter et al., IEEE Transactions on Medical Robotics and Bionics, 2020) gives 3D Slicer users hand-held slicing in a headset.
- Precise selection in clutter benefits from moving candidates onto a flat, spread layout: SQUAD (Kopper, Bacim and Bowman, IEEE 3DUI 2011) beat ray casting in dense scenes by refining on a 2D surface. The plate does that without leaving the graph.
- Hand-held panels are fast when they are held: Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn ("Hand-held windows", IEEE VR 1999; "Towards usable VR", CHI 1999) found a panel held in the non-dominant hand with passive feedback faster and more accurate than a floating one. Tilt Brush (Google, 2016) taught millions the off-hand palette and dominant-hand tool pairing.
- Time as depth works in immersive analytics: Wagner Filho, Stuerzlinger and Nedel's immersive space-time cube (IEEE TVCG 2019, arxiv.org/pdf/1908.00580) found people read trajectories in a stacked space-time view with little training.
- Tangible axes for setting ranges have been tested on data: Cordeil et al., "Embodied Axes" (CHI 2020), let people set ranges by sliding physical handles along axes of a 3D scatterplot; the plate cut is the same act with no extra hardware.
- Word-gesture typing makes the clipboard keyboard tolerable: Chen et al. (CHI 2019 Extended Abstracts) measured about 16 words per minute in VR without training.

### What it would teach us

- Does a 1:1 prop beat two-hand grab and turntables for orientation in a node-link graph, and does it keep arms lower and less tired?
- How thick should the slab be for 16, 400 and 10,000 nodes, and does the flattened slice actually make multi-select of 12 scattered nodes faster than a ray (the checklist's 12 to 21 seconds per node)?
- Is cutting a graph laid out along an attribute a better way to set a threshold than a slider, and do people understand that the axis view is temporary?
- Do stacks (time, sweeps, two runs, two networks) read as one idea, and does sliding through them beat a timeline scrubber for spotting change?
- Do people mind that most commands are on an ordinary panel, or does carrying the object you just picked onto the clipboard make the panel feel object-first? Do they flip, or just press B?
- How reliable is the hands-only version with only two poses: palm plane jitter, pinch-held rotation under the roll cap, and how much slower scale and slab are as wrist-strip keys than as a thumbstick?
- Does framing a picture with the plate answer "which view do I export" for people preparing figures?

### Known risks

- **Arm fatigue from holding the plate in the graph.** Mitigation: the Seated setting puts the graph's home at elbow height about 45 cm away and pins the clipboard in front, below eye level; carry a slice out of the graph to read it with the elbow resting; most work after a pick happens on the pinned clipboard. What remains is the idea's own cost: slicing is an arm-forward act, and a careful cut like step 8's is 5 to 15 seconds with the arm out.
- **The clipboard does most of the work.** Most of the 22 areas are presses on an ordinary panel, so the scheme's novelty covers a minority of acts. Mitigation: own it; make the clipboard a good panel (generated forms, the command field, transposed tables, fingertip pressing), and measure the plate where it is meant to win: dense selection, thresholds, time.
- **Two-stance confusion.** A plate tilted face up in the graph to slice from above could be read as a flip. Mitigation: a flip needs the plate outside the graph's bounds, face up within 30 degrees, held 0.3 s; inside the graph any angle slices; B opens the clipboard with no pose at all, which also covers a graph scaled so large that nothing is outside it; and because slicing dims nothing, a plate that is always inside a surrounding graph costs no overview.
- **A quick select that replaces a careful selection.** Mitigation: selection changes are undo steps, Previous selection is on This, and A toggles without replacing; with hands the pinch only toggles, so a misread pinch adds or removes one node and never replaces a selection.
- **Overlap and clutter inside a thick slab.** Two nodes at different depths project to the same spot, and a slab of hundreds is unreadable. Mitigation: the plate fans overlapping discs apart slightly, labels only the crosshair's node and its 12 nearest, the thumbstick nudge visits each in turn, past 60 nodes it hints to thin the slab, and magnify shows every label.
- **Tight thresholds.** Values a few thousandths apart sit millimeters apart on the axis. Mitigation: the plate snaps into gaps between nodes and to round values, slow hand motion gives fine gain, the ruler names the value, and the cut's value can be typed on the clipboard.
- **Losing your place in the axis view or a stack.** The layout changes under you. Mitigation: these are views, not steps, and animate both ways (cut, not animate, with reduce motion); off thumbstick click always returns to the normal layout before it ever fits.
- **Rendering cost of stacks and the slab.** A stack is several copies of the graph, and fading everything outside the slab each frame would be ruinous if done per node. Mitigation: a stack draws full layers only within a fixed budget of about 40,000 node instances (at 10,000 nodes, the plate's layer and its two neighbors), the rest as one point cloud, and stacks only what the filters show, so a fraud analyst's two years of windows stack over her filtered case graph, not the whole transaction graph; the slab outline is one plane and thickness in the element's renderer, not a style change; previews of more than a few thousand elements draw as a highlight mask, not a restyle.
- **It depends on element features that do not exist.** Laying out along an attribute, stacked and side-by-side copies, the slab focus and two networks per project. Mitigation: they belong in graphty-element, where every consumer gets them; until then the plate still selects, and thresholds and time fall back to clipboard fields.
- **Text and files.** The clipboard keyboard is slow, and files live on the laptop. Mitigation: suggested names everywhere, completions in the command field, formulas from chips, word-gesture typing, fingertip typing with hands, dictation where a recognizer exists, keys entered on the 2D page before entering VR, and the laptop link for files, ids, keys and long text. The link needs a relay service on graphty's origin that does not exist; until it is built, files come only through the headset's picker and leave only to its downloads, and stress journeys 3 and 5 stop at their first file.
- **Hand-tracking quality for the stand-ins.** A flat hand wobbles and a pinch can be missed. Mitigation: only two poses (pinch and flat hand), every other hand verb a labeled key; read rotation from the wrist, freeze the plate's orientation while pinched, lock the slab depth after a rest, pick the target from before the pinch, widen the crosshair snap, keep the two hands apart except for a fingertip on the wrist strip; controllers are the primary input wherever they exist. The cost: with hands, scale and slab are key pokes, slower than a thumbstick.
- **Vision Pro without hand permission.** No prop and no plate. Mitigation: ask for permission at Enter VR with the reason; fall back to the clipboard alone, driven by gaze and pinch.
- **Left-handed users, limited wrist range and one-handed use.** The scheme needs two hands and some wrist roll. Mitigation: handedness swaps every mapping; rotation gain reduces wrist travel; B replaces the flip; one-handed mode makes the dominant grip held outside the graph the prop (hands: a pinch on the Turn handle of the pinned clipboard), pins the clipboard in front with its tabs visible, and keeps scale, slab, home, stack, undo and redo on its top bar, while the plate still slices; a one-handed hands user opens any page by poking the pinned clipboard.
- **What cannot be fixed without abandoning the idea.** Slicing needs the dominant arm in the graph; the plate only beats a ray where the layout has spatial meaning; most commands remain presses on an ordinary panel; and on Vision Pro without controllers, the hands scheme still asks for a held pinch and a held flat hand, plus keys, where a pure panel scheme asks only for gaze and pinch.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: with hands there are now two poses, a pinch and a flat hand. The controller mapping is unchanged.

- The off hand only pinches, to hold the graph, and the hold's roll is capped before the palm faces the head.
- The dominant hand is the plate when flat, and with hands a pinch on the plate only toggles a node in or out; it never replaces the selection (Clear selection is on This and the top bar).
- Undo, Redo, Scale, Slab, Home, Magnify and Menu became a labeled wrist strip on the off forearm, poked by the dominant fingertip. The same keys sit on the clipboard's top bar in every mode and on every device.
- On Vision Pro, transient selects from the off hand are always dropped, so the clipboard is pressed only by the dominant fingertip or a dominant gaze-pinch.
- Gone with hands: the loose fist, the side-of-index Undo tap, middle-finger pinches, the double pinch, the pinch dragged on two axes and the fist held 0.5 s.

Severity 3 findings:

- **The graph-holding fist contains the Undo tap, so turning the graph can silently undo work** (onboarding, VR interaction, WebXR; journey steps 2, 3 and every orbit). Fixed by the two-pose mapping: holding is a pinch, and Undo is a key. Undo now names its step on the plate's edge and the axis cross as well as the top bar.
- **The loose fist is a natural rest pose, so a relaxed hand moves the graph** (VR interaction, red team). Fixed. Holding needs a deliberate pinch, a ring on the axis cross shows "holding", and every hold updates Back to the previous view, so an accidental hold is one press to reverse.
- **Too many similar pinches; a toggle read as a select replaces a 12-pick selection** (VR interaction, WebXR, red team). Fixed. There are two poses, the hands pick only toggles, and every other hands verb is a labeled key.
- **On Vision Pro, off-hand pinches also press whatever clipboard row the eyes rest on** (VR interaction, WebXR). Fixed. Off-hand transient selects are always dropped.
- **The pinch that replaces the fist meets the palm-up system-menu pinch** (red team, VR interaction, WebXR). Fixed. A pinch begun palm-to-face is ignored, and a hold's roll stops counting before the palm faces the head; the rotation gain (1.5x by default with hands) covers the rest.
- **A scaled-up or close-up graph is always dimmed, because the plate is always inside it** (red team; areas 5 and 6, stress journey 6 step 1, a 10,000-account graph). Fixed. Slicing no longer dims the rest of the graph. The slab is drawn as two thin outlines with its nodes ringed, and "Dim outside" is an optional setting and plate-edge key. With hands, the plate also needs a flat hand held still 0.2 s, and never forms within 10 cm of the clipboard. This also states the rule for two fades: the dim is a view effect, never in a picture, an export or undo, while a "fade others" layer is a style layer and is recorded.
- **The axis view has no labeled exit and no hint** (onboarding; journey step 8). Fixed. A "Back to layout" chip appears on the plate's edge and the top bar whenever an axis view or stack is open, with a first-time hint when the axis view opens.
- **The first-run hint and tour are worded for controllers; with hands there is no visible plate** (onboarding; stress journey 1, journey step 3). Fixed. The hints are worded for the tracked input, a ghost plate is drawn on the open dominant hand, a pinch aimed at the graph from outside it gets a hint, and the tour runs on the first loaded graph with Skip.
- **The clipboard is held up on the off arm most of the session, and its parked place is the lap** (ergonomics; journey steps 2 and 4 to 11). Fixed. When seated or one-handed, the clipboard is pinned by default in front, 15 to 25 degrees below eye level and about 50 cm away, and it can be dragged anywhere. The Seated setting also moves the graph's home to elbow height about 45 cm away.
- **A one-handed hand-tracking user cannot open the clipboard** (ergonomics). Fixed. In one-handed mode the clipboard is always pinned with its tabs visible, and a Turn handle on its corner holds the graph.
- **Stacks of a 10,000-node graph exceed the Quest frame budget** (WebXR; area 15, the expert's time stack at the fraud analyst's scale). Fixed:
    - A stack draws full layers only within a fixed budget of about 40,000 node instances. At 10,000 nodes that is the plate's layer and its two neighbors; the rest is one point cloud.
    - A stack holds only what the filters show. Above 10,000 shown nodes, Stack asks you to filter first. The analyst's 24 monthly windows stack over her filtered case graph.
- **Files cannot move between the laptop and the headset** (coverage, red team; areas 1, 14, 16, 19 and 21, stress journeys 3, 5 and 6). Fixed by specifying the laptop link, a 6-digit pairing code shown on Data and Project:
    - The Inbox receives files, ids, the assistant's key and long text.
    - The Outbox is the default destination of every export.
    - Using the headset's own file picker saves the session first and restores it with one press.
    - The relay behind the link is a service graphty does not run; Known risks says so and says what stops until it exists.
- **Recipes cannot target a node or a set, so a fraud investigation cannot be replayed from a new account and "Ring triage" replays onto a missing set** (expert, red team; stress journeys 2 and 6). Fixed:
    - Recipes live in the app's library, not the project.
    - Every node, set and attribute a step used becomes an input slot.
    - Replay stays disabled until each input is filled or skipped, and a step whose count falls to 0 turns amber and names its input.
    - "Run recipe from here" on any node's or set's This replays an investigation from a new account in about 4 acts.
