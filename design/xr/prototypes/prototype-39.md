**Prototype 39: Phone in Hand** -- Your own phone, lying on your lap (or held in the off hand), runs graphty's panels as a paired web page; the headset draws it as a slab where the phone is, with the touch point on it and an enlarged reading copy near the graph, so every list, form, value and word is a touch on real glass you can feel, while the dominant hand points at the graph. While you are in VR, everything on the phone is drawn by graphty's page, never by the phone's operating system, so the headset can show all of it; a laptop can join the same pairing to carry files in and out.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro, each with the person's own phone (iPhone or Android) paired to the session. The default placement is the phone lying on the lap (or a desk), the slab drawn at the spot set at pairing; holding the phone in the off hand is an option in Settings. The dominant hand points (tracked hand, controller ray, or on Vision Pro look and pinch); the off hand's finger touches the phone. The hand set as the phone hand never points or selects on any device, and with the phone on the lap any hand whose fingertip is within 3 cm of the slab is not a pointer. Text is graphty's own keyboard on the phone page, with a page-drawn Dictate button that uses the phone browser's speech recognition as the main way to enter anything longer than a name. Eyes are used only where the headset's own look-and-pinch already uses them (Vision Pro). Voice inside the headset is not used. Everything the dominant hand does also has a phone-only path, so the scheme works one-handed. Optionally, a laptop opens graphty.app/pair and joins the same session as a third device that carries files and pasted text in and out (see "The laptop"). Without a phone, a drawn slab with poke touch shows the same page, slower.

**One rule while in VR: nothing on the phone is drawn by its operating system.** In full VR the person never sees the real phone, only the slab, and the slab can draw only what graphty's page draws. So while the headset is in VR the paired page uses no native keyboard (every field is page-drawn with `inputmode="none"`, no native text input), no native select wheel, no native date picker, no file picker, no share sheet, no autofill and no clipboard callout. Anything that would need one is either done by a page-drawn control, carried by the laptop, or put on the phone's "When you take the headset off" list, which runs with the phone's own controls once you leave VR (the headset browser is then in passthrough, where the real phone can be seen). The price is stated plainly: inside VR there is no phone autocorrect, no swipe typing, no password manager, no phone file picker and no share sheet. What remains is real glass under the finger, the phone's speech recognition, its orientation sensor, and every control graphty draws.

The headset does not stream a video of the phone. The phone page reports its visible layout (boxes, text, control states, scroll offset) to the headset through the pairing, and sends small pictures for what is not text (chart canvases, palette swatches, icons); the headset draws those boxes, texts and pictures. It never lays the phone page out again, so the slab and the phone cannot disagree about where a control is. (Show wide and the pinned copies are laid out by the headset from the same reported data; they are read and pointed at, never touched, so they cannot misaim a touch.) Because the rule above keeps every operating-system surface off the phone in VR, there is nothing on the phone the slab cannot draw.

### When the phone stops being graphty

The phone is still a phone. The page reports `visibilitychange` and `pagehide`, and the headset and phone exchange a heartbeat twice a second over the pairing. When the page is hidden, the tab is suspended, the phone locks, or the heartbeat stops:

- the slab grays out at once with "Phone is not on graphty", and the headset drops every touch until the page is showing again; the reading copy freezes with the same label;
- a banner at the graph's top edge reads "Phone away -- your work is saved" (the headset holds the graph and autosaves; nothing on the phone is lost because the phone holds no state);
- when the page returns, or iOS reloads the tab, it rejoins the session without a code (the pairing is remembered on both devices) and the slab comes back on the page you were on.

What a web page cannot do, stated plainly: it cannot stop an incoming call from taking the screen, and on iOS a call that arrives as a banner over the page does not hide the page, so a blind tap at the top of the slab could answer or decline it. That is why search and Back moved to the bottom bar and the top of the page holds only the status strip, and why pairing asks the person to choose a focus mode that silences calls, not only notifications (the page cannot check that they did). A phone locked by a brushed side button needs the passcode to unlock, which in VR means a peek; the wake lock keeps it from locking on its own.

### When you take the headset off

A list on the Project tab, with its count in the phone's status strip and in the headset banner, holding whatever needs the phone's own operating system: "Share 'Case 2026-114.pdf'", "Open a file from this phone", "Paste from the phone clipboard", "Save the provider key from the password manager". Each item is added by a button that would otherwise have opened an OS surface ("Open a file from this phone" on the Data tab, "Share to ..." on an export). Leave VR is one tap on the list; the headset browser goes to passthrough, the phone shows the list with the phone's own pickers and share sheet working normally, and Enter VR in the headset brings you back to the same page. With a laptop connected, most items are never needed.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** With the headset on and passthrough still showing the room, open graphty in the headset browser and choose the Florentine families sample. The page shows "Pair a phone: graphty.app/pair, code 482 917" in large type. On your phone (or from the link you sent yourself earlier with "Send pairing link to my phone" on the 2D page), open that address and type the six digits. The phone asks once for the motion sensor and says "Choose a focus mode that silences calls"; tap Allow. Lay the phone on your lap. The phone says "Now press Enter VR in the headset"; pinch Enter VR in the headset page.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families floating at chest height. At your lap, a slab with the phone's page on it and a single target that says "Touch here". You touch it; the slab snaps under your real fingertip. Below the graph, the reading copy shows the Object tab for the graph. The tour starts: it has you point at one family, tap a row body and a chevron, turn the graph once on View, press Dictate once and say anything, and Undo once (about 2 minutes, Skip at the top). Next time, the two devices remember each other: opening graphty.app on the phone joins the headset's session without a code.
    - **Controls:** the code (6 digits, in passthrough), one tap (Allow), pinch Enter VR in the headset, one tap (line up).

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Read the graph's subject card in the reading copy. Tap View; drag one finger across the trackpad to turn the graph; pinch out a little. Hold Tilt and turn the phone a quarter turn, then let go.
    - **What you see:** The card reads "Florentine families: 16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci has no marriages), undirected", with the node attributes listed below. The graph turns under your finger like a map; while Tilt is held it turns with the phone, "Tilt: turning" shows beside the graph, and it stops when you let go. Labels show on the largest nodes. If you reach out and pinch the air to grab the graph, nothing happens and a hint at your hand reads "Turn the graph on the phone: View".
    - **Controls:** tap (tab), one-finger drag, pinch, Tilt.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Point at the node labeled Medici with the dominant hand; the ray tip reads "Medici -- Open"; pinch. (If you could not see it: tap Find, type "me"; the suggestion strip shows Medici; tap it.)
    - **What you see:** Medici gets the subject outline. The phone pushes Medici's page: "Medici -- 6 marriages, PageRank not yet computed", followed by the attributes the sample carries, and verb sections: Select, Neighbors, Paths, Pin, Move, Hide, Style, Note, Inspect all.
    - **Controls:** ray and pinch (or Find, two keys and one tap).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** On Medici's page, tap Neighbors. The verb page shows a hops stepper at 1. Tap Select.
    - **What you see:** While the page is open, the six neighbors glow as a ghost preview and the Select button reads "Select 1 hop: 7 families, 7 marriages". On tap, Medici and Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni are selected, the phone pushes the "Selection (7)" page, and it offers Keep as set, Add by pointing, Run on these, Style these, Filter to these.
    - **Controls:** tap, tap.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Tap Analyze, type "pa" on graphty's keyboard in its search, tap "Influence (PageRank)" in the suggestion strip. On the form page, Run on is a two-way switch, Whole graph (16) or Selection (7); leave it on Whole graph. Damping shows 0.85. Tap Run.
    - **What you see:** Every key you touch highlights on the slab and in the reading copy before you lift. The form page shows the plain name "Influence (PageRank)", one line on what it measures, the cost line "under a second", damping as a slider with its value field, and Advanced folded (iterations, tolerance, weight attribute). On Run the headset gives a short tone. If your finger is on the phone when the result is ready, a banner says "PageRank finished -- Open" instead of replacing the page under your finger; otherwise the phone pushes the result page.
    - **Controls:** tap, keyboard (2 letters), tap, tap.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read the ranking in the reading copy below the graph. Swipe up on the list for the next page. Tap the body of the Strozzi row.
    - **What you see:** The ranking, three decimals, large: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.078, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi 0.069, Castellani 0.069, Bischeri 0.068, Peruzzi 0.067 ... 7. **Color and size by PageRank.**

    - **What you see:** Each key highlights before it commits. After Add step, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi, Albizzi and Tornabuoni remain, the Filter tab carries one step "PageRank at least 0.070", and the graph's subject card reads "Showing 5 of 16".

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Point at Medici and pinch. On its page tap Note. Tap Dictate (the wide key at the bottom left of graphty's keyboard) and say "Married into six families; the hub of the network." Tap the word "hub" in the field; type "center". Tap Done.

Tapping "hub" shows it highlighted at reading size before you lift, then selected; typing replaces it. The bottom bar with Undo sits just above the keyboard. Corrections inside the field have their own field undo on the keyboard; the note becomes one named step, "Add note to Medici", on Done. In the graph, a note marker appears on Medici; Medici's page lists the note.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** In the Filter tab you mean to open the PageRank step to look at it, but your finger rests on the step's on/off switch at the row's right edge while you watch the graph, and you lift there. The graph springs back to 16 families. Tap Undo in the bottom bar.

On tap, the filter is back on and the graph shows 5 of 16 again; Redo now reads "Redo: Turn off filter". Your note, your two layers and your run are untouched.

11. **Save.**

    - **What you see:** "Saved Florentine families" on the phone and a short tone; the History tab marks the save. ### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Filter tab, New step, Rule. The rule builder is the desktop's at phone width: one clause per row (attribute menu, comparison menu, value field with graphty's keypad or the page-drawn calendar), and an "Add group" row that nests an OR group. Structural measures (degree, pieces) are offered in the attribute menu even before they are computed, marked "computed when used". "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" takes about 18 taps and two typed numbers, with "keeps 212 of 9,480" updating live above the Add button. A week later, tap the step's chevron in the Filter tab: the same form opens with every clause editable; Save as named filter takes a typed name and a dictated 200-character description.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** On a run's result page, tap Run again with changes: the same form opens with that run's values. Expand Advanced; type 1e-6 into tolerance on the keypad; Reset puts back defaults. For a sweep, every number field in every generated form has a Sweep item in its field menu: type "0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0" into Louvain's resolution, and Run makes four runs, each kept in the runs list with its options in its name. Because Sweep belongs to the number field, not to Louvain, a plugin's number option sweeps the same way.
- **Picking scattered nodes.** On the Selection page, tap Add by pointing: the field is focused and glows on the slab, and the ray tip reads "Add" or "Remove" for the node it is on. Each pinch on a node adds it; a pinch takes a node out only when the ray is exactly on it, never through the snap. The count updates, and nothing else changes subject until you tap Done. Twelve scattered nodes are twelve pinches and two taps; when they are too small to hit, the field also takes typed names (completed from node ids) or a lasso on the flat view.
- **Comparing two runs.** Analyze tab, Runs: tick two runs, tap Compare. The phone shows a table of who moved (family, rank in A, rank in B, change), sorted by the size of the change, and "3 families change rank"; Show wide puts the full table below the graph at reading size. In the graph, families that moved carry the outline; tapping a row body frames one. For two community runs, the table lists groups against groups with overlap counts.
- **Layers on subsets.** View tab, flat view: draw a lasso with a fingertip around the six in-laws (the flat drawing is the graph as you see it now). The result line reads "6 inside, 1 behind others", with a Front only switch; toggle Add or Subtract for corrections; the selection page offers Keep as set, typed "In-laws". Style tab, New layer, Paints: a menu of sets, rules and result groups; pick In-laws; set a thick outline. Drag the layer by its handle above "Color: PageRank" in the list. Why this look: point at a node, its page, "Painted by": the layers that touched it, in order.
- **Recipes.** History tab: slide down the check circles of the steps to keep (the run, the paint, the filter), tap Save as recipe, type "Strong families". On another dataset: History, Recipes, the recipe, Replay: a form asks for each input the recipe needs ("score attribute: PageRank will be computed", "threshold: 0.07") and shows what it will change before Run.
- **Undoing one old step.** History tab: each entry is named with its parameters ("Filter: PageRank at least 0.070", "Move Medici", "Merge 5 into ACME Corp"). Its chevron pushes the page of what that step made (the filter step, the layer, the run, the note) with that object's own off and delete verbs. Revert only this step undoes that one step and keeps every later one: the graph shows the result as a ghost, and the button reads "Revert 'Move Medici', keep 7 later steps". If a later step depends on it (a run on the merged node, a layer painting a removed set), the button lists those steps by name and offers "Revert this and these 2" or Cancel; it never drops a later step silently.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Data tab, Laptop: the shared folder's files, newest first; tap the CSV that was just exported to Downloads. It travels to the headset over the pairing (a progress bar with Cancel). The import page shows the first rows at phone width with one card per column (role menu: node id, source, target, attribute, skip; type menu), the key column picker, and "14 of 16 rows matched node names" before Add; Show wide shows the same mapping as a wide table. The load report is a list of issues, each with its fix as a button. A file that is only on the phone goes to "When you take the headset off: Open a file from this phone".
- **One-handed, seated.** The phone lies on the lap; one hand does everything. Pointing is a tap on the flat view (pinch-zoom it first on a dense graph; the loupe names the node under the finger before you lift); moving a node is its page's Move verb, after which dragging on the flat view moves that node in the view plane (Done ends it); navigation is the trackpad. The dominant hand is never required.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers its descriptor; it appears in its family in the Analyze tab, in search under both names, and its form page is generated from its option list (number, integer, logarithmic, attribute, node, choice, toggle, seed), Advanced folded, Sweep on its numbers. Its result page picks readers from its result fields, and Insert parameters works on its runs because the how-made line comes from the descriptor. Nothing in the headset changes.
- **A new layout.** It joins the layout list; its options form is generated; a node option becomes a node field filled by pointing or typing.
- **A new object type.** It brings a page template: a subject card (its key facts from its descriptor), its verb sections, and its place in search. Its descriptor names the list it belongs in; without one it is listed under Object, More objects. If it exists in the scene (a callout, a time slice), pointing at it opens its page, and its page's Move verb moves it the way node Move does.
- **A new option kind.** A kind the generic form does not know (a gene list, a color ramp) needs one new mobile field, once, drawn by the page like every other field, and then every descriptor that uses it gets it; until then it falls back to a text field.
- **A third-party plugin.** It gets exactly what the desktop app gets, because the phone page is the desktop's generated panels at phone width. A plugin family "Biology: enrichment" is a new section in the Analyze tab; its gene-list option is a text field filled from the laptop's Paste box or a file; its two number options are ordinary number fields.
- **The practical limits.** Width, not count: any number of entries fits, because lists scroll and search; but anything that needs many columns side by side (a 40-column mapping, a 6-metric ranking, a difference table of two networks) is slower on 7 cm of glass and leans on Show wide. A plugin reader that draws its own canvas reaches the headset only as a picture, with no rows to point at and no text to enlarge; a reader built from graphty's table and list parts gets those for free. The plumbing is the third limit: a pairing service, a page that draws its own keyboard and pickers, and the laptop page, none of which graphty has yet.

### Why it should work

- A held or resting physical surface with the dominant hand acting beside it is old and well tested in VR: the Personal Interaction Panel (Szalavari and Gervautz, Computer Graphics Forum 1997) and Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn's hand-held windows (IEEE VR 1999), who found that a real physical paddle under a virtual panel made 2D work in VR faster and more accurate than a floating panel with nothing to touch. This is the argument that survives the one rule above: the controls are drawn by graphty, but the finger lands on real glass, with millimeter touch accuracy and real contact, which a drawn slab with poke touch does not give.
- Phones specifically: TrackCap (Mohr et al., CHI 2019, dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3290605.3300815) tracked a phone with the headset for 3D interaction; BISHARE (Zhu and Grossman, CHI 2020) mapped a design space of phone and head-mounted display working together; SmartVR Pointer (Sensors 2024, doi 10.3390/s24165168) used an ordinary smartphone as a VR pointer and touch input.
- A web control page beside an immersive view is shipped in network biology: VRNetzer (Pirch et al., Nature Communications 2021) keeps its data handling in a web interface that drives the VR network view. Open Brush's API (docs.openbrush.app/user-guide/open-brush-api) shows a running VR app driven by commands from another device.
- Seeing your hands over a real input device restores typing in VR: Knierim et al. (CHI 2018) and Grubert et al. (IEEE VR 2018) measured typing on a physical keyboard in VR, with hand rendering bringing performance close to normal. Here the touch dot and the highlighted key, drawn from the page's own layout, play that role over the phone.
- Lift-to-commit at the final touch point is shipped accessibility: iOS Touch Accommodations, "Use Final Touch Location", lets a person land anywhere and slide to the target before lifting.
- Speech recognition is available on the phone's browser and not in Quest Browser, so the phone gives this scheme sentence entry that the headset alone cannot. That, not the phone's own keyboard, is what carries long text.
- What this design does not claim any more: that the phone's own keyboard, autocorrect, password manager, file picker and share sheet work in VR. They do not, because the person cannot see them. They come back the moment the headset is off, which is what "When you take the headset off" uses.

### What it would teach us

- Can people touch a screen they cannot see? Accuracy and time per tap with the touch dot and lift-to-commit, on the lap and in the hand, against the same page seen through passthrough.
- Words per minute on graphty's keyboard aimed at the slab, against Dictate, and how often people use the id completion.
- How often do people look down at the slab, and does the body-fixed reading copy below the graph remove most of those glances?
- How often does the phone stop being graphty in a 30-minute session (calls, locks, app switches), and how long does recovery take?
- How many items reach "When you take the headset off" in a real session, with and without a laptop?
- Is "point with one hand, touch with the other" faster than drawn panels (Hand Menu and Windows) for the basic journey and for the text-heavy stress journeys (fraud ring ids, evidence notes, the report)?
- Does the phone pull attention away from the graph so much that people stop looking at it, which would defeat the headset?
- Do people understand the one pointing rule once the ray tip names it, or do they still point expecting the other?
- Is the pairing step acceptable to a first-time user, and how often does the connection drop?

### Known risks

- **Aiming at a screen you cannot see.** In full VR the phone is only a drawn copy. Mitigation: touch-highlight-lift, so a touch never commits where it lands, only where it lifts; switches and destructive buttons need their highlight shown for 150 ms; 12 mm targets; every real touch re-aligning the slab; lap placement by default. This is the first thing to measure.
- **Typing is slower than on a phone you can see.** graphty's keyboard has no autocorrect and every key is aimed at the slab: about 10 to 15 words a minute. Mitigation: Dictate for anything longer than a name, completion from the graph's ids and names, Insert parameters and Copy and Insert for values, the laptop's Paste box. A person who cannot or will not speak writes long text slowly. Not fixable inside the idea without seeing the real phone.
- **The phone's own conveniences are gone in VR.** No autocorrect, swipe typing, password manager, file picker or share sheet while the headset is on. This is the price of the one rule, not a gap that will close; the laptop and "When you take the headset off" are the substitutes.
- **The phone behaves like a phone.** A call, an app switch or a lock takes the page away. Mitigation: the slab grays and drops touches the moment the page is hidden or the heartbeat stops, the graph says so, and the pairing resumes without a code. Not fixable from a web page: an iOS call banner that does not hide the page can still be tapped blind, and a locked phone needs a peek to unlock. The pairing asks for a focus mode that silences calls.
- **No phone tracking.** Quest and Vision Pro cannot see the phone. Mitigation: lap placement by default, re-aligned from the touching fingertip on every touch where hands are tracked, Realign on the View tab; held placement follows the off hand's wrist with the yaw offset of the phone's orientation sensor calibrated at line-up.
- **Quest's system gesture.** A phone gripped palm toward the face, with the thumb near the index, opens Quest's system menu, which the page cannot prevent. Mitigation: the lap is the default; held placement says so in Settings.
- **The pairing service does not exist, and graphty.app is a static site.** Two browsers cannot find each other without a small service to introduce them. Mitigation: a tiny pairing service (it only passes the connection offer for a code); after that, a peer-to-peer data channel so the graph and files stay on the local network. On networks that block devices from reaching each other (guest and some office Wi-Fi), traffic goes through a relay instead, the phone says so at pairing, and the touch dot lags 50 to 150 ms; the 150 ms rule above keeps a quick lift on a switch from committing before its highlight is seen. A relay costs bandwidth and money to run.
- **The engineering is not "mostly reuse".** It needs a pairing service and relay, a layout reporter on the phone and a renderer in the headset, a sync protocol so the phone's panels can read and command the headset's graphty-element, a page-drawn keyboard, keypad, menus and calendar, the laptop page, and the desktop's panels at phone width, which do not exist yet. None is a browser limit.
- **Live previews at 10,000 nodes.** Mitigation: previews update a few times a second while a slider moves and fully on release; counts are computed off the render loop; the ghost is one overlay, not a restyle of every node; side by side caps detail on Quest 3S.
- **Two renderings of one page drift apart.** Mitigation: the phone reports its actual boxes, text, control states and scroll offset, plus small pictures of charts and swatches, so the headset draws what the phone drew rather than laying out the page again.
- **Accidental touches.** Mitigation: touches that begin while the phone is moving fast are dropped; any page push cancels a touch in progress, and results arriving during a touch show a banner instead of replacing the page; lift-to-commit lets a wrong landing slide away; confirm sheets with counts on anything destructive; named Undo one tap away, as in step 10.
- **Reading at phone width.** Mitigation: the reading copy, pinned copies, Show wide for wide tables and charts, and a text size setting for all of them.
- **Attention split and neck strain.** Mitigation: the reading copy sits just below the graph, fixed to the body; Show wide opens centered and scaled to fit; ghost previews and counts on the graph while a value changes, so you watch the graph while the finger works.
- **Not everyone has or may carry a phone.** Phones are banned where classified data is handled, and the relay may not be allowed there either. Mitigation: the drawn slab with poke touch (slower, and text goes to graphty's keyboard on the slab); a laptop or tablet can be the paired touch device in place of a phone; a local-network-only pairing that refuses the relay.
- **Pointing rule surprise.** Mitigation: the ray tip names what the next pinch will do; a focused field glows on the slab and in the reading copy; filling is one Undo; Back or tapping outside the field unfocuses it.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: while the headset is in VR, nothing on the phone is drawn by its operating system, and a laptop joins the same pairing as the third device for files.

- Every field is page-drawn with `inputmode="none"`: graphty's keyboard and keypad with lift-to-commit keys, a page-drawn Dictate button as the main path for anything longer than a name, page-drawn menus and a calendar, tap-a-word and arrow keys in place of the OS caret loupe, Copy and Insert in place of the OS clipboard callout.
- The laptop opens graphty.app/pair: Share a folder (Chrome, Edge), a drop box, a Paste box and an Outbox. Imports are listed on the phone; exports land on the laptop. Nothing depends on AirDrop.
- Whatever still needs the phone's own operating system goes on "When you take the headset off", which runs when you leave VR.
- The price is stated in the overview, the hard cases, "Why it should work" and Known risks: no phone autocorrect, swipe typing, password manager, file picker or share sheet in VR. "Why it should work" no longer cites them; it now rests on real glass under the finger and on the phone's speech recognition.
