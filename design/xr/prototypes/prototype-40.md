**Prototype 40: Desk Touch** -- The real table in front of a seated person is calibrated and becomes a large multitouch surface with real contact: a flat projection of the graph to touch, the object's sheet beyond it, one slider rail along the table's real front edge and a drawn keyboard on the bare surface; with no desk, a lap tray, and failing that the off palm, is the touch surface.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR, the graph floating at chest height about 75 cm away, above the far edge of the table. The person sits at a real table or desk with the area in front of them cleared (in full VR they cannot see a mug or a laptop on it); the headset draws the table's outline and its zones where the real surface is, so fingers land on real wood. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Primary input is tracked hands touching the table (fingertip contact, hover height, number of fingers). Controllers, where the device has them, can be set down (the scheme switches to hands) or used as a stylus with one point of contact. Eyes are not used. Voice is optional and only types into the focused field (on Quest it needs a recognizer shipped with the page). With no table, a lap tray calibrated the same way runs the whole scheme; with nothing at all, the off palm is a small touch surface and the dominant index finger points.

### The control vocabulary

That is the whole vocabulary. Anyone who uses a tablet knows all of it except the depth strip, the orbit disc, the edge rail and the resting-hand rule.

### The table

A typical desk gives about 110 by 55 cm in reach; the zones scale to the calibrated surface. Every button, row and chip is at least 2 cm tall, because fingertip position from hand tracking is uncertain by about a centimeter.

| Zone           | Where                                                   | What it holds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Undo and Redo  | two 4 cm buttons at the near-left corner                | Always visible, even while the keyboard or the rail is up                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Object tabs    | a strip along the near edge, beside Undo and Redo       | Graph, Selection, Runs, Layers, Filters, Notes, Views, Project; tapping one puts that object's sheet on the far half                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| The rail       | the table's real front edge, full width                 | Whatever number, range, date or text caret was touched last, with its scale, detents and the value at both ends; a magnifier chip at each end stretches the span tenfold around the current value (tap Whole to return); the tabs slide aside while it is active                                            |
| The projection | the near half, about 60 by 25 cm                        | A flat projection of the floating graph along the graph's viewing direction, nodes as dots shaded by depth and hit as at least 2 cm across, labels where they fit; the orbit disc (7 cm) at its near-right corner; an inset whole-graph map at its far-right corner whenever you are zoomed in, tap to jump |
| Depth strip    | a 4 cm strip along the left edge, running front to back | A histogram of how many nodes lie at each depth; two fingers set a near and a far bound, one finger slides the slab; only nodes inside the slab show in the projection, the rest fade in both views                                                                                                         |
| The sheet      | the far half, about 70 by 25 cm                         | The focused object's sheet: its key facts, its verbs as buttons, its generated forms and tables, about 12 rows deep; a Spread button lays a wide table over both halves (about 25 rows) and shrinks the projection to an inset                                                                              |

### The verb ring

Touch and hold on an object opens up to eight verbs around the finger; the eighth is always More, which puts the object's full verb list on the sheet. The ring shows what each verb will change before you lift.

| Object (where you hold)                                            | The ring                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Node, not selected (projection, a table row, a ranking row)        | Inspect, Neighbors, Path to, Pin, Hide, Style, Note, More                  |
| Edge                                                               | Inspect, Hide, Style, Simulate removing, Note, More                        |
| Selection (hold any selected node, or the Selection sheet's title) | Keep as set, Run on this, Lay out this, Style, Filter to, Hide, Note, More |
| Empty projection                                                   | Select all, Fit, Find, Run, Layout, Filter, Note on graph, More            |
| A result column header or ranking                                  | Color by, Size by, Filter by, Select top, Compare, Sort, How made, More    |
| A layer, filter step or set row                                    | Edit, Hide or turn off, Duplicate, Rename, Delete, More                    |

To get one node's ring while it is part of a larger selection, tap it first (the selection becomes that node), then hold.

### The command line

Tap the input line to type; commands act on the object in focus unless they name another.

| You type                                                                    | It does                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a name or id (`medici`, `ACC-48213`)                                        | Find; results listed above the line with id, degree and type; Enter goes to the first        |
| a verb and an object (`run pagerank`, `layout radial`, `color by pagerank`) | The same command the sheet button runs, its form opened on the sheet with your values filled |
| a rule (`select degree > 5 and created within 30d`)                         | Parsed into rule chips on the sheet for you to check, then applied                           |
| `?` and a question                                                          | Sent to the assistant, when one is set up                                                    |
| part of any command name                                                    | Autocomplete from every command, including plugin ones (the command palette)                 |

### The palm fallback (no table)

| Input                                                          | Meaning                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Off hand palm up at waist height, fingers flat                 | The touch surface: Undo as the top row, then the focused object's sheet as a short list, five rows at a time, scrolled by a dominant-finger drag |
| Dominant index finger held up in front of the graph            | Image-plane cursor: it snaps to the node nearest the fingertip as you see it (bubble cursor)                                                     |
| Thumb tap against the side of the middle finger, dominant hand | Select what the cursor holds; tap again within a second to cycle to the node stacked behind it                                                   |
| Off hand's index finger, dominant fingertip sliding along it   | The rail                                                                                                                                         |

Typing is not possible on a palm. Short text goes to the headset's system keyboard where it has one; otherwise it waits for the 2D page. The fallback is awkward rather than workable: one hand touching the other is where hand tracking loses fingers most, and both arms are raised.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Sit at your desk, with the area in front of you cleared, and put the headset on. The first time, if the headset does not already know the table, touch its front-left corner, front-right corner and one far point with an index finger.
    - **What you see:** The table's outline appears under your hands and settles on the real surface; each calibration touch clicks. The zones fade in: Undo, Redo and the tab strip at the front edge, the projection, the depth strip at the left, the command column at the right. The 16 families float at chest height above the far edge of the table, and their flat projection lies on the near half.
    - **Controls:** three calibration taps (once per desk).

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the Graph tab. Put two fingers on the projection and twist; pinch slightly; double tap with two fingers to fit.
    - **What you see:** The sheet reads "Florentine families: 16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci has no marriages), undirected", with the node attributes listed. The floating graph and its projection turn together under the twist; Pucci sits alone at the edge. The fit returns both to the whole graph.
    - **Controls:** tap a tab, two-finger twist, pinch, two-finger double tap.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Hover a finger over the projection and slide it toward the dot labeled Medici; tap it. (In a graph too big to scan, tap the command line, type `medici`, then Enter.)
    - **What you see:** As the finger hovers, labels and degrees appear under it, and the node under it glows in the floating graph. The tap selects Medici in both views, and the sheet becomes Medici's: "Medici, 6 marriages", its attributes, its neighbors as a list of tappable rows, and its verbs as buttons.
    - **Controls:** hover, tap (or tap, typing on the surface, Enter).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Touch and hold on Medici; slide to Neighbors in the ring and lift. The rail shows Hops at 1; leave it. Tap Keep as set on the Selection sheet; its name field opens with the keyboard under it; type `Medici in-laws` and Enter.
    - **What you see:** While your finger rests on Neighbors, the ring reads "1 hop: 7 families, 7 marriages". On lift, Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni light with Medici in both views. Sliding the rail to 2 would read "2 hops: 12 families". The set appears under the Selection tab with its name.
    - **Controls:** touch and hold, slide and lift (verb ring), rail, tap, typing on the surface.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Tap the Runs tab, then New run. The catalog lies across the sheet, one column per family. Tap "Influence (PageRank)" in the Centrality column. On its form, tap the scope chip Whole graph (it offered the selection first), then Run. (Or tap the command line and type `run pagerank`.)
    - **What you see:** All 29 algorithms visible at once, with their plain and technical names. The form shows damping 0.85, iterations 100, tolerance 1e-6, weight none, and "about 1 ms". On Run, a progress row fills, and the sheet becomes the run's result.
    - **Controls:** tap, tap, tap, tap (or a typed command).

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read the ranking on the sheet. Hover a finger over the rows. Tap Spread to see all 16 at once.
    - **What you see:** A ranked table flat on the desk: Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, and so on; the far half shows 12 rows, Spread shows the rest down to Pucci 0.010, with a histogram strip beside it. The row under your finger lights its family in the projection inset and in the floating graph. The run's header says "PageRank, whole graph, damping 0.85, 16 families".
    - **Controls:** hover, tap.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Touch and hold the PageRank column header; slide to Size by and lift. A new layer's sheet opens with its size range on the rail: put two fingers on the front edge and spread them until it reads 1x to 3x. Then tap the layer's Color field, and tap a sequential palette in the swatch list.
    - **What you see:** Node sizes follow your fingers on the edge live in both views; the rail reads "Size by PageRank: 1x to 3x". The swatch list recolors the graph as you hover each palette, and commits on tap. The Layers tab now shows "PageRank: size and color", and a legend appears on the sheet and beside the floating graph.
    - **Controls:** touch and hold (ring), two fingers on the rail, tap.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Touch and hold the PageRank header again; slide to Filter by and lift. The rail becomes "PageRank at least" over the histogram; slide a finger along the edge to about 0.075, then tap the value at the rail's end and type `0.075`.
    - **What you see:** Families below the line fade from the projection and the floating graph as you slide. The rail reads "PageRank at least 0.075: keeps 4 of 16 families, 2 marriages". After you type the exact value, Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi and Albizzi remain, and the Filters tab reads "Showing 4 of 16 -- 1 filter step".
    - **Controls:** ring, finger along the rail, tap and type an exact value.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Tap Medici, then touch and hold it; slide to Note and lift. A note field opens on the sheet with a caret and the keyboard under it. Type `Married into six families; the hub of the network.` To fix a word, slide a finger along the front edge: with a text field focused the rail moves the caret word by word. Tap Done.
    - **What you see:** Word suggestions above the keyboard's top row; the text lands in the field with a real caret. On Done, the keyboard closes, a note marker appears on Medici in both views and the note joins the Notes tab.
    - **Controls:** tap, ring, typing on the surface, the rail as a caret, tap.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** Reaching back from the sheet, your index finger lands on Medici, still selected from the note, and drags it 10 cm before you notice. Tap Undo at the near-left corner (or put three fingers anywhere on the table and swipe left).
    - **What you see:** Medici had moved in both views, with a ghost where it was, and been pinned where your finger lifted. The undo returns it, and a banner on the sheet reads "Undid: Move Medici -- Redo". Holding Undo would have shown the history bar: "Run PageRank, Size and color by PageRank, Filter PageRank at least 0.075, Note on Medici, Move Medici".
    - **Controls:** one tap (or a three-finger swipe; a hold for the history bar).

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Tap the Project tab, then Save; type `Florentine first look` as the name; press Enter. (Or type `save` on the command line.)
    - **What you see:** The Project sheet reads "Saved: Florentine first look", and the same project appears in the recent list of the 2D page.
    - **Controls:** tap, tap, typing on the surface.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Type `type = account and (degree > 20 or risk_score >= 0.8) and created within 30d` on the command line. The sheet shows it as rule chips, with the OR group indented in its own box and a count under each clause ("degree > 20: 112"). Tap a chip to change its attribute, comparison or value (the value on the rail or typed); drag a chip into or out of a group by its grip to regroup it; hold a group and choose NOT. A week later, the Filters tab lists the step; tapping it opens the same chips to edit, never a retyped string.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** On the run's sheet, tap Options. All options lie on the far half at once (ForceAtlas2's 13 fit in two columns of 2 cm rows; Advanced folded keeps it to one screen); tap a number and it goes to the rail, with the magnifier for fine steps, or tap its value and type it (`1e-6`); a logarithmic number gets a log-scaled rail. Reset restores the defaults. Run again keeps both runs. A resolution sweep: type `0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0` in the resolution field and the run queues one run per value, each labeled with it.
- **Comparing two runs.** On the Runs tab, two-finger tap two runs and tap Compare. The projection splits into left and right halves, each painted by one run, with linked selection (a tap in one lights the same node in the other); the sheet shows the difference table ("3 families change rank"), sortable by tapping headers. The floating graph shows the run under your last touch, so a tap on either half switches it.
- **Layers on subsets.** Set the depth strip's slab around a dense cluster, sweep a one-finger rectangle over it on the projection, hold a selected node and choose Style. The new layer paints "this selection" as a frozen set, or "this rule" if the selection came from a rule. On the Layers tab, reorder layers by dragging rows by their grips with real contact; the floating graph repaints when you lift.
- **Recipes.** Hold Undo for the history bar and tap Open History; two-finger tap the steps to keep, tap Save as recipe and type its name. On new data, the recipe appears in the Runs catalog under Recipes; tapping it opens a mapping form that asks for each input (the weight attribute, the community result) with attribute chips, then replays every step with its count of changes.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** On the laptop, drop the expression CSV onto the 2D page's Send to headset area, or type its URL on the command line. In the headset, Add data opens the column mapping spread across the whole table: 25 columns as cards side by side, each with role chips (Key, Node attribute, Skip) and a type chip, the first rows under each. Tap Key on "gene_symbol"; the sheet reads "412 of 430 rows matched; 18 unmatched", with the unmatched list one tap away. Confirm, and the new columns appear in the node table.
- **The six stress journeys, where they differ from the above.** First session: the start sheet on the desk offers the tour and the samples; Les Miserables is two taps, its statistics are on the Graph sheet; hold the degree column header, Histogram, tap Log-log, sweep one finger across the top band to select it, tap its top row to inspect (about 8 acts). Fraud ring: `ACC-48213` typed with id suggestions after four characters; hold, Neighbors, Hops 2 on the rail and the edge-type chips "shared device" and "shared phone"; the time window on the rail with its magnifier and Step back a week; Path to with `ACC-10077` typed in the To field, Routes 3 on the rail, the routes as rows. Gene list: hold the largest piece in the Components result, Filter to, then Make working network from More; Markov clustering with inflation 2.5 typed; the group table; Filter by size at least 4; a cluster row tap selects it and the Selection sheet shows its mean logFC; Label cluster from the group's ring. Hub genes: two-finger tap the four centrality entries and Run all; four histograms on Spread; `compute hub_score = mean(rank(degree), rank(betweenness), rank(closeness), rank(eigenvector))` on the command line; Select top 10 from its header; Simulate removing from the top hub's More. Disease versus control: Project tab, Add network and Merge with the key chip; two floating graphs with Sync camera and the split projection, a tap in one half lighting the gene in both. Publish: Views tab, the preset list, layer rows dragged by grip, a callout from the node's More with one typed sentence, the journal on Open History, the report's title and summary typed (or written on the laptop), and every export sent to the laptop outbox.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers its descriptor and appears in its family's column in the Runs catalog, in command-line autocomplete under both names, and with a generated form on the sheet: numbers get the rail and typing; logarithmic numbers a log-scaled rail; attributes chips; node inputs a field filled by tapping the projection or typing; choices chips; flags switches; Advanced folded. Its result sheet picks readers from its result fields, and its result headers get the standard ring (Color by, Size by, Filter by, Select top, Compare).
- **A new layout.** It joins the Layout list on the Graph sheet with its generated options form; a focus-node option is filled by a tap on the projection.
- **A new object type.** It brings a sheet template (key facts from its descriptor, its verbs as buttons), up to seven ring verbs plus More, a row in whichever tab lists it, and, if it exists in the scene (a callout, a time slice), a mark on the projection that opens its ring when held.
- **A third-party plugin.** A family "Biology: enrichment" becomes a new column in the catalog; its gene-list option is a text field where a list is typed on the surface or sent from the laptop; its two numbers get the rail; its result reads in the generic readers. Its commands appear on the command line. Nothing in the gesture set changes.
- **The practical limit.** The scheme needs a seated person and a cleared surface: standing, walking or a cramped seat fall back to the palm, which is much weaker. Surface typing depends on hand-tracking quality near a surface, which is the least proven part. Every glance from the floating graph down to the table and back costs time and neck movement; at 10,000 nodes the projection needs the depth slab and zoom to keep targets at least 2 cm apart under a fingertip.

### Why it should work

- A real surface under a virtual one makes 2D work in VR faster and more accurate: Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn's hand-held windows (IEEE VR 1999) found that touching a physical panel beat touching a floating one, and Insko's passive haptics work (2001) showed real surfaces raise both performance and the sense of presence. A desk is the largest, steadiest passive haptic a seated person has.
- Aligning the virtual desk with the real one is shipped: Horizon Workrooms (Meta, 2021) asks the user to trace their real desk so the virtual one sits on it, and people rest their hands on it (skarredghost.com/2021/08/26/horizon-workrooms-review/).
- Touch on any surface from a headset's own hand tracking: TouchInsight (Streli et al., UIST 2024, arxiv.org/abs/2410.05940) detects touch contact and its location on arbitrary surfaces from egocentric hand tracking and models its uncertainty, which is exactly the sensing this scheme needs.
- Typing on a bare surface from hand motion alone has been decoded: Richardson et al., "Decoding Surface Touch Typing from Hand-Tracking" (UIST 2020), had skilled typists type on a plain table with high-precision motion capture of their hands as the only input and reported speeds approaching their physical-keyboard rates. Headset tracking is noisier, so that is the ceiling; the prototype starts with a drawn keyboard tapped key by key and suggestions.
- Multitouch graph exploration on tabletops is studied: Schmidt, Nacenta, Dachselt and Carpendale, "A Set of Multi-Touch Graph Interaction Techniques" (ITS 2010), showed node-link graphs navigated and edited with direct touch on a flat surface.
- Three-finger undo and redo are shipped and learned: iPadOS 13 (2019) introduced three-finger swipe left to undo and right to redo. Moving a text caret by sliding along a strip is shipped too: iOS 12 (2018) turned a held space bar into a trackpad for the caret.
- The palm as a touch surface: Gustafson, Holz and Baudisch's Imaginary Phone (UIST 2011) showed people can operate a phone's layout on their own palm without seeing it; Dezfuli et al.'s PalmRC (2012) used the palm as a remote control for a TV.
- Picking small targets: the bubble cursor (Grossman and Balakrishnan, CHI 2005) always selects the nearest target, making effective target size as large as the space around it; image-plane selection (Pierce et al., I3D 1997) picks what the finger covers in your view.
- Occlusion in 3D selection is a known problem with known answers that add a depth control: Grossman and Balakrishnan's depth ray and lock ray for volumetric displays (UIST 2006). The depth strip is the same idea as a slab on a flat surface.
- Resting forearms on a surface avoids the arm fatigue measured for mid-air input (Hincapie-Ramos et al., "Consumed Endurance", CHI 2014); the resting-hand rule is what lets people actually rest.

### What it would teach us

- Can contact be detected reliably from hand tracking alone, on each headset, without a touch sensor: what rate of missed and phantom taps, and do the one-centimeter band, the stop rule and the resting-hand rule hold up for an hour?
- How fast and accurate is typing on the drawn surface keyboard with each headset's hand tracking, compared with the Quest system keyboard and the phone (Phone in Hand)? Is ten-finger decoding ever good enough to replace key-by-key tapping?
- Does a flat projection of a 3D graph help people select, or does the mismatch between the flat copy and the floating graph confuse them? How often do people use the depth strip, and do they understand it without the tour?
- How often do people look down to the table and back up, and does that cost more, in time and neck strain, than the precision gains?
- Does the real front edge make sliders more precise than drawn sliders in the air, measured on the 0.075 filter and a 1e-6 tolerance, and do people find the rail-as-caret on their own?
- Do iPad users carry over the three-finger undo, and do others use the button instead?
- How weak is the palm fallback in practice, and is a lap tray an acceptable substitute for a desk?

### Known risks

- **Phantom and missed touches.** Without a touch sensor, a finger hovering just above the table can count as a touch, and a light tap can be missed. Mitigation: contact needs the fingertip within the band and stopped; hover starts only above 1 cm, so the two do not overlap; a resting hand never touches; the hover dot shows before contact so the person sees where a touch will land; every touch shows its dot and clicks; destructive verbs need a confirm with counts; Undo is always on the table; a setting adds a short dwell to every tap for people with tremor.
- **Typing quality.** Hand tracking near a surface is noisier and fingers occlude each other. Mitigation: 2 cm keys tapped key by key rather than touch-typed; word suggestions and autocomplete from known ids and commands; a typed id is matched against the data, so near misses still find the node; fallback to the system keyboard (Quest, Galaxy XR) or the laptop; most commands have a tap path that needs no typing at all. Expect 20 to 30 words a minute, enough for names, ids, rules and a sentence or two, not for reports.
- **Calibration drift.** The table plane slides if the headset loses tracking or the person moves the desk. Mitigation: every confirmed contact refines the plane; a Recalibrate button on the Project tab; the room-setup table plane on Quest when present.
- **Gesture conflicts.** The original candidates were removed: tilting moved off three-finger drags (which collided with the three-finger undo swipe) onto the orbit disc, number scrubbing moved onto the rail, and the rectangle is a one-finger drag so it never competes with two-finger pan and pinch. What remains: a tap that drifts becomes a drag (a 1 cm movement threshold), and a hold that drifts becomes a drag (the ring opens only if the finger stays within 1 cm).
- **Looking down and up.** Attention, and the neck, split between the table and the floating graph, and reading at the bottom of the field of view means tilting the head. Mitigation: everything touched lights in the floating graph, so the eyes can stay up for spatial work; reading and forms happen on the far half, the least steep part of the table; text size is a setting.
- **Flat text at an angle.** Text lying on a table is seen foreshortened, so it must be larger than text on a vertical panel at the same distance. Mitigation: about 12 rows per half instead of 25, Spread for wide tables, and a text size setting; skimming long tables stays slower than a monitor.
- **No desk.** Standing users, couches and airplanes. Mitigation: the lap tray, then the palm fallback; the scheme says plainly which areas weaken and sends text work to the 2D page.
- **Objects on the desk.** In full VR the person cannot see a mug or a laptop. Mitigation: the setup asks for a cleared area, the zones cover only the calibrated rectangle, and a contact that stops well above the plane is reported as "something is on the table here".
- **Projection density.** At thousands of nodes the projection's dots are closer than fingertip precision. Mitigation: zoom (which zooms both views), the depth slab, and the rectangle plus rules for selecting many; the hover readout says when nodes under the finger are too close to tell apart.
- **Frame rate.** The projection is a second drawing of the graph. Mitigation: it is one instanced layer of flat dots reusing the node positions, with labels only where they fit and for the hovered node; edges in the projection are drawn only below a size limit.
- **Accidental drags.** As in step 10, a hand returning across the projection can move a node. Mitigation: only a selected node moves, and a drag starting on an unselected node draws a rectangle instead; a moved node shows a ghost where it was until the next change; Undo names the move.
- **Vision Pro gaze-pinch.** Thumb and index touching while typing or tapping can fire a pointer select. Mitigation: drop transient-pointer events while any fingertip is on the table or the keyboard is open; test early on the device.
- **The laptop relay does not exist.** Files in and out depend on a pairing between the headset session and the laptop's graphty page. Mitigation: pair by a short code typed on the surface; until it exists, files land in the headset's downloads folder and open files come from a URL.
