**Prototype 42: Findings Inbox** -- graphty runs cheap analyses as soon as data loads and posts what it finds to an inbox; you triage each finding (Keep, Dismiss, Later, and Dig to open it), while everything you ask for yourself opens beside the inbox in your own slot, and the board of kept findings becomes the report.

No metaphor. It is email triage (Mailbox, Gmail and Superhuman swipe actions) laid over the automatic-insight tools of visual analytics (DataSite, Foresight, Power BI Quick Insights, Lux): the machine does the first pass, the person decides what matters. Everything that is not triage -- opening files, long forms, tables, settings, text entry, the layer and filter ornaments on the volume -- uses Hand Menu and Windows (24) unchanged, including its in-scene keyboard, its arm-then-confirm rule for destructive verbs and its "which one?" list, so this prototype only has to invent the part that is new.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away inside 24's volume, about 45 cm across, with 24's toolbar, filter and layer ornaments and status line. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands everywhere; eyes where the browser exposes them (Vision Pro's gaze-and-pinch pointer; Galaxy XR to be confirmed); controllers on Quest and Galaxy XR; 24's in-scene keyboard everywhere, plus a paired keyboard where the browser passes keys into the immersive page (to be checked per device); dictation where a recognizer exists; files through the laptop link (below) or the headset browser's own file picker (leave VR, pick, re-enter). The near zone holds two things side by side, directly under the volume: the card stack in the middle and your slot on the dominant side, about 55 cm out, tilted 30 degrees up, with the stack's top edge just under the volume's bottom edge (about 20 to 25 degrees below eye level), so the eyes move a short way between a card and its subject. Together they span about 50 cm, within 25 degrees either side. A sheet (a dig sheet, a layer page, a name sheet, a Picture sheet) opens in the place of the card it came from, a sheet opened from a sheet stacks on it with "Done -- back to <card>", and nothing else opens beside them, so the near zone never grows sideways. Their height and distance are a setting ("lower, near the lap" for people who rest their arms; "beside the volume"), and 24's "Bring windows here" brings them too. Whatever field you type into, 24's in-scene keyboard docks at a fixed comfort position independent of the stack: forearm height, 45 to 55 cm out, slightly toward the dominant side, keys at a 2 cm pitch, tiltable flat like a lap desk, with the field's text echoed on a line above the keys so the eyes need not leave it. The findings board is the first tab of 24's main window, 1.0 to 1.3 m away and 30 degrees to the non-dominant side, standing or seated (it never sits under the volume, where the stack is); tear it off to put it elsewhere. So triage needs only a short glance down, and the board a single turn. Everything works seated, with either hand (handedness swaps the sides) and with one hand (the footer buttons and 24's lap tray and Add mode switch).

**The laptop link.** This design builds the headset-to-laptop pairing that Hand Menu and Windows (24) defers; it is part of this prototype, not an assumption. graphty's 2D page on the laptop shows "Link a headset" with a six-character code that expires after five minutes; on the headset, "Link a laptop" on the Start card and in the Project window opens the in-scene keyboard, and six pokes link the two pages (a WebRTC data channel, set up through a small signaling relay graphty would host, with STUN and, on networks that block direct connections, a TURN relay that carries the data; the relay rate-limits code guesses). The pairing is remembered on both sides, so a week later "Link again" on the Start card is one pinch while the laptop page is open. A failed link says why on the Start card ("Link failed: this network blocks direct connections") and offers the headset file picker. Once linked:

- On the laptop, "Share a folder with the headset" (once, before entering) lists that folder's files on the headset: a "Laptop files" row on the Start card and in the Project window opens them from the stack, with no act on the laptop mid-session. Files dropped on the laptop page also arrive, as a Load card, "From laptop: expression.csv, 2.1 MB", with Open, Add as data and Add as network on its face.
- Send to laptop on any export, picture, report or the saved project file puts the file in the laptop page's download list.
- Copy on a table row, a value or a card sends the text to the laptop page's clipboard box. Text typed or pasted there before entering (a key, a prepared abstract, a gene list) waits as a "Text from laptop" card whose Paste fills the field being edited. Typing on the laptop mid-session means lifting the headset, so the design never relies on it: in-session text is the docked keyboard, dictation, or text built from the board (Draft from board, area 19).
- The link survives leaving and re-entering VR; the status line shows "Laptop linked".
  Without a link, the headset browser's file picker is the fallback, and a file that lives only on the laptop is not reachable from the headset.

| Change | Stack. Time data: a change point in a metric over time | time series with the point marked | Go to that moment, Play, Window size, Compare two moments |
| Offer | Stack. An analysis graphty would run but its cost estimate is over the auto-run budget, or it has no estimate. Plugin algorithms not ticked for auto-run share one Offer card per load, and only those whose descriptor's inputs the data satisfies ("3 plugin analyses fit this data", one sub-card each) | the estimate and why it is suggested | Run, Run on a sample, Never offer this |
| What if | Slot. "What if this were gone" from a subject | before/after: components, giant component, routes lost | Keep the simulation as a finding, Dismiss to reset |
| Recipe | Slot. You replay a saved recipe (on this data, on new data, or for each member of a set) | its inputs first, as questions ("Start account?"), then its steps in order as sub-cards, each untriaged, with the recipe step on its why line | Keep (keeps every step), a sub-card's Keep or Dismiss (Dismiss on a step that later steps use is armed and names them: "Dismiss Louvain -- 2 later steps use it"), Apply on a step that paints or filters |
| Assistant | Slot. Ask answered | the answer with its subject | Do it, the answer's own verbs |
| Hint | Stack. The first session, or the first use of a feature | one sentence and the gesture | Show me, Don't show again |

Every numeric result, whatever its card's kind, has a Histogram tab on its Dig with Log x, Log y and Bins, and a band is selected by pinching a bar or pinch-dragging across bars. A new kind of object (one graphty's team or an app extension adds) needs no trigger of its own: its card opens in your slot when you make or point at one, posts on the stack only through a finding rule the type declares, and its reader defaults to its key values.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR (in the headset with nothing loaded, the stack shows a Start card listing the samples, with an "All samples (16)" row; pinch Florentine families). Put the headset on, standing or seated. The first time, one question card asks "Should the view follow each finding? Yes / No, I'll move it" (or takes the platform's comfort setting); this walk answers Yes.
    - **What you see:** The 16 families float inside the volume, Pucci drifting alone. The card stack just under the volume reads "6 new: Load, Overview, Standout, PageRank, Groups, Distribution"; your slot beside it is empty and reads "Your cards open here". ### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "Type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days." Two ways in. From findings: tick the Standout card on degree and the Distribution card on risk_score, pinch Combine, choose OR; the bar opens a Your selection card in your slot with the combined count, and its Dig opens the rule it made in 24's rule editor, where the AND clauses on type and creation date are added with counts per clause. From scratch: New > Filter by rule opens the same editor. Either way, Show only adds the rule as one filter step, a chip in the filter ornament; a week later, pinching that chip and Edit rule opens the same editor to change one clause, and the step's Feed entry shows what the change kept and dropped.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Dig on the Groups card and unfold its form: resolution 1.0. Set 1.5 and pinch Run as new. A second Run card opens in your slot, linked to the first, and carries the comparison as a strip: "Compared with run 1 (resolution 1.0): 4 groups and 5, plus Pucci; the Medici group of 5 splits into 3 and 2; NMI 0.81, adjusted Rand 0.72 -- Open". Nothing posts on the stack, nothing is buried, and the view does not move. The strip appears only when the two runs share their scope and data and differ only in options or seed; a run of the same method on another set (another user's neighborhood) gets no strip, and Compare with on its Dig compares it with any run on purpose. A re-run never repaints: the new card offers Paint, or "Paint this instead" if the first run was painted (one named step that switches the layer to the new run, and one Undo switches it back). Sweep on the same form (a range stepper: from 0.5 to 2.0 in steps of 0.5) opens one Run card with a sub-card per value and a table of groups, modularity and NMI against the first value; Keep the one you want, Dismiss the rest. Sweep over the seed ("5 seeds") opens one card with the mean and spread of NMI and adjusted Rand across seeds: the stability number, in four acts. Run on each group on any Groups card runs one method (degree, PageRank, density) inside every group as one batch card, a table row per group with its top hub, and Next group on a group's profile steps through them.
- **Comparing two runs.** A re-run with new options carries its comparison as a strip. For two different methods (Louvain against Leiden) tick both Run cards, or keep both in your slot, and pinch Compare. For two networks (disease and control), New > Add network opens a Comparison card in your slot with Union, Intersection and Difference verbs, a key picker (matching by id by default) and per-network attribute names ("logFC_disease", "logFC_control"). Once a project holds two networks, every run's shared options carry a network picker (each, disease or control): degree on "each" opens one batch card with a column per network, named with the same suffixes ("degree_disease", "degree_control"), and Paint the difference or a computed attribute reads them. The default view is one volume with a network switch on the view ornament and the difference painted; Side by side on the Comparison card's Dig opens two synchronized half-size volumes in one scene when both networks are under 2,000 nodes each, and says so on the verb above that. A pinch in either posts the element's card with both values.
- **Layers on subsets.** Any set card has Style this on its Dig: the layer it makes paints only that set and lands at the top of the layer ornament; Fade others and Hide others make a layer that fades or hides everything outside the set, the only way a fade reaches a figure. Drag rows there to reorder (top wins), switch them off, rename them; Why on an Element card lists the layers that painted it, top first, with the values used. The three-layer case (diverging logFC centered on zero with gray for missing, size by p-value on a log scale, a border on 6 named genes) is three layer rows: two from Paint on Distribution cards with the layer page set to diverging, center 0, missing gray, and log, one from Style this on a kept set.
- **Recipes.** Two kinds, both plain lists. Triage rules: after you keep the same kind of finding on three datasets, a Hint card asks "Always run Louvain and paint it when data like this loads?"; accepting adds the rule to Settings. Step recipes: Save as recipe on the board's header stores the kept cards' acts (the runs with their options and scope, Paint, Show only, the sets by rule) without the data; ticking entries in the Feed and Save as recipe stores exactly those steps. Saving a recipe lists every step bound to this data -- an id ("Find 48213"), a node picked by pointing, a group picked from a run ("Ring 7") -- and turns each into an input, asked on replay by default ("Start account?", "Target account?") or kept fixed by a switch; a group pick is stored as a rule, not a label ("the group containing the start account"). A replay (Replay a recipe in New, on this data or new data, or Replay for each member on a set card) opens one Recipe card in your slot: its inputs first, as question cards (a Find field, a node pick, an attribute picker for a missing column), then its steps in order as sub-cards, untriaged, each with the recipe step that made it on its why line. The steps depend on each other, so the card files them together: Keep on the Recipe card keeps every step, and Dismiss on a sub-card that later steps use is armed and names them ("Dismiss Louvain -- 2 later steps use it"); dismissing it marks those steps "waiting for Louvain" instead of leaving them broken. Nothing a replay produces is kept, named or painted until the person files or applies it; a recipe step that paints or filters is offered as a verb on its sub-card ("Apply: Paint by community"). Replay for each member runs the recipe once per member, each member's input filled, as one batch card with a row per member and its key result ("user 4411: 2 routes to the top item, 3 hops"); a row's Dig opens that member's steps.
- **The same analysis for many entities.** "For each member" on any set, selection or Found card asks for one method from the catalog (or a recipe, as above) and opens one batch card in your slot with a table row per member, sorted by the method's result; pinching a row frames that member and posts its card. "Sample N" on a selection's Dig makes a random sample (seeded, the seed on its source line) to run it on. Twenty cold-start users from the degree-1 band are: pinch the band (1), Sample N with 20 (2), Save as set (2), For each member (1), pick the saved neighborhood-and-route recipe (2): 8 acts, then reading.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** A file in the laptop's shared folder opens from "Laptop files" on the Start card or the Project window; a file dropped on the linked laptop's page arrives as a Load card with Open, Add as data and Add as network. 24's Project window also opens a file from the headset browser's own picker (leave VR, pick, re-enter; the session resumes), a URL typed on the in-scene keyboard, a sample or a generator. If the format is ambiguous, the first card asks which reader (detected guess first); if it needs mapping, the next card is the column mapping: "Mapped id = gene, source = from, target = to -- 2 guesses", and its Dig opens the preview table in a 24 window with a role picker per column. Then the load report and validation cards post, each with fix verbs ("3 edges point to missing ids: Drop them", "2 nodes look like duplicates by name: Merge"). New > Add data joins a second file by a key column picked from both sides; the result is one Join card in your slot, "Matched 4,812 of 5,000 rows (96 percent); 188 unmatched", whose Dig shows the unmatched rows; the 25 new columns are ranked sub-cards on it (most notable shape first, each a one-line distribution with its own Dig), so the join never floods the stack and never buries the match count.
- **Pattern by example.** Select 3 to 10 nodes that form the shape you care about (a rectangle, or the add modifier), then Find more like this shape on the Your selection card. A small form lists each node of the pattern with a type constraint taken from its attributes (editable: "any", "account", "device") and each edge with its type. Run opens a Found card of the matches in your slot. A pattern that does not exist anywhere in the data to point at is drawn on the 2D page, and its matches come back as a card.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** It registers a descriptor (plain name, family, options, result fields, and the inputs it needs from the data). It appears in New in its family, with its generated form on Dig. It never runs on load until the person ticks it in the auto-run settings; until then it is offered as one sub-card of the single plugin Offer card, and only on data that has the inputs its descriptor names, with what it would cost and whether it sends data anywhere. Its result opens as a card whose reader and headline come from the app's generic field-kind rules (a number per node gives a ranking and a histogram; a group per node gives group bars; a table of pairs gives a Pairs card), and Paint, Show only, Select these and For each member work because they too are driven by field kinds. Its third option is the third row of the generic form.
- **A new layout.** It appears in the layout list on the Overview card's Dig and in New, with its generated options. Running it opens a Run card in your slot (with Stop if it is iterative); Keep puts it on the board so the report records which layout drew each figure.
- **A new object type.** It needs a card: the type declares its key values, its verbs and, if it has one, a framing. Its card then gets the filing acts, Dig and Note, a place on the board (its own column if it is a kind people collect) and a Feed entry like every other object. When it posts needs no new decision: it opens in your slot when the person makes or points at one, posts on the stack only through a finding rule the type declares, and its reader defaults to its key values.
- **A third-party plugin.** A data reader posts its load report and validation issues as Load cards; a format writer joins Export with its options form; a palette joins every palette picker. A plugin family of four enrichment algorithms, one taking a list of gene ids and two number options, appears as one family in New; the gene list option renders as a node-list field filled from a kept set, the selection, or text pasted from the laptop or typed.
- **Finding rules are the app's, not the element's.** Which results become cards, how they rank and what their headlines say are presentation, so they live in the graphty app (Settings, triage rules, recipes), and an app-level extension may add a rule such as "when the data has a column named like an expression value, offer the expression overlay". graphty-element needs no new extension point: it supplies the facts every rule reads (statistics, result fields, cost estimates, validation codes), and an element plugin needs only its descriptor.
- **The practical limit.** The inbox is only as good as what it chooses to post. On large graphs the cheap analyses can produce dozens of standouts; the budget, Quiet start, the "Stop showing this kind" button, the single plugin Offer card and the cap of 10 automatic cards per load keep the stack short, but ranking the cards well is the hard part. Long interactive editing (a 12-attribute merge, a 13-option layout, a 2000-character report written from nothing) is not triage at all; it falls through to 24's sheets and windows, and the scheme is no better than 24 there, except that Draft from board turns kept cards into report text.

### Why it should work

- Proactive analysis helps people find more. DataSite (Cui, Badam, Yalcin and Elmqvist, Information Visualization 2019) ran computations in the background and posted their results to a feed of findings; in its study people covered more of the data and reported more findings than with a manual tool, and used the feed as a starting point rather than a replacement.
- Ranked automatic insights are established: Foresight (Demiralp, Haas, Parthasarathy and Pedapati, VLDB 2017) ranks insights by the strength of a statistical feature; QuickInsights (Ding and colleagues, SIGMOD 2019) shipped as Power BI's Quick Insights; Lux (Lee and colleagues, VLDB 2022) shows always-on recommended charts beside a dataframe. Voyager 2 (Wongsuphasawat and colleagues, CHI 2017) found that suggested views broadened what analysts examined.
- Swipe triage is one of the most practiced gestures there is: Mailbox (2013) made directional swipes the way to clear an inbox, and Gmail, Outlook and Superhuman ship the same pattern, though each maps the directions differently, so the act names on the card's edges matter more than any one product's mapping. Four directions with one meaning each are a marking menu of depth one, which Kurtenbach and Buxton (1993) showed people learn to perform without looking.
- Mixed-initiative design (Horvitz, CHI 1999) says automatic services should be easy to dismiss, should show why they acted and should defer to the person on costly actions. Every card shows why it is there, nothing it finds changes the project until a person acts, and dismissal is one flick.
- A headset is bad at the desktop's strengths (skimming small text, typing, precise pointing) and good at looking around a structure. Letting the machine do the first pass and frame each answer moves the work toward reading one card at a time and looking at the highlighted subject, and away from building queries.
- The base surface is 24, the platform-default app, so everything outside triage behaves as people expect from visionOS and Horizon OS windows; the scheme adds one new surface, not a new way to do everything.

### What it would teach us

- Do people trust and use machine-posted findings in a headset, or dismiss them all and go to New? What share of kept findings came from the inbox versus from New?
- Does the view following each card help orientation or cause disorientation? Do people turn Follow off, and how does comfort with the fade compare with an animated move?
- Is the four-direction flick reliable with hand tracking on each device, and how often is Dismiss mistaken for Later? Do people flick or use the footer buttons? Does Back on the stack's frame get used, and do people miss it on the footer?
- Does "the inbox is the machine's, the slot is mine" hold as one rule? Do people look for their own results in the stack, and does the slot's "Earlier" row fill up unfiled?
- How long a stack do people tolerate before they stop reading? What is the right number of automatic cards per load, and does "stop showing this kind" fix overload?
- Does the board, exported as a report, match what an analyst would have written? Do notes on findings produce better evidence trails than free notes?
- Does a first-time user reach a first analysis in under 5 minutes when the Start card and the first five cards are the tour?
- How many acts do the six stress journeys take against the desktop app, and against 24 alone?

### Known risks

- **False discoveries.** Running many analyses and showing the striking ones invites people to read noise as signal (Zgraggen and colleagues, "Investigating the Effect of the Multiple Comparisons Problem in Visual Analysis", CHI 2018; Binnig and colleagues, "Toward Sustainable Insights", CIDR 2017). Mitigation: cards state their effect size against a baseline, never a bare "interesting"; distribution and comparison cards carry a caveat line when the sample is small; the count of automatic analyses run is on the status line and in the report's methods.
- **Overload on large graphs.** A 10,000-node graph has many standouts. Mitigation: at most 10 automatic cards per load, ranked; similar findings grouped into one card with sub-cards; the auto-run budget; Quiet start; "Stop showing this kind" as an armed button after a Dismiss and on every machine card's Dig, with hidden kinds listed on the counter; plugins share one Offer card; everything the person asks for opens in their own slot, so it never competes with the machine's cards for the stack.
- **The machine sets the agenda.** People may analyze only what the inbox offered. Mitigation: New is always one pinch away on the stack, the first Hint card says so, and the report's methods list which findings were automatic and which were asked for.
- **Disorienting view jumps.** Following each card moves the graph often. Mitigation: a crossfade of the graph only (the panels never dim) instead of a fly-through, the Follow switch, Follow asked at first entry, a 1.5 s hold before following when cards are filed quickly, "Moving to Medici" on the peeking card with a cancel, only machine cards move the view (nothing in your slot ever does), Follow stays off after a reopen until Resume triage, on Vision Pro the view follows only after a pinch on the stack, and the view never moves while either ray or a gaze-pinch target is on the volume, or while you pinch it or a form.
- **Mis-flicks.** Dismiss instead of Later, or a flick read from a drag. Mitigation: flicks count only from a pinch that starts on the current card's body, never on a control on it (the face carries no steppers), and never on a card in your slot; a flick needs distance and a minimum speed; the direction shows as it forms, returning to the middle cancels and tracking loss cancels, and a pinch that moved but did not flick does nothing (it is not Dig); filing never changes the graph, and Dismiss discards only results no verb has used; Back and the piles restore with one act; the flick distance and speed are settings and the footer buttons need no flick at all, with Keep and Dismiss separated by Later and every button at least 2.5 cm tall.
- **Two undo controls.** People may expect Undo to restore a dismissed card. Mitigation: Undo always names the project step it will undo, and Back names the filing it will reverse, so neither surprises; Undo sits on the stack's top edge and Back on its frame beside the counter, and no card's footer carries either; Keep never makes a project step (sets come only from Save as set, confirmed by Save set); after every filing act the line above the stack shows "Dismissed Groups -- Back" for five seconds; the Feed shows both kinds. Keeping them apart is deliberate: a dozen flicks never stand between you and a paint you want to undo.
- **Ranking is opinionated.** Which findings come first is a presentation choice. Mitigation: the ranking lives in the app, not in graphty-element, which only supplies the facts (statistics, result fields, cost estimates, validation codes); the ranking rules are visible in Settings and a recipe can set them.
- **Cost estimates can be wrong.** An auto-run that takes far longer than estimated stalls the start. Mitigation: automatic runs run one at a time in a worker, each under a single-run cap as well as the total budget; one that overruns is cancelled and posted as an Offer with the measured time, and the hands and the stack never freeze.
- **Text entry.** Names, searches, formulas, notes and reports need typing. Mitigation: suggested names on every name field from the finding; 24's in-scene keyboard with completions from the data everywhere (Find matches part of an id, so a digit run is enough), docked at a fixed comfort position with its text echoed above the keys; formulas built from chips; report sections drafted from the board; dictation where a recognizer exists; long text prepared on the laptop before entering and pasted in from a "Text from laptop" card.
- **Head turns between three places.** The stack and slot, the volume and the board. Mitigation: the stack and slot sit just under the volume (about 20 to 25 degrees below eye level), side by side within 25 degrees either way, and every sheet opens in the place of its card, so the near zone never grows sideways; the board is a tab of 24's main window to the non-dominant side; layers and filters stay on the volume's own ornaments, not on the board.
- **Results the person never looked at.** An inbox that runs analyses on load could leave their output in the project, the report and the export unread. Mitigation: automatic results are provisional until a card is kept or a verb uses them; Dismiss discards them; the report's Methods list every run anything depends on, with its seed and scope; the export sheet lists provisional results with a tick box each, so leaving them out is visible; a recipe replay asks for every id and pick and lists its steps untriaged on one Recipe card.
- **The laptop link is new infrastructure.** It needs a hosted signaling relay, STUN and a TURN relay that carries data on restrictive networks, none of which graphty runs today; pairing codes must expire and be rate-limited because the channel carries the person's data and possibly an AI provider key. Without it, files that live on the laptop are not reachable and long text falls back to the docked keyboard and dictation.
- **Frame rate at 10,000 nodes.** The per-card highlight depends on the emphasis channel being built in graphty-element for nodes, edges, arrow caps and labels; through style layers it would repaint every element on every filed card, and an outline on a large subject is a blur pass per eye, which is why outlines are drawn only for subjects under 50 elements. Until the channel exists the design should be tested only below a few thousand nodes.
- **The highlight is not the figure.** The machine's highlight and dim shape what the person sees while triaging, and a figure composed under them would not match its file. Mitigation: the dim shows only while the person is triaging and lifts on any touch elsewhere; the status line always names the emphasis in force or reads "Project look"; a Project look switch on the stack; Picture, the report, exports, recipes and saved views never draw emphasis, Picture's preview is drawn from the same clean scene as the file, and fades that belong in a figure are style layers in the legend.
- **An expert who ignores the agenda.** For an analyst who already knows what to run, the machine-first inbox is mostly overhead. Quiet start (which does not run the automatic analyses at all), a slot that keeps everything they ask for off the stack, comparison strips that never move the view, Sweep over seeds, Run on each group, For each member and keyboard keys cut that overhead, but the idea still puts the machine's findings first; removing that would abandon the design, so for this person the gains are the comparison-as-by-product, For each member and the board as a methods record, not the inbox itself.
- **Tasks that are not triage.** Merging with conflict rules, a 13-option layout, a compound rule built from nothing. Mitigation: they fall through to 24's sheets, windows and rule editor, unchanged; the scheme claims no advantage there.

### Studio revisions

#### Round 1

Severity 3 findings:

- **Dig had no button and depended on a pinch under 0.3 s** (ergonomics; also raised by onboarding, VR interaction, expert and red team). Dig is now a footer button on every card, and a still pinch of any length is Dig; nothing on a card's body is timed.
- **"Flick down and hold" could silently stop a whole kind of finding** (VR interaction). Both hold variants are gone. "Stop showing this kind" is an armed button on the five-second line after a Dismiss, and hidden kinds are listed on the counter with Show again. Choosing a column is "Move to column" on the same line after a Keep.
- **Every card restyled the whole graph, and the threshold slider did so every frame** (WebXR). Highlight, dim and live ghosts now go through a transient per-node emphasis value in graphty-element's shader, set in one buffer write, never a style layer. Slider ghosts update on detents and at most five times a second. A frame budget is stated (one frame or less for 10,000 nodes on a Quest 3). The PageRank preview highlights the top five instead of resizing every node.
- **Files on the laptop were unreachable** (coverage). The laptop link (six-character code, WebRTC data channel, a small hosted relay) is now part of this design:
    - Files dropped on the laptop arrive as Load cards.
    - Send to laptop reaches its download list.
    - Copy and paste go both ways.
      Its cost is listed under the devices table and in Known risks.
- **A 25-column join flooded the stack** (coverage). A join is one Join card: the match count on top, the new columns as ranked sub-cards, and one card toward the cap.
- **Dismissed auto-runs persisted and were published without a record** (red team). Automatic results are provisional until a card is kept or a verb uses them, and Dismiss discards them (Back restores them). The report's Methods list every run anything depends on, kept or not, with its options and seed. Exports carry committed results only. Automatic runs use a project seed shown on their source line.
- **Recipe replay skipped triage and could not map ids or groups** (red team). Saving a recipe turns every id, node pick and group pick into an input asked on replay, and a group pick is stored as a rule. A replay posts its results untriaged under the recipe's name, and nothing it makes is kept, named or painted until the person acts.

The red team's top change:

- **Dig and Note on every card's footer, with the keyboard at the stack.** The footer is now Keep, Dismiss, Later, Back, Dig, Note, on stack, board and pile cards alike. Note also sits on the five-second line after a Keep.
- The note field opens on the card itself. The in-scene keyboard docks at the stack within poke reach whatever field it serves.
- Florentine step 9 is now done from the Medici node's Element card, with no turn to the board: about 7 acts typed, 4 dictated (was 9). The persona's two-sentence note drops from 13 to about 10.

Severity 2 findings fixed along the way, because they share a cause with the above:

- **Keep had two meanings.** Keep only files a card. Sets come from a "Name and keep as set" verb, one named project step.
- **Keep and the filter verb shared a word.** The filter verb is renamed Show only.
- **Cards caused by the person's own acts moved the view.** These are New, re-runs, comparisons from her own filter or edit, and replays. None of them moves the view now. A filter that changes kept findings shows "3 kept findings changed -- Compare" on its chip instead of posting cards.
- **Follow.** It is asked at first entry, and on reopening it waits until a card is filed.
- **The Load card taught a reflexive Dismiss.** A Load card with errors cannot be dismissed, and a clean one files itself. The Start card cannot be dismissed and has an All samples row.
- **Inspecting two nodes.** The inspect slot holds two pointed-at cards side by side, with Compare between them.
- **Flicks.**
    - Steppers moved off the card face.
    - A flick needs a minimum speed as well as distance (4 cm on every device).
    - Tracking loss cancels a flick, and the release frame is ignored.
- **Back and Undo.** Back names what it reverses, and Undo moved to the stack's top edge.
- **Expert iteration and stability.**
    - Comparisons show NMI and adjusted Rand.
    - Sweep runs over seeds.
    - Run on each group and Community graph are on Groups cards.
    - Paired keyboard keys cover the filing acts, Dig, Note, Run as new and the palette.
    - Quiet start posts only Load and Overview.
- **Plugins.** They never auto-run until ticked, and they get the generic field-kind finding rules.
- **Smaller additions.**
    - 24's Overview window serves as the overview map.
    - Kept findings join 24's single list of named views.
    - The stack position is a setting.
    - The layer page opens as a sheet beside the stack, with steppers.
    - A Hint card teaches the palm-up hand menu.
    - Paint says when it would be painted over.
    - On Vision Pro, Add mode replaces the held off-hand pinch.
    - Drawing a region starts from "Draw a region" in dense graphs.
    - The off-hand thumbstick files only while the stack is in view.

Not fixed without abandoning the idea:

- **The expert's verdict that she would go back to the desktop.** Quiet start and the iteration fixes cut the inbox's overhead, but the design still puts the machine's findings first. For an analyst who already knows what to run, its value is the comparison-as-by-product and the board as a methods record, not the inbox itself.
- **Long text without a laptop.** Text is still typed on an in-scene keyboard, and this design is no better than Hand Menu and Windows (24) there.

#### Round 2

Severity 3 finding:

- **The machine's highlight was undefined in the published figure** (red team, publish journey). While any card was current, a dim outside the layer list covered the graph, and Picture never said whether it drew it. Now:
    - Picture, the report's figures, exports, recipes and saved views never draw emphasis. Emphasis is off while a Picture or Export sheet is open, and Picture's preview comes from the same clean scene as the file.
    - The dim shows only while the person is triaging, and lifts on any touch of the slot, a sheet, an ornament, the board or the volume.
    - The status line always names the emphasis in force or reads "Project look", and a Project look switch on the stack turns emphasis off.
    - Fades that belong in a figure come only from style layers (Fade others, Hide others), which show in the legend and the report's Look section.
    - The publish walk now uses Who they connect to (1 hop) and Fade others, instead of hiding the rest of the graph.

The red team's top change:

- **The inbox is the machine's; the slot is yours.** The stack holds only cards the machine posts on its own. Everything the person's own acts produce opens in their slot beside the stack, is filed only by its own footer (Keep, Dismiss, Dig, Note), never goes to Later, never dims the graph and never moves the view. This fixes five findings:
    - **The result you asked for vanished to Later** (expert). The "covered card goes to Later" rule is gone.
    - **Automatic comparisons of unrelated runs** (expert). A re-run carries its comparison as a strip on its own Run card, and only when both runs share scope and data and differ only in options or seed. Compare with on Dig compares anything on purpose.
    - **The Later pile mixed deferrals with moved cards** (coverage). Later now holds only machine cards the person set aside.
    - **Which card is current** (onboarding, VR interaction). The current card is the stack's top card. Flicks, filing keys and the off-hand thumbstick act only on it.
    - **The journey forgot to file the PageRank card** (onboarding). Step 8 now ends by keeping it, which brings Groups up for step 10.
- **The red team's next change for efficiency.** "For each member" on any set, selection or Found card runs one method, or one recipe, over every member as one batch card. With Sample N, the expert's 20 cold-start users drop from about 280 acts to about 8.

Severity 2 findings fixed along the way:

- **Filing and Follow.**
    - Clearing leftover cards after a reopen no longer turns Follow back on. Only Resume triage or the switch does.
    - On Vision Pro, the view follows only after a pinch on the stack.
- **Recipe replay.** A replay is one Recipe card with its steps in order. Dismissing a step that later steps use is armed and names them, and Keep keeps all of them.
- **Back acted on a different card.** Back left the footer for the stack's frame and the line above the stack. Every footer button now acts on its own card, and board and pile cards get Return.
- **Keep confirmed a project step.** The name sheet's button is Save set, and the step is "Make set Medici in-laws". Coverage area 7 is corrected, and "Keep the differing ones" is now "Select the differing ones". The verb is renamed "Save as set".
- **Footer buttons below the minimum target size.** The footer is now two rows, Keep, Later, Dismiss above Dig and Note, each at least 2.5 cm tall with 0.5 cm gutters. Keep and Dismiss are never neighbors.
- **A cancelled flick opened Dig.** Dig needs a pinch that moves less than 1 cm. A pinch that moved but did not flick does nothing, and a move that was too slow says "Flick faster, or use the buttons". The minimum speed is a setting and is measured as the peak over the last 100 ms.
- **The five-second line.**
    - It moved above the stack, away from the footer.
    - It stays while it is looked at or armed, and Stop showing this kind ignores a double press.
    - Both of its acts are also on Dig.
- **Invisible modes.** The target pick shows "target" at the ray tip with a Cancel on the Route card, and Draw a region shows a corner glyph and Cancel. Both end after one use. Draw a region moved to the stack's frame and the view ornament.
- **The off hand's two jobs.** While a selection is being built, the off hand's ray is hidden from the start and the hand menu is suppressed. On Vision Pro, Add mode is off until switched on.
- **The near zone.**
    - The keyboard docks at a fixed comfort position independent of the stack, with its text echoed above the keys.
    - The stack and slot sit just under the volume by default, about 20 to 25 degrees down.
    - Sheets open in the place of their card and stack with "Done -- back to <card>", so the zone never spreads sideways.
    - The board sits to the non-dominant side, never under the volume.
- **The off-hand thumbstick.** It now files the current card whenever the dominant ray is not on a list or form, with a tilt badge on the status line.
- **The teaching Load card filed itself unread.** The first Load card waits for the person, the edge labels stay on the first five cards, and a fingertip swipe on a card is a flick.
- **No way back from the layer page.** It stacks on the dig sheet with "Done -- back to PageRank".
- **Two networks.**
    - Every run's shared options gain a network picker. "Each" gives one batch card with a column per network, so the disease versus control journey now completes.
    - Side by side is two half volumes in one scene, under 2,000 nodes per network. The default is one volume with a network switch.
- **Runs and filters.** Every run's scope (whole graph, visible graph, a set, a network) is on its form and its source line.
- **Link prediction had no reader.** A Pairs kind is added: ranked pairs, a row frames both ends, and Route between.
- **Exports silently dropped provisional results.** The export sheet lists them with a tick box each. Quiet start now skips the automatic analyses instead of running them unseen.
- **Plugin Offers crowded the cap.** Plugins share one Offer card per load, and an Offer posts only when the data has the inputs its descriptor names.
- **A new object type needed a hand-made trigger.** Its card opens in the slot when made, posts on the stack only through a finding rule it declares, and its reader defaults to its key values.
- **Formulas were typed.** A computed attribute is built from chips, with its text shown for checking.
- **Long report text.** Draft from board turns kept cards' headlines and notes into section text to trim.
- **The laptop link.**
    - The laptop shares a folder once, and "Laptop files" opens from the headset with no act on the laptop mid-session.
    - Text from the laptop is prepared before entering.
    - STUN and TURN are named, codes expire and guesses are rate-limited, a failed link says why, and the pairing is remembered.
    - The project file is on the Send to laptop list.
- **The emphasis channel.**
    - It covers edges, arrow caps and labels, and outlines are drawn only for subjects under 50 elements.
    - Automatic runs run in a worker, so Cancel stops them mid-call.
- **The walks.**
    - The fraud-ring walk saves the rule step's filter, not Ring 7.
    - The first-session walk makes the paint it undoes on purpose.

Not fixed without abandoning the idea:

- **The machine still sets the first agenda.** The expert would still do most of a forms-and-tables workflow on the desktop. The slot, Quiet start and For each member cut the overhead, but the stack still leads.
- **Long freshly typed text without a laptop.** Draft from board and chips cut most typing, but a paragraph written from nothing is still the in-scene keyboard or dictation.
- **Patterns absent from the data.** They are still drawn on the 2D page.
- **Keyboard keys.** Whether a paired keyboard's keys reach the immersive page is still to be checked per device. Until then, an expert on Quest has the footer and the hand menu.
- **Quest's palm-up system gesture.** The page cannot suppress it, so a held off-hand modifier still has to be palm down or sideways. Add mode is the path that avoids it.
