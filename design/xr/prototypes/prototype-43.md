**Prototype 43: Variant Grid** -- Whenever a choice has a visible consequence, graphty shows a grid of 4 to 9 live miniature variants beside the graph; you pick by looking, vary the pick subtly or strongly, lock the options you like, and the current state always sits in the top-left corner, so not picking cancels; variants you keep stay on a shelf to compare. Even destructive verbs and history are grids: a before and after pair, or the outcomes of "go back to here" and "revert only this step", side by side.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away inside a volume about 45 cm across, with 24's rim, bar, status line and filter and layer ornaments. The grid opens beside the graph on the dominant side, about 65 cm away, 10 degrees below eye level and centered about 25 degrees from the graph's center, so a glance, not a head turn, goes between them. Default 2x2 tiles of about 11 cm (about 10 degrees wide); 2x3 and 3x3 tiles shrink to 9 cm (about 8 degrees), still five times the eye tracker's error and far larger than a node. A setting puts the grid under the volume instead (the shelf then moves to the off-hand side), which spares a seated neck the sideways glance. The shelf runs under the graph. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands everywhere; eyes where the browser exposes them (Vision Pro's gaze-and-pinch pointer, which suits tiles this large); controllers on Quest and Galaxy XR; for the few text fields, 24's in-scene keyboard everywhere (with completions from the data's names), plus a paired keyboard where the browser passes keys through and dictation where a recognizer exists; the laptop's 2D page (through 24's laptop inbox) or the headset browser's own file picker for files. Everything works with either hand, with one hand and seated.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, choose Enter VR with no data open. Put the headset on, standing or seated.
    - **What you see:** The empty volume, and beside it the start grid: the "as is" tile reads "Nothing open"; eight tiles show sample datasets as miniatures (Florentine families, karate club, Les Miserables, airline routes ...), each with its name and size; the grid bar's All lists all 16 samples, Recent and Open from laptop. A one-time hint reads "Pinch a picture to choose it; hold it to look closer".
    - **Controls:** none yet.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the "Florentine families, 16 families, 20 marriages" tile to peek at it, then release on it to pick it. Pinch the volume's rim with both hands and spread them to 150 percent. Pinch-hold empty volume and choose View on the ring; in the view grid, hold "from above", release diagonally to cancel, hold "isometric" and release on it. Pinch-hold empty volume again and choose Characterize.
    - **What you see:** While you hold the sample tile, the volume shows the network full size; on release it stays, with Pucci drifting alone. The view grid holds "as is" and the 5 camera presets as thumbnails, all turning together as you turn the graph; the cross shows Keep (save as a named view) and dims the arms that do not apply. Characterize opens 24's graph card: "16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci alone), undirected, density 0.17". The status line under the volume reads "Showing 16 of 16 families".
    - **Controls:** hold to peek, release to pick, release diagonally to cancel; two-hand pinch on the rim; verb ring; view grid.

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Look at the node labeled Medici and pinch (on Quest 3, point the ray at it and pinch). In a graph too big to scan, you would poke Find on the hand menu and type "Med" on the in-scene keyboard, or dictate it.
    - **What you see:** Medici is outlined as selected, and in the grid's place a strip of candidate selections opens: "as is: Medici (1)", "neighbors: 7 families, 7 marriages", "within 2 hops: 12 families", "degree 4 or more: 3 (Medici, Guadagni, Strozzi)", "same piece: 15". Had Find matched several names ("Medici", "de' Medici"), its results would be tiles of each candidate's neighborhood, which is how you tell them apart.
    - **Controls:** look-and-pinch (or ray and pinch).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the "neighbors: 7 families" tile in the strip and release on it.
    - **What you see:** While held, the big graph shows the seven inside a soft hull: Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati, Tornabuoni and the Medici. On release the selection is made and the status line reads "Selected 7 families, 7 marriages among them". Moving left before releasing (Vary subtle) would have offered "neighbors without Medici (6)", "neighbors and the marriages between them only", and 2 hops.
    - **Controls:** hold to peek, release to pick.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold empty volume and choose Run. Look over the suggested grid, hold "Influence (PageRank)" to peek, and release on it. Leave Scope on "Whole graph".
    - **What you see:** A 3x3 grid: "as is: no analysis" and eight suggestions for a small undirected graph, each tile painted with its own result -- Connections (degree), Bridges (betweenness), Reach (closeness), Influence (PageRank), Communities (Louvain), Separate pieces, Core depth, How tightly knit. The cost light is green ("all under a second"). The PageRank tile reads "Medici first, 0.145". After the pick the graph looks like the tile: colored by PageRank on viridis. A PageRank chip joins the shelf, the layer ornament gains "PageRank (suggested)", and the status line reads "PageRank done". The Scope switch on the grid bar showed "Whole graph / Selection (7)"; whole graph is the default.
    - **Controls:** verb ring, suggested grid, peek and pick.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Quick-pinch the PageRank chip on the shelf. Pinch-drag the list to scroll; pinch Guadagni's row.
    - **What you see:** 24's reader window, chosen by the result field's kind (a number per node): a ranked list with a histogram tab -- Medici 0.145, Guadagni 0.098, Strozzi 0.088, Albizzi 0.079, Tornabuoni 0.071, Ridolfi and Castellani 0.069 ... down to Pucci, lowest at about 0.010. A line at the bottom: "PageRank, damping 0.85, whole graph, 16 families". Pinching Guadagni frames that family in the volume.
    - **Controls:** quick pinch on a shelf chip, pinch-drag to scroll, quick pinch on a row.

7. **Color and size by PageRank.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold the PageRank chip and choose Paint. In the first grid (which encoding), pick "color and size". In the second grid (how), hold the tile "viridis, 1x to 2x"; while holding it, pinch the palette's lock on the rail with the other hand; then move the holding hand left and release (Vary subtle). In the new grid, pick "1x to 3x".
    - **What you see:** The first grid shows "as is: color", "color only, other palettes", "size only", "color and size", "size and label the top 3". The second grid shows eight versions on the real graph, spread across palette (viridis, plasma, inferno, blues), size range (1x to 2x, 1x to 3x, 1x to 5x) and scale (linear, log), with the rail marking each tile's place on each range. The lock reads "lock palette: viridis". With the palette locked, Vary subtle shows viridis only, at 1x to 2.5x, 1x to 3x and 1x to 3.5x, linear and log. After the last pick the chain applies as one step, and the layer row reads "PageRank: color and size", with its ramp from about 0.010 to 0.145 and a size key. One-handed, you would hold the tile, release diagonally (it stays the focus tile), pinch the lock, then hold it again and move left.
    - **Controls:** run ring, a two-grid chain, held tile plus the other hand on the lock, tile cross (Vary subtle), pick.

8. **Keep only strong families (filter).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold the PageRank chip and choose Filter from. Hold the tile "at least 0.070" to peek, and release on it.
    - **What you see:** A 2x3 grid of cut points, each placed in a gap of the ranking so its count is stable: "as is: 16 of 16"; "at least 0.040: 11 families, 16 marriages"; "at least 0.065: 9, 13"; "at least 0.070: 5, 4"; "at least 0.080: 3, none"; "at least 0.090: 2, none". After the pick, a chip "PageRank at least 0.070 -- 5 of 16" joins the filter ornament and the status line reads "Showing 5 of 16 families, 4 of 20 marriages -- 1 filter".
    - **Controls:** run ring, threshold grid, peek and pick.

9. **Write a note.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-hold the Medici and choose Note. Pick two of the suggested sentence tiles, then pinch the empty sentence and type "the hub of the network" on the in-scene keyboard (or dictate it where a recognizer exists). Pinch Done.
    - **What you see:** A note has no picture to choose from, so 24's note field opens beside the node, with sentence tiles drawn from facts about the Medici: "Highest PageRank of the 16 families (0.145).", "Married into 6 families.", "Guadagni is two marriages away, through Albizzi or Tornabuoni."     - **Controls:** node ring, sentence tiles, in-scene keyboard or dictation.

10. **Undo a mistake.**
    - **What you do:** You open Edit on the PageRank layer to look at the palettes again; you mean to hold "inferno, log size" to peek, but your pinch is quick and the tile is picked. The graph turns dark red and orange with Pucci and the small families nearly as large as the Medici. Had the mistake been several steps old, the History gallery would show it as a picture among the others -- "Open Florentine families", "Select neighbors of Medici", "Run PageRank", "Paint PageRank", "Paint PageRank: color and size", "Filter PageRank at least 0.070", "Note on Medici", "Edit layer PageRank" -- and picking "Edit layer PageRank" would offer "revert only this step" beside "go back to here", so the note and the filter stay.
    - **Controls:** hand menu Undo (named); Back; History gallery.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Turn your off palm up and poke Save. Pick the name tile "Florentine families -- strong families".
    - **What you see:** Because the project is still the unsaved sample, Save shows name tiles built from what you did: "Florentine families -- PageRank", "Florentine families -- strong families", "Medici in-laws", "Florentine families 2026-10-10", plus a Type a name field. After the pick the status line reads "Saved Florentine families -- strong families". Later saves go straight through.
    - **Controls:** hand menu Save, name tiles.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" is built in 24's rule editor, because the structure of a rule (AND, OR, NOT, nesting) has no good picture. Each clause's value is picked from a grid: the degree clause shows cut points with counts ("above 10: 412", "above 20: 96", "above 40: 11"), the date clause shows windows ("last 7 days", "last 30 days", "this quarter"). A final grid shows the whole rule's result next to its near relatives (each clause loosened or dropped, with counts), which is how you see which clause does the work. Edited a week later: pinch-hold the filter chip, Edit rule; every value grid regenerates with this week's counts.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Run chip ring, Re-run with options: the grid varies the options that change the result (for PageRank, damping at 0.5, 0.7, 0.85 and 0.95, and direction), with "3 families change rank" on tiles where the ranking moves. A pick adds a new run beside the old one, so both stay on the shelf; Remove (pair) clears the old one if wanted. Which options are set aside is decided without anything new in the descriptor: options it marks advanced, plus options whose two range ends gave the same result in a quick probe on a sample. Tolerance and iterations therefore vary only from "show hidden options", by decade (1e-4 to 1e-8), and their tiles report what changed rather than show it ("same ranking, 41 iterations"). Katz's 7 options and ForceAtlas2's 13 are refined two at a time, the options with the largest probed effect first (for ForceAtlas2, scaling and gravity, then edge weight influence and overlap prevention), with the rest in the Exact form, which also holds Reset to defaults. A Louvain resolution sweep is one grid at 0.5, 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 ("4 groups, modularity 0.39" ... "7 groups, 0.33"); Keep all puts four runs on the shelf as one sweep.
- **Comparing two runs.** Run chip ring, Compare with: pick the other run from a grid of the runs on the shelf, then pick how to compare from a grid of three outcomes -- side by side (split view, shared positions, linked selection), difference painted on one graph (who moved up and down), and the table of differences in 24's Compare page. Or lift a shelf miniature onto the volume's side for a split view directly.
- **Layers on subsets.** Pinch-hold a set's hull, Style this: the grid shows style variants painted only on the set, over the layers already there, including "fade the others". When a new layer and an old one write the same property to the same nodes, a Reorder grid shows the two orders as they would look ("community color on top" against "ring highlight on top"), and you pick the one that is right.
- **Recipes.** In the History gallery, hold the first step of a run of steps and pinch the last with the other hand (the same Between gesture; one-handed, Between on the bar), then choose Save as recipe; the name comes from name tiles or the keyboard. Recipes are a family in the catalog's All list. Replaying one on new data opens a grid of candidate input mappings ("weight: none", "weight: trade_volume", "group by: community"), each tile showing the recipe's result under that mapping.
- **Joining tables and importing data.** Through 24's Project window: open from the laptop inbox or the headset browser's file picker. When the format or separator is ambiguous, a grid shows the file parsed several ways (comma, semicolon, tab; header row or not), each tile a miniature graph plus its first three rows, so the right parse is obvious. Column mapping shows the likeliest mappings of id, source and target as graphs ("212 genes, 1 piece" against "1,034 nodes, 600 pieces" for a wrong pair), with 24's column-role list under the grid for anything unusual. Joining an expression table shows candidate key columns as tiles with match counts ("gene_symbol: 18,402 of 18,610 rows match", "ensembl_id: 0 match").

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor places it in its family in All. Its tile in a suggested grid is drawn from its suggested style, or, if it has none, painted by its result field's kind (a number per node as a sequential color, a group as categorical colors). Each enumeration option yields one tile per value; each number is sampled at quantiles of its declared range, by decade if logarithmic; a toggle is two tiles; an attribute option is the 8 likeliest compatible columns as tiles, All for the rest; a choose-several option is its likeliest subsets as tiles with a checklist in Exact; a node option is asked first by a "Point at it, or Find" prompt; text is typed in Exact. Options marked advanced, or with no probed effect, wait behind "show hidden options". Its result is read by field kind. Nothing in the scheme changes.
- **A new layout.** It appears as a tile in the Lay out grid, drawn on this graph; its options are bracketed two at a time, largest probed effect first, with the rest in Exact.
- **A new object type.** It declares its verbs (up to seven on its ring), and for each verb whether it opens a grid (it has options with a visible effect), a before and after pair (it destroys something) or a window. Its miniature on the shelf is whatever it paints on the graph.
- **A third-party plugin.** "Biology: enrichment" becomes a family in All; its gene-list option is text, so it opens in Exact with paste, typing, dictation or the laptop inbox, and its two number options are bracketed like any others. The family joins the suggested grid only when its declared preconditions hold.
- **What graphty-element must offer.** Drawing a variant -- a style layer, a run's result, a filter or a layout that is not applied -- into a miniature, and showing it on the big graph during a Peek, without touching the committed state or the undo history. That is a capability any consumer of graphty-element would want for previews, so it belongs in the element, not in the app.
- **The practical limit.** Cost and visibility. A grid of 8 variants is 8 runs: fine for PageRank on thousands of nodes, too slow for betweenness on 50,000. Slow verbs show the cost light first, then compute variants on a sample (marked on every tile), lazily as you peek, or one by one with progress. And some differences cannot be seen at tile size: two Louvain resolutions that differ in three small groups look alike, so tiles fall back to numbers, which is a form with pictures.

### Why it should work

- Choosing among rendered outcomes is a tested way to set parameters people cannot reason about: Marks et al., "Design Galleries" (SIGGRAPH 1997), generated outputs spread across parameter space for browsing; Koyama, Sato and Goto, "Sequential Gallery for Interactive Visual Design Optimization" (ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2020), showed that picking repeatedly from a grid of variants converges on a good setting in fewer steps than sliders.
- Keeping alternatives side by side supports exploration: Terry and Mynatt, "Side Views" (UIST 2002), previews of commands at several settings before applying; Terry, Mynatt, Nakakoji and Yamamoto, "Parallel Paths" (CHI 2004); Hartmann et al., "Juxtapose" (UIST 2008), parallel alternatives with linked parameter tuning, where people explored more and got better results.
- Recommendation galleries work for data: Wongsuphasawat et al., "Voyager" (IEEE TVCG 2016), browsing a gallery of suggested charts widened what analysts looked at; Virtualitics' Smart Mapping (docs.virtualitics.com/ai-analytics/smart-mapping) ships suggested mappings in a VR-capable analytics tool.
- Vary subtle and Vary strong are shipped and used by millions: Midjourney's Variations (docs.midjourney.com) built a creative tool on "four results, pick and vary". Photoshop's Variations dialog (through CS6) did the same for color correction, with the current pick in the center.
- The tile cross is a marking menu: Kurtenbach and Buxton (CHI 1993 and 1994) showed that labeled directions shown after a pause teach novices the same flick experts make without looking.
- Small multiples are the oldest argument for comparison in place (Tufte, "The Visual Display of Quantitative Information", 1983), and recognition beats recall (Nielsen's usability heuristics, 1994): picking a picture needs no knowledge of what "damping" means.
- Exposure bracketing on every camera, and the optometrist's "which is better, one or two?", are everyday precedents for narrowing a value by comparing outcomes.
- In a headset, large targets are what eyes and hands select reliably; tiles are 8 to 10 degrees wide, while nodes in a 400-node graph are under 1 degree. Moving work from the graph to the grid moves it to targets that every device can hit.

### What it would teach us

- How many picks does it take to reach a value people are satisfied with, against sliders (Hand Menu and Windows) and faders (Mixing Desk), and do they reach better values or just faster ones?
- Do people trust variants the system chose, or do they anchor on them and miss better values outside? How often do they open Exact or the rail?
- Can people see the differences between tiles at 9 to 11 cm for real graphs of 100, 1,000 and 10,000 nodes, and which differences need numbers instead?
- Is Peek on hold natural, and how often does a quick pinch pick a tile that was meant to be peeked (step 10)?
- Does the tile cross (subtle, strong, keep, exact) get learned as a flick, or do people wait for the labels every time, or want buttons?
- Is a before and after pair a good confirmation for destructive verbs, or do people pick "after" without looking?
- Does a history of pictures, with "revert only this step" shown as an outcome, beat a history of names for fixing an old mistake?
- What does a grid of 8 variants cost in compute and frame rate on each headset, and where does lazy or sampled computing feel too slow?
- Do first-time users reach a first analysis faster when the first session is only picks?

### Known risks

- **Compute cost.** Each tile is a run. Mitigation: a cost light before computing; variants on a sample (marked) or computed lazily as you peek; descriptors that mark an algorithm slow preview only on request; a 2x2 default, 3x3 on ask.
- **Frame rate.** Nine miniatures multiply what is drawn. Mitigation: flat tiles refreshed two per frame, lower detail, redraw only when the camera rests above 5,000 nodes; the big graph keeps full rate.
- **Invisible differences.** Many option changes do not show at tile size, and some not at all. Mitigation: merge look-alike tiles and say so; print the outcome numbers on every tile; set aside options with no probed effect; Exact always one gesture away.
- **Randomness masquerading as difference.** Louvain or a force layout differ between runs by seed alone, so tiles could differ for no reason. Mitigation: every tile in a grid uses the same seed, set by the app for that grid; Reshuffle seed is its own grid.
- **Anchoring and choice overload.** The system's choice of variants steers people, and nine pictures tire them. Mitigation: four tiles by default; the rail shows the whole range and where the tiles sit; Lock and Between let people go outside the proposals.
- **Peek against Pick.** A quick pinch commits when a peek was meant. Mitigation: picks are one named step each with Undo on the hand menu; Back inside a chain; a setting that makes Pick require hold-and-release (peek first, always), on by default in the first session.
- **Gesture load.** Quick pinch, hold, the four-armed cross, the second-hand pinch and the rim are five things to learn. Mitigation: the cross labels its arms whenever the hand pauses; arms that do not apply are dimmed; every cross action is also on the grid bar or the tile's Exact form; the tour teaches them in the first five picks.
- **Neck and attention.** A grid beside the graph pulls the eyes sideways for every choice. Mitigation: the grid sits 25 degrees off center and lower; Peek shows the variant on the big graph so you can look there; the grid closes on every pick; a setting puts it under the volume for seated use.
- **The grid is not everything.** Tables, text, rules and files are not choosable by sight, so half the areas run through 24's windows. Mitigation: the scheme is honest about it: the grid replaces parameter entry, choice and confirmation, not reading and writing; the windows are the same everywhere so nothing is learned twice.
- **Depends on an element capability.** Variants need graphty-element to draw uncommitted styles, results, filters and layouts into miniatures. Mitigation: this is a general preview capability that belongs in the element for every consumer; until it lands, the scheme cannot be built honestly in the app.
- **Reproducibility.** Choosing by eye can hide the exact values. Mitigation: values are printed on every tile, every pick writes its exact values to the journal, and the reader's "how made" line states them.
- **Vision Pro gaze and Peek.** Without hover, looking alone shows nothing. Mitigation: Peek on hold; an enlarged label strip on the tile the pinch landed on.
