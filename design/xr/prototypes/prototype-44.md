**Prototype 44: Paired Browser** -- The Graph Browser stays the whole surface in the headset: every object has a page of facts and verbs, every mention is a link, forms are generated from descriptors, and Back is kept apart from Undo. One pairing, made once with a six-digit code, adds the three things that browser lacked. The laptop's graphty page holds a folder grant, lists that folder in the headset's Inbox (so a CSV on the laptop is picked from inside the headset), takes Save to laptop back into the same folder, and lends its keyboard to the field you are typing in. A paired phone, when present, is a touch slab with graphty's own keyboard, lift-to-commit and a mirrored highlight, and a pad that scrolls wide tables. Every table opens as a linked reader: rows sorted by a column, a histogram of that column beside them and one cut handle across both, with a wide sheet under the graph for 10 or more columns. Without the phone or the laptop, the in-scene keyboard, paged tables and the headset's own downloads remain, so nothing needs either device to be reachable. One input rule protects the project: only a deliberate, free hand can change it. A hand that is typing is not a pointer, a pinch-drag on a node turns the graph instead of moving the node, and buttons that throw work away (Stop, Delete, Remove) need the hold ring. Every project has one home where its current copy lives, and a replayed recipe always makes a new project.

### Pairing, the Inbox and the Outbox

**The pairing (specified as part of the design, not deferred).**

| Part                                                                        | How it works                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Making a pair, once                                                         | In the headset browser's 2D page (before Enter VR, with the room visible) or on the Devices page in VR, Pair a device shows "graphty.app/pair -- 482 913". On the laptop or phone, open graphty.app/pair and type the six digits. The code lasts 10 minutes, works once, and is replaced after 5 wrong tries. Each side then makes a key pair (WebCrypto, private key not extractable, kept in the browser's storage) and the two keep each other's public key, so the pair survives restarts and later sessions need no code. Pairing is done where you can see both screens, which is why it happens outside the immersive session or through passthrough; it never asks you to read the laptop from inside VR.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| The connection                                                              | A small pairing service introduces the two pages (a Cloudflare Worker with one Durable Object per device pair; graphty's repository already deploys a Worker). The pages then talk over a WebRTC data channel, encrypted end to end (DTLS). On the same Wi-Fi the data goes directly; on networks that block devices from reaching each other, it goes through a TURN relay on port 443, still encrypted, and the Devices page says "through relay". The service sees only that two device ids want to talk, never a file name or a key typed. Two settings keep data off outside machines, for analysts whose case data may not cross them: Connection: Direct only (on the Devices page of each device) turns the relay off, so data goes only device to device on the local network and the pair simply does not connect where that is blocked; and the pairing service is one small program an organization can run on its own server (the same code packaged to run under Node), whose address is set once in graphty's Settings on all three devices, which is the normal case where graphty itself is hosted inside the organization. ### Tables: the linked reader and the wide sheet

**The linked reader.** Every ranked result, attribute page, table page and filter form that reads one numeric column shows that column two ways side by side: on the right, the rows sorted by it; on the left, a histogram of it, drawn upright with high values at the top in the same direction as the sort. One cut handle crosses both. Dragging it on the histogram sets a value ("PageRank at least 0.070"); dragging it in the rows snaps between rows ("Top 5"); either way, a leader joins the value on the histogram to the gap between two rows ("between Tornabuoni 0.071 and Ridolfi 0.069"), and the line under the reader counts what is above the cut ("5 of 16 families, 4 marriages"). A ray resting on a row lights its node and its bar; resting on a bar lights its rows (scrolling them into view) and their nodes. Dragging across bars brushes a band, whose small bar offers Add these to basket, Filter to these..., Style these..., Keep as set.... The reader's header has a Bins stepper (generated like any number field) and a Log scale switch. Wherever a threshold, a top N or a node slot is filled, the same reader is used: a form's "Pick in a table" opens it on any column, and pinching a row fills the waiting slot.

**The wide sheet.** Open wide on any table page (or on a form whose rows are wide: column mapping, merge, compare) moves the table to the wide sheet below the graph: the name column and the sort column frozen at the left with the sort column's histogram beside them, then up to 9 more columns at the same text size, 15 rows. The column chooser pins any columns next to the sort column, so "sorted by one, comparing three others" is four columns side by side with no scrolling. Scrolling: the phone's pad (one finger, with inertia), the thumbstick of the controller pointing at it (both axes; while the ray is on the sheet the stick never flicks Undo), or the rails along its right and bottom edges (pinch-drag); Go to row takes a row number or rank on the keypad. Sorting: pinch a header (again to reverse). Rows are links, as on the page. The panel's page keeps a one-line summary of the sheet and Close wide. The sheet opens with the frozen and pinned columns centered in front of you. Sheet place is a Settings row: below the graph (the default) or at eye level in the graph's place, with the graph shrunk above it while the sheet is open, for longer table work without holding the head down. The sheet is a WebXR quad layer on Quest for sharp text, a high-resolution texture on Vision Pro and Galaxy XR.

### Four kinds of "these nodes"

| Word            | What it is                                                                                                     | Changed by                                                | Taken back by                       |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| In view         | what the graph draws strongly around the current page, plus context up to the dial                             | links, stubs, the "Show more around here" dial            | Back                                |
| In basket       | the selection                                                                                                  | Add to basket, Collect, a cut or band, Region page, Empty | the basket page, Earlier baskets    |
| Showing         | what the filter chain keeps                                                                                    | filter steps, Hide                                        | Undo, or turning the step off       |
| This view shows | in a split view, a view-only condition for one tab ("membership is control or both"), shown in the tab's title | the tab's "This view shows..." form                       | the same form, or closing the split |

Hide on a node, edge or basket page adds a canvas-only step ("Hide Medici"): it leaves the Showing count but algorithms still see it; "Filter what algorithms see" (the default for Filter...) changes both, and the Showing chip adds "(algorithms see 5)" when the counts differ. In view, the canvas-only steps and This view shows need graphty-element to offer a view-only visibility apart from its filters, per view.

"Fade others" is none of the four: it changes how things look, not which things count. It adds a style layer ("Fade all but Ring 7 and 1 hop: 31") to the layer list, so Undo takes it back, the layer list removes or reorders it, and pictures include it like any other layer.

**Comparing two conditions today.** Two graph views side by side need graphty-element to draw two views in one immersive session, which it cannot yet do (a page holds one immersive session and the element owns its scene). Until it can, "Open side by side" is replaced by a Condition switch on the comparison's page and in its tab title: Control, Disease, Both. Each choice applies a style layer that draws the elements outside that condition at zero opacity, so positions and camera stay exactly the same, and one pinch flips between the two networks; node pages still show each value for both. This shows the control network alone and the disease network alone, one at a time, in the same place: it is a flip, not a side-by-side view, and the file does not claim more until the element capability exists.

2. **Get oriented.**

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor puts it in its family on the Algorithms page (a new family is a new section with a jump link), in the address bar under both names, and in the Actions group. Its form is generated from its option list; a logarithmic option is drawn as decade plus mantissa when its descriptor marks the scale or its range spans three or more decades. Its result fields become attribute pages read by kind: a number per node gets the linked reader, so Select top, Filter with a cut, Paint and Compare work with no new code.
- **A new layout.** A link on the Layouts page; its options form generated; a node option becomes a node slot; a live layout gets Stop for free.
- **A new data reader or format writer.** A reader's file types join the Inbox's listed kinds the moment it is registered, and its options form appears on the file page; a writer joins every Export form's format list and the Save to laptop path with its options form.
- **A new option kind.** It needs one new field in the form generator, once, for every descriptor; until then it falls back to a text field with the descriptor's description and default, or "Set this on the 2D page", never to nothing. The phone and laptop need nothing: they only send keys and touches.
- **A new object type.** It declares a page template: facts, verbs (each with an input descriptor that becomes a form), link relations, and whether it can go in the basket. It becomes a page kind, the address bar indexes it, every mention becomes a link, and it can carry notes and be a history step. The chrome does not change.
- **A third-party plugin.** "Biology: enrichment" with four algorithms becomes a family section; the option taking a list of gene identifiers is a node-list slot filled from the basket or from a gene list file in the laptop folder (unmatched names listed as links); its two numbers are steppers; its result is read by kind.
- **A result that is a table, not a column per node.** An enrichment result (term, gene count, FDR, genes) is declared in the plugin's result descriptor as a table: its columns and their kinds. It opens on a generic table page, read by kind with no page template: sortable, Open wide, a number column gets the linked reader's histogram and cut, a column of node lists shows each cell as a link ("14 genes") with Add these to basket and Style these..., and each row is a link to a row page with its values and those verbs. The plugin author writes no XR code; only a new object with its own verbs needs a page template.
- **An object that should be pinched in the graph.** The page template also declares its scene presence (none, at a node, at a region, or around a set's hull); graphty draws a pickable marker of that kind, so a new object type needs no hand-made marker.
- **A data source that queries a service.** It joins Home's Sources list from its descriptor; its options form is generated (a gene-list option is a node-list slot filled from the basket or a list file in the Inbox).
- **The practical limit.** Everything passes through a page or the wide sheet: reading speed is the ceiling, not the catalog. About 12 rows show on a page and 15 by 11 on the sheet; an input that is spatial by nature (a camera path drawn by hand) fits a form poorly. The paired devices add no surface a plugin must design for; they make keys, files and scrolling better for everything at once.

### Why it should work

- The browser model is the most practiced interface there is, and its navigation is measured: Back was about 41 percent of navigation actions (Catledge and Pitkow, "Characterizing browsing strategies in the World-Wide Web", 1995) and 58 percent of page visits were revisits (Tauscher and Greenberg, International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 1997). A graph analysis is mostly revisiting the hub and the ranking. The address bar that finds and never runs follows Chrome's omnibox (2008).
- Pairing a device by a short code typed on the device that has a keyboard is a shipped, standard flow: the OAuth 2.0 Device Authorization Grant (RFC 8628) behind TV sign-in codes, and PairDrop (pairdrop.net), a browser page that pairs devices with a six-digit code and then sends files between them over WebRTC, across networks, with no install.
- Reading and writing a local folder from a web page is shipped: Chrome's File System Access API (Chrome 86, 2020) is what lets vscode.dev open and save a local project folder, and Chrome 122 (2024) added "Allow on every visit" so a granted folder survives restarts.
- Peer-to-peer files between browser pages are standard WebRTC (data channels, RFC 8831) and have been shipped in Snapdrop and its successors since about 2015.
- Lift-to-commit on a touch screen has the fewest errors of the classic strategies (Potter, Weldon and Shneiderman, "Improving the accuracy of touch screens: an experimental evaluation of three strategies", CHI 1988), and iOS ships it as an accessibility setting ("Use Final Touch Location"). A real surface under a virtual panel made 2D work in VR faster and more accurate than a floating panel (Lindeman, Sibert and Hahn, "Hand-held windows", IEEE VR 1999); a phone as a VR touch and typing surface was studied in TrackCap (Mohr et al., CHI 2019) and HoVR-Type (Kim and Kim, 2016).
- Touch typists keep much of their speed on a keyboard they cannot see: Walker, Li, Kristensson and Vertanen ("Efficient typing on a visually occluded physical keyboard", CHI 2017) and Knierim et al. (CHI 2018, typing on a physical keyboard in VR) both measured it, which is the case for lending the laptop's keys to a person seated at it.
- A sorted table beside its distribution, with one control that cuts both, follows Table Lens (Rao and Card, CHI 1994), dynamic queries (Ahlberg, Williamson and Shneiderman, CHI 1992) and brushing (Becker and Cleveland, Technometrics 1987): reading a threshold against the rows it cuts is faster and safer than reading a bare number.
- The panel-and-slab split keeps what each device is good at: the headset for the graph and reading, real keys or real glass for text, the laptop's disk for files. No single surface has to be good at all of them.

### What it would teach us

- Whether the pairing is a one-time cost people accept, and how often reconnecting fails in homes, labs and offices (direct versus relay, laptop asleep, tab closed).
- Whether people pick a laptop file from the Inbox without hesitation, and whether the folder grant (one folder, write but not delete) feels safe.
- Whether touch typists type notes and reports on the lent laptop keys at close to desk speed in full VR, and how non-touch-typists fare; how often the laptop page loses focus.
- Accuracy and speed of typing on the phone slab with lift-to-commit, against the in-scene keyboard, for an id, a name and a sentence.
- Whether three possible keyboards confuse anyone, or whether "keys: phone" in the field's corner is enough.
- Whether the linked reader makes thresholds and top N faster and less error-prone than a value field, and whether people read the cut's neighbors ("between Tornabuoni and Ridolfi").
- Whether the wide sheet's 30-degree downward look and 60-degree span are comfortable for 10 minutes of table work, and which columns people pin.
- Whether the expert who went back to the 2D page for text- and table-heavy work (clustering and labeling a protein network) stays in the headset now.
- Whether people still look at the 3D graph when pages, keys and tables are this good.

### Known risks

- **It is still a desktop app in a headset.** Better text, tables and files make the panel even more attractive, and the graph may become decoration. Mitigation: the graph stays a link surface (pinch to open, hover to name), rows and bars light their nodes, filter previews draw on the graph, and stubs grow the graph in place.
- **The pairing service must exist and run.** graphty.app is a static site; the Inbox, Outbox, phone slab and lent keyboard all need the small pairing service and a TURN relay. Mitigation: the service only introduces devices (a Worker with one Durable Object per pair) and carries no files except through the encrypted relay; if it is down, a pair that already connected on the same network reconnects from the addresses it remembers, and everything else falls back to the unpaired paths, which the pages already show. Its cost is part of the build, not deferred.
- **The laptop page is a single point of failure for files.** If the tab is closed or the laptop sleeps, the folder disappears mid-session. Mitigation: the Inbox says so in words with the time; files already fetched stay in the headset; saves wait in the Outbox and are written on reconnect; Keep a copy in the headset on any Inbox file.
- **Browser support on the laptop.** The folder grant and Keyboard Lock are Chromium only. Mitigation: Safari and Firefox laptops get Add files, downloads and an unlocked lent keyboard, stated on the Devices page; the headset never pretends the folder exists.
- **Security of a paired folder.** A paired device can read and write one folder. Mitigation: a single-use 10-minute code typed on the laptop itself, device keys that cannot be exported, one folder and its subfolders only, no deletes, replace only after a confirm, Revoke on both sides, a device list with last-seen times, and an end-to-end encrypted channel the service cannot read.
- **Lent keys going elsewhere.** If the laptop page loses focus, the person in VR types into some other app without knowing. Mitigation: fullscreen with Keyboard Lock on Chromium; the headset accepts laptop keys only while the laptop page reports focus, and says at once when it does not; lending is an explicit act and ends on Leave VR. On Safari and Firefox this risk is real and is stated when lending.
- **Aiming at a phone you cannot see.** Mitigation: lift-to-commit with the lit key, mirrored under the focused field so aiming and reading happen in one place, 12 mm targets, completions that shorten words. If phone typing proves too slow, the phone still serves as the table pad and the laptop keys carry text.
- **Typing hands firing pinches.** Touch typing and tapping a phone make thumb-to-index contacts that hand tracking reads as pinches, with rays pointing into the panel and the sheet. Mitigation: the typing pause (no pinch from either hand while keys or touches arrive and for 1 s after), no pinch from a hand at the phone's glass, the holding hand's selects always dropped, and "pinches paused while typing" on the field. The cost: a deliberate pinch made within 1 s of the last key is ignored, so finishing a field is Enter or Tab, or a pause; the pause length is a Settings row.
- **Three keyboards.** Mitigation: one rule (keys come from whatever is live), the source named in the field's corner, and the in-scene keyboard always opening when the others are lost.
- **The wide sheet costs the neck.** Below the graph it sits 30 degrees down and spans about 60 degrees. Mitigation: it opens only on request, centered on the frozen and pinned columns; Sheet place can put it at eye level in the graph's place for long table work; text size and sheet height are Settings; Close wide returns to the page.
- **Two copies of a project.** Continue here, Recent and the Inbox can all reach the same project. Mitigation: one home per project, shown on its page and in Recent; opening a laptop-home project always fetches the laptop file; a headset copy with edits the laptop file lacks opens a "two versions" page with both kept; Save as moves the home to the new file; Return to laptop writes only that project's own file; Replay always makes a new project. Headset-only projects on Vision Pro can be cleared by Safari after 7 days unused, and Recent says so with Move home to laptop folder.
- **graphty-element capabilities this needs.** A view-only visibility per view (for In view and canvas-only Hide), runs and layouts that report the seed they used (for replay), and two graph views in one immersive session (for side by side). All belong in graphty-element, so every consumer gets them. Until then, replay marks unseeded steps "may differ", and comparing two networks is the Condition switch in one view, a flip rather than side by side.
- **Build size.** An in-scene browser UI toolkit (pages, forms, tables, the wide sheet, an in-scene keyboard), a laptop page that serves a folder and relays keys, a phone page of three control layouts, and the pairing service. Mitigation: the phone and laptop pages are small and fixed (they never render graphty's panels), and the in-scene toolkit is the one every panel design needs.
- **Frame rate.** Hover hit-testing at 10,000 nodes, two textures (panel and sheet) on Vision Pro, split views. Mitigation: hover tested against the In view set through a spatial index, texture updates throttled while scrolling, the sheet drawn only while open, the second split view at reduced detail on Quest.
- **Small node targets and stray moves.** Mitigation: the Which one? card (8 nearest nodes and edges, More) when the hover cannot settle within 0.5 degrees, Collect for scattered picks, frame on open only when off view, press on release with slide-off to cancel, a drag never moving a node (Move and pin is on the hold list), a second hand's pinch ending the first hand's drag, a sticky ray and Ray smoothing for tremor, the data-changing verbs below a gap on every page, and every move a named Undo.
- **Work thrown away by one pinch.** Stop, Delete, Remove and Clear all need the hold ring and name what they discard; Stop sits on the status line beside its work, away from the Basket and Collect switch. The cost is one hold where a pinch would do, on buttons used rarely.
- **A restricted network.** Analysts whose data may not cross an outside machine get Direct only and a self-hosted pairing service. Where an organization allows neither, the pairing does not exist for them, and files mean leaving VR for the headset's picker: this is a limit of the idea, not hidden here.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: only a deliberate, free hand can change the project.

- **A typing hand is not a pointer.** While keys arrive from the laptop or the phone, and for 1 s after the last one, no pinch from either hand counts. A hand whose fingertip is at the phone's glass never pinches. The field reads "pinches paused while typing". The cost, stated under Known risks: a pinch within 1 s of the last key is ignored.
- **A pinch-drag on a node never moves it.** It turns the graph, as a drag in empty space does. Move and pin is now on the node's hold list (and a node page verb), and the next drag carries the node. A second hand's pinch ends whatever the first hand was doing and starts the two-hand grab.
- **Buttons that discard work need the hold ring:** Stop, Delete..., Remove..., Remove from list and Clear all. A plain pinch on one says "Hold to stop: batch, 2 of 4 done" and does nothing. Stop left the toolbar and now sits on the status line beside the work it stops.
