**Prototype 45: Editable Record** -- One record of sentences is the history, the recipe and the undo: every act, from whatever surface issued it, lands in the record as a sentence of typed slots that names its source; any old sentence's slot can be changed in place, with what will replay and what will break counted and previewed before it applies; and a bracket of sentences becomes a recipe whose slots refill next week.

A hybrid. From Search Everything (25) it takes the command as a sentence of typed slots with a preview line of counts and cost. From Cutting Room (23), fixing an old step in place with a ripple bar that counts replays and breaks. From Hand of Cards (13), lighting everything that depends on a step before you remove it, and removing only that step. From Proofreader's Slate (16), strike and restore, and a bracket that turns a span of steps into a recipe. From One Question at a Time (30), the Check card before anything costly or destructive, and "change one answer and re-run, keep both". From Findings Inbox (42), every change carrying the source that caused it. It drops 25's pie menus, 23's tracks and playhead, 13's card fan and 16's stylus: the record is one list of plain sentences, read and edited with the same pinch as everything else.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph stands at chest height about 75 cm away on a thin ring about 45 cm across. The drawer opens between the record and the graph, joined to the chip it serves by a leader line, so it never covers the record row you are editing and never covers the graph. The reader panel stands on the dominant side at the same distance, mirrored. A setting moves the record below the graph instead (tilted up, the lowest-effort place for a seated reader) and puts the drawer to its side; another swaps sides for left-handed use. "Bring here" on the bar's More re-centers everything on your current heading and height, seated or standing. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S and Samsung Galaxy XR with hands (the system's hand ray and pinch) or controllers; Apple Vision Pro with look-and-pinch (Safari's transient pointer), and PS VR2 Sense controllers where Safari exposes them. Every core act is a pinch, a pinch-drag or a two-hand pinch on the ring, so the scheme needs only standard select events and target rays; it works seated, one-handed (the Add key replaces the held off-hand pinch, the ring's own Scale chip replaces two hands) and without voice. Text: graphty's in-scene keyboard, always in one place -- centered on your heading, 15 to 20 degrees below eye level, between you and the graph's lower edge, keys at least 2 degrees wide -- whichever slot is focused (letters with a digit row, a caret, completions from the data, the record and common English words), a paired keyboard where the browser passes keys into the immersive page (to be checked per device), dictation where a recognizer exists. Files: an Inbox and Outbox paired with graphty's 2D page on the laptop, with a folder the laptop grants once that the headset can browse mid-session (specified below), or, before that channel exists, the file opened on the 2D page before entering VR.

### The record

The record is the project's single history. Every act that changes the project lands in it as one line. Reading (inspecting, opening a reader, scrolling a table) and moving the view (turning, scaling, framing, Back) do not.

**How a row is laid out.** A row is about 90 characters at the minimum text size. The first text row holds the number, the gutter, the verb, the target chip, every chip whose value is not a default, and the source tag at the right; defaults fold into one "+2" chip. Results and counts take a second, indented row ("-- Medici highest, 0.145") only while the line is one of the newest three or its strip is open; otherwise they fold. A sentence whose non-default chips still overflow wraps once more; nothing is cut off with an ellipsis, so the values and the source that tell look-alike lines apart are always on screen. So the panel shows 12 lines when they are short and about 6 when every line carries a result, and "Fold results" in the record's header keeps every line to one row.

**Finding a line in a long record.** The record's header holds a search field (any word or value in any sentence: "0.07", "Ridolfi", "louvain"), kind chips (Runs, Filters, Styles, Selections, Sets, Notes, Data, Outputs), "About this" (only the lines that touched the selected thing: "what was done to Medici?"), "Hide struck", "Hide edit lines" and "Hide superseded" (lines with a newer Keep both twin). Brackets fold. The thumbstick or a pinch-drag scrolls; "Newest" jumps back to the foot.

**What depends on what.** Each sentence is a command record that graphty-element keeps: its parameters, what it read (attributes, results, the selection, sets, the filters in force) and what it wrote. A line uses an earlier line when it read something that line wrote. The record draws those links; the element computes them, so any consumer of the element gets the same facts.

**Results are named by the line that made them.** Two runs of one algorithm never share a name. An attribute picker lists "PageRank (line 3)" and "PageRank (line 9)"; a Paint, a filter or a formula reads one specific line's result, and its chip says which ("Painted [PageRank of line 3]"). When only one run of a kind exists, the chip still carries the line number, so a later Keep both cannot silently redirect it.

**What a slot is bound to.** Every slot holds one of two things, and its chip shows which:

**What left the project is a binding too.** An output line -- an exported picture, table or graph file, Send to laptop, Continue on laptop, a note, a callout, a label, a methods paragraph, a report -- records what it read at the moment it was made: the members of each set it showed (by id), the values and line of each run it showed, the dates of each window, the palette and layers in force, and for a note, callout or label its target as it was (a node and whether it was shown, a set's members, the values of the run it was placed on) -- never the whole project, so a note on the Medici is untouched by a filter that keeps the Medici. An output line never runs again on a replay: a file that left is never rewritten and a note's text is never changed. Instead, a replay that changes anything an output read flags that output "changes meaning", and the Check card stops on it with the difference in words:

| Output                             | The card reads                                                                                           | Choices (first is the default)                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A picture, table or file that left | "Line 18's picture went out Oct 3 with Ring 7 at 23 accounts, window Sep 3 to Oct 3. Ring 7 now has 26." | Leave it as it went out (the line gets an "out of date: Ring 7 now 26" mark, kept in the record and in the methods) / Export again (a new file named with today's date beside the old one; nothing is overwritten)                   |
| A note, callout or label           | "Line 17's note was written about 23 accounts; Ring 7 now has 26."                                       | Keep as written (the marker in the graph and the notes list carry "written Oct 3, about 23 accounts") / Reread it (the note opens in the drawer with its old numbers and the new ones side by side, ready to edit as sentence tiles) |
| A methods paragraph or report      | "The methods sent Oct 3 describe degree above 20; the filter now says 15."                               | Leave it as sent (marked out of date) / Write it again (a new paragraph, dated, the old one kept)                                                                                                                                    |

The ripple bar counts outputs before any replay runs, because what an output read is fixed when it is made: "reaches 2 outputs: line 17's note, line 18's picture". Whenever an edit reaches an output, Keep both is the Check card's default button, not Apply (see Keep both below), so the record the case file came from stays as it was beside the edited copy.

A pick made from a result -- a group table row, a ranked reader's top rows, a histogram bar, a legend entry for a result -- is recorded as a derivation by default, with "keep these exact members" one pinch away on the chip's picker. A pick made by pointing at nodes in the graph is by hand, but the record also notes which result was on screen when it was made (the layers and filters in force, the reader open): those are what the pick read.

**What the replay checks.** Whenever a line replays -- after an edited slot, a strike, a re-pointed Open line or a recipe run -- derivations re-evaluate, and every by-hand binding that read a line whose result changed is flagged, as is every rolling date whose dates moved and every output whose reads changed. Before the replay the ripple bar counts the by-hand bindings and rolling dates downstream as "may change meaning N" (each listed by line, so the person knows where the replay may stop before pressing Apply) and the outputs as "reaches N outputs"; after it, the third count resolves to "changes meaning N", and the Check card stops on each one: "Line 11 picked group 7 of line 9. After the replay, group 7 is 31 different accounts. -- Re-pick (the picker opens over the new result) / Keep the old members / Follow the group holding most of the old members". Nothing replays past a flagged line until it is answered or the whole card is applied with "Keep all as they were". An unseeded run (Louvain, MCL) records the seed it actually drew in its line ("[seed 48121, drawn at run]"), so a replay whose inputs did not change gives the same groups; one whose inputs changed may number them differently, which is exactly what the flag catches.

**Where a sentence starts.** Typed or picked in the line, its target slot starts on the whole graph. Written from an object's strip, it starts on that object. Either way the target is the first chip and the preview line counts what it covers; the target picker lists Whole graph, Selection (with its count and its line), sets, runs and earlier selections.

**Slot kinds.** Each option of each command, built in or a plugin's, is one of these; the picker is chosen from the descriptor, never laid out by hand. Target (point at a lit object, or a list: whole graph, selection, sets, runs, earlier selections (folded steps included), added networks, a rule written as clause rows, then search; a node hidden by a filter still appears, marked, and fills the slot). Many targets (tick search rows, a set, the selection, or a pasted or typed list with a match count: "38 of 40 genes found; 2 unknown"). Number (stepper with the descriptor's step, slider with labeled detents, default marked). Logarithmic number (exponent stepper, 1e-4 to 1e-10). Number list (a sweep). Range (two thumbs over a histogram). Date or date range (a timeline with day, week and month steppers on each end). Choice of few (segmented buttons). Choice of many (a grouped, filterable list with swatches or icons). Values of an attribute (a checklist with counts). Attribute (a grouped list with each column's kind and range). Rule (clause rows of attribute, comparison and value, with AND, OR and NOT grouping). Formula (attribute chips and a short function list, with a live column preview). Toggle. Color or palette (a swatch strip). Text (the keyboard, with suggested names as chips). File (Inbox, URL, recent files). An option of a kind none of these covers renders as a text slot with the descriptor's description, so it is never unreachable.

**The Check card.** It replaces the picker in the drawer whenever Go or Apply would replay a run over 2 seconds, break any later sentence, remove data, merge, act on more than 1,000 elements, or come from a recipe or the assistant. It lists: what changes (each changed slot, old value to new); "Settings we chose for you" (every default, each with Change); the ripple ("replays lines 5, 6 and 9; leaves 7 and 8 alone; breaks line 9: it reads PageRank, which this removes"); each binding that changes meaning, with Re-pick, Keep the old members and Follow; the cost; and what the project gains or loses ("adds 1 result, 1 layer"). Its footer is Apply, Keep both and Cancel. Keep both is the default (first and highlighted) whenever the ripple reaches an output, and Apply is the default otherwise; the footer names what each does ("Keep both: the Oct 3 lines, the note and the picture stay as they were; the edited copy lands at the foot"). Any Change link reopens that slot's picker and comes back to the card.

### Editing an old sentence

1. Pinch a slot chip in any line. Its picker opens in the drawer. The record marks the line as being edited, and the ripple bar appears along the record's edge from that line to the foot: "replays 3 -- breaks 0 -- may change meaning 0 -- reaches 0 outputs -- leaves 2 alone", with the lines that will replay edged amber and any output it reaches marked with an outbox glyph.
2. Change the value. Counts on the slider and the ripple bar update on every tick ("the filter at line 6 will keep 6 of 16"); they come from a sorted copy of the column the filter reads, so a tick costs a lookup, not a replay. The graph previews live (ghosted outlines on the elements that will appear or leave) only when the replay fits in the frame (about 8 ms, or done incrementally across frames); otherwise the graph previews when the value settles for half a second or on release, with the replay running in a worker while the world keeps drawing, and a replay over 2 seconds shows the new value as a ghost chip and the estimate ("replay about 6 s") until Apply. Which lines may change meaning and which outputs the edit reaches are known at once, from the bindings and the outputs' recorded reads; whether each one actually changes meaning is known once the dependent replays have run in the worker, and the count resolves on the bar as they finish.
3. Release. If the edit replays nothing and costs under half a second (true for most edits to the newest line, and for old notes, moves and colors nothing reads), it applies on release, like an "adjust last operation" panel. Otherwise the picker's footer waits for Apply, Keep both or Cancel, and Apply opens the Check card when any of its conditions hold.
4. Applied, the line shows its new value with the edited mark; an edit line lands at the foot ("Changed line 4: damping 0.85 to 0.75 -- replayed 2"), so Undo takes it back first; the dependent lines flash in order as they replay. A line that breaks turns gray, stays in place, and says what it is missing with a fix verb ("reads attribute 'risk', which line 2 no longer loads -- Pick another attribute / Strike"). Nothing after it is deleted.

**Keep both** leaves the old line as it was and writes a copy at the foot with the changed value ("Ran PageRank ... [damping 0.75] -- twin of 4"), linked to its twin, and offers Compare on the new run's strip. The copy runs on the project as it stood at its twin's position, not on the state at the foot, and its reader's header says so ("on the network as of line 4"). Its result is named by its own line, so every later line keeps reading the twin; the footer says so ("Lines 5 and 7 keep reading line 4 -- Switch them to the new run"), and Switch is an ordinary edit with its ripple. A sweep ("0.5, 1, 1.5, 2" in one number slot) writes one copy per value inside one bracket, with a Compare on the bracket.

**Keep both on a line with dependents** (a filter, a time window, an Open line, a rule) copies the edited line and the chain that replays from it to the foot as one folded bracket ("Filter of line 12 edited Oct 10 -- 4 lines"), run on the project as it stood at the edited line; the original lines, their results, their sets and every output made from them stay exactly as they were. Sets in the copy take a dated name ("Ring 7 (Oct 10)"), changeable on the card. Output lines are not copied (nothing is exported or noted twice); the bracket's strip offers Export again and Note for the new state. Compare on the bracket against its original span gives a difference table (members gained and lost per set, rank changes per run). This is also how two time windows are compared (Keep both on the window line, then Compare) and how next month's file is read beside last month's (Keep both on the Open line's file chip).

**What-ifs never change what later lines read.** "Simulate removing [target]" writes a what-if line, marked with a dashed edge: it computes on the state at its position and shows its effect on the graph (removed elements ghosted, cut-off parts outlined) only while its strip or reader is open. Lines after it read the project as if it were not there, so a second scenario is never computed on top of the first. Make it real on its strip turns it into a removal sentence, through the Check card. Changing a what-if's target chip and Keep both is the next scenario in three pinches; Compare on a bracket of any number of what-ifs opens a scenario table (scenario, removed, cut off, pieces, the sum of a chosen attribute lost), exportable.

### Brackets and recipes

**Bracketing.** Pinch-drag the gutter from the first line to the last; or, with one hand and no drag, Bracket from here on the first line's strip, then pinch the last line's number. Struck lines inside a bracket stay out of anything the bracket does.

**Save as recipe** opens the recipe card in the drawer, generated from the bracketed sentences. Every slot is listed once, marked:

| Mark        | Meaning                                                                                                                                                 | Chosen by default for                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Keep        | Use this value every time                                                                                                                               | algorithm and layout options, palettes, thresholds                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Ask         | A hole the person fills before Run                                                                                                                      | files, a node or id picked by hand that does not depend on the recipe's own results, a column the next dataset may name differently                                                                                                                                            |
| Ask there   | A hole filled where the run reaches it: the run pauses at that sentence with its picker open over the fresh result ("Pick the group to keep as Ring 7") | any by-hand pick that read a result the recipe itself computes: a group picked from a group table, a node chosen off a ranked reader, a hand-made selection of top rows; free text from a note, callout or label, which starts empty with "Leave this out" beside the keyboard |
| Follows     | Comes from an earlier sentence in the recipe, re-evaluated on the new data                                                                              | every derivation ("largest group of line 5"), the run a Paint or a filter reads, the set a later sentence acts on                                                                                                                                                              |
| Next period | A date window that moves on by the recipe's period (day, week, month) from the last run's                                                               | date and time windows (pinned dates; a rolling window stays rolling)                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| Leave out   | Not repeated on the new data; listed on the card so it can be turned back on                                                                            | a Move, Pin or Hide of a node picked by hand, and a view's saved camera                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

What a bracket's lines become in a recipe: each line with its current values (an edit line is folded into the line it edited, never replayed as a step of its own); struck lines and what-ifs left out; output lines kept as steps that export or note again on the new data, never under last run's file name (the new file is named with the run number and date), and a note or callout among them marked Ask there, empty, as above.

Set and view names are Keep with the run number added ("Ring 7, run 6"), changeable on the card. The card shows the count of each mark at its top ("Keep 41 -- Ask 2 -- Ask there 3 -- Follows 12 -- Next period 1") and lists Ask and Ask there slots first, so a recipe of 60 sentences is checked by reading five rows, not a hundred.

Pinch a mark to change it. The name is suggested from the sentences ("PageRank, paint, keep top -- recipe"); accept it or type one. Saved recipes are in Recipes on the bar, in the line's search, and in Browse.

**Running a recipe** opens its Check card: the Ask slots first, each pre-filled with last run's value and a "Fill all from last time" key at the top; Next period slots already advanced ("Oct 3 to Oct 10, the week after last run"); a file hole listing the Inbox newest first, with any file whose columns match last run's marked "same columns as last time". Columns the new data lacks become Ask slots with a picker of the columns it has ("weight in the recipe is which column here?"). An Open line at the bracket's head, when its data is already open in the project, offers "Use the open data" instead of asking for a file. Ask there slots are listed with "you will pick this when the run gets there". Run writes the sentences into the record inside one bracket, each sourced "recipe <name>, run <n>", pausing at each Ask there slot; each line is editable afterward like any other. A run of a recipe on the same project can instead re-point the bracket's first sentence (the Open or Join) at the new file, so everything after it replays on the new data, the way a spreadsheet query refreshes; every by-hand binding that read a recomputed result then stops on the Check card as "changes meaning", the same as an edit. Re-pointing is an edit, so it offers Keep both too (the default when anything downstream left the project): the chain is copied onto the new file in one folded bracket and last month's lines, results and outputs stay, so "who is rising since last month" is Compare on the two PageRank lines -- the file chip, the file, Keep both, Apply, then Compare (about 6 acts plus one stop per hand pick), not an add-network-and-merge detour.

What this cannot do: when last week's judgment was made by eye ("this group looks like a ring"), no derivation reproduces it on new data. The record can only stop and ask the person to judge again over the new result; it will never carry the old judgment forward silently, and it cannot make it for them.

**Saved filters, style presets and methods** are brackets too: Save as filter is a recipe of the filter sentences in the bracket (it lists the other lines it left out), with a name and a description, applied, duplicated or deleted from the line's search; Save style preset is a recipe of style sentences, and applying a preset whose layers match layers already in force changes their palette and property slots in place (one edit per layer, with its ripple) instead of stacking new layers on top; Write methods turns the bracket into a methods paragraph from its sentences and values (struck lines left out), dated: every window is written with its dates, every unseeded run with its seed, and for any line edited after an output was made from it, a closing paragraph says which values produced which output ("The picture exported Oct 3 and the note on Ring 7 used degree above 20 and Sep 3 to Oct 3, when Ring 7 had 23 accounts; on Oct 10 the threshold became 15 and Ring 7 has 26"). It is shown in a reader with Send to laptop, and is itself an output line.

### Files between the laptop and the headset

The record works with files through an Inbox and Outbox paired with graphty's 2D page on the laptop. Pairing: Pair laptop on the bar's More shows a six-digit code in the headset; the person types it on the laptop's 2D page (the laptop is the side with a real keyboard). The two pages then talk over a WebRTC data channel; a small stateless signaling relay that graphty deploys (a few hundred lines, for example a Cloudflare Worker) introduces them and never sees file contents. Dropping a file on the 2D page puts it in the headset's Inbox; anything exported in the headset, and "Continue on laptop" for long text, lands in the 2D page's Outbox. At pairing, the 2D page also asks once for a folder ("Let the headset see this folder"; a directory handle, kept until revoked): from then on every file slot in the headset lists that folder and its subfolders, newest first, with "same columns as last time" marks, so a file wanted mid-session is fetched from the headset with no one at the laptop. The folder grant needs a Chromium browser on the laptop; on Safari or Firefox laptops the Inbox stays push-only, and a file not dropped before entering still needs the person to lift the headset or someone at the laptop. A pairing is remembered per pair of browsers until revoked on either side. Until the relay exists, the day-one path is: open the file on the 2D page before entering VR (the record's first line is then "Opened [file] -- source: 2D page", and everything after it can still be edited and replayed in the headset), or use the headset browser's own file picker (leave VR, pick, re-enter; the record and the session survive). Exports before the relay land in the headset's downloads, not on the laptop, and Send to laptop and Continue on laptop do not exist until it does. The relay's cost is real and is counted under feasibility: a signaling service graphty deploys and runs, WebRTC on both pages, pairing and revocation screens on both, and a check per browser that the data channel survives entering and leaving an immersive session.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor's plain name, technical name and aliases become search names; its category places it in Browse (a new category becomes a new family); its options become slots, each by kind, so an option with a logarithmic range gets the exponent stepper with no author work; its result fields choose readers. Its runs are lines: they can be edited, kept both, struck, bracketed and replayed, because the element records every command with its parameters, reads and writes. The "Biology: enrichment" family with a gene-list input and two numbers is a new Browse family, a many-targets slot (a set, the selection, ticked search rows or a list pasted on the laptop and sent through the Inbox, with a match count) plus two number slots, and a ranked reader. In a recipe, its gene-list slot defaults to Ask, because it names data.
- **A new layout.** A value in the layout slot, its engine in the engine slot, its 8 options in the generated form; "lay out [set] as [it]" keeps the rest in place.
- **A new reader or writer.** A reader is a value in the Open line's format slot with its options form; a writer is a value in the export line's format slot with its own.
- **A new object type** (graphty's own team adds it; plugins cannot). It declares its verbs with a priority (the strip shows the top six, More the rest), its pick target in the scene or in a reader, its slot kind (so any slot that takes it lights it during slot picking), and its sentence templates, which come from the commands that create and change it. Its lines join the record, its changes can be struck and replayed, and About this lists what was done to it. No new gesture or surface.
- **A new kind of option.** Until a picker exists for it, it is a text slot with the descriptor's description under it; the line still parses it and the record still shows it.
- **A third-party plugin.** Whatever registers through the six public calls with descriptors is in the line, Browse, the slot pickers, the readers and the record at once, with no headset code. The sentence for a plugin's run is generated ("Ran [plain name] on [target] with [options]") until graphty's own wording exists for it.
- **The practical limit.** The scheme is as good as the dependency facts behind the record: if a plugin command reads something it does not declare, the record cannot light the lines that depend on it, and an edit upstream will not replay it. Reads are tracked at the session's accessors: each command is handed a snapshot view holding only the columns and results its descriptor declares, so an undeclared read fails loudly in development instead of going unrecorded, and a command that declares none is marked "inputs unknown" and replays on every upstream edit. That is two real obligations on a plugin author, not zero: declare every column and result the command reads, and, to run off the frame thread, register a module the worker can load (an ES module URL in the descriptor). A plugin registered only on the main thread still runs, sliced across frames under the 8 ms budget with a "runs on the main thread -- slower" note on its Check card. The other limit is spatial work that is not a sentence: drawing a pattern, outlining a callout by hand, placing many nodes in a shape. Those get a sentence that arms one gesture (a box, a drag per node) or go to the 2D page.

Engineering: the record, drawer and readers are in-scene text panels (no DOM overlay in immersive VR), with one panel toolkit for rows, chips, steppers, sliders, clause rows, the generated form and the keyboard. Reads are tracked at the level of columns and results asked for through the session's accessors, never by tracing typed-array access, which would cost algorithm speed. The frame budget: the frame thread only draws and moves the ray; algorithm runs and replays execute in a worker (a plugin's only when it registers a worker-loadable module; otherwise sliced on the main thread) (or graphty-element's WebGPU path where it exists) with results swapped in when ready, slider counts come from a sorted copy of the column read, "live" graph preview happens only when an update fits about 8 ms or can be spread across frames, and lighting what a strip's line changed toggles one precomputed highlight layer rather than repainting. Saved state: a project saves the parsed data of every Open line and every line's result columns, so reopening replays nothing and a later edit replays only the lines downstream of it; in memory it keeps at most 8 full checkpoints (positions, filters, selection, layer stack), evicting the oldest, and Show the state here on an evicted line recomputes from the nearest checkpoint, with its cost on the Check card when over 2 seconds. The sentence wording is the app's, written from the element's neutral command codes and parameters.

### Why it should work

- Microsoft Power Query (in Excel and Power BI) records every transformation as a named step in an "Applied Steps" pane; any step's settings can be reopened and changed, later steps re-evaluate, inserting or deleting a step warns that later steps may be affected, and the whole list refreshes on next week's data. That is this scheme's record, ripple and recipe, shipped to millions of spreadsheet users.
- OpenRefine keeps an undo and redo history of every operation with its parameters, and lets a span of it be extracted as a file and applied to another project: a recipe made from history rather than recorded on purpose.
- Parametric CAD edits the past: Autodesk Fusion 360's design timeline and SolidWorks' feature tree let a person change an old feature's value and recompute everything after it, marking features that fail. Blender's "Adjust Last Operation" panel keeps the newest command's values editable after it ran.
- Reactive notebooks (Observable) re-run exactly the cells that depend on a changed cell; the record's dependency edges and replay follow that model.
- Selective undo -- undoing one earlier action while keeping later ones -- was studied by Berlage (ACM TOCHI, 1994), who found it understandable when the dependencies are visible; editable graphical histories go back to Kurlander and Feiner (1988), and Heer, Mackinlay, Stolte and Agrawala ("Graphical Histories for Visualization", IEEE InfoVis 2008) found analysts used a browsable history to revisit and communicate their work. VisTrails (Bavoil and colleagues, 2005) keeps visualization provenance as editable, replayable steps.
- "Check your answers" before a commit is the GOV.UK design system's pattern for multi-step services, chosen because it cuts errors on submission; One Question at a Time (30) earned the studio's best webxr score with it.
- Lightroom's develop history and its "Previous" and Sync settings show that people re-apply last time's settings to new material as one act, which is the weekly repeat.
- Our own screening: the history hard case and "re-run with one option changed" are where the strongest finalists are weakest, and Cutting Room (23) was kept for its mode model alone; this scheme keeps that mode model while removing its tracks, playhead, tools and stick shuttle, the parts reviewers found hardest to learn.

### What it would teach us

- Do people who are not programmers or editors read the record as "what I did" and trust that pinching an old chip changes the past -- and do they predict the result from the ripple bar before Apply?
- How often do people use Undo against Strike only this and slot edits, and does "Undo takes the newest line" ever surprise someone who meant the last thing they looked at (step 10)?
- Is one list enough at 200 lines, with search, kind chips, About this and folded brackets, or do people lose their place?
- Do analysts save recipes and run them next week, and how often does a replayed recipe hit a missing column or a broken line?
- Does lighting dependents before a strike prevent "I removed PageRank and lost my colors" mistakes, or do people strike without reading?
- How much replay can a person wait for: at what graph size and replay time does a slot edit stop feeling like editing and start feeling like re-running?
- Does Keep both make comparing runs habitual, or does the record fill with twins nobody compares?
- Act counts for the six stress journeys against the 2D app, and for the persona steps that cost 11 to 17 acts in other designs (a weighted batch, a composite score, a two-clause rule).

### Known risks

- **Replay cost.** Changing an early line on a 10,000-node graph with slow runs after it is not live, and cannot be made live without abandoning the idea that any old slot is editable. Mitigation: replay only the lines that depend on the change; saved results and in-memory checkpoints; counts live on every tick, the graph live only within the frame budget, a ghost value and an estimate above it; the Check card for anything over 2 seconds; every run and replay in a worker with Cancel in the line, and the record shows which lines have caught up.
- **Dependencies the element cannot see.** If a command reads something it does not record (a plugin, a style that reads a result by name), an upstream edit will not replay it and its line will not light. Mitigation: graphty-element hands each command a snapshot view of the columns and results it declares and records those as its reads (never by tracing typed-array access); a line whose inputs are unknown shows "inputs unknown" and is always treated as depending on everything before it.
- **Editing the past surprises people.** Mitigation: nothing in the past changes on a pinch alone (a pinch on a "runs now" verb does act on the present, and says so on the verb); an old slot's change previews on the graph and counts its ripple, including what changes meaning, before Apply; anything that replays a run, breaks a line, rebinds a pick or removes data stops on the Check card; Undo takes the edit back as one line; struck lines are never deleted.
- **Three histories in one.** The red team's attack on this hybrid: Undo, the record and the journal could disagree. Mitigation: there is one list. Undo takes back its newest line, edits and strikes are lines, the journal is the same list with defaults expanded, and recipes are brackets of it. The only other history is the view's one-step Back, which never touches the project.
- **A long record.** Hundreds of lines bury the one you want. Mitigation: consecutive selections fold; recipes and sweeps fold into one bracket row; search over values, kind chips, About this, Hide struck and Hide edit lines; a filter or layer chip jumps to its line.
- **A broken cascade.** Striking or changing an early line can gray out many later ones. Mitigation: the strip states the count before the act ("3 lines use it: 1 turns gray"); gray lines keep their values and offer fixes; Restore or Undo brings everything back in one act.
- **The wrong target.** A sentence runs on the selection when the graph was meant, or the reverse. Mitigation: one rule (the line starts on the whole graph, a strip starts on its object), the target as the first chip, counts in the preview line, and the selection named by its line.
- **Accidental drags.** In round 1 a pinch that drifted 3 degrees moved and pinned a node unnoticed (the old step 9). Now a pinch that starts on a node never drags; moving a node takes the Move verb (or a controller's grip plus trigger) and shows "Moved and pinned -- Strike" at the node; the drag threshold is 2 cm of hand travel; a box needs Select region; tracking loss cancels a drag. The remaining accident is a small unwanted turn of the graph from a drag on empty space, which is never written and Back undoes.
- **Text entry.** Notes, names, ids and reports. Mitigation: suggested names everywhere; part-matching ids on a digit row; sentence tiles with a caret; dictation where a recognizer exists; a paired keyboard; Continue on laptop. A paragraph in the headset is still slow, and Vision Pro has no system keyboard in session.
- **The laptop relay is new infrastructure.** Without it, files from the laptop must be opened on the 2D page before entering VR. Mitigation: the relay is small and stateless, carries no file contents, and the record makes the day-one path less painful than elsewhere, because an Open line made on the 2D page can be re-pointed and replayed later.
- **Dense tables and precise reading.** Readers hold about 20 rows by 6 columns. Mitigation: sort, column choice, select-from and filter-from replace scanning; text size and precision settings; tables can be exported to the laptop.
- **Neck turns between record, graph and readers.** The record sits 25 degrees to one side and readers 25 degrees to the other. Mitigation: verb strips open at the object, so most acts need no look at the record; the drawer sits between record and graph; a setting puts the record below the graph for seated use; Bring here re-centers.
- **Element work.** Editable, replayable history with dependency facts is a capability graphty-element does not have today (it keeps an undo stack and a journal). It is the largest build cost of this prototype, and it belongs in the element, not the app, so every consumer gets it.
- **Judgment does not replay.** A recipe or a re-pointed Open line can re-evaluate every derivation, but a group picked because it looked like a ring, or a ringleader chosen by reading, has no rule behind it. The record stops and asks again over the new result ("Ask there", "changes meaning"); it never carries the old pick forward silently. Recipes whose value is mostly such judgments save fewer acts than the weekly repeat suggests: stress journey 6's recipe run asks four times.
- **Binding by derivation can still pick the wrong thing.** "The group holding ACC-48213" is right only while that account stays in the ring. Mitigation: every Follow shows the old and new membership on the Check card ("23 before, 26 now, all 23 kept"), and a Follow that keeps under half the old members stops as "changes meaning" instead.
- **What left the project cannot be called back, and prose is checked by what it read, not by what it claims.** The record can keep the lines an exported picture or a sent note came from, mark them out of date and default to Keep both, but it cannot recall a file already attached to a case, and it does not understand sentences: a note is flagged when its target changes (Ring 7's members, the run it sits on), not when a claim in it ("share four devices") stops being true for a reason outside that target. Fixing that would mean parsing the analyst's prose, which is not this design. Mitigation: dated methods that say which values produced which output, and Reread it, which shows the note beside the old and new numbers.
- **Keep both by default lengthens the record.** Every edit that reaches an output adds a folded bracket rather than replacing lines. Mitigation: the bracket folds to one row named by its edit and date, Hide superseded (in the record's header) hides lines with a newer twin, and recipes leave superseded twins out.
