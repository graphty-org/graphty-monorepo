**Prototype 46: Fast Ring over Pages** -- Two speeds on one object-then-verb pattern: pinch and hold any node, edge, chip or row (the whole graph and the selection each have a chip of their own, so scope is never guessed from where a pinch lands) and a ring of its six commonest verbs opens below the graph with the target named at its center and a count on every verb; a beginner holds and reads, an expert flicks the same way without waiting; sliding further along an arm raises its count (hops, top N, routes) live; a verb that needs a value offers three or four counted outcomes with the current state as one of them; and the ring's top arm always opens the object's page, which holds every verb and every generated form. Hands alone, no voice, arms low: the hand rests and presses, while the head (Quest and Galaxy XR hands) or the eyes (Vision Pro) aim.

**Setup.** Fixed environment: solid colored background in full VR; the graph floats at chest height about 75 cm away inside a volume about 45 cm across. Directly below the graph, about 55 cm from the eyes and 25 to 30 degrees below eye level, is the command spot, where rings and choice strips open; below it the status strip; under the graph's base the base bar. The page panel (about 50 cm wide, 40 cm tall, text at least 1.3 cm tall, rows at least 2.5 cm) stands left of the graph at the same distance, angled toward you, its center 10 degrees below eye level, so a glance of about 25 degrees goes between page and graph; a setting docks it below the status strip instead, tilted up like a lectern, which spares a seated neck the sideways turn. Devices: Meta Quest 3 / 3S, Samsung Galaxy XR, Apple Vision Pro. Inputs: tracked hands everywhere, and the whole scheme is designed for them; eyes only where the platform aims a pinch with them (Vision Pro's transient pointer); controllers on Quest and Galaxy XR as an optional mapping; no voice anywhere. The hand rests on the thigh or a desk in front of you and only presses; something else aims. On Vision Pro the gaze at the moment of the pinch aims. On Quest and Galaxy XR hands the default is Head aim: a small dot at the center of view (the head ray, which every headset exposes) is the aim point, and a pinch from the resting hand presses on whatever the dot is on; the 2-degree cone and the "which one?" list absorb head-aim error, and the graph's 45 cm width is about 33 degrees, so aiming is a head turn of at most about 16 degrees. If Chrome on Galaxy XR passes gaze as a transient pointer, the Vision Pro mapping applies there instead. A setting, Hand ray, uses the system hand ray instead; it is honest about its posture: the ray runs from the shoulder through the hand, so the forearm floats at belly height, off the leg, for each aim. Every ring is steered by 2 to 12.5 cm of relative hand travel from where the pinch began, so with Head aim or gaze the forearm can stay on the leg. Either hand does everything; one hand is enough for every operation; a second hand is used only to scale the graph from the base bar, which also has a one-handed path. Text goes through graphty's in-scene keyboard (letters, a digit row, a caret, completions from the data), suggested values first, a paired Bluetooth keyboard where the browser passes keys through, or the paired laptop (below). Files and long text cross through a laptop pairing that is part of the design: graphty's 2D page on the laptop and the headset page pair once by a six-digit code, the laptop page grants one folder, and the headset's Inbox and Outbox read and write that folder without the headset coming off.

Its labels, like those on strips, toasts and tags, are at least 1 degree tall and grow with the Text size setting (the ring grows with them); a strip wider than 35 degrees wraps into two rows. ### The pages

The page panel is Graph Browser's (28) reader, unchanged in its anatomy: an address bar that finds object pages and verbs by any part of a name (plain or technical: "katz" finds "Influence at a distance (Katz)"; "48213" finds ACC-48213), never running anything by itself; Back and Forward; a breadcrumb; a page in four sections -- Title, Facts, Verbs, Links -- for every object kind (graph, node, edge, selection, set, rule, run, algorithm, layout, attribute, style layer, filter step, note, view, network, project, history, journal, recipe, settings, help); and a minimap. The panel shows the page of the last thing you quick-pinched or the page a ring's Up arm opened; a pin on the panel keeps one page while you work elsewhere.

### The journey

1. **Enter.**
    - **What you do:** On graphty's 2D page, open the Florentine families sample and choose Enter VR. Put the headset on, seated, and rest your dominant hand on your thigh. (Starting in the headset with nothing loaded, the empty volume holds one card at the command spot, "Start with a sample", beside the start page in the panel; the first-run hint waits until a graph has loaded.)
    - **What you see:** The 16 families float in the volume, Pucci alone at the edge. Under the graph, the base bar (turn handle, Fit, View back, View forward, View), and below it the status strip, whose first chip is the graph chip: "Florentine families -- 16 of 16", then "nothing selected", Undo, Home. Holdable chips carry a small ring glyph. The filter chips on the left read "No filters"; the layer chips on the right read "Default look". The page panel to the left shows the graph page. A one-time hint at the command spot reads, on Quest and Galaxy XR hands, "Look at something -- the dot shows what -- then pinch and hold to see what you can do with it. Your hand can rest on your leg." (on Vision Pro: "Look at something, then pinch and hold..."), with a Tour link (five steps on the graph, about 2 minutes, skippable, and on Help later).
    - **Controls:** none.

2. **Get oriented.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch-drag the base bar's turn handle a quarter turn. Then aim at the graph chip, the first chip on the status strip, pinch and hold, and read the ring before sliding up to Page and releasing.
    - **What you see:** The ring opens below the graph: at its center "Whole graph -- 16 families, 20 marriages, 2 pieces (Pucci alone)"; around it Page (up), Find, Run, Filter, Color by, Region, Save, and a dimmed Repeat ("nothing to repeat yet"). On release the panel shows the graph page: density 0.17, average 2.5 marriages, undirected, and suggested analyses as links. View back on the base bar would undo the turn if you overdid it.
    - **Controls:** pinch-drag (turn handle); hold, slide, release (graph chip's ring, Page).

3. **Find the Medici.**
    - **What you do:** Aim at Medici and quick-pinch. (In a graph too big to see it: hold the graph chip, slide left to Find, type "med" on the in-scene keyboard, pinch the result.)
    - **What you see:** Medici is outlined and selected; the status strip reads "1 selected: Medici"; the panel turns to "Node: Medici -- 6 marriages", its Verbs section starting Neighbors, Route to..., Hide, Style this..., Move and pin, Note.... Had two labels sat within the pointing cone, the "which one?" list would have opened with "Medici MA" and "Tornabuoni TA".
    - **Controls:** quick pinch (or graph chip's ring Find, keyboard, pinch).

4. **Who they married into.**
    - **What you do:** Pinch and hold Medici. Slide left onto Neighbors, keep sliding to the next rung to see 2 hops, come back to the first, release.
    - **What you see:** The center reads "Medici -- node -- 6 marriages"; the left arm reads "Neighbors 6". Resting there lights Acciaiuoli, Albizzi, Barbadori, Ridolfi, Salviati and Tornabuoni in the graph above. The next rung reads "2 hops: 12 families, 14 marriages" and lights five more; back to the first, release. A hull wraps the seven; the status strip reads "7 selected -- 7 marriages among them"; the toast reads "Selected Medici and 6 neighbors -- Undo". An expert does the same with a 0.2 s flick left; the arm still lights and shows "6" as the hand passes.
    - **Controls:** hold, slide along the arm (rungs), release.

5. **Who matters most (PageRank).**
    - **What you do:** Pinch and hold the graph chip; slide right to Run and release. On the strip, pinch "Influence (PageRank)".
    - **What you see:** The center reads "Whole graph -- 16 families (the 7 selected stay selected)": the graph chip always means the whole graph, and the selection's own ring is reached only from the selection chip beside it, whose Run arm says "Run on 7 selected". The strip reads "As is | Influence (PageRank) -- under 1 s | Communities (Louvain) -- under 1 s | Bridges (betweenness) -- under 1 s | All 29 algorithms...". PageRank runs with damping 0.85; a "PageRank" chip joins the status strip; the toast reads "Ran PageRank on 16 families -- Undo"; the panel lands on the run page.
    - **Controls:** hold, slide, release (graph chip's ring, Run); pinch a strip chip.

6. **Read the result.**
    - **What you do:** Read the run page in the panel. Pinch Guadagni's row.

11. **Save.**
    - **What you do:** Hold the graph chip; slide down-right to Save; release. Pinch the first suggested name.

    - **Controls:** hold, slide, release (graph chip's ring, Save); pinch a strip chip.

### Advanced journeys

- **Compound rule.** Hold the graph chip, Filter, then "Build a rule..." on the strip. The rule form is 28's rows under ALL OF, ANY OF and NONE OF groups, with two additions. Every value field offers counted chips before the keypad ("degree above 5: 1,980 | above 10: 640 | above 20: 412 | Exact"), and every attribute row shows how many elements have a value. "Start from a node" (pinch a node or a tag) fills one clause per attribute of that node as chips with their counts, and you pinch out the ones you do not want. "type is account AND (degree above 20 OR risk_score at least 0.8) AND created in the last 30 days" is: start from a flagged account (type and created filled), change "created on" to "in the last 30 days" from its chips, add an ANY OF group with two chips. A live line reads "matches 41 of 9,812". A week later: hold the rule's filter chip, Edit rule, and the same rows reopen.
- **Tuning algorithm options.** Hold a Louvain run chip, slide to Re-run: the strip reads "As is: resolution 1.0, 4 groups | 0.5: 3 groups | 1.5: 5 groups | 2.0: 6 groups | Sweep these | Exact..." (the graph is small, so each outcome was computed before the strip opened). Pinch 1.5 to keep both runs, or Sweep these for three more. Katz's 7 options: Re-run's Form band, or Exact, opens its form with tolerance as decade 1e-6 plus mantissa, the weight attribute as a list of amounts columns, the rest under Advanced. ForceAtlas2's 13 options are the same form, longer, with Advanced folding half. Once the form is submitted, a release on the ring's Re-run arm runs with those values, and Repeat runs them on another run chip or another set.
- **Comparing two runs.** Hold a run chip, slide right to Compare: the strip lists the other runs of a comparable kind with their difference already counted ("Louvain 1.5: 5 groups, 3 families move | Leiden 1.0: 4 groups, 0 move"). Pinch one: the difference page opens, chosen by field kind (a rank-change table for rankings; an overlap table for groups). "Open side by side" splits the graph into two smaller views with Same camera, Same positions and Same selection switches, and each view carries its own view-only filter chip ("this view: disease only"), so two conditions can each show their own edges.
- **Layers on subsets.** Hold the selection chip, slide up-right to Style this: the strip's first chip keeps the selection as a set with a suggested name ("Style as set 'Medici and 6 neighbors'"), the layer chip is named after that set and targets it, and its edit strip opens. - **Repeat on new targets.** Hide one isolated family, then hold each other isolated family and slide down: "Repeat: Hide" (a flick down only opens Repeat's strip, one pinch more). Step the time window back a week with the time chip's Step back rung, then hold and slide down on the same chip to step another week. Undo the last Hide and Repeat forgets it: the arm then offers the command before it. Note one node in a set, then Repeat's Form band on the next node opens the note form with the first note's words, ready to edit.
- **Undoing an old step among look-alikes.** Home, History: entries are named by object and values ("Filter: PageRank at least 0.07", "Style: PageRank layer, size 1x to 3x"), each with its tag. Hold the entry eight steps back, slide right to Revert only this step: the strip reads "As is | Revert only this: filter removed, 7 later steps kept, showing 16 of 16 | Exact..." and, on the down-right arm, Go back to here's strip reads "As is | Go back to here: drops 7 later steps (kept in Redo)". Pressing either chip previews it on the graph before you pick it.
- **Typing an id (fraud ring).** Hold the graph chip, Find. Hold the account (holding a selected node still opens its node ring), Route to: the aim point wears a crosshair, the command spot reads "Pick the target: pinch a node or a tag, or Find -- Cancel", and a strip offers recent nodes ("ACC-10077 -- found 2 min ago"). Pinch it; the route page lists the steps; the Route arm's rungs give 2 or 3 routes.

### How a new feature plugs in

- **A new algorithm with options.** Its descriptor puts it in its family section on the catalog page (a new family makes a new section), in the address bar under both names, in the Run strip once it is suggested or recently used, and in Run several. Its options render on the generated form; its main option (the first non-advanced number, or one the descriptor marks) feeds the Re-run strip's nearby values; a logarithmic option gets decade plus mantissa. Its result fields choose their readers, and its run chip gets the standard ring, so Paint, Filter from, Select from, Compare and Repeat work with no new code. The "Biology: enrichment" family with a gene-list input and two numbers is a new section, a form whose list input is a set slot (the selection or any set; a long pasted list arrives through the laptop pairing), two steppers, and a ranked reader.
- **A new layout.** A link on the Layouts page with its engines, a generated form, Re-run and Stop on the job chip if it is iterative.
- **A new data source or format writer.** A reader appears in Open and Add data with its options form; a writer in Export with its options form; a service that turns a gene list into a network takes the same set slot.
- **A new object type.** graphty's own team declares it once: a page template (facts, verbs, links), its pick target in the scene or its chip on a surface, and its six ring verbs, each assigned to one of the eight direction classes (Left explore, Right compute, Up-left show or hide, Up-right style, Down-left keep or arrange, Down-right note or end). Up (Page) and Down (Repeat) come free. No gesture, glyph or surface is designed for it.
- **A third-party plugin.** It never takes a ring position, so learned flicks stay valid: plugin verbs on existing objects join those objects' pages after the built-in verbs and the Actions group of the address bar. A person can put any page verb on a ring arm of their own (hold the verb on the page, "Put on ring"), which changes only their own rings.
- **The practical limit.** Six fast verbs per object; everything else is one more act (Page) and then reading. A strip holds at most four outcomes, so a choice among many is a page list (saved views, for one). A newly declared verb with no dry-run preview shows its label and cost only, and a destructive one still goes through the before-and-after strip. Counted outcomes need the counts to be cheap; on large graphs, outcomes that are expensive show their cost and value instead of a count, and the fast layer becomes a little less telling exactly when the graph is big.

### Why it should work

- Marking menus were designed for exactly this two-speed use: Kurtenbach and Buxton ("The limits of expert performance using hierarchic marking menus", CHI 1993) showed that novices who wait for the menu and experts who flick without it make the same movement, so practice moves people from one to the other; Kurtenbach, Sellen and Buxton ("An empirical evaluation of some articulatory and cognitive aspects of marking menus", Human-Computer Interaction 1993) found the switch happens with use, not training. Gebhardt and colleagues ("Extended pie menus for immersive virtual environments", IEEE TVCG 2013) carried them into headsets.
- The usual cost of a fast layer is a dip when people switch from the slow path to the fast one; Scarr, Cockburn, Gutwin and Quinn ("Dips and ceilings: understanding and supporting transitions to expertise in user interfaces", CHI 2011) name it and recommend fast paths that are rehearsed by the slow path. Holding the ring rehearses the flick.
- The Hotbox (Kurtenbach, Fitzmaurice, Owen and Baudel, CHI 1999) and Maya's option box showed that one press can both run a command with its last settings and open its full form, chosen by where you release; Blender's Repeat Last and Adjust Last Operation, and vi's dot command (Bill Joy, 1976), made repeating the last command on a new target a one-key habit.
- Counts before the choice steer people: Flamenco (Yee, Swearingen, Li and Hearst, CHI 2003) showed that faceted links with counts let people narrow without dead ends; choosing among side-by-side outcomes with the current one kept as a choice follows exposure bracketing in cameras and Microsoft Office's Live Preview (2007).
- Magnified lists for ambiguous picks select cluttered small targets more accurately than ray casting (Kopper, Bacim and Bowman, "Rapid and accurate 3D selection by progressive refinement", IEEE 3DUI 2011); printed two-letter hints are long shipped in keyboard browsing (Vimium, 2009), turning a hard pointing task into reading a large label.
- Pinching from a resting hand with the target chosen by gaze was faster and less tiring than reaching (Pfeuffer, Mayer, Mardanbegi and Gellersen, "Gaze + Pinch Interaction in Virtual Reality", SUI 2017), which visionOS then shipped; relative steering of a held pinch is how Vision Pro's own pointer behaves.
- An object page with facts, verbs and generated forms as the complete fallback follows the browser and property-sheet tradition that Graph Browser (28) drew on (Shneiderman's "details on demand", 1996; generated property sheets in Visual Basic, 1991, and the Unity inspector); its screening put it at the top of the board with no criterion below 3.
- Arming destructive verbs as a before-and-after choice needs no hover, as iOS's swipe-to-delete (swipe arms, tap confirms) and Variant Grid's (43) outcome pairs showed.

### What it would teach us

- Whether people move from holding to flicking on their own, how many uses that takes per verb, and whether flick errors between adjacent arms (Neighbors and Hide) stay rare enough that a toast and Undo are enough.
- Whether the eight direction classes help experts guess where a verb is on a ring they have not used, or whether people learn each object's ring separately.
- How often people go up to Page, and which verbs earn a ring arm: the ratio of ring acts to page acts per journey.
- Whether counted outcome strips cover most value choices, or whether "Exact..." is pressed most of the time for thresholds and options; where the offered cut points miss what people want.
- Whether Repeat (a fixed arm on every ring) is used, and for which commands.
- Whether relative steering from a hand on the thigh is reliable on Quest's hand tracking for 30 to 60 minutes, and what travel distances suit hands, controllers and Vision Pro's transient pointer.
- Whether Tags and the "which one?" list make 400- and 3,000-node graphs pickable without zooming, and how often each appears.
- Whether counts can be computed in time on 10,000-node graphs, and how much a ring that shows a cost instead of a count loses.
- How many acts the six stress journeys take compared with the desktop app and with Graph Browser (28) alone, which is the measure of what the fast layer buys.

### Known risks

- **Two layers to learn.** The ring and the page could feel like two interfaces. Mitigation: the ring's six verbs are the page's first six verbs in the same words, the Up arm is always the page, and the page's rows open the same rings; one pattern (object, then verb) at two depths.
- **Flick errors between adjacent arms.** A fast flick drifts into the neighboring arm (step 10). Mitigation: 45-degree arms with 10 degrees of hysteresis, the lit arm's label and count shown even during a flick, destructive verbs that never run from an arm (they open a before-and-after strip), a toast naming every step with Undo, and the flick's sensitivity as a setting.
- **Commit on release when tracking flickers.** Mitigation: a release commits only after 100 ms of continuous joint poses (WebXR gives no confidence value, so continuity is the test, tuned per device) and 80 ms on the lit arm; a lost hand or pinch cancels with a toast, and every drag snaps back; dropouts under 0.1 s are ignored on Quest and Galaxy XR and always cancel on Vision Pro.
- **A select pinch that drifts.** A hand settling after a pinch moves 1 to 3 cm, usually down, where Repeat is. Mitigation: one threshold (2 cm) separates a select from a hold, a flick needs 4 cm and a speed floor, anything between waits as a hold, and Repeat, Save and destructive verbs never run from a flick.
- **Head aim costs head movement.** Aiming with the head turns the neck up to about 16 degrees across the graph for every pick. That is the price of a resting arm on Quest and Galaxy XR hands, which expose no gaze; the Hand ray setting trades it back for a lifted forearm, and controllers aim with a wrist tilt. Whether head aim over 30 to 60 minutes is less tiring than a lifted forearm has to be tested.
- **Hands low drop out of view.** A hand resting at your side or far down on the lap can leave Quest's camera view. Mitigation: the hint asks for the hand on the thigh in front; a lost hand cancels; controllers rest anywhere. A quick pinch counts only after 200 ms of a still, open hand, so curled resting fingers do not select.
- **Tremor and slow hands.** A 0.3 s hold under 2 cm of travel can be hard to meet. Mitigation: the Tap ring setting: a quick pinch on an object opens its ring and leaves it open, and a second quick pinch on an arm (aimed by the ray or gaze) picks it; rungs become plus and minus chips; travel distances, hold time and dead zone are settings.
- **Counts that cost too much, and ghosts that drop frames.** Counts for hiding, neighbors, filters and cut points are cheap; outcomes of algorithm variants are not; and restyling 9,000 nodes every time the lit arm changes would drop frames while the hand steers. Mitigation: the cost estimator decides; expensive outcomes show value and cost, never a guessed count; counts are computed off the main thread; a ghost appears only after 150 ms on one arm, fades rather than flashes, becomes an outline under Reduce motion, and is drawn as a preview overlay, not a style-layer repaint.
- **graphty-element has to answer "what would this do".** Counts and ghosts before commit need a dry-run preview from graphty-element (how many elements a hide, filter, selection or style would touch), and per-view filters in a split view need a view-only visibility apart from filters. These are graphty-element capabilities that every consumer would want, so they belong there: a dry-run count, a preview overlay written once per change, and view-only visibility. Until they exist, ring arms show labels and costs without counts or ghosts, and split views share one filter. That is the honest day-one ring: the counted feedforward this design leans on arrives with those capabilities, and on a 10,000-node graph some counts will still arrive late or show a cost instead.
- **The pairing is a service graphty does not run yet, and files and long text depend on it.** Inbox, Outbox and laptop typing need a small relay (or WebRTC signaling) that the graphty site hosts, a six-digit code, encryption keyed to it, and the File System Access API on the laptop (Chromium; elsewhere files are dropped onto the 2D page one at a time). Mitigation: the headset browser's own file picker and downloads folder work today, at the cost of leaving the session; graphty saves the session before leaving and resumes it on return. Without the relay, files and long text are weak and the last step of publishing (moving the files to a laptop) means taking the headset off. That cannot be fixed inside the headset, only by building the relay.
- **A monitor in a headset.** Long work moves to the page panel. Mitigation: the common acts never leave the graph and the command spot below it, the page follows what you pinched, and a result's rows light their nodes.
- **Six arms are not enough for some objects.** A run has nine common verbs. Mitigation: the ring holds the six most used, Page holds every verb one act away, and a person can put their own choice on a ring arm.
- **The command spot is below the graph.** Reading a ring means a glance down from a node near the top of the graph. Mitigation: the target is named at the ring's center with a leader line; experts flick without reading; a setting moves the command spot beside the graph instead.
- **Text entry.** Mitigation: suggested names everywhere a name is needed, part matching for ids, typed tags, the in-scene keyboard with a caret, sentence tiles, the paired laptop's keyboard for touch typists, and the 2D page for reports.

### Studio revisions

**Round 1.**

The red team's top change, adopted: scope is never inferred from where a pinch lands.

- Holding a node or an edge always opens that node's or edge's ring, selected or not.
- The whole graph's ring opens only from the graph chip (the first chip on the status strip, showing the count), and the selection's only from the selection chip.
- Hulls are drawn but never picked, and a pinch on empty space inside the graph does nothing. The first stray drag there makes the base bar's turn handle glow with "Turn the graph here".
- This removes the hull that swallowed "empty space", the selected node that opened the selection's ring, and Run flicks that ran on the selection.
